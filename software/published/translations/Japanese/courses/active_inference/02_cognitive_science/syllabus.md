## Course Description

This course examines how Active Inference is implemented in the brain and expressed through behavior. Building on the philosophical foundations from Course 1, we explore the neural correlates of prediction, precision, and attention; the neuroscience of perception, motor control, and learning; clinical applications in psychiatry and neurology; and the developmental trajectory from infancy to adulthood. Each module connects Active Inference theory to empirical findings in cognitive neuroscience, behavioral psychology, and clinical science.

---

## Prerequisites

- Course 1: The Philosophy of Active Inference (or equivalent familiarity with Markov Blankets, generative models, free energy, and the perception-action loop)
- Basic familiarity with neuroscience terminology (neurons, synapses, cortex) is helpful but not required; key terms are introduced as needed

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you should be able to:

1. **Identify** the neural substrates associated with key Active Inference processes (prediction, precision, model updating)
2. **Explain** how predictive coding in the visual cortex implements variational inference
3. **Analyze** clinical conditions (ADHD, autism, schizophrenia, anxiety, depression) as specific aberrations of precision or model updating
4. **Describe** how synaptic plasticity, neuromodulation, and sleep implement the learning dynamics of Active Inference
5. **Apply** the Active Inference framework to interpret empirical findings from fMRI, EEG, and behavioral studies
6. **Evaluate** the strengths and limitations of Active Inference as a unifying theory of brain function

| Final Project | Empirical integration paper or clinical case portfolio | End of course |

### Final Project Options

1. **Clinical Case Portfolio**: Analyze 3 clinical conditions through the Active Inference lens, specifying the proposed precision or model aberration for each
2. **Empirical Review**: Review 5-7 empirical papers that test predictions of Active Inference and evaluate the strength of evidence
3. **Experimental Proposal**: Design a novel experiment that would test a specific prediction of Active Inference about brain function or behavior
