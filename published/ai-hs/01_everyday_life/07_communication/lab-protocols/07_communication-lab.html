Lab: The Whispering Gallery -- How We Synchronize Our Models

Objective

Explore how communication works as a process of synchronizing internal models between agents, and investigate why miscommunication happens when models diverge.

Prerequisites

  • Completed modules on Systems through Learning
  • Read the module on Model Synchronization

Part 1: Telephone Game Analysis

Goal: Experience how generative models drift when signals pass between multiple agents.

Play the telephone game (also called "broken telephone") with at least 4 people:

  1. Person 1 whispers a detailed sentence to Person 2.
  2. Each person whispers what they heard to the next person.
  3. The last person says the sentence aloud.

Record the original and final sentences:

Stage Message
Original message
Person 2 heard
Person 3 heard
Person 4 heard
Final message

Analysis: 1. Where did the biggest distortion occur? 2. Why did each person's generative model "fill in" different details? 3. How does this relate to the Active Inference idea that perception is prediction?

Part 3: Empathy as Prediction

Goal: Practice using your generative model of another person's internal states.

Watch a short video clip (a movie scene, a news interview, or a conversation between friends) with the sound off. Based only on visual cues:

  1. What emotions do you think each person is feeling? Why?
  2. What internal states are you inferring (latent states you cannot observe directly)?
  3. Now watch with sound. How accurate were your predictions?
  4. What does this tell you about empathy as a form of social inference?

Part 4: Shared Narrative Exercise

Goal: Build a shared generative model with a partner.

With a partner, take turns adding one sentence to a collaborative story. Each person must build on what the other said while staying consistent. After 10 sentences:

  1. Did you develop a shared model of the story's world?
  2. Where did your models of the story diverge?
  3. How did you resolve disagreements about where the story was going?
  4. How is this like real-world communication, where two people must align their models?
