Lab: Biological Boundaries -- Cells, Organs, and the Markov Blanket of Life

Objective

Investigate how biological systems at multiple scales (cells, organs, body systems) maintain boundaries that separate their internal states from the environment, and connect these boundaries to the concept of the Markov blanket.

Prerequisites

  • Completed the Everyday Life section on Systems
  • Read the module on Biological Boundaries
  • Basic biology knowledge (cell structure, organ systems)

Part 3: Nested Boundaries

Goal: Explore how biological systems are nested inside each other.

Trace the nesting from smallest to largest:

  1. Organelle (e.g., mitochondrion): What is its boundary? What does it regulate?
  2. Cell: How does the cell boundary contain organelles while having its own blanket?
  3. Tissue: How do cells organize into a tissue with a collective boundary?
  4. Organ: How does the organ boundary emerge from tissues?
  5. Body: How does skin function as the outermost Markov blanket?

Draw or describe this nesting structure. How does each level maintain its own homeostasis while contributing to the level above?
