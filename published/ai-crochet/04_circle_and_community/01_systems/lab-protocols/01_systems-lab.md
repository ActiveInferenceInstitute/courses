# Lab: Mapping Your Circle's Boundaries

## Group Activity — Systems

### Objective

Work together as a circle to map the system boundaries of your own group, identifying internal states, external states, and the Markov blanket that separates them.

### Materials

- Large sheet of paper or poster board
- Markers in three colors (one for internal, one for external, one for boundary)
- Sticky notes
- Your current crochet projects (to work on during discussion)

### Duration

45-60 minutes

### Setup

Gather your circle around a table with the large paper in the center. Choose three marker colors and assign them: one for internal states, one for external states, and one for blanket states.

### Activity Steps

**Step 1: Draw the Boundary (10 minutes)**

As a group, draw a large circle (or oval) on the paper. This represents the Markov blanket of your crochet circle. Discuss as you draw:
- What defines who is "in" the circle? Is it attendance? An invitation? Just showing up?
- Is the boundary sharp or fuzzy? (It is almost certainly fuzzy — that is fine. Mark the fuzzy zones.)
- Where does the boundary sit physically? (The meeting space? The group chat? The shared pattern library?)

**Step 2: Map Internal States (10 minutes)**

Using sticky notes and the "internal" color marker, identify and place inside the circle everything that belongs to the group's internal states:
- Shared knowledge and memories ("the time we all tried granny squares")
- Ongoing projects and group challenges
- Social bonds, inside jokes, shared vocabulary
- Group norms and unwritten rules
- Collective skills the group has developed together

Each person should contribute at least two sticky notes. Read them aloud as you place them.

**Step 3: Map External States (10 minutes)**

Using the "external" color, identify and place outside the circle:
- The wider crafting community
- Local yarn shops, craft stores, online retailers
- Online crochet communities, social media, YouTube
- Each member's life outside the circle
- Broader cultural trends in crafting

**Step 4: Map Blanket States (10 minutes)**

Using the "boundary" color, identify and place on the circle's edge the mechanisms through which inside and outside interact:
- Sensory states: How does outside information reach the group? (Members bring patterns, share social media finds, report on yarn shop visits)
- Active states: How does the group influence the outside world? (Teaching friends, selling at craft fairs, posting online, requesting items from yarn shops)
- Physical mediators: The meeting space, the group chat, shared digital folders

**Step 5: Discussion (10-15 minutes)**

While continuing to stitch, discuss as a group:
1. Were there items that were hard to classify? Why?
2. Look at the boundary — is it thick or thin? What does that say about how open or closed your circle is?
3. Imagine a new person joining next week. What would change on the map?
4. Imagine a core member leaving. What internal states would be at risk?
5. What does this map tell you about what holds your circle together?

### Reflection Prompts

Write individually (or discuss aloud) after the activity:
- What surprised you about the mapping exercise?
- Did you discover internal states you had not consciously recognized before?
- How does thinking about your circle as a "system" change how you understand its value or fragility?

### Extension

Take a photo of your completed map. At next week's meeting, revisit it briefly. Has anything changed? Has a new external state entered through the boundary? This is the circle as a living system — always in motion, always maintaining itself.
