Key Concepts

Day one +

The newcomer sits at the table, watches others stitch, listens to conversation, absorbs the pace and culture of the circle. They may not crochet at all, or they may work on simple chains. They are at the periphery.

Explore how Day one connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Week four +

The newcomer is working on a simple project — a dishcloth, a scarf in single crochet. They ask occasional questions. They begin to understand the circle's vocabulary and norms.

Month three +

The newcomer has moved inward. They contribute to conversations about technique, try new stitches with the group's support, and begin to develop their own style.

Year one +

The newcomer is now a full participant. They teach newer newcomers, contribute to group decisions, and carry a significant portion of the circle's shared knowledge.

Watching hands +

The newcomer sees how experienced members hold their hooks, tension their yarn, and move through stitch sequences. These observations build a visual and motor generative model — predictions about what the motions should look like — before the newcomer attempts the motions themselves.

Explore how Watching hands connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Watching projects +

Seeing the range of projects at the table (from simple to complex, from conventional to experimental) builds a model of what is possible and what the circle values.

Explore how Watching projects connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Watching interactions +

How people ask for help, how help is given, how mistakes are treated — all of these social observations build the newcomer's model of how to participate in the community.

Explore how Watching interactions connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Modification +

A daughter learns her mother's granny square but uses different colors, reflecting her own aesthetic generative model.

Explore how Modification connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Innovation +

A young member learns the traditional join but discovers a new variation that works better for the yarn types available today.

Explore how Innovation connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Loss +

Some techniques are not transmitted — they die with their last practitioner. This is the dissolution of a generative model that no one inherits.

Explore how Loss connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Revival +

Sometimes a lost technique is rediscovered through old pattern books or the internet, and re-enters the circle's generative model.

Explore how Revival connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Safety +

Errors are normalized. Frogging is celebrated as part of the process. This reduces the social cost of prediction error, allowing learners to take risks.

Explore how Safety connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Scaffolding +

More experienced members naturally provide support that is calibrated to the learner's level — showing simpler alternatives, offering just enough help without taking over. This is social scaffolding, and it maps directly to the Active Inference concept of adjusting the environment to reduce unnecessary prediction error.

Explore how Scaffolding connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Diversity +

As we discussed in Module 2, the range of skill levels in a circle means there is always someone slightly ahead who can model the next step and someone slightly behind whom you can help. This creates a zone of proximal development — the sweet spot where learning happens most effectively.

Explore how Diversity connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Continuity +

The circle meets regularly, providing repeated opportunities for practice, observation, and feedback. Learning in Active Inference requires many cycles of prediction, action, error, and update. The weekly meeting provides this rhythm.

Explore how Continuity connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.
