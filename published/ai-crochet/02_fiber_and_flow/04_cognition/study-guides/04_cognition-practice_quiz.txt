Practice Quiz: Cognition (Fiber & Flow)

Part A: Multiple Choice

Flow state in crochet corresponds to:
A) Maximum prediction error — the crocheter is constantly surprised
B) Low prediction error — the generative model closely matches the task
C) Zero cognitive engagement — the mind is blank
D) Only working with brand-new materials

A) A model that only predicts yarn color
B) A multi-level model predicting physical, stitch, fabric, and care properties of the yarn
C) A shopping list for yarn stores
D) A model that requires a computer to run

Embodied cognition in crochet means that:
A) All thinking happens in the brain
B) Cognition is distributed across brain, hands, and the interaction with material
C) The yarn thinks for itself
D) Only written patterns contain knowledge

Counterfactual thinking in fiber arts is:
A) Counting stitches backward
B) Mentally simulating what would happen with a different yarn, color, or technique without actually doing it
C) Copying another crocheter's work
D) Only possible with digital tools

Frogging (ripping out work) in Active Inference terms is:
A) A failure of the crocheter
B) A cognitive reset when the model-reality divergence becomes too large
C) Something only beginners do
D) A random destructive act

Why can experienced crocheters work while watching TV?
A) They are not paying attention to their crochet
B) Their prediction error load is low enough that conscious attention is freed
C) TV makes them crochet better
D) They are only pretending to crochet

Gauge drift during a project most likely reflects:
A) A change in the yarn itself
B) Gradual shifts in the crocheter's cognitive and physical state (fatigue, tension changes, environmental factors)
C) A manufacturing defect
D) An imaginary problem

Part B: Short Answer

Describe the cognitive difference between a beginner picking up a new yarn and an expert picking up the same yarn. What does the expert's generative model predict that the beginner's cannot?

Give a specific example of counterfactual thinking in crochet. A crocheter has a pattern that calls for cotton — describe the mental simulation they would perform to evaluate substituting wool.

Why is frogging actually a sign of a well-functioning cognitive agent rather than a failure? What would happen if the crocheter could not detect the model-reality divergence?
