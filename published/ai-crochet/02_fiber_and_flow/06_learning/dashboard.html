Key Concepts

Learning Through Swatching +

A gauge swatch is a structured experiment that updates the crocheter's model of a yarn.

Swatching tests predictions about stitch size, drape, and fabric feel. The swatch is evidence that updates beliefs. Without swatching, the crocheter relies on untested priors.
Relevance to Active Inference

Yarn Substitution as Model Transfer +

Substituting one yarn for another tests how well the generative model transfers across materials.

When a pattern calls for a specific yarn you cannot find, substitution forces you to map your model of the original onto a new material. Transfer success depends on model depth.
Relevance to Active Inference

Mental Library as Bayesian Prior +

Every yarn the crocheter has handled contributes to a growing library of material priors.

An experienced crocheter has a rich library of fiber priors. Each new yarn is compared against this library. The richer the library, the faster the crocheter can classify and predict.
Relevance to Active Inference

Social Learning in the Circle +

Watching others handle yarn and hearing their experiences accelerates model building.

Crochet circles are social learning environments. Observing someone else's technique or hearing about their yarn substitution failures updates your own model vicariously.
Relevance to Active Inference
