# Practice Quiz: Learning — From Following Patterns to Writing Them

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. The progression from following patterns to writing them reflects:
A) Getting older
B) Increasing generative model complexity and sophistication
C) Spending more money on yarn
D) Having more free time

2. When a project fails, the primary learning benefit is:
A) Practicing frogging
B) Strong model updating at the exact points where predictions were wrong
C) Saving yarn for future projects
D) Learning patience

3. Writing a crochet pattern is best described as:
A) Simply writing down what you did
B) Encoding your generative model so another agent can use it
C) Copying existing patterns
D) A purely creative act

4. An experienced crocheter who can reverse-engineer a finished object is demonstrating:
A) Photographic memory
B) A powerful generative model that infers causes from effects
C) Cheating
D) Simple counting

5. Trying a new technique for the first time demonstrates:
A) Starting from zero
B) Extending the generative model with partial transfer from existing knowledge
C) Ignoring established skills
D) Random experimentation

6. Community learning (crochet circles, online forums) accelerates model growth through:
A) Social pressure only
B) Observation, instruction, feedback, and shared model exchange
C) Competition between crocheters
D) Access to cheaper yarn

7. The emotional sting of frogging a large section serves a learning function because:
A) Pain is always educational
B) Strong emotional signals ensure deep and lasting model updates
C) Emotional crocheters learn faster
D) Frogging builds hand strength

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Map your own learning journey in crochet: first project, first modification, first original design (if applicable). For each milestone, what capability did your generative model gain?

2. Explain why teaching a beginner to crochet reveals implicit knowledge in your own model. Give an example of something you would need to make explicit that you normally take for granted.

3. A crocheter has been following patterns for 5 years but has never modified one. What might be preventing them from moving to Stage 2, and what exercises would help develop their modification capabilities?
