Applications

In Middle School, we see Perception manifest in: * Specific Example 1: When your phone's camera app detects faces and draws little boxes around them, that is computational perception. The app does not "see" faces the way you do -- it runs each frame through a model trained to predict where face-like patterns appear, comparing pixel data against learned predictions of what eyes, noses, and mouths look like. When the prediction matches the data strongly enough, it draws the box. * Specific Example 2: Autocorrect on your phone is a perception system for text. When you type "teh," the system perceives those three letters, compares them against its prediction model of what word you probably meant, and suggests "the." Sometimes it fails hilariously because its model predicts the wrong word -- proof that computational perception, like human perception, depends on the quality of its internal predictions.
