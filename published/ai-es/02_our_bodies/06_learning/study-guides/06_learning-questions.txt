Course — Module 06 — Think About It

What is something you are really good at now but were bad at before?
Why do babies have to learn to walk? (Why are they not born running?)
Does practice make perfect? Or does practice make better?
How does your brain feel when you are learning something really hard? (Tired? Frustrated? Excited?)
Can you learn to wiggle your ears? Would it take a lot of practice?
If you stop practicing piano, what happens? (The brain path grows "grass" again!)
Do you have to think about walking anymore? Why not? (Muscle Memory!)
Can you learn from watching someone else? (Yes! Mirror neurons!)
What is the hardest thing you have ever learned?
Is it okay to cry when you make a mistake? (Yes! Then take a breath and try again.)
Try the mirror drawing lab. Draw a star while looking only in a mirror. How did it look? Draw your first try and your last try side by side.
Think of something you learned this year. Draw three stages: (1) The Wobbly Start, (2) The Practice, (3) The Smooth Finish.
Write your name with your non-dominant hand five times in a row. Does it get better? Take a photo or draw each attempt.
Tell a story about a time you failed at something but kept trying. What happened in the end?
Draw a path through tall grass. The first time it is overgrown and hard. After many walks, it is clear and easy. This is your brain learning!
Interview someone older than you. Ask: "What is something that was hard to learn but is easy now?" Write down what they said.
Imagine you are a baby chick learning to fly. Tell the story of your first flight. How many times did you fall?
Make a "Learning Timeline" for a skill: Day 1 (terrible), Day 10 (okay), Day 100 (great!). Draw pictures for each stage.
Try patting your head and rubbing your tummy at the same time. Hard? That is your brain trying to learn two paths at once!
Write an encouraging letter to someone who is struggling to learn something new. Tell them why mistakes are actually good.
