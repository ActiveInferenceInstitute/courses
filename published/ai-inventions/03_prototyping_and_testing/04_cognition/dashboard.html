Core Concepts

1. Bayesian Updating in Practice

Prior beliefs + evidence = posterior beliefs, weighted by precision. Proportional updating means strong evidence warrants large changes, weak evidence warrants small changes. Inventors commonly violate this by either ignoring evidence entirely or catastrophically abandoning their design after a single bad test. Testing is progressive narrowing, not a single verdict.

2. Signal vs. Noise

Not all data carries equal weight. Precision (inverse of variance) determines evidential strength. Check repeatability (same result on retest?), consistency (converging results from different methods?), and magnitude (large vs. small effect?). Deming warned that reacting to noise as if it were signal causes over-correction, increasing variability.

3. When Results Contradict Your Model

Three responses of increasing severity: parameter updating (adjust values, keep structure), structure revision (add missing variables), model replacement (start over with new approach). The Wright brothers first tried parameter updates to Lilienthal's lift data, then recognized the entire aerodynamic model needed rebuilding — a critical structure revision.

4. Cognitive Biases in Testing

Confirmation bias: asymmetric weighting of evidence. Anchoring: over-weighting first results. Motivated reasoning: desire contaminating inference. Base rate neglect: ignoring background failure rates. Each is a natural consequence of generative model structure, not irrationality — but each distorts reasoning and requires active countermeasures.

5. Structured Reasoning Protocols

Pre-registration: commit to hypotheses and criteria before testing. Analysis of Competing Hypotheses: evaluate multiple explanations, not just the preferred one. Decision matrices: map results to actions in advance. Red team reviews: external reasoners provide alternative interpretations. These protocols enforce disciplined inference by constraining interpretive freedom.
