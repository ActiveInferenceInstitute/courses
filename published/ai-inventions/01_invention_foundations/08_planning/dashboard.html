Expected Free Energy and Planning

Planning means selecting actions that minimize expected free energy — balancing pragmatic value (goal progress) and epistemic value (uncertainty reduction). Early stages should weight learning heavily; later stages should weight results. This is computed, not guessed.

Staged Development

Break projects into phases: problem validation, concept validation, user validation, feasibility, market launch. Each phase generates information for the next. Irreversible decisions are deferred until uncertainty is reduced. This minimizes total project free energy.

Decision Trees and Branch Points

Plans are trees, not lines. Explicit branch points specify what evidence triggers which path. Option value means keeping multiple paths open can be worth the cost. Decision criteria should be written before emotional investment clouds judgment.

Adaptive Roadmaps

Plans must include mechanisms for their own revision. Review points, pivot criteria, and learning integration ensure the roadmap stays calibrated to reality. The planning horizon limits detailed planning to the range of useful prediction — beyond that, only directional intent.

Resource Allocation Under Uncertainty

Invest where expected free energy is highest. Early: fundamental viability questions. Later: operational optimization. Premature optimization (e.g., designing packaging before confirming the mechanism works) misallocates resources to low-uncertainty areas while high-uncertainty questions remain.
