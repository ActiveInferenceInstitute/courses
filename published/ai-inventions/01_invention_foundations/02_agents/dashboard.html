The Inventor as a Free Energy Minimizing Agent

Inventors are agents who minimize free energy by changing the world to match their predictions rather than changing their predictions to match the world. When an inventor sees groceries falling off a bicycle, they build a cargo attachment rather than accepting the inconvenience. Invention is the active path of free energy minimization.

Generative Models and Worldview

An inventor's generative model determines what problems they perceive and what solutions they imagine. A chemist sees molecular interactions; an ecologist sees ecosystem dynamics. George de Mestral's engineering model let him see burrs as mechanical attachments, leading to Velcro. Your model is both your greatest asset and your greatest limitation.

Prior Preferences as Goals

In Active Inference, goals are prior preferences — states the agent expects to find itself in. These operate at multiple scales: immediate (this prototype should work), project (this device should help the elderly), and identity (I want to be a sustainability innovator). Multi-scale preferences create a hierarchy that constrains and guides the inventive process.

Identity Priors

Identity is a deep prior — a foundational belief about what kind of agent you are. It determines what domains you consider yours, what solutions you instinctively pursue, and what evidence you attend to. Kodak's identity as a "film company" blinded it to digital photography despite having invented the technology. Examining identity priors reveals both strengths and blind spots.

Multi-Agent Invention Teams

Most significant inventions come from teams with diverse generative models. The Bell Labs transistor team combined a theorist, an experimentalist, and a visionary manager. Effective teams maintain enough model diversity to explore widely while sharing enough structure to evaluate collectively. The Post-it Note required Silver's chemistry model plus Fry's everyday-problem model.
