Lab: The Iteration Log — Tracking Learning Across Prototype Cycles

Objective

Build a systematic learning protocol for your invention project. You will practice documenting predictions, observing outcomes, analyzing prediction errors, classifying learning types (parameter vs. structure), and planning next iterations. By the end of this lab, you will have an iteration log template and at least one complete learning cycle documented.

Materials and Prerequisites

  • Your invention idea and any prototype work from previous labs
  • A notebook or digital document for your iteration log
  • Optionally: a prototype to test during this lab (even a very simple one)
  • No technical background required

Discussion and Debrief

  1. Learning classification: Was your experiment's learning primarily parameter or structure? How did you make this determination? Was the distinction always clear?

  2. Surprise value: How surprised were you by the results? In Active Inference terms, was the prediction error large enough to significantly update your model? If not, how could you design experiments that generate more informative surprises?

  3. Documentation discipline: How difficult was it to make your predictions explicit before testing? Many inventors skip this step, but it is the foundation of systematic learning. What strategies will help you maintain this discipline?

  4. Cumulative learning: If you have done multiple experiments (across this course or independently), do you see a learning trajectory? Is your uncertainty decreasing over time? Are you converging on a design or still exploring?

  5. Structure learning anxiety: Structure learning can feel like failure — abandoning an approach you invested in. Have you experienced this? How can you recognize the difference between premature abandonment and necessary structure change?
