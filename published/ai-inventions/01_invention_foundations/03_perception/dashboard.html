Perception as Active Inference

Perception is not passive data collection but active prediction-error detection. Your brain generates expectations and flags deviations. What you can invent depends on what you can perceive, which depends on the sophistication of your generative model. Domain expertise refines perception, but rigid models can suppress important anomalies.

Noticing Unmet Needs

Unmet needs are chronic prediction errors that people have accommodated rather than solved. The rolling suitcase waited until 1970 because travelers normalized carrying heavy bags. Three strategies for detection: fresh eyes (outsider models), analogical perception (cross-domain transfer), and empathic perception (adopting another user's model).

Evidence Gathering as Active Sensing

Active sensing means choosing observations that maximally reduce uncertainty. Seek disconfirming evidence, not just confirmation. Key techniques: contextual inquiry, lead user interviews, failure analysis, and boundary testing. IDEO observed shoppers in stores rather than brainstorming in offices.

Precision Weighting

Not all evidence is equally trustworthy. Precision determines how much each observation updates your beliefs. Common errors: overweighting personal experience, underweighting extreme users, overweighting expert opinion for paradigm-shifting inventions, and underweighting quantitative data. The goal is appropriate weighting, not equal weighting.

Signal vs. Noise

Not every prediction error is an invention opportunity. Signal: persistent, broad, severe, structurally caused, tractable. Noise: transient, idiosyncratic, low-impact, behavioral, intractable. Aoyagi's pulse oximeter came from reinterpreting fluctuation noise as arterial information signal. WiFi-connected juicers come from mistaking personal preference for universal need.
