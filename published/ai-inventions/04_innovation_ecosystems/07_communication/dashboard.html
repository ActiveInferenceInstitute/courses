Marketing as Model Transmission

Effective marketing transmits a compressed generative model from the inventor to the adopter. The best marketing constructs a narrative model of the user's life — problem, solution, evidence — that enables the adopter to make better predictions. High-precision claims with evidence update models more effectively than vague promises.

Example: Steve Jobs presented the iPhone as solving the prediction error of carrying three devices. The live demo provided evidence the audience could immediately evaluate.

Branding as Compressed Model

A brand is a compressed generative model in the public mind. Encountering a brand activates instant predictions about quality, values, and experience. Brand consistency is critical because every interaction either confirms or damages the model. Brand crises are costly because a single large prediction error can destroy decades of model building.

Example: Patagonia's every communication reinforces environmental responsibility — enabling customers to predict that any purchase aligns with their values.

Investor Communication

Investor pitches should follow the investor's evidence hierarchy: Problem, Solution, Market, Product, Traction, Team, Business Model, Ask. Each element updates a specific component of the investor's model. The most effective communicators (like Buffett) explicitly share their models, acknowledge errors, and provide calibrated uncertainty.

Example: Buffett's annual letters state his model, describe supporting evidence, acknowledge prediction errors from previous years, and provide updated predictions with explicit uncertainty.

Open Source as Radical Transparency

Open source makes the invention's complete model publicly inspectable, creating trust that proprietary approaches cannot match. Adopters can verify rather than trust. Contributors can improve the model directly. The trade-off is between openness (ecosystem trust) and proprietary advantage (value capture).

Example: Linux system administrators trust the OS because they can inspect every line of code, not because of marketing claims.

Standards as Ecosystem Communication

Standards establish shared generative models for interoperability, reducing ecosystem-wide uncertainty. Participating in standards bodies lets you shape the shared model. Adopting standards signals compatibility to potential customers. The HTML5 vs. Flash battle was a competition between open and proprietary shared models.

Example: Qualcomm embedded its patented technology into cellular standards, ensuring that the shared model of mobile communication included its innovations.
