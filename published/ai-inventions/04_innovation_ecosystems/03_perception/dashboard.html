Market Research as Active Perception

Market research is not passive data collection but active sampling of the environment to test predictions. Each interview, survey, or experiment generates observations that confirm or disconfirm your model. The key shift: ask not "What do customers want?" but "Where was my prediction wrong?"

Example: Steve Blank's Customer Development instructs entrepreneurs to test hypotheses through structured conversations — active perception in practice.

Precision-Weighted Evidence

Different evidence sources carry different precision. Past behavior is high-precision evidence of actual decision-making. Stated intentions are low-precision evidence filtered through self-models. The "Mom Test" teaches asking about behavior rather than opinions, maximizing the precision of customer discovery evidence.

Example: Toyota's "genchi genbutsu" — go and see for yourself — bypasses precision-degrading reporting hierarchies for direct observation.

MVP as Sensory Organ

The Minimum Viable Product is not a product — it is a perception instrument. Its purpose is to generate the highest-precision evidence about whether your model of customer needs is accurate. Design MVPs for maximum evidence generation, not minimum development cost.

Example: Dropbox's explainer video MVP tested demand before building the product — a pure sensing strategy that measured signup conversion as evidence of need.

Competitive Intelligence as Model Inference

Competitor actions — product launches, hires, patents, pricing — are observable outputs of their internal generative models. By reverse-engineering what beliefs would produce those actions, you can infer their strategy and predict their next moves. Patents reveal technology predictions; hiring patterns reveal capability investments.

Example: When Google started hiring automotive engineers, competitors could infer an autonomous vehicle investment before any official announcement.

Regulatory Signal Detection

Regulatory changes emit signals with varying precision and lead time. Academic publications and policy proposals provide early, lower-precision signals. Draft regulations provide later, higher-precision signals. Companies that monitor the full signal hierarchy can anticipate regulatory shifts years in advance.

Example: GDPR was predictable years before 2018 enforcement from EU data protection discourse, Snowden revelations, and early legislative drafts starting in 2012.

Signal vs. Noise in Trends

Genuine trends increase model evidence for systematic change across multiple independent signal sources. Noise does not converge across sources. The smartphone revolution was presaged by converging signals in mobile internet adoption, component miniaturization, mobile content growth, and early Asian market adoption.

Example: The Gartner Hype Cycle's "Trough of Disillusionment" often represents genuine trends undergoing posterior correction after overly precise initial hype.
