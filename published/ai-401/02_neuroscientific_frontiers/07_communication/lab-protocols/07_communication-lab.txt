Lab: Social Neuroscience and Mirror Systems

> Learning Goal: Analyze social brain circuits through Active Inference, evaluating mirror neuron accounts and language processing.

Part 1: Social Brain Network Mapping

Exercise: For a specific social scenario (someone telling you an emotional story), map the activation of each social brain region:

| Brain Region | What It Computes | Active During Story Because... | Prediction Generated |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------------------|---------------------|
| mPFC (dorsal) | Deliberate mentalizing | Inferring the storyteller's mental state | "She feels sad about the loss" |
| mPFC (ventral) | Automatic social prediction | Rapidly predicting emotional tone | "The next part will be even sadder" |
| TPJ (right) | Self-other distinction | Distinguishing your reaction from hers | "I feel empathy, but she feels grief" |
| STS | Action/gesture interpretation | Reading body language and facial expression | "Her downcast eyes signal sorrow" |
| Anterior insula | Empathic resonance | Mapping her interoceptive state onto yours | "I feel a tightness in my chest" |
| FFA | Face processing | Reading minute facial expressions | "Micro-expression of pain around eyes" |
| Temporal pole | Narrative/semantic context | Tracking the story's meaning | "This connects to her broader life story" |

Write a 200-word analysis: How do all these regions coordinate to produce the experience of "understanding someone"?

Part 2: Mirror Neuron Debate

> Learning Goal: Evaluate competing accounts of mirror neuron function.

Exercise: Compare the original and Active Inference accounts:

| Feature | Original Mirror Theory | Active Inference Account |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Mechanism | Direct motor matching — simulation | Predictive model generating same predictions for self and observed actions |
| Required hardware | Dedicated mirror neuron system | Any sufficiently sophisticated generative model |
| Experience effects | Not well explained | Naturally explained (better model → stronger predictions) |
| Context modulation | Not well explained | Naturally explained (top-down context shapes predictions) |
| Lesion effects | Should abolish action understanding | Should degrade but not abolish (other routes to understanding) |
| Developmental trajectory | Innate or early-developing | Develops with experience and model refinement |

Write a 300-word analysis: Which account is better supported by current evidence? What experiment could definitively distinguish them?

Part 3: Language Prediction Experiment

> Learning Goal: Analyze language processing as hierarchical prediction.

Exercise: Consider the following sentence: "The cat sat on the ."

At the phonological level, what consonant cluster does the brain predict will start the next word?
At the lexical level, what specific words are highly predicted? (mat, rug, chair, floor...)
At the syntactic level, what word class is predicted? (noun)
At the discourse level, what semantic field is predicted? (furniture/surfaces)
If the sentence ends with "moon," what prediction errors fire at each level?
Which ERP components (N400, P600) would you expect? Why?

Now design a more complex example using pragmatic prediction: "It was my birthday, and everyone had gathered. I opened the box, and inside was a ."

Part 4: Interbrain Synchronization Analysis

> Learning Goal: Analyze neural coupling between interacting minds.

Exercise: Design a hyperscanning experiment:

Scenario: Choose an interactive scenario (conversation, cooperative game, teaching)
Measurement: What brain signals would you record from both participants? (EEG? fNIRS? fMRI?)
Synchronization metric: How would you quantify brain-to-brain coupling?
Active Inference predictions: What conditions would increase synchronization (shared priors, aligned models) vs. decrease it (disagreement, different priors)?
Control condition: How would you distinguish genuine social coupling from shared stimulus-driven activity?

Part 5: Reflection

In 300 words, reflect: Active Inference treats social cognition as inference about other generative models — "thinking about thinking." But is this what social interaction feels like? Phenomenologically, understanding another person feels immediate and embodied, not computational. Can Active Inference account for this phenomenological directness, or does it miss something essential about social connection?
