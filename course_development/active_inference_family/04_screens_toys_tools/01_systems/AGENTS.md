# AGENTS: Module 01 — Systems

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Technology and media in early childhood
- **Topic**: Systems
- **Subtitle**: The Home Media Ecosystem
- **Course**: Screens, Toys & Tools
- **Lab Type**: Media Activity — The Media Audit
- **Key Concepts**: media ecosystem, signal-to-noise ratio, digital boundaries, toy ecosystem

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring technology and media together
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference or technology assumed

### Tone
- Balanced, curious, and non-judgmental
- Present technology as a tool, not inherently good or bad
- Use the "digital neighborhood" metaphor — some places are safe, some need supervision
- Avoid guilt about screen time; focus on thoughtful, intentional choices

### Active Inference Integration
- **Media ecosystem** is a system with inputs (content, notifications), outputs (responses, clicks), and boundaries (parental controls, family rules)
- **Signal-to-noise ratio** maps to the quality of information — good media has high signal (learning, connection); poor media is mostly noise
- **Digital boundaries** are the Markov blanket of the family's media life — what comes in, what stays out
- **System balance** means finding the right mix of screen time, play time, and family time
- **Entropy** increases when media use is unstructured — chaos of endless scrolling vs. intentional choosing

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve surveying, counting, and mapping the family's media and toys
- Include a family media inventory: how many screens? what apps? how much time?
- Use physical mapping (drawing the media landscape of the home)
- Younger children count screens and toys; older children categorize by type and purpose
- Always include a non-judgmental discussion: "Is this working for our family?"
