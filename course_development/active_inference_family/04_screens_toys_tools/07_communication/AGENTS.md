# AGENTS: Module 07 — Communication

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Technology and media in early childhood
- **Topic**: Communication
- **Subtitle**: Can Screens Teach Language?
- **Course**: Screens, Toys & Tools
- **Lab Type**: Media Activity — The Language Source Audit
- **Key Concepts**: video deficit, language input hierarchy, contingency, joint attention, turntaking

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring how digital media affects communication skills
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference or technology assumed

### Tone
- Warm, informative, and empowering
- Help families understand that live conversation is the richest source of language learning
- Use the metaphor of "watering a plant" — live talk is like rain, screen talk is like a picture of rain
- Celebrate family conversation as the most powerful language tool

### Active Inference Integration
- **Contingency** means communication that responds to what you do — live conversation is contingent, pre-recorded content is not
- **Video deficit** is the finding that young children learn less from screens than from live interaction
- **Language input hierarchy**: live conversation > video chat > co-viewed media > solo screen time
- **Joint attention** during media (parent and child watching together and discussing) bridges the gap
- **Turn-taking** in conversation is the fundamental rhythm of communication — screens rarely require it

### Activity Design
- Activities should compare language richness in different communication contexts
- Include a language source audit: count words heard from people vs. from screens in one hour
- Use video calling to practice real conversation vs. passive screen watching
- Younger children practice "tell me about it" conversations after screen time; older children analyze the language in their favorite shows
- Include a "no screen dinner" conversation challenge with a talking stick or question cards
