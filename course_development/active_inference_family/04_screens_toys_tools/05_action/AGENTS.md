# AGENTS: Module 05 — Action

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Technology and media in early childhood
- **Topic**: Action
- **Subtitle**: Physical vs Digital Play
- **Course**: Screens, Toys & Tools
- **Lab Type**: Media Activity — The Body Movement Log
- **Key Concepts**: motor demands, proprioceptive gap, construction play, embodied vs disembodied interaction

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children comparing physical and digital forms of play and action
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference or technology assumed

### Tone
- Active, enthusiastic, and body-positive
- Celebrate the joy of physical movement and real-world building
- Present video games as a form of action, but highlight what the body misses
- Use the metaphor of "whole body play" vs. "thumbs only play"

### Active Inference Integration
- **Embodied action** engages the full body — proprioception, balance, strength, coordination
- **Disembodied action** (screen tapping) uses only fine motor skills — the body stays still
- **Proprioceptive gap** means the body does not get feedback about its position in space during screen time
- **Construction play** (blocks, Legos, art) provides rich motor and cognitive feedback
- **Controllers** are the boundary between the agent and the digital world — a simplified action interface

### Activity Design
- Activities should compare physical and digital versions of the same action
- Include side-by-side challenges: digital drawing vs. real drawing; video game sports vs. real sports
- Use a body movement log to track how much the body moves in different activities
- Younger children do movement challenges; older children design experiments comparing physical and digital action
- Include a "no screen challenge" period followed by all-screen period and a family discussion

### Key Connections
- Link back to Module 04 (Cognition): How we think affects how we act; screens change both
- Link forward to Module 06 (Learning): Does learning from screens transfer to real life?
