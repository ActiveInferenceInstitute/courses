# AGENTS: Module 06 — Learning

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Technology and media in early childhood
- **Topic**: Learning
- **Subtitle**: Does 'Educational' Mean Learning?
- **Course**: Screens, Toys & Tools
- **Lab Type**: Media Activity — The Transfer Test
- **Key Concepts**: transfer of learning, recognition vs generalization, four pillars, parent as bridge

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring what "educational" really means
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference or technology assumed

### Tone
- Honest, curious, and empowering
- Help families critically evaluate "educational" claims on apps and shows
- Use the metaphor of "real learning" vs. "feeling like learning" — recognition is not the same as understanding
- Celebrate the parent's role as the bridge between screen content and real understanding

### Active Inference Integration
- **Transfer of learning** is the true test: can the child use screen-learned knowledge in the real world?
- **Recognition vs. generalization** — screens often test recognition (tap the right answer) but real learning means generalization (use the concept in new situations)
- **Four pillars of learning** (active, engaged, meaningful, socially interactive) — evaluate media against these
- **Parent as bridge** means co-viewing and discussing screen content, helping the child connect it to their generative model
- **Bugs and updates** in software mirror the learning process — mistakes lead to better versions

### Activity Design
- Activities should test whether screen learning transfers to real life
- Include experiments: learn something from a video, then try it in real life — did it work?
- Use a "learning test" checklist: Was I active? Was I engaged? Was it meaningful? Did I interact with someone?
- Younger children do simple transfer tests; older children evaluate their own apps and shows
- Include a "bug report" activity where children identify mistakes and suggest fixes

- Link back to Module 05 (Action): Learning requires action, not just watching
- Link forward to Module 07 (Communication): Can screens teach language as well as real conversation?
