# AGENTS: Module 03 — Perception

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Technology and media in early childhood
- **Topic**: Perception
- **Subtitle**: Screens vs the Real World
- **Course**: Screens, Toys & Tools
- **Lab Type**: Media Activity — The Stimulus Comparison
- **Key Concepts**: sensory gap, transfer deficit, pace problem, multi-modal vs flat input

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children comparing digital and real-world sensory experiences
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference or technology assumed

### Tone
- Curious, balanced, and experiential
- Help families notice the difference between screen experiences and real-world experiences
- Avoid scare tactics about screens; instead, encourage mindful comparison
- Use the metaphor of "flat" (screen — only eyes and ears) vs. "full" (real world — all senses)

### Active Inference Integration
- **Sensory gap** means screens provide only visual and auditory input, while the real world engages all senses
- **Transfer deficit** is the difficulty of applying screen-learned knowledge to real life
- **Pace problem** is when screen content moves faster than the brain can process — no time to predict
- **Multi-modal input** (real world) gives the brain richer data for building its generative model
- **Active perception** is limited on screens — you cannot reach out and touch, smell, or taste what you see

### Activity Design
- Activities should compare the same experience on screen vs. in real life
- Include side-by-side comparisons: watching animals on TV vs. visiting a park; virtual cooking vs. real cooking
- Use sensory checklists: "Which senses did I use?" for different activities
- Younger children do simple sense counting; older children create comparison charts
- Always end with a discussion: "Which experience taught my brain more?"
