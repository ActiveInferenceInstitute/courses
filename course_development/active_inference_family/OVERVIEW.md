# Active Inference for Families — Pedagogical Overview

## Vision

This curriculum opens a window into one of the most profound ideas in modern brain science — **Active Inference** — and places it directly into the hands (and hearts) of parents and caregivers. The goal is not to make parents into neuroscientists, but to give them a deeper, more compassionate lens for understanding the small humans they love.

When a newborn startles at a loud noise, they are experiencing **prediction error** — the world didn't match what they expected. When a toddler insists on the same bedtime story for the hundredth night in a row, they are **consolidating a generative model** — building a map of how stories work. When a parent soothes a crying baby by rocking gently and humming, they are providing **co-regulation** — sharing their own calm predictions with a nervous system that doesn't yet have enough of its own.

Every insight in this curriculum is rooted in the Free Energy Principle and the research of Karl Friston, Thomas Parr, and others — but you won't find equations here. Instead, you'll find stories, activities, and a new way of seeing the child in front of you.

---

## Tone & Style

**Warm. Practical. Evidence-based. Never preachy.**

- Use everyday language. If a scientific term is needed, define it in the moment.
- Favor real-world parenting examples over abstract theory.
- Acknowledge that parenting is hard, messy, and deeply personal.
- Frame Active Inference as a lens, not a prescription — "here's one way to see what's happening."
- Include activities that parents can do with children in real time (not just homework).
- Every module should make parents feel *more confident*, not more overwhelmed.

## Course Map

### Course 1: [Growing Together](./01_growing_together/)

**Perspective**: Daily family life, routines, and rhythms.

This course zooms out to the level of the whole family system. Families maintain themselves through routines (morning, meals, bedtime), and these routines are how both parents and children reduce the unpredictability of the world. When routines break (travel, illness, a new sibling), the whole family system feels the "prediction error" — and this course helps parents understand and repair it.

Modules: [Systems](./01_growing_together/01_systems/) · [Agents](./01_growing_together/02_agents/) · [Perception](./01_growing_together/03_perception/) · [Cognition](./01_growing_together/04_cognition/) · [Action](./01_growing_together/05_action/) · [Learning](./01_growing_together/06_learning/) · [Communication](./01_growing_together/07_communication/) · [Planning](./01_growing_together/08_planning/)

### Course 2: [Tiny Bodies, Big Brains](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/)

**Perspective**: Infant and toddler bodily and brain development.

This course goes inside — into the body and brain of your baby. It explains what's happening neurologically when your infant tracks a face, reaches for a rattle, babbles in response to your voice, or melts down at the end of a long day. The Active Inference lens makes developmental milestones feel less like checkboxes and more like chapters in a story about a growing mind learning to predict its world.

Modules: [Systems](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/01_systems/) · [Agents](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/02_agents/) · [Perception](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/03_perception/) · [Cognition](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/04_cognition/) · [Action](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/05_action/) · [Learning](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/06_learning/) · [Communication](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/07_communication/) · [Planning](./02_tiny_bodies_big_brains/08_planning/)

### Course 3: [Patterns in Play](./03_patterns_in_play/)

**Perspective**: Learning through play — counting, sorting, building, imagining.

This course focuses on the cognitive work that children do through play. When a child sorts blocks by color, lines up cars by size, or pretends a banana is a telephone, they are building and testing generative models of the world. This course helps parents see play not as "just messing around" but as the primary engine of a child's cognitive development.

Modules: [Systems](./03_patterns_in_play/01_systems/) · [Agents](./03_patterns_in_play/02_agents/) · [Perception](./03_patterns_in_play/03_perception/) · [Cognition](./03_patterns_in_play/04_cognition/) · [Action](./03_patterns_in_play/05_action/) · [Learning](./03_patterns_in_play/06_learning/) · [Communication](./03_patterns_in_play/07_communication/) · [Planning](./03_patterns_in_play/08_planning/)

### Course 4: [Screens, Toys & Tools](./04_screens_toys_tools/)

**Perspective**: Navigating technology, toys, and tools in modern childhood.

This course applies Active Inference to one of the most anxiety-inducing parts of modern parenting: screen time, toy selection, and the role of technology in childhood. Rather than giving rigid rules, it helps parents think about *what kind of predictions* a given tool (iPad, wooden blocks, robot toy) allows a child to build — and whether those predictions are rich, varied, and transferable to real life.

Modules: [Systems](./04_screens_toys_tools/01_systems/) · [Agents](./04_screens_toys_tools/02_agents/) · [Perception](./04_screens_toys_tools/03_perception/) · [Cognition](./04_screens_toys_tools/04_cognition/) · [Action](./04_screens_toys_tools/05_action/) · [Learning](./04_screens_toys_tools/06_learning/) · [Communication](./04_screens_toys_tools/07_communication/) · [Planning](./04_screens_toys_tools/08_planning/)

## Cross-Course Connections

The same 8 topics appear in every course, building a spiral of understanding. In Course 1, "Systems" means your family routines. In Course 2, it means your baby's body. In Course 3, it means the little world of a play session. In Course 4, it means the ecosystem of toys and screens. Each pass deepens your understanding and gives you more tools for real life.

See [Cross-Course Map](./resources/cross_course_map.md) for detailed connections.

## How to Use This Curriculum

- **Sequential**: Work through one course fully before starting the next (recommended for first-time parents).
- **Parallel**: Study the same module across all 4 courses (e.g., "Agents" in Growing Together → Tiny Bodies → Patterns in Play → Screens & Tools).
- **Just-in-time**: Jump to the module that addresses what you're going through right now (e.g., your toddler won't stop saying "no?" → Course 1, Module 5: Action).
- **Group study**: Use one module per week in a parent group, book club, or childbirth education class.
