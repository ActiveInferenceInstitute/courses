# Syllabus: Patterns in Play

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](./README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../README.md)

## Course Description

Patterns in Play explores how children learn about the world through play — building systems, creating agents, testing predictions, forming categories, and planning adventures. Each module connects everyday play behavior to an Active Inference concept, helping parents see the remarkable cognitive work happening inside every game of peek-a-boo, every tower of blocks, and every imaginary tea party.

## Instructor Notes

This course is designed for use by parents at home, parent group facilitators, early childhood educators, and family therapists. Each module is self-contained but builds on previous modules. The "Family Activities" (labs) are designed for children aged 1–6, with age adaptations noted.

## Assessment Philosophy

There are no tests or grades. The "practice quizzes" are self-checks for parents to reinforce their own understanding. The "study questions" are invitations for reflection and conversation. The family activities are the real assessment — if you're doing the activities with your child and noticing the Active Inference concepts in action, you're learning.

## Materials Needed

Across all 8 modules, you'll need only:

- Blocks (any kind — wooden, Duplo, cardboard boxes)
- A few dolls or figurines (or stuffed animals)
- Household objects for sorting (spoons, cups, socks)
- Paper and crayons
- Blankets and pillows
- Common kitchen items (cups, bowls, measuring spoons)

No specialized equipment. No apps. No purchases required.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, parents will be able to:

1. **Observe** play behavior with an Active Inference lens — seeing prediction, surprise, model-building, and exploration.
2. **Support** play without hijacking it — understanding when to scaffold and when to step back.
3. **Explain** to themselves (and others) why play is the primary engine of cognitive development.
4. **Choose** toys and activities that support rich, open-ended model-building.
5. **Connect** play-based learning to the broader Active Inference concepts from Courses 1 and 2.
