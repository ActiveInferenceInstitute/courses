## Overview

This course is about the most important thing your child does all day: **play**.

When your toddler sorts blocks by color, they are building a generative model of categories. When they line up toy cars from smallest to largest, they are discovering order. When they pretend a banana is a telephone, they are testing the boundaries of what's possible. When they dump out a bin of Legos for the tenth time, they are confirming their predictions about gravity, scattering, and the satisfying sound of plastic on the floor.

In Active Inference terms, play is **epistemic action** — action taken not to get a reward, but to reduce uncertainty about the world. Children play because their brains are hungry for data, and play is the richest data source they have.

This course helps you see the cognitive architecture hidden inside ordinary play. By the end, you'll never watch your child play the same way again.

## Key Ideas in This Course

- **Play is not leisure — it is the brain's primary research program** in early childhood.
- **Pretend play builds generative models** — imagining something that isn't there is the core skill of prediction.
- **Mess is data.** A child who dumps, scatters, and reorganizes is gathering evidence about how the world works.
- **"Again!"** is not stubbornness — it is the sound of a prediction being confirmed and consolidated.
- **Simple toys produce complex play** because they leave room for the child's model to do the work.
