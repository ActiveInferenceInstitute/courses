# AGENTS: Module 07 — Communication

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Communication
- **Subtitle**: Stories, Secrets, and Shared Worlds
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Story Builder
- **Key Concepts**: shared generative model, joint attention, turn-taking, narrative, model alignment
- **Learning Goals**: Practice communication through collaborative storytelling, understand how shared play requires shared understanding

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring teamwork and communication through play
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Collaborative, creative, and connective
- Present teamwork as agents aligning their models — "We need to agree on the rules!"
- Use team language: "together," "our team," "we figured it out"
- Celebrate the moments when family members understand each other without words

### Active Inference Integration
- **Shared generative model** is what teammates build together — a common understanding of the game and strategy
- **Communication** during play is how agents synchronize their models — "Pass me the ball!" shares a prediction
- **Turn-taking** in conversation and games is practice for the back-and-forth of active inference
- **Narrative** (storytelling) is a way to transmit complex generative models from one agent to another
- **Model alignment** happens when teammates develop shared expectations — "I knew you would pass there!"

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve cooperative play, team challenges, and collaborative storytelling
- Include relay races, partner puzzles, and story-building games
- Use communication challenges where teams must coordinate without seeing each other
- Younger children play simple team games; older children design team challenges and reflect on communication
- Include a debrief discussion about what helped the team succeed or struggle
