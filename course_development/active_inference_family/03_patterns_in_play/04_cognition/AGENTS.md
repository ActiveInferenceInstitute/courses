# AGENTS: Module 04 — Cognition

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Cognition
- **Subtitle**: Sorting the World Into Boxes
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Sorting Station
- **Key Concepts**: generative model, categorization, belief updating, object permanence, cognitive flexibility
- **Learning Goals**: Practice strategic thinking through games, understand how the brain sorts and categorizes

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring thinking and strategy through play
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Thoughtful, strategic, and encouraging
- Present strategy as "thinking ahead" — what will happen if I do this?
- Celebrate both winning and losing as opportunities to learn
- Use chess, checkers, and card games as examples of cognitive play

### Active Inference Integration
- **Strategy** is the brain running simulations of possible futures — "If I move here, they might move there"
- **Categorization** through sorting games demonstrates how the brain organizes its model of the world
- **Belief updating** happens when a strategy fails — "I thought that would work, now I know better"
- **Cognitive flexibility** is the ability to switch strategies when the current one is not working
- **Planning ahead** (temporal depth) grows through practice with games that require thinking multiple steps ahead

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve sorting, matching, strategy games, and puzzle-solving
- Include both competitive and cooperative games
- Use blocks, cards, buttons, and household items for sorting activities
- Younger children sort by one or two features; older children play strategy games and design their own
- Include discussion about how thinking ahead helps in games and in life

- Link forward to Module 05 (Action): Strategy leads to taking your turn
