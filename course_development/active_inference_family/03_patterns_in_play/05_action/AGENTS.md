# AGENTS: Module 05 — Action

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Action
- **Subtitle**: The Tower Must Be Built
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Engineering Challenge
- **Key Concepts**: active inference, motor prediction, proprioception, cause and effect, hypothesis testing
- **Learning Goals**: Experience action as hypothesis testing, understand that play actions generate learning data

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring physical play and turn-taking
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Energetic, encouraging, and hands-on
- Present turn-taking as the rhythm of games — patience and action in balance
- Celebrate the satisfaction of building, stacking, and creating
- Use action language: "stack," "throw," "catch," "build," "knock down"

### Active Inference Integration
- **Action in games** is the moment of testing a prediction — "I think this block will balance"
- **Turn-taking** is a social rule that constrains when agents can act, reducing chaos
- **Cause and effect** is directly observable in physical play — stack a block, see if it falls
- **Hypothesis testing** happens naturally: "Will this tower stand? Let me try and find out"
- **Motor prediction** improves with practice — throwing becomes more accurate, building becomes more stable

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve physical building, throwing, catching, or constructing
- Include block towers, ball games, and obstacle courses
- Use common toys and household items (blocks, balls, pillows, cardboard)
- Younger children build and knock down; older children engineer challenges with constraints
- Include measurement and comparison: "How tall can you build? How far can you throw?"
