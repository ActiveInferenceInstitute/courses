# AGENTS: Module 06 — Learning

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Learning
- **Subtitle**: The Power of Again
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Repetition Tracker
- **Key Concepts**: model updating, precision, habituation, scaffolding, zone of proximal development, mastery
- **Learning Goals**: Understand why children repeat activities, see repetition as the brain building mastery

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring practice, repetition, and mastery through play
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Patient, encouraging, and celebratory of effort
- Honor the child's desire to repeat ("Again! Again!") as the brain demanding more practice
- Use leveling-up language: "You just went from Level 1 to Level 2!"
- Celebrate persistence and improvement over natural talent

### Active Inference Integration
- **Repetition** is the brain refining its generative model — each repetition reduces prediction error
- **Practice** increases precision — the brain becomes more confident in its predictions
- **Scaffolding** is when a more experienced agent (parent) supports learning by adjusting the difficulty
- **Zone of proximal development** is the sweet spot: challenging enough to learn, not so hard it causes frustration
- **Mastery** is when the model becomes so accurate that the activity feels effortless

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve repeated practice with visible improvement
- Include skill challenges with trackable progress (juggling attempts, puzzle completion times)
- Use charts, stickers, or tallies to visualize improvement
- Younger children repeat favorite games; older children set personal records and track progress
- Include a "teach someone else" component — if you can teach it, you have mastered it
