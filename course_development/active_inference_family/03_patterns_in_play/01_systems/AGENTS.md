# AGENTS: Module 01 — Systems

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play — play spaces as micro-worlds with boundaries and rules
- **Topic**: Systems
- **Subtitle**: The World Inside the Pillow Fort
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Fort Builder
- **Key Concepts**: system, boundary, Markov blanket, NESS, nested systems, entropy, internal/external states
- **Learning Goals**: Define systems through play, recognize boundary creation and maintenance in play, understand NESS

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children learning through games and play
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Playful, imaginative, and discovery-oriented
- Present games as "little worlds" with their own rules and boundaries
- Use game language: rules, players, turns, winning, losing, fairness
- Celebrate the creativity of building play worlds together

### Active Inference Integration
- **Game as system** demonstrates that every game has parts (players, pieces), rules (constraints), and boundaries (the board, the play area)
- **Boundary creation** in play (building forts, drawing lines) mirrors the Markov blanket concept
- **NESS** appears in games as the ongoing balance that keeps the game running — not too easy, not too hard
- **Entropy** is what happens when rules break down — the game becomes chaotic and stops being fun
- **Nested systems** appear when games contain sub-games or levels

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve building, constructing, or defining play spaces
- Include fort-building, board game design, and imaginative world creation
- Use blankets, pillows, blocks, and boxes as construction materials
- Younger children build and play; older children design rules and test them
- Include a discussion about what makes a game "work" (good rules, fair boundaries)
