# AGENTS: Module 03 — Perception

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Perception
- **Subtitle**: The Joy of Peek-a-Boo
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Surprise Tracker
- **Key Concepts**: prediction error, active perception, surprise, habituation, sensory play
- **Learning Goals**: Experience prediction and surprise through play, understand how expectation shapes perception

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring surprise and expectation through games
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Delightful, surprising, and full of wonder
- Use the excitement of peek-a-boo and hide-and-seek as the entry point
- Frame surprise as the brain's favorite teacher — "Your brain just learned something!"
- Celebrate the joy of being surprised and the satisfaction of predicting correctly

### Active Inference Integration
- **Prediction** is what the brain expects to happen next — games are fun because they balance prediction and surprise
- **Surprise** (prediction error) is when reality differs from expectation — the core engine of learning
- **Habituation** is when surprises stop being surprising — the brain has updated its model
- **Active perception** means seeking out information — "I am going to look behind the curtain!"
- **Sensory play** engages multiple senses, giving the brain rich data to build its model

### Activity Design
- Activities should create moments of prediction and surprise
- Include hiding games, guessing games, and sensory surprise activities
- Use everyday objects that can be hidden, revealed, or transformed
- Younger children play peek-a-boo and simple hide-and-seek; older children design their own surprise games
- Include tracking: "Was I surprised? Did I predict correctly?"
