# AGENTS: Module 08 — Planning

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Learning through play
- **Topic**: Planning
- **Subtitle**: Treasure Hunts and Goal-Setting
- **Course**: Patterns in Play
- **Lab Type**: Play-Based Activity — The Treasure Hunt Designer
- **Key Concepts**: policy selection, expected free energy, temporal depth, goal hierarchy, collaborative planning
- **Learning Goals**: Design games and treasure hunts as planning practice, understand that designing a game requires thinking about the future

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring game design and creative planning
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Creative, empowering, and forward-looking
- Present game design as the ultimate planning challenge — you must imagine what players will experience
- Use design language: "create," "design," "test," "improve"
- Celebrate the shift from playing games to creating them

### Active Inference Integration
- **Game design** requires planning at a deep level — predicting what players will do, feel, and learn
- **Policy selection** is choosing the best strategy from multiple options — game designers build multiple paths
- **Expected free energy** (simplified) is asking "Which design will be the most fun and least frustrating?"
- **Temporal depth** is demonstrated by treasure hunts with multiple clues — each clue leads to the next
- **Collaborative planning** means the family designs together, combining multiple perspectives

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve designing, building, and testing games or treasure hunts
- Include treasure hunt creation (writing clues, hiding objects, mapping routes)
- Use craft supplies, paper, and household objects for game-building
- Younger children hide objects and give hints; older children write multi-step clue chains
- Include playtesting — try the game, then improve it based on what surprised you

### Key Connections
- Link back to Module 07 (Communication): Designing games requires communicating the plan
- Link to course overview: Game design ties together all eight concepts — systems, agents, perception, cognition, action, learning, communication, and planning
