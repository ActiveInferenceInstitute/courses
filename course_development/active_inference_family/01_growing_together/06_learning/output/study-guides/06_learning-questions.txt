Growing Together — Module 06: Learning — Think About It

Part A: Family Discussion Questions

What is the hardest thing you ever learned? (Walking? Reading? Riding a bike?).
How did it feel when you finally got it right? (Proud? Relieved? Excited?).
What does "Surprise" teach us? (That our brain needs an update!).
Why is it okay to make mistakes? (Mistakes are just signals that say "Update Required!").
Can grown-ups still learn new things? What is something Mom or Dad learned recently?
What does the word "YET" do? ("I can't ride a bike" vs. "I can't ride a bike YET").
Have you ever changed your mind about something? (Like a food you used to hate but now you like?).
Why do babies need so much sleep? (The brain is doing upgrades!).
What is something you want to learn next? How will you practice?
Who is the best teacher in your family? Why?

Part B: Hands-On Activities and Reflection

Create a "Mastery Museum": Find something you used to be bad at but are good at now (an old drawing, a photo, a video). Display it proudly and tell the story of how you learned.
Play the "YET" game for a whole day. Whenever someone says "I can't," the rest of the family shouts "YET!" Count how many times you use the magic word.
Teach a family member something you know how to do (fold a paper airplane, do a dance move, draw a star). Was it harder to teach or to learn?
Try something brand new together as a family (a recipe, a craft, a game). Write down your mistakes and what you learned from each one. Celebrate the mistakes!
Find a photo or video of yourself from one year ago. How have you changed? What new things can you do now? Your brain updated its model!
Make a "Learning Ladder" on paper. Write something you want to learn at the top. Below it, write the steps to get there. Color each step as you complete it.
Ask each family member: "What mistake taught you the most?" Share your stories at dinner. The best learners make the most interesting mistakes.
Try doing something with your non-dominant hand (write your name, eat with chopsticks, brush your hair). How does it feel to be a beginner again?
Read a book or watch a short video about something none of you know about. Then discuss: "What did our Family Brain learn today?"
Before bed, name one thing you learned today — even something small ("I learned that ants can carry 50 times their weight!"). Every day, our model gets a little better.
