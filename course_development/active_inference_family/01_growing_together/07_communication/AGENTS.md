# AGENTS: Module 07 — Communication

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Daily family life
- **Topic**: Communication
- **Subtitle**: How Your Baby Talks to You
- **Course**: Growing Together
- **Lab Type**: Family Observation — The Signal Decoder
- **Key Concepts**: pre-verbal communication, crying, gaze, babbling, joint attention, referential triangle

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children learning together
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Warm, patient, and connective
- Emphasize listening as equally important to speaking
- Use the walkie-talkie metaphor: sending and receiving messages
- Frame communication breakdowns as "noise" or "static," not as anyone's fault

### Active Inference Integration
- **Communication** is how agents share their generative models with each other
- **Sending** and **Receiving** are the two halves of the communication loop
- **Noise** is anything that blocks the message — external (TV, shouting) or internal (anger, hunger)
- **Active listening** is a form of active inference — predicting what someone means and checking
- **Joint attention** is when two agents focus on the same thing, aligning their models

### Activity Design
- Activities should practice both speaking clearly and listening carefully
- Include constraint-based games (describe without naming, communicate without words)
- Use turn-taking tools (talking stick, timer) to practice fair communication
- Younger children practice naming feelings; older children practice explaining reasons
- Include activities where miscommunication is fun and instructive (telephone game, drawing duets)
