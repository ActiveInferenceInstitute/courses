Growing Together — Module 04: Cognition — Think About It

Part A: Family Discussion Questions

  1. Who makes the big decisions in your family? (Parents? Everyone together?).
  2. What is one family rule you think is really important? Why?
  3. If you could make one new rule for the house, what would it be?
  4. What happens when you break a rule by accident? (Surprise! Time to learn).
  5. How does your family solve problems? (Talk it out? Vote? Ask a grown-up?).
  6. What is a "Value"? Can you name one thing your family believes in?
  7. Why do rules change as you grow up? (Because your brain gets smarter and you can handle more!).
  8. What is the silliest rule you can think of? Why would it NOT work?
  9. When was the last time your family had to make a decision together? How did it go?
  10. Why is it important to listen to everyone's ideas, even the youngest person's?

Part B: Hands-On Activities and Reflection

  1. Hold a "Family Meeting" and vote on something fun (movie night, dinner menu, weekend activity). Everyone gets one vote. How does thinking together feel different from thinking alone?
  2. Pick a family rule and trace it back to its reason. ("Why do we wash hands?" -> "To stay healthy" -> "So we can play more!"). Draw the chain of reasons.
  3. Invent a silly rule and try following it for 10 minutes (like "Walk backward to the kitchen"). Why is it silly? What makes a rule useful vs. useless?
  4. Draw a picture of what your family believes in — your "Family Crest." Include favorite activities, foods, values, and a family motto.
  5. Play the "What Would You Do?" game. A parent describes a problem ("The dog ate your homework!"). Everyone shares their solution. Compare ideas!
  6. Make a list of five family rules. Next to each one, write whether you think it is "Helpful" or "Needs Updating." Discuss together.
  7. Think of a time you changed your mind about something. ("I used to think broccoli was gross, but now I like it!"). That is your brain updating its model!
  8. Together, design a "Decision Tree" for a common family choice. (Should we go to the park? -> Is it raining? -> Yes: Stay home and build a fort. No: Go to the park!).
  9. Ask each family member: "What is the smartest thing you know?" Share your answers. Your Family Brain is the combination of everyone's knowledge!
  10. Before bed, think about the best decision you made today. What made it a good one? Write or draw your answer to share tomorrow.
