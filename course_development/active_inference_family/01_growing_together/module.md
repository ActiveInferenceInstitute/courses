# Unit 1: Growing Together — Our Family Is a System

## Learning Objectives

1. Understand that a **family** is a **system** — a group of connected people who depend on each other.
2. Notice that families have patterns and routines, and that **surprise** is what happens when the pattern changes.
3. Discover that parents and children are constantly adjusting to each other — and that this back-and-forth is a form of intelligence.

## Introduction: Families Work Together

Growing Together is about discovering that families themselves are systems — groups of people who are connected, who depend on each other, and who grow and change together. This unit is designed for parents and young children to explore side by side, using everyday family moments as windows into how systems work. No science background is needed. The ideas are woven into activities that families already do: cooking dinner, tidying up, taking walks, and talking about their day.

The eight lessons in this unit gently introduce each part of the Active Inference spine through the lens of family life. We start by noticing that our family is a system with members who each play a role. Then we discover that every family member is an agent — someone who senses, thinks, and acts. We explore perception by paying attention to how we notice things together (sounds, smells, changes in mood). We look at cognition through the family conversations where we figure things out. We celebrate action as all the things we do to take care of each other. We marvel at learning by watching how the youngest family members grow and gain new skills every day. We practice communication by finding better ways to share our feelings and needs. And we wrap up with planning — how families make decisions together about what to do tomorrow, next weekend, or next year.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The Family System

A family is a system of people, routines, and roles. Someone cooks. Someone tidies. The toddler makes a creative mess. Each person is a "part" that the system depends on — and when one part changes (Dad is traveling, the baby gets sick), the whole family adjusts.

### 2. Expectations and Routines

Families run on patterns: pasta on Tuesday, bedtime stories before lights-out, grandma visits on Sunday. These patterns are comfortable because everyone's brain knows what to expect. When the pattern changes — a surprise guest, peas flying off the table — the family has a small moment of *surprise*, and then it adapts.

### 3. The Family Boundary

Every family has a kind of invisible boundary — the "us" and the "them." Who sits at the dinner table? Who is included in the bedtime routine? This boundary is not a wall; it is more like a gently flexible membrane. Things flow in (new foods, new friends, neighbors saying hello) and things flow out (stories shared at school, a wave from the car window). The boundary helps define who is "part of the system."

## Activities

### 🍝 Activity 1: Dinner Table System

Tonight at dinner, play The System Game. Each person names their "job" in the dinner system (chopping, stirring, setting the table, taste-testing!). Then talk about what happens when one job does not get done. What if nobody sets the table? The system has to adjust. Draw a picture of your family dinner system and label all the parts.

### 🌙 Activity 2: Bedtime Routine Map

With your child, draw a map of your bedtime routine: Bath → Pajamas → Story → Song → Goodnight. Is this the same every night? What happens when you skip the story? Your child might protest — because their brain *predicted* a story! Talk about how the routine helps everyone feel safe and calm.

## Summary

Your family is a system of connected, loving people who adjust to each other every day. Routines create expectations, surprises break expectations, and adapting together is how families grow. Throughout this unit, you will discover that the science of Active Inference describes what families have always done: predict, notice, and adjust.
