# AGENTS: Module 03 — Perception

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Daily family life
- **Topic**: Perception
- **Subtitle**: What Your Baby Notices
- **Course**: Growing Together
- **Lab Type**: Family Observation — The Sensory Diary
- **Key Concepts**: prediction error, active perception, surprise, habituation, sensory preferences, prior beliefs

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children learning together
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference assumed

### Tone
- Warm, wonder-filled, and exploratory
- Celebrate the amazing things our senses can do
- Use sensory-rich language ("What do you smell? What do you hear?")
- Frame perception as a family superpower — noticing when someone needs help

### Active Inference Integration
- **Perception** is introduced as "gathering clues" about what is happening around us and inside us
- **Prediction error** is the surprise we feel when reality does not match expectations — the blindfold taste test makes this tangible
- **Active perception** means we do not just passively receive information; we seek it out (looking, listening, sniffing)
- **Interoception** (inside senses) is introduced as "body feelings" — hunger, tiredness, excitement
- **Habituation** is when we stop noticing something because it became expected (the ticking clock you forget about)

### Activity Design
- Activities should engage multiple senses — touch, taste, smell, sight, hearing
- Include a mystery or guessing element to make prediction error fun
- Use safe household items (foods, textured objects, familiar sounds)
- Younger children focus on naming senses; older children explore how senses connect
- Always include a safety note for activities involving blindfolds or unfamiliar objects

### Key Connections
- Link back to Module 02 (Agents): Agents use perception to understand their world
- Link forward to Module 04 (Cognition): What we perceive becomes the raw material for thinking
