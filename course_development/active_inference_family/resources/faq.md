# FAQ: Active Inference for Families

> Common questions from parents, answered in plain language.

---

### What is Active Inference?

Active Inference is a theory from neuroscience that says all living things — from bacteria to babies to you — survive by making **predictions** about what will happen next and then acting to make those predictions come true. When predictions fail, the organism either updates its mental model (learning) or changes the world (action). Your baby does this every waking minute.

### Do I need a science background to use this curriculum?

Not at all. This curriculum is written specifically for parents, grandparents, and caregivers with no scientific training. Every concept is explained through real parenting scenarios. If you've ever soothed a crying baby or played peek-a-boo, you already have the intuition — we're just giving it a name.

### What ages is this designed for?

The curriculum is designed for families with children aged 0–6, but the concepts apply to all ages. If you have a newborn, start with Courses 1 and 2. If your child is a toddler or preschooler, Courses 3 and 4 will feel most immediately relevant. But the ideas build on each other, so starting from Course 1 is always recommended.

### How long does each module take?

Each module lesson is about a 10-minute read. The family activity can range from 5 minutes (observation exercises) to 30 minutes (play-based experiments). The quiz and reflection questions are optional extras for parents who want to go deeper.

### Can I use this in a parent group or class?

Absolutely. Each module has a lesson, discussion questions, a family activity, and a quiz — making it perfect for weekly parent group sessions, childbirth education, or family therapy contexts. One module per week works well for most groups.

### Is this curriculum anti-screen-time?

No. Course 4 (Screens, Toys & Tools) takes a nuanced, evidence-based approach. Rather than giving rigid rules, we help parents think about *what kind of predictions* a given toy or screen experience allows a child to build. Some screen experiences are rich and interactive; others are passive and repetitive. The Active Inference lens helps you tell the difference.

### What's the difference between this and a regular parenting book?

Most parenting books give advice: "Do this, don't do that." This curriculum gives you a **mental model** for understanding *why* your child behaves the way they do. Once you understand that your baby is a prediction machine trying to make sense of the world, you don't need a rule for every situation — you can think through new situations on your own.

### Will this tell me if my child is developing normally?

This curriculum is not a diagnostic tool. It explains the *mechanisms* of development (how babies build models, explore, and learn) rather than prescribing timelines. We always emphasize that there is a wide range of "normal" in child development. If you have concerns about your child's development, please consult your pediatrician.

### What is a "generative model" and why should I care?

A generative model is your baby's internal "map" of the world — their collected expectations about what happens, when, and why. It starts incredibly simple at birth ("when discomfort → cry → someone comes") and becomes fantastically complex over the first few years. Understanding this concept helps you see *why* routines matter, *why* repetition is important, and *why* your toddler melts down when plans change unexpectedly.

### What is "prediction error" and is it bad?

Prediction error is just the gap between what your child expected and what actually happened. It's not inherently bad — in fact, it's the engine of all learning. A small, manageable prediction error (like a peek-a-boo surprise) is delightful and promotes learning. A large, unmanageable prediction error (like a parent suddenly disappearing without explanation) is distressing. Your job as a parent is to help your child encounter *the right amount* of surprise.

### Can two parents use this together?

Highly recommended! The discussion questions are designed as reflection exercises that can easily be done as a couple. The family activities work with any caregiver-child pair. Many parents find that understanding Active Inference helps them communicate better with each other about their parenting approach.

### How does this relate to attachment theory?

Beautifully. Attachment theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth) describes the deep bond between caregiver and child. Active Inference explains the *mechanism* behind it: a securely attached child has a reliable generative model that includes "my caregiver will respond when I need them." This reduces baseline prediction error and frees up mental energy for exploration. The two theories complement each other perfectly. See our [Glossary](./glossary.md) entry on Attachment.
