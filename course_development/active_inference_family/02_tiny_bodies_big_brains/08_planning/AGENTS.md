# AGENTS: Module 08 — Planning

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Developmental neuroscience
- **Topic**: Planning
- **Subtitle**: Executive Function Development
- **Course**: Tiny Bodies, Big Brains
- **Lab Type**: Development Tracker — The Planning Challenge
- **Key Concepts**: prefrontal cortex, inhibitory control, working memory, cognitive flexibility, temporal depth

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring routines, sequences, and future thinking
- No prior science background assumed

### Tone
- Warm, structured, and forward-looking
- Present routines as "autopilot programs" that free the brain for more interesting thinking
- Use step-by-step language that children can follow
- Celebrate the satisfaction of completing a plan and the flexibility to change one when needed

### Active Inference Integration
- **Planning** is the brain simulating possible futures and choosing the path with the lowest expected free energy (best predicted outcome)
- **Executive function** is the brain's control center — it holds goals in mind, resists distractions, and switches between tasks
- **Inhibitory control** means the ability to stop an impulse — "I want a cookie, but dinner is soon"
- **Working memory** holds the steps of the plan while the body carries them out
- **Temporal depth** grows with age — toddlers plan seconds ahead, children plan hours, adults plan years

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve creating and following multi-step plans
- Include routines (bedtime, morning) and special plans (party, trip)
- Use visual aids like picture schedules, timelines, and checklists
- Younger children sequence 3-4 steps with pictures; older children design full weekend plans
- Include a "plan change" element where something unexpected happens and the family adapts

### Key Connections
- Link back to Module 07 (Communication): We use words to share and coordinate plans
- Link to course overview: Planning ties all brain development topics together — it requires systems thinking, agency, perception, cognition, action, learning, and communication
