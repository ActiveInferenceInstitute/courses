# AGENTS: Module 06 — Learning

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Developmental neuroscience
- **Topic**: Learning
- **Subtitle**: Brain Plasticity in Action
- **Course**: Tiny Bodies, Big Brains
- **Lab Type**: Development Tracker — The Sensitive Period Observer
- **Key Concepts**: synaptic pruning, sensitive periods, myelination, sleep consolidation, plasticity

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring how the brain learns and changes
- No prior science background assumed

### Tone
- Patient, encouraging, and growth-oriented
- Present mistakes as the brain's best teacher — "Oops is a lesson!"
- Use construction metaphors: the brain builds highways for things we practice, and removes roads we do not use
- Celebrate the process of learning, not just the result

### Active Inference Integration
- **Learning** is the brain updating its generative model based on prediction errors (surprises)
- **Synaptic pruning** is the brain's way of making its model more efficient — removing connections that are not used
- **Sensitive periods** are windows when the brain is especially ready to learn certain things (language, music, movement)
- **Sleep consolidation** means the brain practices and strengthens new learning while we sleep
- **Plasticity** is the brain's amazing ability to rewire itself — it never stops learning

### Activity Design
- Activities should demonstrate that practice changes ability over time
- Include before/after skill comparisons and progress tracking
- Use repetition games that show improvement with practice
- Younger children celebrate each "level up"; older children track their own improvement with charts or journals
- Include activities that involve teaching someone else — teaching deepens learning
