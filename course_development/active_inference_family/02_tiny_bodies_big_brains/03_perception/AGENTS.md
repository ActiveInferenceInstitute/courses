# AGENTS: Module 03 — Perception

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Developmental neuroscience
- **Topic**: Perception
- **Subtitle**: How the World Comes Into Focus
- **Course**: Tiny Bodies, Big Brains
- **Lab Type**: Development Tracker — The Perceptual Milestone Checklist
- **Key Concepts**: visual development, auditory development, cross-modal integration, perceptual narrowing

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring senses together
- No prior science background assumed

### Tone
- Playful, sensory-rich, and discovery-oriented
- Present each sense as a "superpower" that can be trained and explored
- Use vivid descriptions that engage the reader's own senses
- Celebrate the incredible detail our senses can detect

### Active Inference Integration
- **Perception** is how the brain builds its model of the world from sensory data
- **Cross-modal integration** means combining information from different senses — seeing and hearing a clap at the same time
- **Perceptual narrowing** is the brain becoming specialized for its environment (babies can hear all language sounds; adults cannot)
- **Active perception** means we move our eyes, turn our heads, and reach out to gather more data
- **Prediction** shapes what we perceive — we hear what we expect to hear, see what we expect to see

### Activity Design
- Activities should isolate and combine senses (blindfold tests, sound matching, texture sorting)
- Include comparison activities: how does removing one sense change perception?
- Use safe household items for sensory exploration (foods, fabrics, sounds)
- Younger children do simple matching and naming; older children explore illusions and tricks
- Always include safety notes for blindfold activities and food allergies
