# AGENTS: Module 05 — Action

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Developmental neuroscience
- **Topic**: Action
- **Subtitle**: How the Body Learns to Move
- **Course**: Tiny Bodies, Big Brains
- **Lab Type**: Development Tracker — The Motor Map
- **Key Concepts**: cephalocaudal, proximodistal, body schema, motor planning, myelination, milestone ranges

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring movement and coordination together
- No prior science background assumed

### Tone
- Energetic, celebratory, and physical
- Present every movement as an achievement — rolling, crawling, walking, jumping, writing
- Use the metaphor of "moving mountains" — even small actions change the world
- Celebrate the variety of ways bodies move and the milestones children have already passed

### Active Inference Integration
- **Action** is the agent's way of changing the world to match its predictions and goals
- **Motor prediction** means the brain plans a movement before it happens, predicting what it will feel like
- **Proprioception** is the body's sense of where it is in space — the hidden sense that makes coordination possible
- **Practice** refines the motor model — each attempt updates the brain's prediction of what works
- **Body schema** is the brain's model of the body itself — its size, strength, and reach

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve physical movement and body awareness
- Include obstacle courses, balance challenges, and coordination games
- Use measurement (how far can you jump? how long can you balance?) to make progress visible
- Younger children do large motor activities (jumping, running); older children do fine motor challenges (drawing, building)
- Always include warm-up and safety considerations

### Key Connections
- Link back to Module 04 (Cognition): The brain plans the action before the body does it
- Link forward to Module 06 (Learning): Every action teaches us something about our body and the world
