# AGENTS: Module 01 — Systems

## Conventions
- **Perspective**: Developmental neuroscience
- **Topic**: Systems
- **Subtitle**: The Developing Nervous System
- **Course**: Tiny Bodies, Big Brains
- **Lab Type**: Development Tracker — Neural Network Mapper
- **Key Concepts**: neurons, synapses, neural circuits, myelination, experience-dependent wiring

## Content Guidelines

### Audience
- Families with children ages 3-12
- Parents and children exploring the body together
- No prior science background assumed

### Tone
- Warm, wonder-filled, and awe-inspiring
- Present the body as "the most amazing machine in the world"
- Use simple analogies: the heart is an engine, the brain is a computer, blood vessels are roads
- Celebrate how amazing it is that bodies grow, heal, and adapt

### Active Inference Integration
- **Body as system** demonstrates nested systems — the digestive system within the body, the body within the family
- **Homeostasis** is the body's version of staying at a non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) — always adjusting to stay "just right"
- **Inputs and outputs** mirror the sensory and active states of the Markov blanket
- **Growth** is the system upgrading its own structure — building new neural pathways, strengthening muscles
- **Sleep** is when the system consolidates learning and repairs itself

### Activity Design
- Activities should involve observing and measuring the body (heartbeat, breathing, height)
- Include before/after comparisons (resting vs. active heart rate)
- Use large paper for body tracing and drawing internal systems
- Younger children focus on naming body parts; older children explore how systems connect
- Keep activities safe and non-invasive — no sharp objects, no strenuous exercise for very young children
