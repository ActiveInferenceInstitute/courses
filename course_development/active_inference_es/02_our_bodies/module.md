# Unit 2: Our Bodies — We Are Agents

## Learning Objectives

1. Understand that **you** are an **Agent** — a living being that senses, thinks, and acts.
2. Learn that your body has an **Inside** (organs, feelings) and an **Outside** (the world around you).
3. Discover that your body is always trying to keep things "just right" (not too hot, not too cold, not too hungry).

## Introduction: Your Body Is Amazing

Now it is time to learn about Our Bodies! In the Story Time unit, we learned what systems are. In this unit, we discover that we ourselves are agents — living beings that can sense the world, think about it, and do things. Our bodies are amazing systems full of parts that work together: our eyes see, our ears hear, our brains think, and our muscles move. Each of us is an agent that lives inside the bigger systems around us, like our classroom, our neighborhood, and our planet.

Across the eight lessons in this unit, kids will explore their own bodies and senses while learning the Active Inference ideas in a hands-on way. We start by looking at our bodies as systems with parts that work together. Then we learn what it means to be an agent — someone who notices things and does things on purpose. We explore how our senses help us perceive the world, how our brains think about what we sense, how our bodies take action, how we learn new skills by practicing, how we communicate with words and gestures, and how we plan ahead (like packing a backpack the night before school). Every lesson includes movement, drawing, or hands-on activities so that learning happens through the whole body.

## Key Concepts

### 1. You Are an Agent

An agent is anyone (or anything!) that can sense the world and do something about it. You are an agent! A dog is an agent. Even a caterpillar is an agent. Agents have an *inside* (their body) and an *outside* (everything else).

### 2. Your Skin Is Your Boundary

Remember boundaries from Unit 1? Your skin is the most important boundary you will ever know. It keeps your insides safe from the outside. Light comes *in* through your eyes, sound comes *in* through your ears, but your organs stay safely inside. Your skin is like the glass of the fish tank!

### 3. Keeping Things "Just Right"

Your body has an idea of what "comfortable" feels like: a warm temperature, a full tummy, a calm heartbeat. When something changes — you run really fast and your heart pounds — your body notices the difference between what it expected and what it feels. Then it acts: you breathe harder, you rest, and things go back to normal. Scientists call this keeping things "just right."

## Activities

### ❤️ Activity 1: Heartbeat Detective

Close your eyes and put your hand on your chest. Feel your heart beating? That is your body's steady rhythm. Now do 20 jumping jacks. Put your hand on your chest again. Is it faster? Your body expected a calm heartbeat, but now it feels a fast one — a surprise! Wait one minute. Feel it slow down? Your body is an agent, fixing the surprise all by itself.

### 🐛 Activity 2: The Caterpillar Agent

Think about a caterpillar on a leaf. The caterpillar is an agent too! Its skin is its boundary. It can sense sunlight and smell whether a leaf is good to eat. When the caterpillar is hungry, there is a difference between what its tummy expects (full!) and what it feels (empty!). So the caterpillar acts — it crawls to a new leaf and starts munching. Draw a caterpillar and label its boundary (skin), its senses (feelers), and its action (chomping!).

## Summary

You are an agent! Your body senses the world through your eyes, ears, nose, fingers, and tongue. Your brain thinks about what you sense, and your muscles take action. Your skin is the boundary that separates your inside world from the outside world. And your body is always working to keep everything "just right."
