## Introduction

This module explores **Agents**. In the **Es** curriculum, we approach this topic with a focus on specific applications and theoretical depth appropriate for the audience. Agents is a critical component of the 8-part Active Inference spine, bridging the gap between Story Time and Counting Patterns.

Now it is time to learn about Our Bodies! In the Story Time unit, we learned what systems are. In this unit, we discover that we ourselves are agents -- living beings that can sense the world, think about it, and do things. Our bodies are amazing systems full of parts that work together: our eyes see, our ears hear, our brains think, and our muscles move. Each of us is an agent that lives inside the bigger systems around us, like our classroom, our neighborhood, and our planet.

Across the eight lessons in this unit, kids will explore their own bodies and senses while learning the Active Inference ideas in a hands-on way. We start by looking at our bodies as systems with parts that work together. Then we learn what it means to be an agent -- someone who notices things and does things on purpose. We explore how our senses help us perceive the world, how our brains think about what we sense, how our bodies take action, how we learn new skills by practicing, how we communicate with words and gestures, and how we plan ahead (like packing a backpack the night before school). Every lesson includes movement, drawing, or hands-on activities so that learning happens through the whole body.

## Applications

In Es, we see Agents manifest in:
*   **Specific Example 1**: You are an agent! Close your eyes and put your hand on your chest. Can you feel your heart beating? Your heart pumps blood all through your body without you even thinking about it. Your body knows what it expects to feel -- a steady heartbeat, a comfortable temperature, a tummy that is not too hungry. When something changes -- like when you run really fast and your heart beats super quick -- your body notices the difference between what it expected and what it feels. Then it does something about it: you breathe harder to get more air, and after you rest, your heart slows back down. You are an agent because you have an inside (your body) and an outside (the playground), and you are always working to keep your inside feeling just right.
*   **Specific Example 2**: Think about a caterpillar on a leaf. The caterpillar is an agent too! It has a tiny body with a skin boundary that separates its insides from the outside world. The caterpillar can sense things: it feels the leaf under its feet, it notices sunlight and shade, and it can smell whether a leaf is good to eat. When the caterpillar is hungry, there is a difference between what its tummy expects (full!) and what it feels (empty!). So the caterpillar acts -- it crawls to a new leaf and starts munching. The caterpillar does not know fancy science words, but it is doing the same thing you do: sensing, thinking (in its simple caterpillar way), and acting to make things better. Even tiny creatures are agents in their own systems!
