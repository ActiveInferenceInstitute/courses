# Station: Perception (Our Bodies)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Simple body science
- **Topics**: Perception
- **Lab Style**: Drawing & Coloring Lab
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Present the five senses as "Five Spies" who bring secret reports to Captain Brain.
- Use the metaphor: Eyes = Agent Eyes, Nose = Agent Nose, Hands = Agent Hands, Ears = Agent Ears, Tongue = Agent Tongue.
- Emphasize that the Brain has to GUESS what the clues mean -- perception is inference, not passive reception.
- Include illusions and tricks to show that the brain's guesses can be wrong.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Perception** is the brain's best guess about what caused the sensory data. The brain combines prior expectations with new evidence.
- For K-5: "Captain Brain sits in the dark. The Five Spies bring clues. The Captain puts the puzzle pieces together and guesses: 'It is an apple!'"
- Connect to Cognition (the guessing process) and illusions (when guesses go wrong).

## Lab Design Principles

- Labs should involve sensory mystery activities: the Mystery Box (touch without seeing), blindfolded taste tests.
- Materials: cardboard box, household objects, blindfold.
- Focus on the moment of guessing and the surprise when guesses are wrong.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "Which sense is your favorite?" "Can your eyes trick you?"
- Questions 11-20: "Draw the Five Spies bringing clues to Captain Brain." "Play the Mystery Box game."
