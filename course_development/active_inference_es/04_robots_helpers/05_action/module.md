# Module 05: Action — Robot Movers

## Learning Objectives

1. Understand that **Actuators** (Motors) make robots move.
2. Learn the difference between **Wheels** (Rolling) and **Legs** (Walking).
3. Know that every action uses **Power** (Battery).

## Introduction: Robot Movers

How do you move?
You use muscles.
How does a robot move?
It uses **Actuators**!

* **Motors**: Spin round and round (like a fan).
* **Pistons**: Push in and out (like a pump).

Without actuators, a robot is just a smart rock. It can think, but it can't DO anything!

## Key Concepts

### 1. Wheels vs. Legs

* **Wheels**: Fast on flat ground. Easy to build. (Cars).
* **Legs**: Slow but can climb rocks and stairs. Hard to build! (Boston Dynamics Spot).
Which one is better? It depends on where you are going!

### 2. Grippers (Hands)

Robots need to pick things up.
They use **Grippers**.
Some look like clamps. Some look like suction cups.
They are the robot's hands.

### 3. Precision

Robots can move very perfectly.
A surgeon robot can sew a grape!
A painting robot calculates every spray.
This is called **Precision**.

## Activities

### Activity 1: The Human Crane

Work with a partner.
Partner is the **Crane**.
You are the **Controller**.
Tell them how to move their arm to pick up a pencil.
"Lower... Lower... Close Gripper (Hand)... Lift!"
Robots need simple steps to move.

### Activity 2: Wheel or Leg?

Look at different terrains (Grass, Road, Stairs, Mud).
Decide: Would you use Wheels or Legs?

* Road -> Wheels (Fast!).
* Stairs -> Legs (Wheels get stuck!).

## Summary

Action is how robots change the world. By using motors, wheels, and grippers, they can travel to Mars, build cars, or clean your floor!
