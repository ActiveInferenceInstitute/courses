# Station: Communication (Story Time)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Narrative & imagination
- **Topics**: Communication
- **Lab Style**: Storytime Activity
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Introduce Communication through **The Ants' Secret Message** -- Andy the Ant uses a scent trail to tell other ants about a strawberry.
- Present Communication as "sending a message from one brain to another brain."
- Show multiple ways to communicate: talking, pointing, drawing, writing, even smelling (like ants!).
- Emphasize that communication helps agents work as a team -- alone Andy is weak, together the ants are strong.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Communication** in Active Inference is the sharing of generative models between agents, enabling collective inference and coordinated action.
- For K-5: "You have a map in your head. Your friend has a different map. When you talk, you share your maps! Now you both know more."
- Connect to Perception (receiving messages) and Planning (coordinating with others).

## Lab Design Principles

- Communication labs should involve **back-to-back drawing** or **secret codes** -- activities where one person describes and another acts on the description.
- Materials: paper, pencils, simple pictures to describe.
- Children should experience the challenge and joy of sending a clear message.
- Reflection should ask: "Did your friend understand your message? What made it hard or easy?"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "How do ants communicate?" "Can you communicate without making a sound?"
- Questions 11-20: "Create a secret code with a friend." "Draw a picture to tell someone a story without words."
- Focus on the many creative ways we share ideas.
