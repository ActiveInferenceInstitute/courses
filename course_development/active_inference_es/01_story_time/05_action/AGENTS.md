# Station: Action (Story Time)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Narrative & imagination
- **Topics**: Action
- **Lab Style**: Storytime Activity
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Introduce Action through **The Busy Beaver** -- Benny predicts a quiet pond and then builds a dam to make it happen.
- Emphasize that Action is "doing something to change the world" -- not just thinking about it.
- Show two kinds of action: changing the world outside you (cleaning a room) and changing yourself (putting on a coat).
- Use physical, energetic language: *Chomp, chomp, splash, drag, push, pat.*

## Active Inference Integration

- **Action** in Active Inference is how agents change the world to match their predictions. Rather than just updating beliefs (perception), action changes sensory input at its source.
- For K-5: "Thinking helps you plan, but Action is how you actually change the world! Benny did not just wish for a pond -- he built a dam."
- Connect to Cognition (predicting what you want) and Planning (choosing the best action).

## Lab Design Principles

- Action labs should involve **physical movement and charades** -- acting out words without speaking.
- Materials: paper slips with action words, a bowl, friends or family.
- Children should experience the connection between intention (what I want to show) and action (how I move my body).
- Reflection should ask: "What did you act out? Did your friend guess it? Were you a good actor?"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "What is your favorite action?" "How does action change the world?"
- Questions 11-20: "Act out an animal." "Draw a before-and-after picture showing how action changed something."
- Focus on the excitement and power of doing things.
