# Station: Systems (Story Time)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Narrative & imagination
- **Topics**: Systems
- **Lab Style**: Storytime Activity
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Introduce Systems through the **Forest Team** story (Squeaky the Squirrel, Mr. Oak Tree).
- Use the metaphor of "team" and "working together" to explain what a system is.
- Keep all examples concrete and visible to young children: bicycles, gardens, families, classrooms.
- The boundary concept should be taught as "edge" or "fence" -- something that shows inside vs. outside.

## Active Inference Integration

- A **System** is a group of parts that work together. In Active Inference, systems have internal states and external states separated by a boundary (the Markov Blanket).
- For K-5: "A system is like a team. It has parts inside and a world outside. The edge keeps the team together."
- Connect to the next module (Agents) by noting that some parts of a system can make choices.

## Lab Design Principles

- The Systems lab should involve **drawing and labeling** parts of a system.
- Materials: paper, crayons, imagination.
- Children should identify parts, draw boundaries, and show how parts help each other (arrows).
- Reflection should ask: "What is your system? What are its parts? What does it do?"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "Can you find a system in your kitchen?" "What happens if a part is missing?"
- Questions 11-20: "Draw your favorite system." "Tell a story about a system that lost a part."
- Use wonder language: "What would happen if..." rather than "Explain why..."
