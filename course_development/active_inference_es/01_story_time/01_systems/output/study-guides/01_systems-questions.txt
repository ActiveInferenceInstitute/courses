Course — Module 01 — Think About It

Can you find a system in your kitchen? What are its parts?
What would happen to a bicycle system if it lost its chain?
Is a pile of sand a system? Why or why not?
How are you and your friends like a system when you play a game together?
What is the "edge" of your house system?
Can a system be part of a bigger system? (Think about a leaf on a tree in a forest!)
Why do you think parts need to work together?
What is your favorite system? What makes it special?
If Squeaky the Squirrel left the forest, would the forest still be a system?
What is one system you use every single day?
Draw a picture of a system you love (like a video game console or a garden). Label all the parts!
Imagine you are a tiny ant living inside a computer. Tell a story about what you see -- what are the parts of the computer system?
Draw a fish tank system. Show the fish, the water, the plants, and the air pump. Draw arrows to show how they help each other.
Make up a silly system! What if socks, shoes, and feet were a team? Draw it and explain how they work together.
Tell a story about a system that lost one of its parts. What happened? Was the system sad? Did it find a new part?
Draw your family as a system. Who are the parts? What is the edge of your family system?
Imagine you are building a treehouse system. What parts do you need? Draw your plan!
Act out being a part of a system. Pick a body part (like a heart or lungs) and show what it does. Can your friends guess which part you are?
Draw two systems and show how they are connected. (Example: A garden system and a kitchen system -- the garden grows food for the kitchen!)
Write a short poem or song about systems. Start with: "We are a team, we work together..."
