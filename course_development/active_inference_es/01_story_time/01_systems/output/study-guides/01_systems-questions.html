Course — Module 01 — Think About It

  1. Can you find a system in your kitchen? What are its parts?
  2. What would happen to a bicycle system if it lost its chain?
  3. Is a pile of sand a system? Why or why not?
  4. How are you and your friends like a system when you play a game together?
  5. What is the "edge" of your house system?
  6. Can a system be part of a bigger system? (Think about a leaf on a tree in a forest!)
  7. Why do you think parts need to work together?
  8. What is your favorite system? What makes it special?
  9. If Squeaky the Squirrel left the forest, would the forest still be a system?
  10. What is one system you use every single day?
  11. Draw a picture of a system you love (like a video game console or a garden). Label all the parts!
  12. Imagine you are a tiny ant living inside a computer. Tell a story about what you see -- what are the parts of the computer system?
  13. Draw a fish tank system. Show the fish, the water, the plants, and the air pump. Draw arrows to show how they help each other.
  14. Make up a silly system! What if socks, shoes, and feet were a team? Draw it and explain how they work together.
  15. Tell a story about a system that lost one of its parts. What happened? Was the system sad? Did it find a new part?
  16. Draw your family as a system. Who are the parts? What is the edge of your family system?
  17. Imagine you are building a treehouse system. What parts do you need? Draw your plan!
  18. Act out being a part of a system. Pick a body part (like a heart or lungs) and show what it does. Can your friends guess which part you are?
  19. Draw two systems and show how they are connected. (Example: A garden system and a kitchen system -- the garden grows food for the kitchen!)
  20. Write a short poem or song about systems. Start with: "We are a team, we work together..."
