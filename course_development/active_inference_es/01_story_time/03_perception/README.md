## Learning Goals

1. **Name** all five senses: seeing, hearing, smelling, touching, and tasting.
2. **Understand** that your senses bring messages to your brain about the world.
3. **Discover** that your brain makes guesses about what your senses are telling you.
4. **Notice** that sometimes your senses can trick you (illusions and surprises!).
5. **Play** games that test your senses and challenge your brain's guesses.

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | What It Means |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Perception** | How you know what is happening around you, using your senses |
| **Senses** | Your five helpers: eyes, ears, nose, skin, and tongue |
| **Guess** | What your brain thinks is happening based on the clues from your senses |
| **Illusion** | When your senses trick your brain into making a wrong guess |

## Parent/Teacher Notes

This module teaches **Perception** through the story of Oliver the Owl, who uses his senses to investigate a mysterious sound in the forest. The key Active Inference idea is that perception is not passive -- the brain actively guesses what is causing the sensory input. Children naturally experience this when they see a shadow and think "monster!" before realizing it is harmless. Encourage children to pay attention to moments when their first guess was wrong. This builds intuition for the predictive nature of the brain.
