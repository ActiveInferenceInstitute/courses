# Station: Perception (Story Time)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Narrative & imagination
- **Topics**: Perception
- **Lab Style**: Storytime Activity
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Introduce Perception through **The Sleepy Owl** -- Oliver uses his ears, eyes, and nose to figure out what made a sound in the dark forest.
- Teach the five senses as "messengers that bring news to your brain."
- Emphasize that perception involves guessing: the brain takes clues and makes a prediction about what is out there.
- Use sensory language (*Snap! Crunch! Sniff, sniff*) to make the lesson immersive.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Perception** in Active Inference is the process of inferring the hidden causes of sensory data. The brain does not passively receive information -- it actively predicts what it expects to sense.
- For K-5: "Your brain is always guessing what your senses are telling you. Sometimes the guess is right (it's a bear eating berries!), sometimes it's wrong (it's not a monster, just a coat rack!)."
- Connect to Cognition (the guessing part) and Action (moving to get more clues).

## Lab Design Principles

- Perception labs should involve **sensory exploration** -- mystery sounds, touch boxes, taste tests.
- Materials: household objects, blindfolds, boxes with holes.
- Children should experience the gap between what they sense and what they guess.
- Reflection should ask: "What clues did you get? Was your guess right?"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "What is your favorite smell?" "Can you tell where you are with your eyes closed?"
- Questions 11-20: "Draw what you hear." "Tell a story about a time your senses tricked you."
- Focus on wonder and surprise, not technical terminology.
