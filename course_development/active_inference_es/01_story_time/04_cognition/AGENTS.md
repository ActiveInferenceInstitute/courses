# Station: Cognition (Story Time)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Narrative & imagination
- **Topics**: Cognition
- **Lab Style**: Storytime Activity
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Introduce Cognition through **The Thinking Cap** -- Sally sees grey clouds and predicts rain.
- Present the brain as a "Prediction Machine" that is always asking "What will happen next?"
- Use the metaphor of "maps in your head" to explain how the brain stores knowledge and uses it to make guesses.
- Emphasize that wrong guesses are not failures -- they are opportunities for the brain to update its map.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Cognition** in Active Inference refers to the brain's generative model -- the internal representation of the world used to generate predictions about sensory input.
- For K-5: "Your brain keeps a map of the world. It knows that clouds mean rain and fire is hot. When something new happens, you update your map!"
- Connect to Perception (incoming clues) and Learning (updating the map when surprised).

## Lab Design Principles

- Cognition labs should involve **pattern prediction** -- finishing sequences, guessing what is in a box, predicting outcomes.
- Materials: pattern cards, gift boxes, simple objects to shake and guess.
- Children should practice making predictions and checking if they were right.
- Reflection should ask: "What was your prediction? Were you surprised? Did you update your guess?"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "What are you thinking about right now?" "How did Sally know it would rain?"
- Questions 11-20: "Draw what you think is inside a mystery box." "Finish the pattern and show what comes next."
- Focus on the fun of guessing and the excitement of finding out.
