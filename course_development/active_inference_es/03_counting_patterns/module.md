# Unit 3: Counting Patterns — Prediction Detectives

## Learning Objectives

1. Understand that **Perception** means noticing **Patterns** in the world.
2. Learn that your brain makes **Predictions** (guesses) about what comes next.
3. Discover that **Surprise** happens when a pattern breaks — and that surprise helps you learn!

## Introduction: Patterns Are Everywhere

Welcome to Counting Patterns! In Story Time we learned about systems, and in Our Bodies we learned that we are agents who sense and act. Now we are going to use counting and patterns to understand how noticing things works. Patterns are everywhere — in numbers, in nature, in music, and in our daily routines. When we learn a pattern, we start to expect what comes next. And when something breaks the pattern, we notice right away! That moment of surprise is at the heart of how perception works.

The eight lessons in this unit connect math and pattern skills to Active Inference ideas. We start by finding patterns in systems (like the pattern of seasons or the pattern of a school day). Then we look at how agents use patterns to make guesses about what will happen next. We explore perception as pattern matching — comparing what we expect to see with what we actually see. Cognition becomes sorting and counting: organizing our observations into groups. Action is what we do when a pattern breaks and we need to fix our guess. Learning is getting better at recognizing patterns over time. Communication is sharing our pattern discoveries with friends. And planning is using patterns to predict what comes next — like knowing that after Monday comes Tuesday, and after Tuesday comes Wednesday!

## Key Concepts

### 1. Pattern = Prediction

When you see Red, Blue, Red, Blue, Red... your brain *predicts* that Blue comes next. You do not have to think about it — your brain does it automatically! Whenever you recognize a pattern, your brain is building a prediction.

### 2. Surprise = Prediction Error

What if the pattern goes Red, Blue, Red, Blue, Red, **GREEN**? Whoa! Your brain expected Blue, but it got Green. That surprised feeling is called a **prediction error**. Your brain just learned something new.

### 3. Counting the Surprises

Here is where math meets brains! You can *count* how many times a pattern breaks. If the pattern breaks a lot, it means your prediction is wrong and you need a new one. If the pattern breaks just a little, maybe it was a one-time mistake. Counting prediction errors is like scoring how well your brain's guess is doing.

## Activities

### 👏 Activity 1: Clap Pattern

Clap along: *clap-clap-stomp, clap-clap-stomp, clap-clap-stomp*. After a few rounds, everyone in the class knows what comes next. Now the teacher changes it: clap-clap-CLAP! Surprise! Your brain predicted stomp, but it heard clap instead. Count: how many surprises happened today?

### 🌳 Activity 2: Nature Walk Predictions

Before going outside, draw what you think you will see: three trees, a blue sky, some birds. This is your brain's prediction. Now go outside and *count* what you actually see. Five trees! A gray sky! No birds, but a squirrel! The differences are your prediction errors. The more nature walks you take, the better your predictions get.

## Summary

Perception is like being a detective: your brain looks for patterns, makes predictions about what comes next, and then checks its predictions against reality. When the prediction is right, everything feels smooth. When the prediction is wrong, you get a moment of surprise — a prediction error — and your brain updates its guess for next time. Counting and patterns help us measure how well our brain is doing!
