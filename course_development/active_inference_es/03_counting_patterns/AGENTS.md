- **Course**: Counting & Patterns
- **Number**: 3
- **Perspective**: Shapes, sorting, counting
- **Lab Type**: Hands-On Puzzle
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime. No formulas. Drawings and songs.

2. Frame all concepts through the lens of shapes, sorting, counting.
3. Include hands-on puzzle activities.

## Section Theme

- The **Kingdom of Numbers** is a place where everything has an order, a pattern, and a count.
- Numbers are tools that Agents use to understand and predict the world.
- Detective Brain uses counting, sorting, and pattern-finding to solve mysteries.
- Shapes, sequences, and estimation are ways the brain organizes information.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Systems** = the number system (ordered rules that keep everything predictable).
- **Agents** = the Counter (you, the detective who uses numbers to understand the world).
- **Perception** = pattern detection (noticing sequences like Red, Blue, Red, Blue...).
- **Cognition** = estimation and guessing (your brain's "best guess" before counting).
- **Action** = moving math (counting jumps, steps, claps -- doing math with your body).
- **Learning** = practice makes shortcuts (turning slow counting into fast knowing).
- **Communication** = numbers as a universal code (symbols that everyone agrees on).
- **Planning** = saving and spending (using numbers to make choices about the future).

## Content Guidelines

- Use "Detective Brain" as a recurring character who solves number mysteries.
- Make every concept hands-on: beans to count, patterns to build, shapes to trace.
- Present math as a superpower, not a chore. Counting solves mysteries. Patterns predict the future.
- Always connect back to the child's world: cookies, toys, steps, fingers.
- Celebrate estimation: "Close enough" is a valid and useful answer.
- Mistakes are "surprise signals" that help the brain fix its map.

## Lab Design Principles

- Labs should involve counting real objects, building patterns with beads or blocks, and estimation challenges.
- Materials: beans, buttons, colored beads, chalk, paper, pencils, dice, coins.
- Focus on the joy of discovery: "Can you find the rule?" "How close was your guess?"
- Include a log or table for recording observations with numbers.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: Understanding and recall. "What comes next in the pattern?" "How many fingers do you have?"
- Questions 11-20: Creative and hands-on. "Make your own pattern." "Draw your estimation vs. the real count."
- All questions should make children want to count, sort, and find patterns in their world.
