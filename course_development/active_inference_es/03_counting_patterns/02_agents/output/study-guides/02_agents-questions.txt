Course -- Module 02 -- Think About It

If you couldn't count, how would you know if you lost a toy?
Can you count your toes? Do you have the same number on both feet?
Who counts things in real life? (Shopkeepers? Scientists? Referees?)
Is it possible to count the stars?
What happens if you count too fast and skip a number?
If you have 2 eyes and your friend has 2 eyes, how many eyes are looking at me?
Can a robot count better than a human? Why?
Why do we start counting at 1?
What is the biggest number you know?
If you count your steps to the kitchen, is it always the same number?
Be an Agent Detective! Walk around your room and count every object you can see. Write the total in your Detective Log.
Play the Cookie Mystery: put 5 toys in a line, close your eyes, have a friend take some away. Count what is left. How many are missing? Draw a picture of the before and after.
Count the same thing two different ways (by ones, then by twos). Do you get the same answer? Draw both ways.
Find 3 things in your house that come in pairs (shoes, socks, mittens). Draw them. How many total items?
Make a counting book: draw 1 sun, 2 trees, 3 birds, 4 flowers, 5 stars. Write the number next to each page.
Interview a grown-up. Ask them: "What do you count at work?" Write down what they say.
Can you count by 5s to 50? Clap each time you say a number. Write the sequence down.
Play "How Many Fingers?" with a friend. One person holds up fingers behind their back and shows them quickly. The other person guesses how many. Play 10 rounds. How many did you get right?
Count all the letters in your first name. Count all the letters in your last name. Which has more? Draw a bar chart showing both.
Write a short detective story where the hero solves a mystery by counting something important.
