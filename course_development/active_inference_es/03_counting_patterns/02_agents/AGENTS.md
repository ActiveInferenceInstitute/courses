# Station: Agents (Counting & Patterns)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Shapes, sorting, counting
- **Topics**: Agents
- **Lab Style**: Hands-On Puzzle
- **Audience**: Grades K-5
- **Tone**: Wonder, play, storytime

## Content Guidelines

- Present the child as "The Counter" -- an Agent Detective who uses numbers to understand the world.
- Use the "Case of the Missing Cookies" story: 5 cookies in the jar, now only 2. Who ate 3?
- Teach One-to-One Correspondence: touch one item, say one number.
- Introduce Cardinality: the last number you say tells you "how many in the whole pile."
- An Agent uses numbers as tools to reach a Goal (finding out how many, solving mysteries).

## Active Inference Integration

- **Agents** are things that sense, count, and act to reach a goal. The child-as-counter actively gathers information about the world through counting.
- For K-5: "YOU are the Agent Detective! When you point at each cookie and say a number, you are doing an important job. The last number tells you how many are in the pile."

## Lab Design Principles

- Labs should involve counting objects in the real environment (room, backpack, kitchen).
- Materials: notebook, pencil.
- Focus on the detective metaphor: "How many? Let's investigate!"

## Question Design Standards

- Questions 1-10: "If you couldn't count, how would you know if you lost a toy?" "Can a robot count better than a human?"
- Questions 11-20: "Be a detective -- count everything in your room." "Play the Cookie Mystery game."
