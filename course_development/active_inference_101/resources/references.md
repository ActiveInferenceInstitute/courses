# References: Active Inference 101: College First Semester

> Recommended reading and resources for College 1st semester undergraduates.
> Readings are organized by topic and marked with difficulty level.

## Foundational Texts

These are the primary references for the entire curriculum. All students should read at least one.

1. Parr, T., Pezzulo, G., & Friston, K. J. (2022). *Active Inference: The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, and Behavior*. MIT Press. -- **The core textbook.** Covers all four course perspectives. Chapters 1-3 for conceptual foundations, 4-6 for mathematical detail, 7-10 for applications.

2. Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory? *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127-138. doi:10.1038/nrn2787 -- **The seminal paper.** Accessible introduction to the free energy principle as a unifying framework for brain function.

3. Friston, K. (2009). The free-energy principle: a rough guide to the brain? *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 13(7), 293-301. doi:10.1016/j.tics.2009.04.005 -- Shorter and more accessible overview of the core ideas.

## Course 1: Cognitive Science Perspective

4. Clark, A. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(3), 181-204. doi:10.1017/S0140525X12000477 -- Comprehensive review of predictive processing as a framework for cognitive science.

5. Clark, A. (2016). *Surfing Uncertainty: Prediction, Action, and the Embodied Mind*. Oxford University Press. -- Book-length treatment of the predictive mind hypothesis with rich examples from perception, action, and cognition.

6. Hohwy, J. (2013). *The Predictive Mind*. Oxford University Press. -- Philosophical perspective on how prediction error minimization explains perception, attention, and consciousness.

7. Seth, A. K. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Penguin. -- Accessible popular science book connecting predictive processing to consciousness and selfhood.

8. Kirchhoff, M. D., & Kiverstein, J. (2019). *Extended Consciousness and Predictive Processing*. Routledge. -- Explores how the free energy principle relates to embodied and extended cognition.

## Course 2: Computational Neuroscience Perspective

9. Bogacz, R. (2017). A tutorial on the free-energy framework for modelling perception and learning. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 76, 198-211. doi:10.1016/j.jmp.2015.11.003 -- **Highly recommended.** Clear, step-by-step tutorial with worked examples. Bridges math and neuroscience.

10. Bastos, A. M., Usrey, W. M., Adams, R. A., Mangun, G. R., Fries, P., & Friston, K. J. (2012). Canonical microcircuits for predictive coding. *Neuron*, 76(4), 695-711. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2012.10.038 -- How cortical circuits implement predictive coding through top-down and bottom-up message passing.

11. Rao, R. P., & Ballard, D. H. (1999). Predictive coding in the visual cortex: a functional interpretation of some extra-classical receptive-field effects. *Nature Neuroscience*, 2(1), 79-87. doi:10.1038/4580 -- Classic paper establishing predictive coding in visual cortex.

12. Adams, R. A., Shipp, S., & Friston, K. J. (2013). Predictions not commands: active inference in the motor system. *Brain Structure and Function*, 218(3), 611-643. doi:10.1007/s00429-012-0475-5 -- Active Inference applied to motor control and action.

## Course 3: Mathematical Frameworks

13. Da Costa, L., Parr, T., Sajid, N., Vesber, S., Ryan, V., & Friston, K. J. (2020). Active inference on discrete state-spaces: A synthesis. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 99, 102447. doi:10.1016/j.jmp.2020.102447 -- **Essential.** Complete mathematical treatment of Active Inference in POMDPs.

14. Beal, M. J. (2003). Variational algorithms for approximate Bayesian inference. *PhD Thesis*, University College London. -- Foundational reference for variational inference methods.

15. Friston, K. J., FitzGerald, T., Rigoli, F., Schwartenbeck, P., & Pezzulo, G. (2017). Active inference: A process theory. *Neural Computation*, 29(1), 1-49. doi:10.1162/NECO_a_00912 -- Formal process theory connecting the free energy principle to specific update equations.

16. Sajid, N., Ball, P. J., Parr, T., & Friston, K. J. (2021). Active inference: Demystified and compared. *Neural Computation*, 33(3), 674-712. doi:10.1162/neco_a_01357 -- Clear comparison of Active Inference with reinforcement learning and other frameworks.

## Course 4: Implementation & Simulation

17. Heins, C., Millidge, B., Demekas, D., Klein, B., Friston, K., Couzin, I. D., & Tschantz, A. (2022). pymdp: A Python library for active inference in discrete state spaces. *Journal of Open Source Software*, 7(73), 4098. doi:10.21105/joss.04098 -- **The pymdp library paper.** Essential reference for all implementation modules.

18. Smith, R., Friston, K. J., & Whyte, C. J. (2022). A step-by-step tutorial on active inference and its application to empirical data. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 107, 102632. doi:10.1016/j.jmp.2021.102632 -- Practical tutorial with code for applying Active Inference to behavioral data.

19. Friston, K. J., FitzGerald, T., Rigoli, F., Schwartenbeck, P., O'Doherty, J., & Pezzulo, G. (2016). Active inference and learning. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 68, 862-879. doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2016.06.022 -- Covers parameter learning and Dirichlet updates in detail.
