Part 2: Tracing the Action Loop

> Learning Goal: Follow the neural pathway from perception to action.

Scenario: You see a friend across the room and wave to them.

Trace the neural pathway through each structure, specifying what each does:

Visual cortex → What does it process?
Temporal cortex → What recognition occurs?
Prefrontal cortex → What decision is made?
Basal ganglia → How is the action selected?
Motor cortex → What command is issued?
Cerebellum → What prediction is generated?
Spinal cord / muscles → What happens?

Part 3: Neuromodulator Analysis

> Learning Goal: Understand how different neurotransmitters tune the system.

Case studies: For each drug/condition, identify which neuromodulator is affected and predict the Active Inference consequences.

Caffeine (increases noradrenaline): What happens to arousal and precision?
Nicotine (stimulates acetylcholine receptors): What happens to sensory attention?
L-DOPA (restores dopamine, used in Parkinson's): What improves? What might worsen?
SSRI antidepressants (increase serotonin): How might this affect temporal planning?

Part 4: The Cerebellum Challenge

> Learning Goal: Explore forward models through motor prediction.

Hands-on exercise:

Close your eyes and touch your nose with your index finger. Success? Your cerebellum's forward model is working.
Now try to write your name with your non-dominant hand. Notice the prediction errors — your forward model is less calibrated for this hand.
Why does practicing a motor skill (like typing) make it more automatic? Explain using cerebellar forward models.
