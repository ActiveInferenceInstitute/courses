# Practice Quiz: Action (Computational Neuroscience)

**Name**: _________________ **Date**: _________________

## Part A: Multiple Choice

**1.** In Active Inference, the motor cortex sends:
A) Direct commands to muscles
B) Proprioceptive predictions that spinal reflexes act to fulfill
C) Random signals that muscles interpret
D) Only inhibitory signals

**2.** The basal ganglia direct pathway:
A) Suppresses all movement
B) Facilitates selected actions by releasing them through the thalamus
C) Only processes sensory information
D) Is responsible for memory

**3.** Dopamine's role in the basal ganglia is to:
A) Strengthen the No-Go pathway
B) Tip the balance toward the Go pathway, favoring action initiation
C) Block all neural activity
D) Only affect emotional processing

**4.** Climbing fibers in the cerebellum carry:
A) Motor commands to muscles
B) Error signals from the inferior olive that correct forward model predictions
C) Visual information
D) Pleasure signals

**5.** Mirror neurons fire when:
A) You look in a mirror
B) Both when you perform an action and when you observe someone else performing the same action
C) Only during sleep
D) Only during intense concentration

**6.** Huntington's disease causes involuntary movements because:
A) The motor cortex is damaged
B) Loss of indirect (No-Go) pathway neurons means competing actions are not suppressed
C) The cerebellum is too active
D) Too much dopamine is produced

**7.** Motor learning in the cerebellum involves:
A) Growing new neurons
B) Modifying synapses between parallel fibers and Purkinje cells based on climbing fiber error signals
C) Memorizing conscious rules about movement
D) Increasing blood flow

## Part B: Short Answer

**1.** Explain why a patient with Parkinson's disease can sometimes move normally when catching a ball thrown unexpectedly (the "kinesia paradoxica" phenomenon). Use the distinction between basal ganglia policy selection and direct reflex-based movement.

**2.** Trace the cerebellar learning circuit for learning to ride a bicycle. What are the initial prediction errors? How do they change over weeks of practice? What role do climbing fibers play?

**3.** How do mirror neurons support Active Inference's claim that perception and action are fundamentally the same process? Explain using the concept of generative models.
