# Station: Perception (Cognitive Science)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Mind, brain, behavior
- **Topics**: Predictive processing, prediction error, Bayesian inference, precision weighting, illusions
- **Lab Style**: Essay & Discussion
- **Audience**: College 1st semester undergraduates
- **Tone**: Rigorous but accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module reframes perception as active construction rather than passive reception. Content should:

1. **Challenge naive realism**: Students typically assume perception is a direct window onto reality. This module should present compelling evidence (illusions, change blindness, the McGurk effect) that perception is a constructive, prediction-driven process.
2. **Define key terms precisely**:
   - **Predictive processing**: The framework in which the brain generates top-down predictions and compares them with bottom-up sensory data
   - **Prediction error**: The difference between predicted and actual sensory input, driving belief updating
   - **Precision**: The confidence assigned to a prediction or sensory signal, controlling how much weight it receives
   - **Bayesian inference**: Updating beliefs by combining prior expectations with new evidence
3. **Introduce precision as the "volume knob"**: This metaphor is central to understanding attention, anxiety, and clinical disorders in later modules.
4. **Connect perception to action**: Preview Module 05 by noting that prediction error can also be resolved through action rather than belief updating.

## Active Inference Integration

- Perception is one half of the free energy minimization story: updating the approximate posterior q(s) to better match p(s|o)
- Precision weighting corresponds to the inverse variance of prediction errors (Feldman & Friston, 2010)
- The Bayesian brain hypothesis is the cognitive science framing of variational inference

## Assessment Alignment

Questions should test the ability to:
- Explain how a specific illusion results from the brain's predictive processing
- Describe the role of precision in determining whether priors or sensory data dominate perception
- Apply the prior-likelihood-posterior framework to a novel perceptual scenario
