## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Mind, brain, behavior
- **Topics**: Shared inference, theory of mind, language as shared generative model, cultural norms, social media dynamics
- **Lab Style**: Essay & Discussion
- **Audience**: College 1st semester undergraduates
- **Tone**: Rigorous but accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module extends Active Inference from single agents to interacting agents. Content should:

1. **Reframe communication**: Communication is not mere information transfer but coupled inference -- two agents aligning their generative models to predict each other.
2. **Define key terms precisely**:
   - **Shared inference**: The process by which two or more agents align their generative models through interaction
   - **Theory of Mind (ToM)**: Modeling another agent's hidden states (beliefs, desires, intentions) within one's own generative model
   - **Cultural norms**: Large-scale shared priors that enable efficient prediction across a community
   - **Echo chamber**: A social environment where agents' priors are reinforced rather than challenged, reducing model diversity
3. **Cover multiple levels of communication**: Non-verbal (facial expressions, body language), verbal (language, prosody), and cultural (norms, institutions, rituals).
4. **Apply to modern challenges**: Echo chambers, misinformation, and polarization follow naturally from Active Inference principles.

## Active Inference Integration

- Communication as generalized synchrony between generative models (Friston & Frith, 2015)
- Theory of Mind as hierarchical inference: modeling another agent who is itself performing inference
- Cultural evolution as selection pressure on shared generative models (Veissiere et al., 2020)

## Assessment Alignment

Questions should test the ability to:
- Analyze a miscommunication using the concept of misaligned generative models
- Explain how echo chambers emerge from prediction error minimization
- Describe Theory of Mind as a multi-level inference process
