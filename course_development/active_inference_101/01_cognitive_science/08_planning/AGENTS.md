# Station: Planning (Cognitive Science)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Mind, brain, behavior
- **Topics**: Planning as inference, deep temporal models, imagination, counterfactual reasoning, habits, bounded rationality
- **Lab Style**: Essay & Discussion
- **Audience**: College 1st semester undergraduates
- **Tone**: Rigorous but accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module extends Active Inference into the future, showing that planning uses the same inferential machinery as perception. Content should:

1. **Unify perception and planning**: Perception infers what is happening now; planning infers what could happen next. Both minimize free energy -- one current, one expected.
2. **Define key terms precisely**:
   - **Planning as inference**: Evaluating possible action sequences by simulating their expected outcomes and selecting those that minimize expected free energy
   - **Deep temporal model**: A hierarchical generative model with predictions at multiple timescales (milliseconds to years)
   - **Counterfactual reasoning**: Running the generative model in "what if" mode to evaluate actions not yet taken
   - **Habit**: A policy with a strong prior, selected without full EFE evaluation, efficient but inflexible
   - **Bounded rationality**: Rational decision-making under computational and informational constraints
3. **Connect imagination to perception**: Imagination uses the same neural and computational machinery as perception, but with sensory input suppressed.
4. **Explain habits as optimized policies**: Habits free up cognitive resources for novel situations, but can be maladaptive when contexts change.

## Active Inference Integration

- Planning as inference over policies: selecting action sequences that minimize expected free energy (Friston et al., 2021)
- Deep temporal models implement hierarchical planning across timescales (Friston et al., 2018)
- The horizon problem and satisficing connect Active Inference to bounded rationality (Simon, 1955)

## Assessment Alignment

Questions should test the ability to:
- Compare planning and perception as parallel inference processes
- Analyze a habit using the concepts of policy prior and expected free energy
- Explain why perfect long-term planning is computationally intractable and how agents cope
