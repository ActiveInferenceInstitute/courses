## How Agents Organize and Update Beliefs

Part of **Cognitive Science** -- this module covers variational free energy, attention, cognitive biases, and the dark room problem.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

1. **Explain** variational free energy as a "badness score" for the agent's model, capturing how well it fits reality
2. **Analyze** the accuracy-complexity trade-off as the brain's fundamental design principle (Occam's razor for cognition)
3. **Evaluate** cognitive biases (confirmation bias, anchoring, overconfidence) as natural consequences of precision settings rather than irrational flaws
4. **Synthesize** an Active Inference response to the "dark room problem" using the concepts of preferences, epistemic value, and biological imperatives
5. **Apply** the concept of precision weighting to explain attention, ADHD, and anxiety

## Prerequisites

- Module 02: Agents (generative model, prediction error)
- Module 03: Perception (Bayesian inference, precision)

## Key Concepts

- **Variational free energy (VFE)**: A quantity measuring model fit -- high VFE means poor fit, low VFE means good fit
- **Accuracy-complexity trade-off**: The brain prefers models that explain data well without unnecessary complexity
- **Attention**: Precision optimization -- adjusting the weight given to different information channels
- **Epistemic value**: The value of actions or observations that reduce uncertainty
- **Dark room problem**: The objection that surprise-minimizing agents should seek boring environments (and why it fails)
