## What Are Living Systems?

Part of **Cognitive Science** -- the first module introduces systems thinking as the foundation for Active Inference.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

1. **Define** what a system is and explain the concept of emergence in their own words
2. **Distinguish** open systems from closed systems and identify why all living organisms are open systems
3. **Analyze** a given system by identifying its Markov blanket: internal states, external states, sensory states, and active states
4. **Compare** homeostasis (reactive regulation) and allostasis (predictive regulation) and explain why the distinction matters for understanding cognition
5. **Evaluate** how the concept of far-from-equilibrium dynamics connects to the free energy principle

## Prerequisites

- No prior modules required (this is the starting module)
- Basic familiarity with biological systems (high school biology level)

## Key Concepts

- **System**: A set of interacting parts producing emergent collective behavior
- **Open system**: A system that exchanges matter and energy with its environment
- **Markov blanket**: The statistical boundary (sensory + active states) separating a system's internal states from external states
- **Homeostasis**: Reactive regulation that corrects deviations from a setpoint
- **Allostasis**: Predictive regulation that anticipates future needs before deviations occur
- **Far from equilibrium**: The thermodynamic condition of living systems, which maintain order against entropy through constant energy input
