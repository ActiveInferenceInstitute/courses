# Station: Systems (Cognitive Science)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Mind, brain, behavior
- **Topics**: Living systems, open vs. closed systems, Markov blankets, homeostasis, allostasis
- **Lab Style**: Essay & Discussion
- **Audience**: College 1st semester undergraduates
- **Tone**: Rigorous but accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module introduces the foundational concept of **systems** as the starting point for Active Inference. Content should:

1. **Begin with intuition**: Start from everyday examples (cells, ecosystems, universities) before introducing formal definitions. Students at this level need concrete anchors before abstraction.
2. **Define key terms precisely**:
   - **System**: A set of interacting parts producing emergent collective behavior
   - **Open system**: Exchanges matter and energy with its environment (all living things)
   - **Markov blanket**: The statistical boundary (sensory + active states) separating internal from external states
   - **Homeostasis**: Reactive regulation maintaining a setpoint
   - **Allostasis**: Predictive regulation anticipating future needs
3. **Emphasize the cognitive science angle**: Frame the Markov blanket not just as a mathematical abstraction but as the basis for understanding how minds relate to bodies and environments. Connect to embodied cognition and ecological psychology.
4. **Scaffold toward subsequent modules**: This module sets up the system-agent distinction (Module 02), the perception-action loop (Modules 03 and 05), and free energy minimization (Module 04).

## Active Inference Integration

- Introduce the Free Energy Principle as the organizing framework: living systems persist by minimizing surprise (or free energy)
- Connect far-from-equilibrium dynamics to the thermodynamic motivation for the free energy principle (Friston, 2013)
- Present the Markov blanket formalism as the mathematical tool that makes Active Inference precise

## Lab Design for This Module

- Provide a case study of a living system (e.g., a cell responding to its environment)
- Ask students to identify internal states, external states, sensory states, and active states
- Include a group discussion on where the boundaries of the "self" lie

## Assessment Alignment

Questions should test the ability to:
- Distinguish open from closed systems with examples
- Identify the four partitions of a Markov blanket in a given scenario
- Compare homeostasis and allostasis and explain why the distinction matters for cognition
