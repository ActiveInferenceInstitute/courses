# Section Lab: Cognitive Science of Active Inference

## Objective

This integrative lab brings together all eight modules from the Cognitive Science course. You will analyze real-world cognitive phenomena through the lens of Active Inference, drawing on systems thinking, agent models, perception, cognition, action, learning, communication, and planning.

## Prerequisites

- Completion of all eight Cognitive Science modules (01_systems through 08_planning)
- Familiarity with key Active Inference concepts: generative models, prediction error, free energy minimization, Markov blankets, precision weighting, and expected free energy

## Part 3: Action and Exploration (Thought Experiment)

**Scenario**: After the confusing lecture, the student must decide what to do next: (A) re-read the syllabus, (B) ask a classmate for help, (C) visit the professor's office hours, or (D) drop the course.

**3a.** Analyze each option in terms of expected free energy (EFE). Which options are primarily pragmatic (reward-seeking)? Which are primarily epistemic (information-seeking)? How does the student's uncertainty about the course difficulty influence the exploration-exploitation balance?

**3b.** Now consider how the precision parameter (confidence in one's own model) affects the decision. A student with high precision on their prior belief "I am bad at this subject" will choose differently from a student with low precision on that belief. Describe how precision weighting shapes action selection for each case.
