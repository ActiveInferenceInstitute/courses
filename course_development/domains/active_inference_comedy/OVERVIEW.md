# Active Inference: Comedy & "Who's on First" — Pedagogical Overview

## Vision

This curriculum makes Active Inference accessible to comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the incurably curious through five complementary lenses drawn from the craft, science, and social life of comedy — all threaded through Abbott & Costello's immortal "Who's on First?" routine.

"Who's on First?" is not a metaphor for Active Inference. It *is* Active Inference — a dynamical system in which two agents share a syntactically identical but semantically divergent generative model. The word "Who" occupies two incompatible roles (a question pronoun and a proper noun), producing prediction errors that cannot be resolved because the agents' models are structurally isomorphic but referentially incommensurable. The routine never converges. Free energy is not minimized — it is *harvested* for laughter. Every exchange is a failed Bayesian update, and the audience knows it before either performer does.

Each course covers the same 8 foundational Active Inference topics but from a different aspect of the comedic art — from the mechanics of a single joke to the social dynamics of a live audience to the evolution of comedic tradition across centuries.

## Tone & Style

Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending. Like a theoretical physicist who also does improv. Real comedy craft knowledge — timing, callbacks, tag lines, blow, the rule of three — alongside real Active Inference formalism. The humor is in the material, not pasted on. We respect comedy as sophisticated embodied and social cognition, not a trivial illustration.

## Cross-Course Connections

The same 8 topics appear in every course, building a spiral of understanding from the single joke to the evolution of comedic tradition. A concept introduced at the bit-mechanics level in Bit & Boundary reappears as a performer strategy in Straight & Setup, a perceptual phenomenon in Timing & Tension, a social dynamic in Crowd & Callback, and an evolutionary principle in Riff & Repertoire. See [Cross-Course Map](./resources/cross_course_map.md) for detailed connections.

## The "Who's on First?" Thread

Across all five courses, the Abbott & Costello routine serves as the primary case study. The routine is analyzed at increasing depth:

1. **Bit & Boundary** — The exchange is mapped as a dynamical system with internal states, external states, and a Markov blanket at the point of dialogue.
2. **Straight & Setup** — Abbott and Costello are modeled as agents with distinct generative models, precision profiles, and action policies.
3. **Timing & Tension** — The perceptual parsing of "Who" is analyzed at the phonemic, syntactic, and pragmatic levels.
4. **Crowd & Callback** — The audience's role as a third inference agent is formalized, and the routine's cultural propagation is studied.
5. **Riff & Repertoire** — The routine's 80+ year evolution, its variations, and its status as a comedic attractor state are examined.

## How to Use This Curriculum

- **Sequential**: Work through one course start to finish — begin with Bit & Boundary for a structural foundation.
- **Parallel**: Study the same module across all 5 courses to see how one Active Inference concept manifests at every scale of comedy.
- **Exploratory**: Use the [Learning Pathways](./resources/learning_pathways.md) to follow your interests.
- **Performance-Based**: Use Straight & Setup alongside any other course for a performance-practice experience.
