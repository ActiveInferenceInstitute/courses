# Study Questions: Systems

1. When Abbott says "Who's on first," identify the **internal states**, **external states**, and **blanket states** of the comedic system. What counts as "inside" the routine and what counts as "outside"?

2. The dialogue between Abbott and Costello is described as the Markov blanket of the routine. Why is the spoken word — rather than the performers' thoughts — the boundary? What happens to the system if the performers stop speaking?

3. Compare a live performance of "Who's on First?" to a recorded version played to an empty room. In what sense does the recorded version still have a Markov blanket? In what sense has the system's boundary changed?

4. How does the audience's laughter serve as an **external state** that feeds back into the system? Does laughter change the internal states of the routine?

5. When Costello hears "Who's on first," he receives an observation at the Markov blanket. Explain why this observation is *degenerate* — meaning it is consistent with two incompatible internal models.

6. Abbott's internal model assigns "Who" the meaning of a proper noun. Costello's internal model assigns "Who" the meaning of a question word. These are two hidden states behind the same observation. How does this relate to the concept of a **likelihood mapping** in Active Inference?

7. Describe what happens when a comedian "bombs" in terms of the system's Markov blanket. Which coupling breaks down?

8. The routine has been performed thousands of times in different venues, decades, and media. In what sense is "Who's on First?" the *same* system across all these performances? What is invariant?

9. If you translated "Who's on First?" into a language where "Who" cannot also be a name (perhaps because the question word sounds nothing like any possible proper noun), what happens to the system? Does the Markov blanket still form?

10. Consider the audience member who has seen "Who's on First?" many times before. Their external states include prior knowledge of the routine's resolution. How does this change the system's dynamics? Why is the routine still funny?

11. In the routine, Costello occasionally makes progress — he understands that "I Don't Know" is on third base. This is a moment where internal states successfully update through the blanket. Why doesn't this progress propagate to the other positions?

12. A heckler shouts from the audience during a live performance of "Who's on First?" In what sense is the heckler injecting an unexpected external state into the system? How might the performers respond at the boundary?

13. The system of "Who's on First?" is described as *non-ergodic* — it does not reach equilibrium. Explain this claim in terms of free energy: why does the free energy of the system fail to decrease over time?

14. Compare the Markov blanket of a single joke (setup → punchline) to the Markov blanket of the entire "Who's on First?" routine. How do they differ in temporal extent and complexity?

15. Abbott's tone of voice — patient, slightly exasperated, matter-of-fact — is a feature of the active states at the Markov blanket. How does his tone modulate the system's dynamics? What would change if he delivered the same words in a different tone?

16. Consider "Who's on First?" as a system with three agents (Abbott, Costello, the audience). Draw the Markov blankets for each agent. Where do they overlap?

17. The phrase "Third base!" appears repeatedly as a kind of reset point in the routine. From a systems perspective, is this a genuine reset (returning internal states to their initial configuration) or a pseudo-reset (the internal states have changed but the blanket states cycle back)?

18. How does the *length* of the routine contribute to its systemic properties? Would a 30-second version of "Who's on First?" form the same kind of system as the full-length routine?

19. In Active Inference, systems tend to resist dissolution — they maintain their Markov blanket over time. How do Abbott and Costello actively maintain the routine's boundary? What habits or practices keep the system intact?

20. Reflect on the lifecycle of a "Who's on First?" performance — from the first word to the final exchange. At what point does the system "come into existence"? At what point does it cease? What remains after the performers leave the stage?
