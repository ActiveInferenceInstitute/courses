# Practice Quiz: Systems

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In "Who's on First?" viewed as an Active Inference system, what represents the **Markov blanket**?
A) Abbott's private knowledge of the players' names
B) Costello's confusion about what is being said
C) The spoken dialogue — the words exchanged between the performers
D) The audience's laughter

2. When the routine "bombs" (fails to generate laughter), what happens from a systems perspective?
A) The internal states improve
B) The coupling between boundary states and external states breaks down
C) The Markov blanket becomes stronger
D) Nothing changes — the system is self-sustaining regardless of audience response

3. The word "Who" in the routine is best described as:
A) An internal state of Abbott's model
B) A degenerate observation — a single sensory input consistent with two incompatible hidden states
C) An external state unrelated to the system
D) A planning variable used by the audience

4. Which of the following is an **internal state** of the "Who's on First?" system?
A) The acoustics of the performance venue
B) The audience's collective knowledge of baseball
C) Abbott's certainty that "Who" is a player's name
D) The cultural era in which the performance takes place

5. The audience in "Who's on First?" can detect the mismatch between Abbott's and Costello's models. They do this by:
A) Directly reading the performers' minds
B) Inferring the hidden internal states from the public boundary states (dialogue)
C) Being told in advance what the joke is
D) Having no generative model of their own

6. The routine is described as *non-ergodic*. This means:
A) It always reaches equilibrium quickly
B) Free energy steadily decreases over time
C) The system does not converge to a steady state — confusion escalates rather than resolving
D) The system has no external states

7. If "Who's on First?" were performed in a language where "Who" cannot be misheard as a name, the result would be:
A) The routine would be even funnier
B) The system's Markov blanket could not form because the degenerate observation is eliminated
C) The internal states would become visible
D) The audience would laugh harder

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how Abbott's utterance "Who's on first" functions simultaneously as an active state (from Abbott's perspective) and a sensory state (from Costello's perspective). Use the Markov blanket concept to clarify.

2. A comedian tells a joke to a wall. Is this a system in the Active Inference sense? Describe what is missing compared to a live performance of "Who's on First?"

3. The routine has been performed for nearly a century. Describe the system-level properties that make it so durable. What is invariant across performances, and what varies?
