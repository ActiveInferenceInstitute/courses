# Lab: The Routine as a Dynamical System

## Objective

Analyze a recording of "Who's on First?" and physically map its internal states, external states, and Markov blanket in real time, identifying the system boundaries of the comedic exchange.

## Materials

* A recording of Abbott & Costello's "Who's on First?" (the 1945 film version from *The Naughty Nineties* is recommended; widely available online)
* Printed or handwritten transcript of the routine (first 2 minutes)
* Three colored highlighters or pens (e.g., blue, red, green)
* Blank paper for diagramming
* A stopwatch or timer
* A partner (optional but recommended)

## Prerequisites

* Reading of Module 01: Systems lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with the terms: Markov blanket, internal state, external state, active state, sensory state

## Steps

### Part 1: Before the Performance (10 minutes)

1. **Before playing the recording**, lay out your transcript and highlighters. Assign colors: blue for INTERNAL states, red for EXTERNAL states, green for BLANKET (boundary) states.
2. On a blank sheet, draw three concentric regions: an inner circle (INTERNAL), a ring around it (BLANKET), and the space outside (EXTERNAL). Label them.
3. Before the routine begins, ask yourself: where are the system's states? Abbott and Costello exist, but no routine has started. Where is the Markov blanket? Write your answer on the diagram.
4. Prediction: Write down what you expect will happen to the system's boundary as the routine progresses. Will it strengthen? Weaken? Oscillate?

### Part 2: Mapping the First Exchange (15 minutes)

1. Play the first 30 seconds of the recording. Pause after Abbott's first statement about the team.
2. On your transcript, highlight in green every word that crosses the boundary between performers — these are the **blanket states**. In the first exchange, this is the dialogue itself.
3. In blue, annotate what you infer about each performer's **internal state** at this moment. What does Abbott believe? What does Costello believe? Remember: you are inferring — you cannot directly observe internal states.
4. In red, note the **external states**: What does the audience know? What is the venue contributing? What cultural knowledge is required for the routine to work?
5. Play another 30 seconds. Repeat the annotation. Notice: the internal states have changed (Costello is now confused), the blanket states have accumulated (more dialogue), but the external states (audience knowledge) remain roughly stable.

### Part 3: The System Under Pressure (15 minutes)

1. Play the routine from the 1-minute mark to the 2-minute mark. As you listen, track the moment when Costello's frustration peaks. Mark this on the transcript.
2. At the frustration peak, draw a new version of your concentric diagram. How has the internal region changed? Is it larger (more accumulated state) or more turbulent (higher free energy)?
3. Identify the moment when Costello says "Third base!" and seems to partially resolve the confusion about I Don't Know. Mark this as a **partial resolution** — a moment where free energy briefly decreases.
4. Does the partial resolution propagate to the other player names? Why or why not?

### Part 4: The Audience as External State (10 minutes)

1. If you have access to a version with audible audience laughter, play it again and note *exactly* when the audience laughs. Mark these moments on the transcript with a star.
2. Compare the laughter moments to the moments of maximum mismatch between Abbott's and Costello's internal models. Do they coincide?
3. The audience's laughter is an external state that the performers can perceive. How might this feedback affect the system's dynamics? Would the routine be different in a silent room?

### Part 5: Reflection and Discussion (10 minutes)

1. On a fresh sheet, draw a final diagram showing:
    * Internal states (both performers' models, accumulated exchange history)
    * External states (audience, venue, cultural context)
    * Blanket states (dialogue, tone, timing)
    * Arrows showing how information flows across the boundary

## Discussion Questions

* At what exact moment does the system of "Who's on First?" come into existence — when Abbott first mentions the team, when "Who" is first spoken, or when Costello first misunderstands?
* Can you identify moments where the Markov blanket is particularly "thin" (easy for the audience to infer internal states) vs. "thick" (internal states are ambiguous)?
* If you watched the routine with subtitles only and no audio, which boundary states would you lose? How would this change the system?
* How does this analysis change your experience of watching the routine?

## Wrap-Up

You have mapped a comedy routine as a dynamical system with formal internal, external, and blanket states. Keep your annotated transcript and diagrams — they will serve as reference material for the remaining modules in this course.
