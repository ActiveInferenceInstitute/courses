## Learning Objectives

1. **Identify** the boundary between a comedy routine's internal logic and its environment using the concept of a Markov blanket
2. **Recognize** how performers, words, and audience form an interacting dynamical system with internal and external states
3. **Map** the components of "Who's on First?" onto the formal structure of a system in Active Inference
4. **Distinguish** between the system's internal states (performers' models, exchange history) and external states (audience, culture, venue)
5. **Analyze** why certain routines persist as bounded systems across decades while others dissolve after a single performance

## Key Terms

- **System**: A bounded entity that maintains itself as distinct from its surroundings
- **Markov blanket**: The statistical boundary separating internal from external states, consisting of sensory and active states
- **Internal states**: Hidden variables shielded from direct observation (performers' beliefs, accumulated history)
- **External states**: Everything outside the blanket (audience, cultural context, venue)
- **Active states**: How the inside affects the outside (spoken words, gestures)
- **Sensory states**: How the outside affects the inside (heard responses, audience reactions)

## Prerequisites

- No prior Active Inference knowledge required — this is the entry point
- Familiarity with "Who's on First?" helpful but not required (the module introduces it)
