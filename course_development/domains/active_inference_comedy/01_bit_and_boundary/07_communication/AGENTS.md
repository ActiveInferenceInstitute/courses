## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Pragmatic failure as engineered ambiguity — how "Who's on First?" systematically violates the rules of cooperative communication
- **Topics**: Gricean maxims, pragmatic inference, communicative failure vs. success, engineered ambiguity
- **Lab Style**: Routine Analysis Lab — mapping Gricean violations exchange by exchange
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

"Who's on First?" is a masterpiece of **engineered communicative failure**. Abbott systematically violates Gricean maxims (the cooperative principles of conversation) — not through incompetence, but through the structure of the names he deploys. The routine creates a paradox: communication between the characters fails spectacularly, while communication between the performers and the audience succeeds perfectly. Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- **Gricean maxims** (quantity, quality, relation, manner) are clearly explained and their violations in the routine are catalogued
- The distinction between **character-level communication failure** and **performance-level communicative success** is maintained throughout
- **Pragmatic inference** is connected to Active Inference — the listener infers the speaker's intended meaning through a generative model of communicative intent
- The routine is shown to exploit the gap between what is literally said and what is pragmatically inferred

## Key Mappings for This Module

| Communication Event | Active Inference Concept | Comedy Consequence |
