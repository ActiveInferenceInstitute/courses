# Study Questions: Communication

1. Identify the four **Gricean maxims** (Quality, Quantity, Relation, Manner). Which maxims does Abbott violate? Which does Costello obey perfectly?

2. Abbott tells the truth — Who *is* on first — but his communication fails because of maxim of **manner** violation (ambiguity). Is a truthful but ambiguous statement a communicative success or failure?

3. Distinguish between the two levels of communication in the routine: (Level 1) character-to-character communication that fails, and (Level 2) performer-to-audience communication that succeeds.

4. The audience recognizes Abbott's maxim violation. How does this recognition function as **pragmatic inference** — inferring why the speaker said what they said?

5. Sarcasm involves saying the opposite of what you mean (violating the Quality maxim). Compare sarcasm's communicative mechanism to the mechanism in "Who's on First?"

6. Costello follows all Gricean maxims perfectly — he is truthful, informative, relevant, and clear. Why is perfect cooperation insufficient for successful communication?

7. An **inside joke** is a communication that works only because the participants share specific prior knowledge. How does "Who's on First?" function as an inside joke between the performers and the audience (with Costello excluded)?

8. Abbott uses multiple communication channels simultaneously: verbal (words), prosodic (tone), and gestural (body language). Do these channels reinforce or contradict each other? How does this affect the comedy?

9. The routine demonstrates that **shared language** is necessary but not sufficient for communication. What additional requirement must be met? (Answer: shared priors / shared generative models.)

10. In Relevance Theory (Sperber & Wilson), communication succeeds when the listener correctly infers the speaker's **informative intention**. How does the routine exploit the failure of informative-intention inference?

11. A comedian's use of silence (the deliberate pause) is a communicative act. What does silence communicate that words cannot?

12. Cross-cultural comedy often fails because humor does not translate. Explain this failure using the concept of **shared generative models**: the communicative success depends on the audience having the right priors.

13. In written comedy (e.g., a joke book), the communication is one-directional. How does the absence of performer-audience feedback change the communicative dynamics compared to live performance?

14. A deadpan delivery is a communication style where the face contradicts the content (saying something funny with a serious expression). Analyze this as a **cross-channel prediction error**.

15. In the routine, Abbott sometimes uses emphasis ("WHO is on first!") without resolving the ambiguity. How does emphasis function as a communicative act that modulates precision without changing content?

16. Compare "Who's on First?" to a game of telephone (Chinese whispers). Both involve communication failure, but what structural feature makes the Abbott-Costello exchange funnier?

17. The routine has been quoted, referenced, and parodied for nearly 90 years. This is itself a form of communication — the routine is *transmitted* across culture. What features make it transmissible?

18. In Active Inference, communication can be modeled as two agents trying to align their generative models through the exchange of observations. Why does alignment fail in "Who's on First?"

19. A bilingual audience member who speaks both English and a language where "Who" cannot be a name would experience the routine differently. Describe the communicative implications of having access to two linguistic models.

20. If Abbott and Costello communicated by text message instead of speech, would the routine work? What communicative channels would be lost?
