# Practice Quiz: Communication

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Abbott's communication in "Who's on First?" violates primarily which Gricean maxim?
A) Quality (truthfulness) — he lies about the names
B) Quantity (informativeness) — he gives too much information
C) Relation (relevance) — he changes the subject
D) Manner (clarity) — he is systematically ambiguous

2. The two levels of communication in the routine are:
A) Level 1: performer-to-performer (succeeds), Level 2: performer-to-audience (fails)
B) Level 1: character-to-character (fails), Level 2: performer-to-audience (succeeds) 
C) Both levels fail
D) Both levels succeed equally

3. For communication to succeed, speakers need to share:
A) Only a common language (vocabulary and grammar)
B) A common language AND common priors (shared generative models)
C) Nothing — communication always succeeds
D) Only physical proximity

4. The audience's recognition of Abbott's ambiguity is an example of:
A) Failed perception
B) Pragmatic inference — understanding why the speaker said what they said, not just what was said
C) Random guessing
D) Inability to parse English

5. Deadpan delivery creates comedy through:
A) Lack of any communication
B) Cross-channel prediction error — the face (serious) contradicts the verbal content (funny)
C) Matching all channels perfectly
D) Speaking very quietly

6. A comedian's silence (deliberate pause) is:
A) Not a communicative act
B) A communicative act that conveys processing, anticipation, and precision accumulation
C) Only meaningful if followed by speech
D) An external state of the venue

7. "Who's on First?" remains transmissible across nearly 90 years because:
A) Everyone memorizes it perfectly
B) Its core structure (degenerate observation + incompatible models) exploits universal features of language
C) It is forced on every generation
D) The specific cultural context of 1930s baseball is universally familiar

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain why Costello's perfect adherence to Gricean maxims is insufficient for communication. What does this tell us about the prerequisites of successful communication beyond shared language?

2. A stand-up comedian walks on stage and says "So..." followed by a two-second pause. The audience laughs. Analyze the communicative dynamics: what is being communicated, through what channels, and why does it produce laughter?

3. Design a "Who's on First?"-style routine for a non-English language. Identify the specific linguistic feature (ambiguous word type) that could replicate the degenerate observation, and explain why your choice would or wouldn't work.
