## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Why "Who's on First?" is funny even when you know every word — the paradox of repeatable comedy
- **Topics**: Model learning vs. performance learning, habituation, novelty, structural humor
- **Lab Style**: Routine Analysis Lab — comparing reactions across repeated viewings
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

This module addresses a deep puzzle: if humor depends on prediction error (surprise), why does "Who's on First?" remain funny on the hundredth viewing? The answer lies in the distinction between **model learning** (updating beliefs about the world) and **structural prediction error** (the routine's architecture generates fresh prediction error on every pass because the listener re-enters Costello's model each time). Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- The distinction between **Costello's failure to learn** (within the routine) and **the audience's learning** (across viewings) is sharply drawn
- **Habituation** (decreased response to repeated stimuli) is contrasted with the routine's resistance to habituation
- The concept of **structural humor** is introduced — comedy where the architecture, not the content, generates prediction error
- The performers' refinement across thousands of performances is treated as a separate learning process from the audience's experience

## Key Mappings for This Module

| Learning Phenomenon | Active Inference Concept | Comedy Instance |
|--------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Costello never learning the names | Failed model updating within the routine | Structural necessity — if he learned, the routine would end |
| The routine staying funny | Structural prediction error resistant to habituation | The architecture regenerates surprise on each pass |
| Performers refining across shows | Performance learning / policy optimization | Abbott and Costello tightened timing over thousands of shows |
