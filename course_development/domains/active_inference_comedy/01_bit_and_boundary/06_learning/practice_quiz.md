# Practice Quiz: Learning

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Costello's inability to learn that "Who" is a name is best explained by:
A) Low intelligence
B) His generative model has high model evidence — the observations are compatible with his model — so there is no pressure to revise
C) He is not listening
D) Abbott is actively preventing learning

2. When an audience re-watches a routine they already know:
A) No prediction error occurs because they know what comes next
B) Fresh micro-level prediction errors occur through timing, social context, and voluntary re-engagement with the ambiguity
C) They are simply remembering laughter rather than experiencing it
D) The routine relies entirely on surprise, so repeated viewing cannot be funny

3. Abbott and Costello refining the routine across thousands of performances is an example of:
A) Rote memorization with no adaptive change
B) Gradient descent on the comedic free energy landscape — optimizing timing, phrasing, and audience effects
C) Random variation with no learning
D) Learning that only benefits Costello's character

4. An open mic performance corresponds to which phase of the exploration-exploitation tradeoff?
A) Pure exploitation (performing proven material)
B) Exploration — testing new actions with high epistemic value in a low-stakes environment
C) Neither exploration nor exploitation
D) A fully converged policy

5. A joke becomes "hack" when:
A) The comedian deliberately makes it worse
B) The audience's generative model has absorbed the punchline, reducing prediction error to zero
C) The joke changes its structure
D) Cultural context becomes irrelevant

6. The "tight five" is best described as:
A) Any five minutes of material regardless of quality
B) A precision-optimized, converged action policy refined through hundreds of performances
C) An improvised five-minute set
D) The least funny five minutes a comedian has

7. Costello's non-learning is essential to the routine because:
A) Learning is irrelevant to comedy
B) If Costello learned (resolved the ambiguity), the comedic system would collapse — the prediction error engine requires ongoing model mismatch
C) Non-learning is always funnier than learning
D) The routine is too short for learning to occur
