## Learning Objectives

1. **Model** Abbott's sequencing of names and questions as an expected free energy optimization across a temporal horizon
2. **Analyze** the structure of the routine as a planned action policy with temporal depth — each name introduction builds on the last
3. **Apply** planning as inference to the construction and direction of comedy routines
4. **Distinguish** between character-level planning (Abbott-the-character is not trying to be funny) and performer-level planning (Abbott-the-performer has optimized every beat)
5. **Evaluate** why the routine's planning structure is so robust that it has survived nearly a century of performance with minimal modification

## Key Terms

- **Planning as inference**: Selecting future actions by evaluating their expected outcomes under a generative model
- **Expected free energy**: The anticipated surprise and information gain associated with a future action — the criterion for action selection
- **Action policy**: A planned sequence of actions designed to achieve a goal across a temporal horizon
- **Temporal depth**: How far into the future a plan extends — Abbott's plan spans the full routine
- **Blow / blow line**: The terminal high-amplitude prediction error — the routine's planned climax

## Prerequisites

- Modules 01-07 (all prior Bit & Boundary modules) — this module integrates all concepts into the planning framework
- Understanding that comedy routines are deliberately constructed, not spontaneous accidents
