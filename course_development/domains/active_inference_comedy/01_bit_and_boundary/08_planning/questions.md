# Study Questions: Planning

1. Abbott's sequencing of player names — introducing Who first, then What, then I Don't Know — is a planned order. Why does this specific order maximize confusion? Could a different ordering be more effective?

2. **Expected free energy** (EFE) combines anticipated surprise and anticipated information gain. When Abbott plans his next utterance, he is selecting for maximum EFE in Costello. Explain how this works.

3. The temporal structure of "Who's on First?" follows a setup → development → blow pattern. Map each phase to a planning concept: what is the objective of each phase?

4. Abbott could resolve the confusion at any moment. His *plan* is not to resolve it. In Active Inference, what does it mean for an agent to plan *non-resolution*?

5. A comedian building a 20-minute set must plan the order of jokes. What Active Inference principles guide this ordering? (Consider: warmup, escalation, callback, closer.)

6. Compare Abbott's planning to a chess player's planning. Both evaluate future states and select moves for downstream effects. What is the "checkmate" in comedy?

7. Abbott introduces "I Don't Know" (third base) later than "Who" (first base). This is a planning choice: "I Don't Know" is a multi-word name that creates a different kind of ambiguity. Why might he save it for later in the routine?

8. In **model predictive control**, an agent re-plans at each time step based on current observations. Does Abbott re-plan during the routine, or does he execute a fixed pre-planned sequence?

9. The blow line (the routine's climactic punchline) depends on the accumulated tension from all preceding exchanges. How does this illustrate the concept of **planning depth** — how far ahead the planner must look?

10. A comedian at an open mic has a rough plan (the set list) but must adapt to audience reactions. Describe this as **adaptive planning** — a combination of pre-planned policy and real-time adjustment.

11. An improv comedian has no pre-planned script at all. How does an improviser plan in real time? What Active Inference mechanism supports real-time planning without scripts?

12. The routine begins with a seemingly innocuous conversation about a baseball team. Why is this low-key opening part of the plan? What would happen if the routine started at maximum intensity?

13. Abbott's planning operates at multiple temporal scales: within a single exchange (micro), across the routine (meso), and across a career of performances (macro). Describe each scale.

14. When Costello achieves partial understanding ("Third base!"), Abbott's plan incorporates this: he lets Costello feel progress before re-introducing confusion. Why is this planned oscillation (hope → confusion) effective?

15. A comedy album is a permanently fixed sequence of material. The planning is complete — every word is set. How does this compare to Abbott's planning in the live routine?

16. The concept of **planning horizon** — how far into the future the planner considers — varies between agents. What is Abbott's planning horizon? What is Costello's?

17. A comedian writing a new bit must plan its internal structure: setup → development → punchline → tag → tag → blow. How many separate planning decisions go into a single 90-second bit?

18. Abbott and Costello performed the routine in different contexts (radio, film, television, live). Did the plan change for different media? What adaptations would be necessary?

19. In Active Inference, planning is equivalent to inference about future states. Abbott *infers* what Costello will say before it happens. How accurate are Abbott's predictions about Costello? Does their accuracy matter for the comedy?

20. Reflect on the routine as a whole: is "Who's on First?" primarily an achievement of planning (Abbott's strategy) or an emergent phenomenon (the collision of two models)? Can it be both?
