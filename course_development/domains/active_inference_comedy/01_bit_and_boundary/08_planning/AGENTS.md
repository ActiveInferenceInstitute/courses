## Conventions

- **Perspective**: How Abbott orchestrates the routine — the sequencing of names as expected free energy optimization
- **Topics**: Planning as inference, action policies, expected free energy, temporal depth, routine structure
- **Lab Style**: Routine Analysis Lab — reverse-engineering the planning structure of "Who's on First?"
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

Abbott does not introduce the names randomly. The sequence — Who (first base), What (second base), I Don't Know (third base) — is a planned escalation of ambiguity and prediction error. This module treats the routine's structure as a **planned action policy**: a sequence of moves designed to maximize comedic payoff across a temporal horizon. Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- **Planning as inference** is grounded in Abbott's sequencing decisions — each name introduction is analyzed as an expected free energy calculation
- The **temporal depth** of the routine's planning is made explicit — Abbott manages the audience's model across the full duration
- **Action policies** in comedy are connected to set construction, routine ordering, and the architecture of escalation
- The distinction between Abbott-the-character (who is not planning comedy) and Abbott-the-performer (who absolutely is) is maintained
