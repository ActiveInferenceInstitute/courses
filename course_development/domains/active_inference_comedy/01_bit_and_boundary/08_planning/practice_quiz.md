# Practice Quiz: Planning

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Abbott's sequencing of player names in the routine is best understood as:
A) Random and unplanned
B) An expected free energy optimization — introducing names in the order that maximizes Costello's confusion
C) Alphabetical by position
D) Determined by Costello's questions

2. The temporal structure of "Who's on First?" follows:
A) No discernible pattern
B) A planned setup → development → blow architecture that builds tension and prediction error
C) A flat, even distribution of comedy
D) A single joke repeated

3. Expected free energy (EFE) in comedy direction means:
A) Abbott selects utterances that minimize his own surprise
B) Abbott selects utterances that maximize Costello's anticipated confusion and the audience's anticipated delight
C) Free energy has nothing to do with comedy
D) Both performers minimize EFE equally

4. Model predictive control in live comedy involves:
A) Never changing the plan regardless of audience response
B) Re-planning at each moment based on real-time observations of audience reactions
C) Having no plan at all
D) Following the audience's instructions exactly

5. The blow line is the terminal action of the routine. Its effectiveness depends on:
A) Being unrelated to everything before it
B) The accumulated tension and prediction error from all preceding exchanges
C) Being the quietest moment in the performance
D) Having no connection to the setup

6. An improv comedian's planning differs from Abbott's because:
A) Improv has no planning at all
B) Improv planning is done in real time using rapid evaluation of expected free energy for candidate actions
C) Improv follows a script but hides it
D) Improv is always funnier than scripted comedy

7. Abbott's planning horizon (how far ahead he plans) is:
A) Only the next word
B) The entire routine — each utterance is selected for its effects on the whole
C) Exactly two exchanges ahead
D) He does not plan at all

## Part B: Short Answer

1. A comedian is building a 20-minute set. Using expected free energy as the guiding principle, explain why they should put their weakest material first and strongest last (the "build" principle) rather than frontloading their best jokes.

2. Abbott lets Costello achieve partial understanding ("Third base!") before re-introducing confusion. Explain this planned oscillation (hope → confusion → hope → confusion) as a free energy strategy for maintaining audience engagement.

3. Compare the planning demands of a scripted routine ("Who's on First?") to the planning demands of improv comedy. What computational advantages does having a script provide? What does improv require that scripts cannot?
