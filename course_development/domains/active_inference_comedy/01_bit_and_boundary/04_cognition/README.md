## Learning Objectives

1. **Explain** belief perseveration — the inability to revise models despite seemingly disconfirming evidence
2. **Analyze** the cognitive dynamics of model rigidity in both the straight man and the comic
3. **Apply** the concept of model evidence to explain why Costello's parsing of "Who" as a question word is locally rational
4. **Distinguish** between cognitive failure (Costello genuinely cannot update) and cognitive discipline (Abbott deliberately will not update)
5. **Evaluate** how comedy depends on the audience performing the model comparison that the performers cannot

## Key Terms

- **Belief perseveration**: Maintaining beliefs despite contradictory evidence, often because the model's priors are too strong
- **Model evidence**: The probability of observed data under a given generative model — determines which model "wins"
- **Cognitive rigidity**: Resistance to model updating, which can be functional (the straight man) or structural (the comic)
- **Model comparison**: Evaluating which of multiple generative models best explains the data
- **Free energy**: A measure of surprise under a model — high free energy means the model is a poor fit

## Prerequisites

- Modules 01-03 (Systems, Agents, Perception) — understanding of system structure, agent models, and perceptual inference
- Comfort with the idea that rational agents can reach different conclusions from the same evidence
