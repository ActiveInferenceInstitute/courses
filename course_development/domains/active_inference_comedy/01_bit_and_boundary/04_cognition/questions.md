# Study Questions: Cognition

1. Costello's generative model parses "Who" as a question word. His model is wrong in this context but correct in general English. Explain the concept of a **locally optimal, globally wrong** model.

2. In Active Inference, belief updating requires that the observation carry **disambiguating information**. Explain why the word "Who" fails to carry disambiguating information for Costello.

3. Abbott's cognitive discipline involves maintaining high precision on his priors despite Costello's escalating confusion. What would happen to the routine if Abbott's precision dropped — if he started doubting what the names were?

4. The audience holds two models of the routine simultaneously: Abbott's version and Costello's version. This is an example of **meta-cognition**. Explain what meta-cognition adds to the comedy.

5. Costello occasionally shows signs of near-understanding ("Third base!"). What causes these moments of partial clarity, and why don't they propagate to full understanding?

6. How does the concept of **confirmation bias** relate to Costello's inability to update? Is he confirming his prior, or is the evidence genuinely uninformative?

7. Compare Abbott's cognition to a sensor with very high signal-to-noise ratio and Costello's cognition to a sensor with very low signal-to-noise ratio. Where does the "noise" come from? 

8. The **curse of knowledge** means that knowing something makes it hard to imagine not knowing it. How does Abbott demonstrate the curse of knowledge? How does this drive the comedy?

9. Why does Costello's frustration escalate even though no new information is entering the system? What is accumulating if not information?

10. In what sense is the audience's cognition the most computationally expensive of the three agents' (Abbott, Costello, audience)? What extra computations does the audience perform?

11. Could a different opening strategy by Abbott (e.g., introducing names with context) bypass Costello's cognitive trap? Design an alternative opening and explain why it would or wouldn't work.

12. Costello sometimes asks Abbott to repeat himself. In Active Inference, repetition is a request for additional evidence. Why does repeated evidence fail to help Costello?

13. Model the "Third base!" moment as a partial belief update. What allowed this particular name ("I Don't Know") to be decoded when "Who" and "What" remain stuck?

14. How do performers manage their own cognitive states during performance? Is Abbott genuinely patient, or is his patience itself a cognitive performance?

15. In the routine, both performers occasionally speak over each other. What does overlapping speech imply about the rate of each agent's internal processing?

16. Compare the cognitive demands of watching "Who's on First?" for the first time vs. the hundredth time. What changes in the audience's cognitive processing?

17. Is Costello's inference process an example of **bounded rationality** — optimal reasoning with limited resources — or is it an example of genuine irrationality?

18. How does the concept of **model evidence** (marginal likelihood) explain why Costello's model never loses: his model always has high evidence because the observations are compatible with it.

19. If Costello were suddenly told "Who is a name, not a question" by a third party, would his confusion end instantly? Or would there be a transition period? Explain in terms of model revision dynamics.

20. The comedy depends on the audience recognizing the cognitive gap between performers. If the audience failed to detect this gap, what would they experience instead of comedy?
