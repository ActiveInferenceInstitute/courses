# Module 04: Cognition in Comedy

## Learning Objectives

1. Explain **belief perseveration** — the inability to revise models despite disconfirming evidence.
2. Analyze the cognitive dynamics of model rigidity in the straight man and model volatility in the comic.
3. Apply the concept of **model evidence** to explain why Costello's model never updates.

## Introduction

Costello is not stupid. This is perhaps the most important sentence in this module. Costello is a fully functional inference agent with a perfectly reasonable generative model of the English language. He knows that "Who" is a question word. He knows that when someone says "Who's on first?", they are asking a question. His model is right — in every context except this one.

The tragedy of "Who's on First?" — and its comedy — is that Costello's model *cannot update*. In Active Inference, belief updating occurs when the posterior (belief after evidence) differs from the prior (belief before evidence). But Costello's evidence is degenerate: every observation he receives is equally consistent with his current model. He hears "Who's on first," and his model says "Yes, that's a question about who is on first." He asks "Who's on first?", and Abbott says "Yes." His model says "He confirmed my question." Free energy does not decrease. The loop continues.

## Key Concepts

### Belief Perseveration: Costello's Epistemic Trap

In Active Inference, an agent updates its beliefs when observations carry information — when the likelihood P(o|s) favors one hidden state over another. Costello is trapped because his observations carry no disambiguating information. The word "Who" has equal likelihood under both the name-hypothesis and the question-hypothesis. This is not a failure of Costello's inference machinery — it is working perfectly. The failure is in the *environment*: the evidence available to Costello genuinely cannot resolve his uncertainty.

This phenomenon is called **belief perseveration** — the persistence of a belief even when the environment is sending signals that should (in principle) trigger an update. In Costello's case, perseveration is rational: he is not ignoring evidence; the evidence is genuinely uninformative.

Compare this to everyday cognition: how often do we persist in a belief not because we are ignoring evidence, but because the evidence we are receiving is compatible with our current model? Much of what looks like stubbornness may be rational perseveration under ambiguous evidence.

### Abbott's Cognitive Discipline: Precision as Weapon

Abbott's cognition is equally interesting, though less dramatic. He knows the names. His model is correct. But maintaining a correct model in the face of Costello's escalating confusion requires cognitive discipline — the sustained deployment of high precision.

Every time Costello asks "Who's on first?", Abbott must parse this as a statement confirmation rather than a question. Every time Costello expresses confusion, Abbott must resist the urge to rephrase, clarify, or accommodate. This is cognitively demanding: Abbott must suppress the natural tendency to adapt to his interlocutor's model. In Active Inference terms, Abbott maintains extremely high precision on his priors and very low precision on incoming observations from Costello. He treats Costello's confusion as noise, not signal.

### The Audience's Superior Cognition: Comic Irony

The audience's cognitive position is unique: they hold *both* models simultaneously. They understand that "Who" is a name. They understand that Costello thinks "Who" is a question. And they understand the gap between these models.

This dual-tracking is a form of **dramatic irony** — the audience knows something the character does not. In Active Inference, the audience's generative model has more states than either performer's: it includes the performer models as sub-models. The audience's cognition is *meta-cognitive*: they think about how the performers think.

The laughter occurs when the gap between models becomes locally salient — when a specific exchange makes the cognitive mismatch vivid. The audience does not laugh continuously (even though the mismatch is continuous); they laugh at moments of maximum *salience*, when the prediction error generated by tracking both models peaks.

## Applications

- **Confirmation Bias as Rational Perseveration**: Much of what psychology calls "confirmation bias" can be reanalyzed as rational belief perseveration under degenerate evidence. Costello's cognition is a comedy-scale demonstration of this phenomenon.

- **The Curse of Knowledge**: Abbott cannot understand why Costello doesn't understand. This is the "curse of knowledge" — the difficulty of modeling another agent's ignorance when you yourself know the answer. In Active Inference, it is a failure of theory of mind: Abbott's model of Costello does not adequately represent Costello's prior beliefs.

## Conclusion

Costello's mind is not broken — it is trapped. His generative model works perfectly on ambiguous data, producing the only inference it can. Abbott's mind is disciplined — maintaining certainty against the tide of confusion. The audience's mind is the most complex: it holds both models and laughs at the gap between them. Comedy is cognition made visible. In the next module, we turn from thinking to doing — from internal states to the actions that performers take.
