## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Every utterance, pause, and gesture as an action in the Active Inference sense — epistemic vs. pragmatic actions in comedy
- **Topics**: Action selection, epistemic actions, pragmatic actions, action as boundary crossing
- **Lab Style**: Routine Analysis Lab — cataloguing and classifying the actions in "Who's on First?"
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

In "Who's on First?", every question Costello asks is an **epistemic action** — an action taken to gather information and reduce uncertainty. The tragedy (and comedy) is that each epistemic action reproduces the same ambiguity it was designed to resolve. Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- Every utterance is treated as an **action** — a deliberate boundary crossing that changes the state of the system
- The distinction between **epistemic actions** (information-seeking) and **pragmatic actions** (goal-achieving) is clearly drawn
- Costello's questions are analyzed as epistemic actions that paradoxically increase rather than decrease uncertainty
- Abbott's statements are analyzed as pragmatic actions — he is simply stating facts, not seeking information
- The concept of **action as active state** (crossing the Markov blanket from internal to external) is grounded in specific dialogue
