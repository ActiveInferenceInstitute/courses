# Lab: Action Repertoire Analysis

## Objective

Map the complete action repertoire of a comedy performance: every word, pause, gesture, and audience response as an action in the Active Inference sense.

## Materials

* A recording of "Who's on First?" (video version preferred for gesture analysis)
* A printed transcript of the first 2 minutes
* Colored pens (4 colors): one for Abbott's actions, one for Costello's actions, one for pauses, one for audience reactions
* Stopwatch
* Blank paper for action sequence diagramming

## Prerequisites

* Module 05: Action lecture (module.md)

## Steps

### Part 1: Action Annotation (20 minutes)

1. Play the first 2 minutes of the routine. For each event, mark it on your transcript:
   - **Abbott's verbal actions** (blue): every word he says
   - **Costello's verbal actions** (red): every word he says
   - **Pauses** (green): every deliberate silence longer than 0.5 seconds, noting which performer is pausing
   - **Audience reactions** (orange): every audible laugh, gasp, or applause
2. For each action, annotate its **type**:
   - E = Epistemic (seeking information)
   - P = Pragmatic (achieving a goal)
   - S = Strategic (calibrated to affect the other agent's model)
   - R = Reactive (involuntary or emotional)

### Part 2: The Epistemic Loop (10 minutes)

3. Identify all instances of Costello's core epistemic action pattern: Ask → Hear "Who/What" → Ask again.
4. Count the number of loop iterations in the first 2 minutes.
5. For each loop, note: does the action change *at all* between iterations? Does Costello's phrasing, tone, or intensity shift?
6. If the action shifts, what is changing? (The answer is usually precision: Costello's actions become more frustrated, higher-pitched, faster — his precision on the question-interpretation is increasing even as his uncertainty remains.)

### Part 3: Abbott's Strategic Actions (10 minutes)

7. For each of Abbott's responses, evaluate: could he have phrased this differently to resolve the ambiguity?
8. List three alternative phrasings Abbott could have used and explain why each would break the comedic system.
9. Identify the specific features of Abbott's chosen phrasings that preserve the ambiguity: brevity, declarative tone, no spelling or definition.

### Part 4: Non-Verbal Actions (10 minutes)

10. If using a video recording, note Abbott's and Costello's physical actions: gestures, facial expressions, posture changes, eye contact direction.
11. For each non-verbal action, determine whether it reinforces or contradicts the verbal content.
12. Pay special attention to Costello's physical escalation: does his body language show increasing frustration independently of his words?

### Part 5: Audience as Action Agent (5 minutes)

13. Map the audience's laughter pattern: where does it cluster? What actions from the performers trigger it?
14. Note any moments where audience laughter *feeds back* into the performers' actions (e.g., the performers pause to let laughter subside before continuing).

## Discussion Questions

* If you removed all pauses from the routine (performers spoke in continuous, unbroken stream), what would happen to the comedy? What essential function do pauses serve as actions?
* Which performer's actions are more complex — Abbott's or Costello's? Which are more diverse?
* The blow line is the routine's terminal action. Is it the "biggest" action in terms of volume, duration, or prediction error generated?
* How does analyzing actions change your understanding of the routine's craft?

## Wrap-Up

You have mapped the complete action repertoire of a comedy routine. Every word, pause, gesture, and audience response is an action with inferential consequences. Keep your annotated transcript — the action patterns you identified will connect to the communication analysis in Module 07 and the planning analysis in Module 08.
