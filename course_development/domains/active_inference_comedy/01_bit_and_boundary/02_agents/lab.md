# Lab: Competing Inference Engines

## Objective

Perform a two-person comedy exercise to experience the dynamics of competing generative models firsthand, then analyze the experience using Active Inference agent concepts.

## Materials

* A partner (required for this lab)
* A printed list of five ambiguous words (provided below)
* A recording device (phone is fine) to record your exercises
* Pen and paper for reflection
* Timer / stopwatch

## Ambiguous Word List

Use these words, each of which can be a proper noun (name) and an ordinary English word:

1. **Grace** — a name / elegance or a prayer before meals
2. **Mark** — a name / a stain or target
3. **Bill** — a name / a financial charge or a proposed law
4. **Rose** — a name / a flower or past tense of "rise"
5. **Art** — a name / creative expression

## Prerequisites

* Reading of Module 02: Agents lecture (module.md)
* Completion of Module 01 lab (recommended)
* Willingness to perform and look a little silly

## Steps

### Part 1: Building the Routine (15 minutes)

1. Assign roles: one partner is the **Straight Man** (plays Abbott's role — knows the names), the other is the **Comic** (plays Costello's role — does not know the names).
2. The Straight Man chooses one word from the list (e.g., "Grace"). The premise: Grace is the name of a new employee. The Comic does not know this.
3. The Straight Man begins: "Grace is starting on Monday." The Comic responds naturally, interpreting "Grace" as the ordinary word.
4. Continue the exchange for 2 minutes. The Straight Man must never explain; the Comic must never realize (within the bit) that it is a name.
5. Switch roles and try a different word. Record both attempts.

### Part 2: Analyzing Your Agent Dynamics (15 minutes)

1. Listen to your recordings. For each exchange, annotate:
   * Moments where the Straight Man's **high precision** is audible (confident tone, matter-of-fact delivery)
   * Moments where the Comic's **model volatility** is audible (confusion, rising pitch, repeated questions)
   * Moments where the audience (you, listening back) detects the mismatch between models
2. On paper, draw two columns: STRAIGHT MAN MODEL and COMIC MODEL. For each exchange in your recording, write what each agent believes at that moment.
3. Circle the exchanges where the gap between models is widest. Are these the funniest moments?

### Part 3: The Audience Agent (10 minutes)

1. Play your recording for a third person (if available) or listen to it yourself as if hearing it fresh. Note:
   * When does the "audience" first detect the mismatch?
   * Does the audience understand both models simultaneously?
   * When does laughter (or the urge to laugh) occur?
2. On your agent diagram from Step 7, add a third column: AUDIENCE MODEL. Track what the audience believes about each performer at each exchange.

### Part 4: Breaking the System (10 minutes)

1. Perform the exercise one more time, but now the Straight Man deliberately **reveals** the name midway through ("No, Grace is her name. G-R-A-C-E."). Note what happens to the comedy. Does it survive the resolution?
2. Perform it once more, but now *both* performers are confused — neither knows it is a name. Note what happens. Is it funnier or less funny than the original version?

## Discussion Questions

* Which role was harder to play — the Straight Man or the Comic? Relate your answer to the cognitive demands of maintaining high precision vs. operating with a volatile model.
* When you broke character (if you did), what happened to the system? How does "corpsing" (laughing during performance) relate to the agent's Markov blanket?
* How does your experience compare to Abbott and Costello's dynamic? What did the professional performers do that you did not?
* Can you identify the "expected free energy" strategy of the Straight Man? What kinds of statements maximize the Comic's confusion?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced the agent dynamics of comedy from the inside. The straight man's discipline, the comic's spiral, and the audience's dual-tracking are not abstract concepts — they are felt experiences. Keep your recordings and notes for reference in later modules.
