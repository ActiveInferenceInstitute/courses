# Study Questions: Agents

1. In Active Inference, what makes something an "agent"? Identify the three features (Markov blanket, generative model, action) in both Abbott and Costello.

2. Abbott's generative model assigns "Who" the status of a proper noun. What is the **precision** of this assignment, and why is high precision essential to the routine's comedy?

3. Costello's generative model assigns "Who" the status of a question word. His model is *wrong* but *reasonable*. Explain why reasonableness matters — why would the routine be less funny if Costello's interpretation were bizarre rather than natural?

4. Describe the **audience** as a third inference agent. What information does the audience track that neither performer tracks?

5. Theory of mind requires maintaining a model of another agent's model. The audience of "Who's on First?" maintains models of *both* performers' beliefs simultaneously. Why is this dual-tracking necessary for the comedy to work?

6. Compare Abbott to a thermostat and Costello to a weather vane. In what sense do these metaphors capture their respective agent properties? Where do the metaphors break down?

7. When Costello says "That's what I want to find out!" after Abbott says "Who's on first," what does this reveal about Costello's generative model? What would a different response reveal about a different model?

8. Abbott never says "The player's name is Who — W-H-O, a proper name." He could resolve the confusion instantly. Why doesn't he? Answer in terms of Abbott's action policy and expected free energy.

9. In improv comedy, performers follow the "Yes, And" principle — accepting the other performer's contributions and building on them. Does Abbott follow "Yes, And"? Does Costello? How does this relate to their agentive properties?

10. If you replaced Costello with a very smart AI language model that could detect the name/question ambiguity, would the routine still be funny? What agentive property would the AI need to lack?

11. The straight man is often described as the harder role. In Active Inference terms, why is maintaining very high precision consistently more cognitively demanding than having a volatile model?

12. Costello sometimes directs his frustration at Abbott — "You're the manager, you should know!" At this moment, Costello is modeling Abbott's *epistemic state*. What does this tell us about Costello's theory of mind?

13. Consider a version of the routine where *both* performers are confused (neither knows the names). Would this produce more or less comedy? Answer in terms of the precision contrast between agents.

14. The audience member who has never heard "Who's on First?" before and the audience member who has heard it a hundred times are both inference agents. How do their generative models differ? Why does the routine work for both?

15. In the routine, Costello occasionally says "Third base!" as if he has learned something. Model this moment: has Costello's agent actually updated its generative model, or is he performing a pseudo-update?

16. Abbott's tone — patient, slightly exasperated, matter-of-fact — is part of his agentive presentation. How does tone function as part of the agent's active states at the Markov blanket?

17. If Abbott were to break character and laugh, he would momentarily cease being a high-precision agent. How would this affect the system? (Consider the concept of "corpsing" in theater.)

18. Model a **heckler** as an adversarial inference agent who attempts to destabilize the performer's generative model. How does the heckler's agentive strategy differ from Costello's?

19. In many comedy duos (Laurel & Hardy, Key & Peele, Wayne & Shuster), the same straight-man/comic dynamics appear. Is this convergent evolution of agent structures? What selective pressures produce this pair?

20. Reflect on yourself as an inference agent watching or reading about "Who's on First?" What is your generative model of the routine? How does your model of Abbott differ from your model of Costello?
