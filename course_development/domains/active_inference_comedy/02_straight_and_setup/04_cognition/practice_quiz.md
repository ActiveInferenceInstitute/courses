# Practice Quiz: Cognition in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. "Dual-track cognition" in comedy performance refers to:
A) Thinking about two different jokes at the same time
B) Running the character's model and the performer's meta-model simultaneously
C) The audience thinking one thing while the performer thinks another
D) Performing in two different venues

2. Extensive rehearsal reduces cognitive load by:
A) Eliminating the need for any cognition during performance
B) Automating lower-level processes (dialogue, basic timing), freeing resources for higher-level monitoring
C) Making the performer forget the material so they must improvise
D) Replacing the performer's model entirely

3. "Corpsing" occurs when:
A) The performer forgets their lines
B) The audience stops laughing
C) The performer's meta-model breaches the character's Markov blanket — genuine amusement leaks through
D) The session ends

4. The comedian's theory of mind requires modeling:
A) Only the audience's preferences
B) The audience's priors, their likelihood mapping, and their prediction timing
C) Only the other performer's next line
D) The venue's financial situation

5. Character comedy requires rapid model switching. The cognitive cost of this is analogous to:
A) Sleep
B) Task-switching costs measured in psychology laboratories
C) Learning a new language
D) Counting backwards

6. Abbott's cognitive strategy requires him to:
A) Rapidly update his model based on Costello's confusion
B) Suppress normal conversational accommodation and maintain certainty under social pressure
C) Ignore the audience entirely
D) Improvise new material every performance

7. The fundamental cognitive challenge of comedy performance is:
A) Memorizing long scripts
B) Running multiple cognitive tracks simultaneously while making effortful work appear effortless
C) Being naturally funny with no preparation
D) Understanding mathematics

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain why "corpsing" (breaking character) is often funnier than the planned joke. What does it reveal about the audience's model of the performance?

2. The module claims comedy performance is "as demanding as any virtuoso performance." What evidence from the module supports this claim? What counter-evidence might weaken it?

3. Describe the dual-track cognitive structure when a solo comedian does an "act-out" — switching from narrating a story (straight man mode) to performing a character within it (comic mode). What has to happen cognitively during the switch?
