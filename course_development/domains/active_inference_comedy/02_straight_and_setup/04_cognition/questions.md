# Study Questions: Cognition in Straight & Setup

1. The module identifies "dual-track cognition" in performers: the character model and the performer's meta-model. How are these two tracks analogous to the distinction between System 1 and System 2 thinking in cognitive psychology?

2. Abbott's character model is simple ("I know the names"). His meta-model is complex ("we are at the third exchange, the energy is building"). What would happen if the character model became complex? Could a straight man have a complex character model and still be funny?

3. Cognitive load management depends on rehearsal reducing lower-level processes to automaticity. What evidence from neuroscience supports the claim that highly rehearsed behaviors require less conscious cognitive control? How would you test this in a comedy context?

4. "Corpsing" is described as a meta-model breach — the performer's genuine amusement leaking through the character's Markov blanket. Why is corpsing often funnier than the planned joke? What does this reveal about the audience's model of the performer?

5. The comedian's theory of mind involves modeling the audience's priors, their likelihood mapping, and their prediction timing. Which of these three is hardest to estimate in real time? Which requires the most experience?

6. Character comedy (sketch shows) involves "switching between radically different generative models in seconds." What are the cognitive costs of model switching? Do performers experience a switch cost similar to task-switching costs measured in psychology labs?

7. The module asks: "What goes on inside Abbott's head during the routine?" Can we ever know the answer to this question from the outside, or are internal cognitive states always inferred? How does this relate to the Markov blanket concept?

8. Abbott must "suppress every natural impulse to clarify, rephrase, or accommodate." From a social cognition perspective, this suppression violates normal conversational norms (Grice's Cooperative Principle). How does the comedic frame license this violation?

9. The module describes the performer's meta-model as tracking audience reactions, partner timing, energy dynamics, and routine structure simultaneously. Is this actually parallel cognition, or rapid serial switching? What empirical evidence might distinguish the two?

10. If extensive rehearsal reduces cognitive load at lower levels, what happens when something unexpected occurs (a heckler, a technical failure)? How does the performer recruit cognitive resources back from automatized processes?

11. Compare the dual-track cognition of a comedian to that of a surgeon performing a routine operation while monitoring for complications. What structural parallels exist? Where does the analogy break down?

12. The module states that "comedy looks easy because the hardest cognitive work is hidden behind the Markov blanket of character." Is this true of all expert performance (music, sports, surgery)? What makes comedy performance uniquely demanding?

13. Theory of mind is described as "the cognitive foundation of all social comedy." Can comedy exist without theory of mind? Consider slapstick, absurdism, and physical comedy — do they require modeling the audience's expectations?

14. When a performer breaks character intentionally (an aside to the audience, a knowing look at the camera), they are deliberately thinning the character's Markov blanket. Why is this effective? What information crosses the boundary that normally wouldn't?

15. The module describes the performer as tracking "where we are in the routine." This temporal self-awareness requires a cognitive map of the routine's structure. How is this map represented? Is it sequential (step by step) or hierarchical (nested chunks)?

16. Abbott maintains "absolute certainty" while tracking complex meta-level dynamics. Is this a contradiction? Can a system have high certainty at one level and high uncertainty at another simultaneously?

17. The module mentions that many actors describe the straight man's role as harder than the comic's. Design a cognitive experiment to test this claim. What would you measure? What would confirm or disconfirm it?

18. Consider a comedian who has never studied theory of mind formally but is excellent at reading audiences. Is their theory of mind implicit (procedural) or explicit (declarative)? Does studying Active Inference change their performance?

19. Cognitive load peaks during improvised moments — when the script fails and the performer must generate material in real time. How does the transition from scripted to improvised performance change the dual-track structure?

20. The module concludes that the straight man's discipline is "as demanding as any virtuoso performance." Compare the cognitive demands of a professional straight man to a professional classical pianist. What are the shared features of expert performance across domains?
