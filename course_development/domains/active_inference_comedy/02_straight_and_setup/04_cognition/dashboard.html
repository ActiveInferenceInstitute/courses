Key Concepts

Dual-Track Cognition +

Every performer runs two cognitive tracks simultaneously: the character model (what the character knows and feels) and the performer's meta-model (tracking audience, timing, partner, routine progress). This is the hidden engine of comedy.

The character model is simple; the meta-model is complex. The audience sees only the character.

Centrality to module

Cognitive Load Management +

Rehearsal reduces lower-level processes to automaticity, freeing cognitive resources for higher-level monitoring. The seasoned performer's words flow automatically while their mind tracks the room.

Expert performance looks effortless because the hardest cognitive work is hidden behind the Markov blanket of character.

Centrality to module

Theory of Mind +

The comedian's theory of mind involves modeling the audience's priors, their likelihood mapping, and their prediction timing. This is the cognitive foundation of all social comedy.

Theory of mind enables the comedian to predict what the audience expects and to violate those expectations with precision.

Centrality to module

Corpsing as Meta-Model Breach +

When the performer breaks character through genuine amusement, the meta-model has breached the character's Markov blanket. This reveals the dual-track structure and is often funnier than the planned joke.

Corpsing shows the audience there's a real person behind the character — a meta-model that finds the situation funny.
