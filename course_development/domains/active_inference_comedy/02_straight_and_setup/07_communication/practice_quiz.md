# Practice Quiz: Communication in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. The performer communicates through which channels?
A) Verbal only
B) Verbal, prosodic, gestural, postural, facial, and temporal
C) Written text and verbal
D) Only channels the performer is conscious of

2. Cross-channel contradictions (e.g., saying something funny with a deadpan face) produce:
A) Confusion that undermines the comedy
B) Additional prediction error, as the audience's model must reconcile conflicting signals
C) No effect on the audience
D) The same result as channel consistency

3. When a performer is silent, they are:
A) Not communicating
B) Communicating through the absence of speech — silence carries contextual meaning
C) Wasting the audience's time
D) Resting their voice

4. In podcast comedy, the loss of visual channels is compensated by:
A) Adding more guests
B) Enriching vocal channels — more varied prosody, explicit scene-setting, deliberate pacing
C) Using shorter jokes
D) Visual channels are not important in comedy

5. Written comedy (tweets, text messages) operates:
A) Identically to live performance
B) Through the verbal channel alone, requiring word choice, structure, and punctuation to carry all communicative weight
C) Without any prediction error
D) Only for audiences who have seen the comedian perform live

6. Abbott's vocal profile (moderate pitch, even rhythm, declarative prosody) communicates:
A) Boredom and disengagement
B) Authority and certainty through every vocal parameter
C) Uncertainty and questioning
D) The same thing as Costello's vocal profile

7. The audience integrates information across channels to form:
A) A unified percept — a single overall impression of the performer's state
B) Separate percepts for each channel independently
C) A written transcript
D) No percept until the performance concludes

## Part B: Short Answer

1. A comedian tells a sad story with a big smile on their face. Analyze this cross-channel contradiction: what prediction error does it create? Is the result comedy, discomfort, or both?

2. Compare the communication challenges of performing stand-up comedy in a 50-person club vs. a 5,000-person arena. Which channels are preserved at scale? Which are degraded? How must the performer adapt?

3. The module describes silence as having different meanings depending on context. Give two specific performance scenarios where a 2-second silence communicates completely different things. What contextual cues determine the meaning?
