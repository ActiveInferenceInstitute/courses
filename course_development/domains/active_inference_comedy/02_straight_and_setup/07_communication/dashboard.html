Key Concepts

Six Communication Channels +

Verbal (words), prosodic (tone/rhythm), gestural (hand movements), postural (body position), facial (expressions), and temporal (timing/pauses) — each carries partial information about the performer's model.

No single channel provides complete information; the audience integrates across all channels to form a unified percept.

Centrality to module

Cross-Channel Contradiction +

Saying something funny with a serious face creates multi-channel conflict. The audience's model must reconcile the contradiction, generating additional prediction error that amplifies the comedy.

Deadpan comedy is the systematic exploitation of cross-channel contradiction for comedic effect.

Centrality to module

Silence as Communication +

Silence is not the absence of communication — it carries meaning. A pause can communicate processing, anticipation, frustration, or comedic timing depending on context.

The meaning of silence is determined by what precedes and follows it, and by the performer's other channels during the silence.

Centrality to module

Medium-Dependent Channels +

Live performance uses all channels; podcasts lose visual; text loses everything except verbal. Successful comedians in restricted media compensate through enriched remaining channels.

Understanding which channels are available in each medium is essential for effective performance across formats.
