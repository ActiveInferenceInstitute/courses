# Lab: The Communication Toolkit

## Objective

Catalogue, experiment with, and analyze the multi-channel communication toolkit available to comedy performers, experiencing how different channels carry different information and how cross-channel effects generate prediction error.

## Materials

* A partner (recommended)
* A smartphone for recording (video required for this lab)
* A simple one-liner joke (provided below)
* Access to two comedy clips: one stand-up, one podcast comedy (YouTube)
* Blank paper and pen

## Prerequisites

* Reading of Module 07: Communication in Straight & Setup (module.md)
* Completion of Modules 01-06 Labs
* Familiarity with: multi-channel communication, cross-channel contradiction, silence as communication

## The Joke

**"I used to play piano by ear, but now I use my hands."**

## Steps

### Part 1: Channel Isolation Challenge (15 minutes)

1. Deliver the joke using ONLY the verbal channel — monotone, no gestures, no facial expressions, seated, still. Record on video.
2. Deliver the joke using ONLY gestures and facial expressions — no words. Try to communicate the joke silently. Record on video.
3. Deliver the joke in a whisper — verbal + prosodic, but minimal volume and no visual emphasis. Record on video.
4. Deliver the joke with maximum investment in ALL channels — vocal variation, gestures, facial expressions, body movement, deliberate timing. Record on video.
5. Watch all four recordings. Rate each delivery on a 1-5 funniness scale. How much does each additional channel add to the communication?

### Part 2: Cross-Channel Contradiction (10 minutes)

1. Deliver the joke while looking ANGRY — furious facial expression, aggressive tone, but the words are the same lighthearted joke. Record on video.
2. Deliver the joke while looking SAD — tearful expression, quavering voice, same words. Record on video.
3. Deliver the joke while looking CONFUSED — as if you don't understand your own joke. Record on video.
4. Watch the three contradiction recordings. Did any of them produce a DIFFERENT kind of humor than the straight delivery? What prediction error did the contradiction create?

### Part 3: Silence Analysis (10 minutes)

1. Using any stand-up comedy clip, pause the video at three specific silences (moments where the comedian stops talking). For each silence, write down:
   * What came immediately before the silence
   * What the silence communicates (processing? anticipation? frustration? comedic timing?)
   * How long the silence lasts (estimate in seconds)
   * What the audience does during the silence (held attention? nervous laughter? shifting?)
2. Reflect: are you now more aware of silence as an active communication choice? Do you notice how different-length silences communicate different things?

### Part 4: Medium Comparison (10 minutes)

1. Watch 2 minutes of a stand-up comedy special (video). Tally how many communication channels are actively in use.
2. Listen to 2 minutes of a comedy podcast (audio only). Tally the active channels.
3. Read a comedian's Twitter/X thread or text-based comedy (written). Tally the active channels.
4. Create a comparison table:

| Channel | Stand-up | Podcast | Written |
|---------|----------|---------|---------|
| Verbal  |   Yes    |   Yes   |   Yes   |
| Prosodic |        |         |         |
| Gestural |        |         |         |
| Postural |        |         |         |
| Facial   |        |         |         |
| Temporal |        |         |         |

1. Which medium loses the most? Which comedians are most successful in channel-restricted media? What compensatory strategies do they use?

### Part 5: Persona Communication (5 minutes)

1. Pick a well-known comedian you admire. Describe their communicative persona across channels:
   * Vocal style (pitch, pace, volume patterns)
   * Physical presence (movement, stillness, gestures)
   * Facial expression tendencies
   * Timing patterns (fast, measured, dramatic pauses)
2. Is their persona consistent across channels, or is there a signature contradiction (e.g., deadpan face with absurd words)?
3. If time permits: try to deliver your joke IN THEIR STYLE. What changes about your delivery?

## Discussion Questions

* After isolating channels in Part 1, which single channel do you consider most important for comedy? Did the answer surprise you?
* The cross-channel contradictions in Part 2 — were they funnier, less funny, or differently funny? What determines whether a contradiction is comedic vs. confusing?
* Did the medium comparison in Part 4 change how you think about comedy in different formats? Are some comedians better suited to restricted channels?
* After this lab, are you more aware of your own multi-channel communication in everyday conversation?

## Wrap-Up

You have catalogued, tested, and analyzed the multi-channel communication toolkit used in comedy performance. The key insight: comedy is not just what you say — it is what you say ACROSS all channels, including the silence between them.
