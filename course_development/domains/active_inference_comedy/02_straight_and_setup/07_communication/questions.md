# Study Questions: Communication in Straight & Setup

1. The module identifies six communication channels: verbal, prosodic, gestural, postural, facial, and temporal. Rank these by bandwidth — which can transmit the most information per second? Which transmits the least? Justify your ranking.

2. Cross-channel contradictions (saying something funny with a serious face) "generate additional prediction error." Why? What makes a contradiction between channels more surprising than consistency? How does the audience's model handle multi-channel conflicts?

3. Abbott's voice communicates authority (moderate pitch, even rhythm, declarative prosody). Design a voice profile that communicates authority through completely different parameters. Is there more than one vocal formula for "certainty"?

4. The module claims that "silence can communicate" processing, anticipation, frustration, or comedic timing. How does the audience determine which meaning a silence carries? What contextual cues disambiguate silence?

5. In podcast comedy, visual channels are absent. The module says comedians must compensate through enriched vocal channels. But many successful podcasts feature minimal vocal variation. How do they compensate for the loss of visual channels?

6. Written comedy (tweets, text messages) operates through the verbal channel alone. Yet humor thrives on Twitter. What elements replace the missing channels? Punctuation as prosody? Capitalization as volume? Line breaks as timing?

7. The vocal contrast between Abbott (certainty) and Costello (uncertainty) is itself described as "a communication." Who is this meta-level communication directed at — the audience, the performers, or both? Does it operate at a conscious or unconscious level?

8. The module describes each channel as carrying "partial information." If no single channel provides complete information, how many channels must be active for the audience to form a unified percept? Is there a minimum channel requirement for comedy?

9. "The audience integrates across channels to form a unified percept." What happens when integration fails — when the audience cannot reconcile signals from different channels? Does this always produce comedy, or can it produce confusion?

10. Abbott's silence communicates "patient certainty" while Costello's silence communicates "cognitive overload." These are identical physical events (absence of speech) with opposite communicative content. How does the audience assign opposite meanings to the same signal?

11. A comedian communicates through "the spaces between sentences." Analyze a specific comedy routine: what information is carried in the between-sentence pauses that is NOT carried in the sentences themselves?

12. The module lists "micro-expressions" as part of the facial channel. In a large venue, can the audience read micro-expressions from 50 feet away? If not, how does the facial channel function at scale?

13. Compare the communication demands of stand-up comedy to those of a TED talk. Both involve one person addressing an audience. What communication channels does the comedian use that the TED speaker does not, and vice versa?

14. Costello's rising pitch communicates uncertainty. But rising pitch also communicates questions (interrogative prosody). How does the audience distinguish between genuine questions and performed uncertainty?

15. The module describes communication as "multi-channel." But is the performer conscious of all channels simultaneously? Can a performer deliberately control verbal, prosodic, gestural, postural, facial, and temporal channels in parallel?

16. A comedian's communication style is often described as their "voice" or "persona." In terms of multi-channel communication, what IS a comedic persona? Is it a stable pattern across all channels, or a signature in specific channels?

17. When a comedy duo communicates on stage, they operate two sets of communication channels simultaneously. How does the audience handle this doubled signal load? Do they attend to one performer at a time, or integrate both?

18. The module mentions "breath coordination" as an inter-performer communication. How does breathing communicate between performers? What information does shared breath timing carry?

19. Consider a comedian who uses an earpiece to receive suggestions from an off-stage partner (a common format on some comedy shows). This creates a hidden communication channel. How does this secret channel affect the visible performance? Does the audience detect the hidden channel?

20. The module concludes that comedy is "a multi-channel communication act optimized for maximum prediction error delivery." But not all channels deliver prediction error — some channels (postural, temporal) provide context. How do context channels interact with error-delivery channels?
