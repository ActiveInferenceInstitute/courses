# Study Questions: Systems in Straight & Setup

1. In Course 1, you mapped the performer-audience system from the outside. Now, from the performer's perspective, what does the Markov blanket *feel* like? How is the boundary experienced differently when you are inside the system?

2. The module describes the performer as "an internal state of the comedic system." But performers also observe parts of the system (audience reactions). How can an entity be simultaneously an internal state AND a perceiver of external states? Resolve this apparent contradiction using nested Markov blankets.

3. A warm room creates "tight coupling" and a cold room creates "loose coupling." Define coupling strength in terms of the precision of the likelihood mapping between the performer's actions and the audience's responses. What happens to free energy when coupling is tight vs. loose?

4. Abbott doesn't watch the system — "he IS the fixed reference around which the system organizes." What does it mean for an agent to be a fixed reference point? How does this relate to the concept of high-precision priors in Active Inference?

5. The module identifies three nested Markov blankets: body, stage, and venue. For each, give a specific example of information that is filtered OUT (hidden) and information that is filtered THROUGH (transmitted). What would happen if one of these blankets collapsed?

6. Stage fright is described as "the performer's model predicting high free energy in the approaching system." Why is this prediction often wrong? What prior experiences update the model to reduce stage fright over time?

7. In ensemble comedy, performers form "inter-performer blankets" through eye contact, verbal cues, and physical positioning. How do these blankets differ structurally from the performer-audience blanket? Which requires higher precision signals? Why?

8. The performer's heartbeat, next line, and model of audience expectations are described as internal states. But the heartbeat is physiological while the audience model is epistemic. Should these be treated as the same kind of internal state? What does Active Inference say about this distinction?

9. Compare "reading the room" (estimating coupling strength) with Bayesian inference. What is the prior? What are the observations? What is the posterior? How does the estimate update across the first 30 seconds of a performance?

10. The module states that the performer's job is to "tighten the coupling — to make the system cohere." Is this always true? Can you think of comedy forms where deliberately loosening coupling (alienating the audience) is the point?

11. A comedian walks on stage to complete silence. No reaction at all. From the performer's perspective, describe the free energy landscape. What actions are available to minimize free energy? Which are epistemic (gathering information) and which are pragmatic (changing the situation)?

12. The body "reveals your choices but hides your doubts." This is a claim about what information crosses the body's Markov blanket. Under what conditions can an audience detect a performer's hidden doubts despite the blanket? What leaks through?

13. Consider a comedian performing via Zoom vs. live on stage. How does the technology change the Markov blanket structure? Which nested blankets are preserved, which are altered, and which are eliminated?

14. The module describes the "shared fiction of comedy" as something contained by the venue's Markov blanket. What happens to this shared fiction when the performance is recorded and watched later? Does the system boundary change? Is the viewer part of the same system as the original audience?

15. From the performer's perspective, when does the system "come into existence"? Is it when they walk on stage, when they first speak, or when the audience first responds? Argue for one position using the concept of coupling initiation.

16. Multiple performers in an improv troupe share the stage. Each is an internal state. How does the system handle conflicting internal states (e.g., two performers who make incompatible offers)? What is the systemic equivalent of a model conflict?

17. The module contrasts viewing the system "from the outside" (Course 1) with viewing it "from the inside" (Course 2). In Active Inference terms, what is the formal difference between these two perspectives? Is one more fundamental than the other?

18. When a comedian adjusts their delivery based on audience reactions, they are performing active inference. Describe this as a perception-action loop. Where does the loop begin? Where does it end? How quickly does it cycle?

19. The stage as a Markov blanket "amplifies your voice but mutes your whispers." This means the blanket is non-uniform — it transmits some signals better than others. How does this non-uniformity shape the performer's action policy? Do they emphasize actions the blanket transmits well?

20. Consider a performer who has performed a routine 1,000 times. Has the system changed across these performances, or is it the same system reinstantiated? What aspects of the performer-audience coupling are invariant across performances, and what aspects are context-dependent?
