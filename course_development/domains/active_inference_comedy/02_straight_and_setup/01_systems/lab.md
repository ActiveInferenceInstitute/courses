# Lab: Stepping Inside the System

## Objective

Experience the performer-audience system from the inside by performing a short comedy exchange and mapping the coupling dynamics, nested Markov blankets, and system feedback in real time.

## Materials

* A partner (required — one will play the straight man, one the comic)
* A simple comedy exchange to perform (provided below)
* A smartphone or timer
* Blank paper and pen for diagramming
* A small audience of 2-5 people (friends, classmates, or family)

## Prerequisites

* Reading of Module 01: Systems in Straight & Setup (module.md)
* Familiarity with Course 1 concepts: Markov blanket, internal states, external states, coupling

## The Exchange

Memorize and perform this short routine:

**A**: "I just got a job at the calendar factory."
**B**: "Really? What do you do?"
**A**: "Oh, I just got my days off."
**B**: "No, I mean what's your JOB?"
**A**: "I told you — I got my days off."
**B**: "You got FIRED?"
**A**: "No, I got my days off. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday..."
**B**: "That's... that's not what I asked."
**A**: "And I'll get more days off next month."

## Steps

### Part 1: Pre-Performance Body Scan (5 minutes)

1. Before performing, sit quietly and notice your internal states: heartbeat, breathing rate, muscle tension, mental rehearsal of lines. Write down three internal states you can identify.
2. Draw a diagram of YOUR body as a Markov blanket: what information will leak through (your voice, gestures, facial expressions) and what will stay hidden (your nervousness, your line-by-line plan, your awareness of the script)?
3. Predict: will the system (you + partner + audience) feel like tight coupling or loose coupling? Write your prediction.

### Part 2: The First Performance (10 minutes)

1. Perform the exchange for your small audience. Do NOT try to be funny — simply deliver the lines.
2. Immediately after, write down: What did you perceive from the audience? (Laughter? Silence? Confused looks? Smiles?) These are the sensory states that crossed back through your blanket.
3. Rate the coupling strength on a 1-5 scale: (1) no audience response, (5) immediate, energetic response to every line.
4. Switch roles and perform again. The person who was A becomes B, and vice versa.

### Part 3: Reading the Room (10 minutes)

1. Perform the exchange a second time. This time, pay attention to the MOMENT you first detect an audience response. Mark this moment internally.
2. After performing, answer: At what line in the exchange did you first feel the system "come alive"? What sensory signal told you?
3. Compare notes with your partner. Did you both detect the system's activation at the same moment?

### Part 4: Nested Blankets Mapping (10 minutes)

1. On a fresh sheet, draw three concentric boundaries:
   * **Inner**: Your body (what it hides vs. reveals)
   * **Middle**: The performance space (the area between you and the audience)
   * **Outer**: The room (what separates this comedy event from the outside world)
2. For each boundary, list one thing that passed THROUGH and one thing that was BLOCKED.
3. Discuss with your partner: did you experience the body blanket differently as the straight man vs. the comic? The straight man hides more (suppresses reactions); the comic reveals more (expresses confusion).

### Part 5: Warm Room vs. Cold Room (10 minutes)

1. Ask your audience to deliberately give NO reaction during a third performance — no laughter, no smiles, no sounds. Perform the exchange to this "cold room."
2. Write down how the experience changed. What happened to your internal states? Did you feel the coupling weaken? Did you want to change your delivery?
3. Now ask the audience to react enthusiastically — laugh at everything, even if it's not funny. Perform again.
4. Compare the cold room and warm room experiences. Describe the coupling difference in your own words.

## Discussion Questions

* How did it feel to be a "node in the system" rather than an observer of it? Did the inside perspective change your understanding of the Markov blanket concept?
* Which role felt more cognitively demanding — the straight man or the comic? Why?
* When the audience gave no reaction (cold room), what happened to your model of the system? Did you experience something like stage fright or free energy increase?
* Could you feel the nested blankets during performance, or is that an analytical framework applied after the fact?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced the performer-audience system from the inside. Keep your notes and diagrams — they document a first-person account of coupling dynamics, nested Markov blankets, and room-reading that will serve as reference for the remaining modules in Course 2.
