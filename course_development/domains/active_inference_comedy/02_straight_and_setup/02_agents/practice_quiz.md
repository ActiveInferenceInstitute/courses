# Practice Quiz: Agents in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. The straight man's defining cognitive feature is:
A) Confusion and rapid model updating
B) Sustained high precision on priors, resisting accommodation
C) Ignoring the audience entirely
D) Memorizing more lines than the comic

2. The comic's "apparent confusion" is best described as:
A) Genuine inability to understand the routine
B) Controlled computation — running a volatile generative model while performing its output
C) A lack of rehearsal
D) An accident that the audience misinterprets as comedy

3. The shared "meta-model" between performers in a scripted routine:
A) Is visible to the audience through the dialogue
B) Operates behind the Markov blanket of character and is invisible to the audience
C) Is unnecessary if the routine is well-rehearsed
D) Only exists in improvisation

4. Micro-signals between performers (timing adjustments, eye contact, breath coordination) cross:
A) The performer-audience blanket
B) A private inter-performer blanket invisible to the audience
C) No boundary at all
D) The venue's Markov blanket

5. A solo stand-up comedian who switches between narrating and acting out is:
A) Performing two shows simultaneously
B) Embodying both agent archetypes — switching between precision profiles
C) Breaking the fourth wall
D) Using a teleprompter

6. "Corpsing" (breaking character to laugh) occurs when:
A) The audience stops laughing
B) The performer's meta-model breaches the character's Markov blanket
C) The straight man becomes the comic
D) The routine reaches its planned conclusion

7. The fundamental difference between the straight man and the comic is best described in terms of:
A) Who has more lines
B) Precision profiles — the straight man maintains high precision, the comic runs volatile updates
C) Physical size and vocal range
D) Which one the audience likes more

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain why the statement "Abbott and Costello share a meta-model invisible to the audience" does not make their performance deceptive in the ordinary sense. How does the audience's awareness of the comedic frame change the ethics of the hidden model?

2. Describe how a comedian performing alone on stage can embody both the straight man and comic archetypes. What signals indicate the switch between roles?

3. The module argues that the straight man's role is "harder than the comic's." Construct a counter-argument: in what specific ways might the comic's role be more demanding?
