# Study Questions: Agents in Straight & Setup

1. Abbott "became" a straight man through years of vaudeville. In Active Inference terms, describe this becoming as a convergence process — from what initial state to what stable attractor? What precision profile defines the straight man attractor?

2. The module claims the straight man must "suppress the natural tendency to accommodate the other speaker's confusion." What is the Active Inference interpretation of this suppression? Is it a precision adjustment, an action policy constraint, or something else?

3. Costello's "apparent confusion is a sophisticated computational feat." Unpack this claim: what cognitive operations is the comic performing simultaneously? How does the performer's meta-model differ from the character's displayed model?

4. The performers share a "meta-model of the routine" that is invisible to the audience. This meta-model operates behind the Markov blanket of character. How is this different from ordinary deception? What makes it comedic rather than manipulative?

5. Micro-signals between performers (timing adjustments, breath coordination, eye contact) cross a "private blanket." Design an experiment to detect these signals. What would you measure?

6. The module identifies three levels of model in the duo: Abbott's character model, Costello's character model, and the shared performer meta-model. Draw a diagram showing these three models and their Markov blankets. Where do the models interact?

7. Why is the straight man's role described as "harder than the comic's"? Frame your answer in terms of cognitive demands: which role requires higher sustained precision? Which requires more rapid model updating?

8. "The chaos is controlled chaos." What is the difference between controlled chaos and actual chaos from an information-theoretic perspective? How does the audience distinguish between them?

9. The module describes solo stand-up comedians as "internal duos" who switch between straight man and comic roles. How does a single brain maintain two precision profiles simultaneously? Is this switch instantaneous or gradual?

10. Abbott's "iron certainty" requires active cognitive work. Describe this work in terms of Active Inference: what must Abbott do to maintain high precision on his priors in the face of Costello's contradicting evidence?

11. Consider a comedy duo that has never rehearsed together. They are performing an improvisational scene. Without a shared meta-model, how do they establish coupling? What signals do they use?

12. The module says performers' coupling is maintained through eye contact. But in "Who's on First?" the performers are often facing each other or the audience, not making deliberate eye contact. How else is the coupling maintained?

13. Compare the agent archetype of the "straight man" in comedy with the concept of a "reference frame" in physics. What properties do they share? Where does the analogy break down?

14. Costello "runs a volatile, rapidly-updating generative model." What is the update signal? How fast must the updates be? What happens if the update rate is too slow (the comic falls behind the exchange)?

15. The distinction between "personality type" and "functional role" is central to this module. Give an example from outside comedy where functional roles are similarly mistaken for innate personality types. What does Active Inference offer as an alternative framing?

16. In modern sitcoms, the "normal friend" serves as the straight man. But sitcom actors play the same character across hundreds of episodes. How does this extended temporal horizon change the straight man's precision dynamics compared to a single performance?

17. The module describes the comic's role as requiring "rapid-fire inference under uncertainty." Compare this to the cognitive demands of simultaneous translation or emergency medicine. What structural features do they share?

18. When Abbott says "Who's on first" for the hundredth time in a career, does his character model change? Does his performer meta-model change? Discuss the relationship between repetition and model stability at both levels.

19. The "private blanket" between performers carries micro-signals invisible to the audience. But audiences can "feel" when a duo is well-connected vs. when they are struggling. What observable evidence allows the audience to infer the state of the private blanket?

20. Consider what happens when a comedy duo permanently dissolves (Abbott and Costello separated in 1957). What happens to the coupled agent system? Does the meta-model persist in each individual? Can it be reinstantiated with a new partner?
