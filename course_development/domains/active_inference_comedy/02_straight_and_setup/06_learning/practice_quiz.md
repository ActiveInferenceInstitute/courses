# Practice Quiz: Learning in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Rehearsal in comedy performance is best described as:
A) Memorizing lines word for word
B) Refining the performer's generative model — from coarse structure to precise timing and emphasis
C) Eliminating all improvisation
D) Replacing the performer's natural style with a scripted one

2. Audience feedback functions as a training signal because:
A) The audience gives written feedback after each show
B) Audience reactions (laughter, silence, groans) provide evaluation signals that the performer uses to update their action policy
C) The comedian asks the audience to rate each joke
D) Audience feedback is unrelated to learning

3. The comedian's career trajectory from open mics to headlining shows mirrors:
A) A random walk with no direction
B) The Active Inference learning curve from high-uncertainty exploration to low-uncertainty exploitation
C) A fixed trajectory predetermined at career start
D) A purely exploitation strategy from the beginning

4. Automaticity through rehearsal is valuable because:
A) It eliminates the need for any thinking during performance
B) It reduces cognitive load on lower-level processes, freeing resources for higher-level monitoring and adaptation
C) It means every performance is identical
D) It replaces the performer's generative model entirely

5. Deliberate practice in comedy differs from ordinary performance because:
A) There is no audience
B) It targets specific weaknesses with focused repetition, designed to reduce uncertainty in a specific domain
C) It involves performing the entire set repeatedly
D) It requires no feedback

6. The mentor-student relationship in comedy functions as:
A) A way for the mentor to feel important
B) Transfer of a compressed generative model — decades of learning delivered as guidance
C) A purely financial arrangement
D) An outdated tradition with no modern relevance

7. A comedian who performs the same material for 20 years without change has:
A) Achieved perfection
B) Over-exploited — potentially missing shifts in audience models and cultural context
C) The optimal strategy
D) Maximized exploration

## Part B: Short Answer

1. A comedian performs a new joke 50 times. The first 10 times it gets big laughs; the next 20 it gets moderate laughs; the last 20 it gets silence. What should the comedian infer? Is the joke getting worse, or is something else changing?

2. Explain why a comedian can learn more from a bad set (bombing) than from a good set (killing). What specific information does failure provide that success does not?

3. The module describes the mentor as providing a "compressed generative model." What are the limitations of this compression? What important information might be lost when decades of experience are compressed into advice?
