# Study Questions: Action in Straight & Setup

1. The module opens with an exercise: say "Who's on first" with different emphases. Each variation is "a different action." In Active Inference terms, how do different emphases change the precision weighting of the signal that crosses the Markov blanket?

2. Abbott's vocal action profile (moderate volume, even speed, falling intonation, controlled pitch) encodes certainty. Design a contrasting vocal profile that would encode uncertainty. Which specific parameters would you change?

3. Costello's physical actions (hands thrown up, head shaking) are described as "embodied generative models that the audience reads directly." What does it mean for a physical action to BE a generative model rather than merely expressing one?

4. The module describes the audience's mirror system processing Costello's physical confusion "as if they were experiencing it themselves." How does mirror neuron activation relate to the concept of prediction error in Active Inference? Is empathetic confusion a form of shared free energy?

5. Timing is described as "the management of temporal precision." What does it mean for a pause to "create a prediction window"? How does the audience's precision increase during a pause, and why does this make the subsequent punchline hit harder?

6. Physical comedy "bypasses linguistic processing entirely." If this is true, should physical comedy work across language barriers? What evidence supports or contradicts this claim?

7. The microphone is described as "a technological extension of the Markov blanket." What other technologies serve this function in comedy? How do cameras, screens, speakers, and recording devices modify the performance boundary?

8. Abbott's physical stillness is described as "the visual equivalent of high precision." How can absence of movement communicate precision? Doesn't stillness convey less information than movement?

9. The module lists pitch, volume, speed, and prosody as vocal action parameters. Are there others? What about vocal quality (gravelly vs. clear), accent, and register? How do these additional parameters contribute to the performer's action repertoire?

10. Consider a comedian who performs in sign language. Which action channels are preserved and which are lost? Is comedy fundamentally vocal, or can it be fully expressed through visual action?

11. The module states that "every vocal parameter communicates a precision weight." Take a single sentence — "I went to the store" — and describe five different ways to deliver it, each communicating a different precision weight on different words.

12. When a comedian deliberately pauses before a punchline, they are choosing NOT to act. How does Active Inference handle the decision not to act? Is choosing silence itself an action?

13. Compare the action repertoire of a stand-up comedian to that of a stage actor in a drama. What action channels are shared? Which are unique to comedy? Is there a specifically comedic action vocabulary?

14. Costello's physical comedy communicates confusion through exaggerated movement. Abbott's straight man communicates certainty through minimal movement. What happens when you reverse these — a physically active straight man and a still comic? Does the comedy still work?

15. The module describes timing as "bodily management of temporal precision." Is timing a physical ability (like athletic timing), a cognitive ability (like knowing when to pause), or both? Can timing be trained?

16. A held breath and a suspended gesture during a pause create a "prediction window." What would happen if the performer filled the pause with movement or sound instead? Why does emptiness build more precision than activity?

17. The module focuses on how actions cross the performance boundary. But actions also cross the inter-performer boundary. How do Abbott's vocal and physical actions affect Costello's inference during the routine?

18. Consider a comedian performing on radio vs. television vs. live. How does each medium alter the available action channels? Which medium demands the most from the performer's action repertoire?

19. The relationship between action and perception is described as a loop. In the context of comedy timing, trace one complete perception-action cycle: the performer perceives something → decides on an action → executes it → perceives the consequence → adjusts.

20. The module concludes that "comedy is not just clever words — it is clever bodies." Evaluate this claim. Can comedy exist as pure text (novels, tweets) without any embodied action? If so, what takes the place of the action channel?
