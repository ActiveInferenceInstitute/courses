# Practice Quiz: Action in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Vocal actions in comedy include all of the following EXCEPT:
A) Pitch (high vs. low)
B) Volume (loud vs. soft)
C) The venue's acoustics
D) Prosody (rising vs. falling intonation)

2. Abbott's vocal profile communicates certainty because:
A) He speaks louder than Costello
B) His moderate volume, even speed, and falling intonation encode high precision
C) He uses a microphone and Costello doesn't
D) His voice is deeper

3. A comedic pause before a punchline functions by:
A) Confusing the audience
B) Creating a prediction window — the audience's precision increases during the pause, amplifying the subsequent prediction error
C) Allowing the comedian to remember the next line
D) Giving the audience time to leave

4. Costello's physical comedy (hands thrown up, head shaking) works because:
A) It is louder than speech
B) The audience's mirror system processes the physical confusion directly, bypassing linguistic processing
C) Physical comedy requires no skill
D) The audience copies the movements

5. The microphone is described as:
A) An obstacle to communication
B) A technological extension of the Markov blanket — amplifying vocal actions while filtering physical ones
C) Unnecessary for modern comedy
D) A prop used only for stand-up

6. Abbott's physical stillness communicates:
A) Boredom
B) A lack of preparation
C) High precision — the visual equivalent of absolute stability
D) Waiting for a cue from backstage

7. Timing in comedy is best described as:
A) Simply knowing when to pause
B) The bodily management of temporal precision at the intersection of vocal, physical, and temporal control
C) An innate talent that cannot be improved
D) Following a metronome

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Take the phrase "I went to the store yesterday." Describe two deliveries: one that communicates certainty and one that communicates uncertainty. Which vocal parameters change between the two?

2. Explain why a pause creates more precision in the audience's prediction than continuous speech. Use the concept of prediction windows.

3. The module claims physical comedy "bypasses linguistic processing." If a comedian performs only through gesture and facial expression with no words, can they still generate prediction error? Explain how.
