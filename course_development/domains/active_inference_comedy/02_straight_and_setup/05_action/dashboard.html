Key Concepts

Vocal Action Parameters +

Pitch, volume, speed, and prosody are controllable parameters — each communicates a precision weight. Abbott's falling intonation encodes certainty; Costello's rising pitch encodes uncertainty.

Every vocal parameter is an active state that crosses the performance boundary and shapes the audience's inference.

Centrality to module

Physical Comedy as Embodied Model +

Physical actions (gestures, posture, facial expressions) are embodied generative models that the audience reads directly through their mirror system, bypassing linguistic processing.

Physical comedy works across language barriers because it operates through pre-linguistic perceptual channels.

Centrality to module

Timing as Temporal Precision +

A pause creates a prediction window: the audience's precision increases during the silence, making the subsequent punchline generate maximum prediction error. Timing is the management of temporal precision.

The pause is not empty — it is the active construction of a high-precision prediction that the punchline will violate.

Centrality to module

The Microphone as Extended Blanket +

Technology extends the Markov blanket: the microphone amplifies vocal actions while filtering physical ones. Each medium (live, radio, TV, text) alters the available action channels.

Understanding which channels are available in each medium is essential for effective performance.
