## Learning Objectives

1. **Catalog** the performer's vocal action parameters (pitch, volume, speed, prosody) and connect each to its communicative effect
2. **Analyze** physical comedy as embodied generative models — how the audience reads body language directly through mirror processing
3. **Understand** timing as the most sophisticated action in comedy — the bodily management of temporal precision
4. **Distinguish** between Abbott's vocal action profile (moderate, even, declarative) and Costello's (variable, rising, interrogative)
5. **Evaluate** the microphone as a technological extension of the Markov blanket that amplifies vocal actions while filtering physical ones

## Key Terms

- **Vocal action**: The use of pitch, volume, speed, and prosody to communicate precision weights and emotional states
- **Physical action**: Gesture, posture, and facial expression as embodied generative models the audience reads directly
- **Timing as action**: The deliberate management of pauses, acceleration, and rhythm to create prediction windows
- **Prediction window**: The temporal gap created by a pause, during which the audience's precision builds before the punchline lands
- **Blanket extension**: Any technology (microphone, lighting, camera) that modifies the performer's Markov blanket
