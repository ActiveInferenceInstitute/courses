# Practice Quiz: Planning in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. A comedy set is best described as:
A) A random collection of jokes
B) An action policy — a planned sequence of behaviors designed to maximize laughter across a temporal horizon
C) A fixed script that cannot be altered during performance
D) An entirely improvised experience

2. Contingency planning in comedy involves:
A) Hoping nothing goes wrong
B) Pre-computing alternative action sequences and deploying them based on real-time observations
C) Having a backup comedian in the wings
D) Planning one joke at a time

3. "Model predictive control" in comedy means:
A) The comedian follows the plan exactly with no deviation
B) The plan sets the trajectory while moment-to-moment adjustments keep the performance on track
C) The audience controls the performance
D) Planning is unnecessary

4. The "energy arc" of a set refers to:
A) The venue's electricity usage
B) How the set builds, peaks, and resolves across its temporal duration
C) The comedian's physical fitness
D) The brightness of the stage lights

5. A Netflix comedy special feels spontaneous because:
A) It is actually improvised
B) The performer's real-time responsiveness is seamlessly integrated into a meticulously planned structure
C) It is filmed in one take with no preparation
D) The editing removes all planned elements

6. "Finding your voice" as a comedian corresponds to:
A) Vocal training
B) The convergence of a long-horizon policy optimization — settling into a style that works
C) Speaking louder
D) Imitating other comedians

7. When a heckler interrupts, the experienced comedian:
A) Ignores them
B) Deploys a pre-computed contingency response based on the type of heckler, adjusting the performance trajectory
C) Ends the show immediately
D) Calls security before attempting any response

## Part B: Short Answer

1. A comedian must cut her 30-minute set to 15 minutes because the show is running late. Describe the planning decisions she must make: what stays, what goes, and what criteria guide the cuts?

2. Explain the difference between a comedian whose set is entirely scripted and one whose set is entirely improvised. Frame the difference in terms of planning vs. real-time inference. What are the advantages and risks of each approach?

3. The module describes career planning as "long-horizon policy optimization." Give a concrete example of a career planning decision a comedian might make, and explain how that decision involves balancing exploration (trying new things) vs. exploitation (refining what works).
