# Study Questions: Planning in Straight & Setup

1. "Set construction is an action policy." Unpack this claim: what is the state space, what are the available actions, and what is the objective function? How does the comedian evaluate expected free energy for each decision?

2. The module describes contingency planning as "policy branching." How deep should a comedian's contingency tree be? Is there a point of diminishing returns where additional contingencies add complexity without improving resilience?

3. Abbott and Costello had a script but room for variation. The module calls this "model predictive control." What is the control variable? What is the reference trajectory? What are the disturbances that require adjustment?

4. "No plan survives contact with the audience." This is a fundamental challenge for any planning system. How does Active Inference handle the tension between planned sequences and real-time adaptation? Is planning useful if plans always change?

5. A comedian's set has an "energy arc" — how the set builds, peaks, and resolves. Design an energy arc for a 20-minute set. Where should the energy peak? How should it resolve? What happens if the arc is flat?

6. Set construction involves "expected free energy calculations." When a comedian asks "If I put this joke here, what is the downstream effect?", what information are they using? Past performance data? Theory of mind? Intuition?

7. The module describes contingency plans for hecklers. From an Active Inference perspective, a heckler introduces unexpected observations. How should the performer's model incorporate heckler probability into their planning?

8. The tension between script and spontaneity is described as fundamental. But some comedians are entirely scripted (Seinfeld) and some are entirely improvised (improv performers). Can both extremes be successful? What does each sacrifice?

9. A Netflix special is "meticulously planned but feels spontaneous." How does the comedian achieve this illusion? What specific techniques make planned material feel unscripted?

10. "Finding your voice" is described as "the convergence of a long-horizon policy optimization." What is being optimized? Is it comedic style, audience selection, material type, or something more abstract?

11. A comedian plans a 30-minute set but the host tells them they only have 15 minutes. How does the comedian re-plan on the fly? Which parts of the set are dropped first? What determines priority?

12. The module discusses "policy branching" for contingencies. But in practice, comedians often describe responding to unexpected events by "going with it" rather than deploying pre-planned branches. Is this spontaneous response a form of planning or a failure of planning?

13. Compare a comedian planning a set to a chess player planning a game. What structural similarities exist in the planning problems? What key differences make comedy planning unique?

14. The opener of a set "sets the parameters for the rest of the performance." What parameters does the opener establish? (Tone? Energy level? Audience expectations?) How costly is it if the opener fails?

15. Career planning is described as "long-horizon policy." But comedians rarely describe their careers in such formal terms — they describe following instinct, seizing opportunities, and learning from mistakes. Is career planning implicit or explicit?

16. The module mentions callbacks — references to earlier jokes. Planning callbacks requires modeling the audience's memory. How long does the audience remember a joke? How far apart can a setup and callback be before the connection is lost?

17. A comedian performs in a comedy festival with a tight 5-minute slot. Versus a headlining show with 60 minutes. How does the time horizon change the planning problem? Is a short set harder or easier to plan?

18. The module describes the set as having a "closer" — the final bit. Why is the closer important? What purpose does it serve in the audience's overall experience? What makes a good closer vs. a weak one?

19. Planning involves selecting material. A comedian has 100 jokes in their notebook. They can use 20 in tonight's set. What criteria guide the selection? Audience demographics? Venue type? Current events? Personal preference?

20. The concluding paragraph summarizes all of Course 2: performer-audience systems, agent archetypes, perception, cognition, action, learning, communication, and planning. Reflect on how these eight modules build on each other. Could they be taught in a different order? Is the sequence essential?
