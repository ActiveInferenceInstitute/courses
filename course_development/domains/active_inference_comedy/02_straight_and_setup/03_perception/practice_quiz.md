# Practice Quiz: Perception in Straight & Setup

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. "Reading the room" is best described in Active Inference terms as:
A) Passively receiving audience reactions
B) Active inference about the audience's hidden states from sparse, noisy signals
C) Ignoring the audience to focus on the material
D) Asking the audience directly how they feel

2. When silence follows a punchline, the performer should interpret this as:
A) The audience didn't hear the joke
B) The prediction error either failed to register or was perceived as threatening rather than benign
C) A technical problem with the microphone
D) A sign to repeat the same joke louder

3. Abbott's perceptual strategy in "Who's on First?" is:
A) High precision on Costello's signals, low precision on own model
B) Equal precision on all inputs
C) Near-zero precision on Costello's confusion, near-infinity on his own model
D) Complete perceptual shutdown

4. The performer perceives the audience through which channels?
A) Auditory only
B) Auditory, visual, haptic, and temporal
C) Visual and verbal only
D) Through audience surveys collected after the show

5. "Crowd work" (asking audience questions) functions as:
A) Time-filling between jokes
B) Epistemic actions — actively gathering data to update the model of the audience
C) A way to embarrass audience members
D) An admission that the comedian has run out of material

6. Partial laughter indicates:
A) The joke was half-funny
B) A split audience — some models updated while others did not
C) Poor acoustics in the venue
D) The audience is being polite

7. The difference between "hearing" and "listening" in performance is:
A) Volume — listening requires louder signals
B) Precision weighting — listening is attending to informative signals while attenuating noise
C) Equipment — listening requires monitors
D) There is no meaningful difference

## Part B: Short Answer

1. A comedian opens with a joke and gets complete silence. Describe the perceptual inference the comedian should make. What hidden states of the audience are most likely? What should she do next — and why?

2. Explain why temporal perception (how quickly audience reactions come) is informative. If laughter arrives 0.5 seconds after the punchline vs. 3 seconds after, what does each latency tell the performer?

3. Abbott's perceptual strategy (ignoring Costello's confusion) is the opposite of what social convention demands. Why is this asymmetric precision weighting essential for the routine to work? What would happen if Abbott perceived Costello normally?
