# Study Questions: Perception in Straight & Setup

1. The module opens with a sensory scene: breathing, coughing, chair creaks, laughter. Each of these is a signal at the performer's Markov blanket. Rank these signals by their **informativeness** about the audience's hidden state. Which tells you the most? Which tells you the least?

2. "Reading the room" is described as active inference about hidden states. What are the hidden states the performer is estimating? List at least five distinct audience states that the performer infers from observable signals.

3. The performer perceives the audience through auditory, visual, haptic, and temporal channels. Which channel is most reliable in a dark comedy club where you can barely see the audience? How does channel availability change the performer's inference?

4. Abbott sets "the precision on incoming signals from Costello to near-zero." What would happen if Abbott's perceptual strategy were reversed — if he set high precision on Costello's signals and low precision on his own model? How would this change the routine?

5. "Early laughter means the audience is fast inferrers." Is this always a good thing? Can an audience be TOO fast — anticipating punchlines before delivery? What happens to the comedy system when the audience's inference speed exceeds the performer's delivery speed?

6. Silence after a punchline is described as "the most informative signal of all." Why is the absence of an expected signal more informative than the presence of an expected signal? Connect this to the concept of surprise in Active Inference.

7. Crowd work is described as "epistemic actions to gather perceptual data." How does asking "Where are you from?" reduce the performer's uncertainty about the audience? What specific model parameters does this question update?

8. Partial laughter (some laughing, some not) indicates a split audience. From the performer's perspective, how should they respond? Do they adjust toward the laughing portion or try to bring the non-laughing portion along? What does Active Inference predict?

9. Compare "hearing" vs. "listening" as precision-weighted perception. Give a specific example of a sound a performer hears but ignores (low precision) and a sound they listen to intently (high precision). How is this weighting learned?

10. The module describes the "energy of the room" as a haptic perception. Can this be explained in Active Inference terms, or is it genuinely mysterious? What physical signals might contribute to this feeling?

11. A performer's "ear is calibrated through thousands of hours of performance." Describe this calibration as a Bayesian updating process. What are the priors for a beginner? How do they change with experience?

12. Visual perception from the stage is limited: the lights are in your eyes, the audience is dark. Yet experienced performers report being able to "see" individual reactions. What compensatory strategies do performers develop?

13. Abbott's perceptual strategy (low precision on Costello, high precision on own model) is deliberate. But Costello's perceptual strategy is different — he must perceive Abbott's words with HIGH precision (to generate the misunderstanding). Compare these two perceptual strategies.

14. When a performer perceives a negative audience response (silence, groaning), how quickly must they update their model? Is real-time perceptual updating always desirable, or can it lead to over-correction?

15. The module distinguishes between perceiving the audience's "mood" vs. their "comedy IQ." Are these different model parameters? How would you gather evidence about each?

16. In a comedy duo, does each performer perceive the audience independently, or do they share perceptual data? How might one performer's perceptual estimate influence the other's?

17. A comedian performing for a foreign-language audience cannot understand their verbal reactions. Which perceptual channels remain available? Is comedy still possible with severely reduced perception?

18. The temporal channel includes "how quickly or slowly reactions come." Design a simple measurement system: how would you quantify audience reaction latency? What would fast vs. slow latency tell you?

19. The module describes perception from the performer's perspective as fundamentally different from the scientist's analysis of the same system. What does the performer perceive that the scientist misses? What does the scientist capture that the performer cannot?

20. After reading this module, re-read the opening scenario (closing your eyes on stage). Does the module's framework change how you imagine that experience? What would you now attend to that you might have missed before?
