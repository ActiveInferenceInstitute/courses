## Introduction

A live comedy audience perceives socially. They do not just hear the comedian — they hear other audience members laughing (or not laughing), they see heads turning, they feel the energy of the collective body. Social perception is layered: perception of the performer, perception of the crowd's reaction, and perception of one's own reaction relative to others.

This module examines social perception in live comedy — how the audience's perceptual experience is shaped by the social context of collective viewing.

## Key Concepts

### Social Perception in Live Comedy

At the heart of this module is social perception in live comedy — the way perception operates within social dynamics and collective inference. In 'Who\'s on First?', this manifests as the interplay between performers, audience, and the comedy tradition, examined through the temporal, social, and evolutionary dimensions that Crowd & Callback foregrounds.

### Active Inference Formalization

Active Inference provides the formal toolkit for understanding perception in this context. The key constructs are:

- **Generative model**: The agent's internal model of how perception works in comedy — what patterns to expect, what signals to attend to, and what actions to take.
- **Prediction error**: The mismatch between expected and observed perception dynamics — the signal that drives updating and adaptation.
- **Precision weighting**: The confidence assigned to predictions about perception — higher precision means stronger expectations and larger prediction errors when they fail.
- **Expected free energy**: The anticipated surprise and information gain associated with future actions related to perception.
