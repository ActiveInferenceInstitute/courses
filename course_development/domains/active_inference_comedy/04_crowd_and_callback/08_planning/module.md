## Introduction

A comedy set designed for a corporate event is different from one designed for a late-night club show. The audiences differ in demographics, expectations, energy levels, and sensitivity thresholds. Planning for groups requires modeling the collective agent — estimating the crowd's shared priors, precision profiles, and violation thresholds.

This module examines set design for diverse audiences: how comedians adapt their material, pacing, and energy to different collective agents, and how this adaptive planning maps onto expected free energy optimization for specific populations.

### Active Inference Formalization

Active Inference provides the formal toolkit for understanding planning in this context. The key constructs are:

- **Generative model**: The agent's internal model of how planning works in comedy — what patterns to expect, what signals to attend to, and what actions to take.
- **Prediction error**: The mismatch between expected and observed planning dynamics — the signal that drives updating and adaptation.
- **Precision weighting**: The confidence assigned to predictions about planning — higher precision means stronger expectations and larger prediction errors when they fail.
- **Expected free energy**: The anticipated surprise and information gain associated with future actions related to planning.
