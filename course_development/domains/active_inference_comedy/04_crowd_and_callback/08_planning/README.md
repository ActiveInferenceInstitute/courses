## Learning Objectives

1. **Understand** audience-adaptive planning through the lens of Active Inference and social dynamics and collective inference
2. **Analyze** how planning manifests in "Who's on First?" from the Crowd & Callback perspective
3. **Apply** universal comedy to practical comedy craft and performance
4. **Connect** niche comedy to broader patterns in comedy as cultural practice
5. **Evaluate** how the Crowd & Callback perspective on planning reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points

## Key Terms

- **Audience-adaptive planning**: Designing comedy sets that work for heterogeneous audiences with diverse models
- **Universal comedy**: Material that generates prediction error across the widest possible range of generative models
- **Niche comedy**: Material that targets specific generative models -- high prediction error for the in-group, none for outsiders
- **Demographic awareness**: The comedian's model of the audience's demographic composition and its implications for comedy planning
- **Context-sensitive planning**: Adjusting set design based on venue, event type, and expected audience characteristics

## Prerequisites

- Prior modules in Crowd & Callback and foundational concepts from Courses 01-02
- Familiarity with Active Inference concepts of generative models, prediction error, and precision weighting
