# Lab: Action Workshop (Crowd & Callback)

## Objective

Practice and analyze specific comedy actions, examining how they generate prediction error.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 05: Action lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to crowd work as social action.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where action dynamics from the Crowd & Callback perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights crowd work as social action.
5. Focus on how social dynamics and collective inference shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did action dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of crowd work as social action.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Crowd & Callback perspective change the way you understand action in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify crowd work as social action effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of crowd work as social action?
* What aspects of action in comedy can only be understood through the lens of social dynamics and collective inference?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Crowd & Callback?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced crowd work as social action through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of action into the remaining modules.
