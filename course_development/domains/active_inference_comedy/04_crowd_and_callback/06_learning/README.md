## Learning Objectives

1. **Understand** comedy literacy through the lens of Active Inference and social dynamics and collective inference
2. **Analyze** how learning manifests in "Who's on First?" from the Crowd & Callback perspective
3. **Apply** genre expectations to practical comedy craft and performance
4. **Connect** cultural learning through comedy to broader patterns in comedy as cultural practice
5. **Evaluate** how the Crowd & Callback perspective on learning reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points

## Key Terms

- **Comedy literacy**: The learned ability to recognize and respond to comedy conventions and structures
- **Genre expectations**: The audience's learned generative model of what to expect from a given comedy format
- **Cultural learning through comedy**: How comedy transmits cultural norms, values, and knowledge through humor
- **Audience sophistication**: The depth of an audience's comedy literacy -- how refined their comedy generative model is
- **Convention violation**: Breaking learned comedy conventions to generate meta-level prediction error

## Prerequisites

- Prior modules in Crowd & Callback and foundational concepts from Courses 01-02
- Familiarity with Active Inference concepts of generative models, prediction error, and precision weighting
