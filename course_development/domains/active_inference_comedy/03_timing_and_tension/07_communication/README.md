## Learning Objectives

1. **Identify** rhythm as a distinct communication channel — temporal patterns communicate mood, energy, and intent independently of words
2. **Analyze** tempo shifts as communicative signals: acceleration communicates escalation, deceleration communicates emphasis or significance
3. **Map** the call-and-response rhythm of "Who's on First?" as a rhythmic communication structure
4. **Connect** the musicality of comedy dialogue to formal rhythmic analysis — comedians say good dialogue has "rhythm," and they mean it literally
5. **Evaluate** how rhythmic communication functions differently across comedy formats (stand-up, sketch, improv, dialogue)

## Key Terms

- **Rhythm as communication**: Temporal patterns that carry meaning (mood, energy, status) independent of verbal content
- **Tempo shift**: A change in the pace of delivery that signals a change in the communicative state
- **Call-and-response**: A rhythmic dialogue pattern where one utterance predicts and invites the next
- **Comedic musicality**: The quality of comedy dialogue that makes it feel rhythmically satisfying
- **Temporal communication channel**: The layer of meaning carried by timing, pace, and rhythm
