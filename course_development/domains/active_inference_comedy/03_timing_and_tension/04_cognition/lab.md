# Lab: Cognitive Analysis (Timing & Tension)

## Objective

Investigate the cognitive processes involved in comedic understanding, testing how anticipation and model updating create humor.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 04: Cognition lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to anticipation and the cognitive beat.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where cognition dynamics from the Timing & Tension perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights anticipation and the cognitive beat.
5. Focus on how precision dynamics and temporal control shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did cognition dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of anticipation and the cognitive beat.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Timing & Tension perspective change the way you understand cognition in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify anticipation and the cognitive beat effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of anticipation and the cognitive beat?
* What aspects of cognition in comedy can only be understood through the lens of precision dynamics and temporal control?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Timing & Tension?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced anticipation and the cognitive beat through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of cognition into the remaining modules.
