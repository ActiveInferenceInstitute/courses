# Lab: Agent Analysis (Timing & Tension)

## Objective

Analyze the agent properties of performers and audiences in a comedy context, mapping precision profiles and action policies.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 02: Agents lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to the timer and the timed.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where agents dynamics from the Timing & Tension perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights the timer and the timed.
5. Focus on how precision dynamics and temporal control shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did agents dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of the timer and the timed.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Timing & Tension perspective change the way you understand agents in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify the timer and the timed effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of the timer and the timed?
* What aspects of agents in comedy can only be understood through the lens of precision dynamics and temporal control?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Timing & Tension?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced the timer and the timed through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of agents into the remaining modules.
