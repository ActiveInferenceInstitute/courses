# Agent Guidelines: Agents (Timing & Tension)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md) | [Domain AGENTS](../../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: The timer and the timed — agents defined by their temporal precision profiles
- **Topics**: Temporal agent roles, Abbott as tempo-setter, Costello as tempo-reactor, the audience as temporal tracker
- **Lab Style**: Timing Experiment — measuring each agent's temporal behavior and comparing timing signatures
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

In the temporal dimension, agents are defined not just by what they believe but by how they control and respond to timing. Abbott controls the tempo (he is the timer); Costello reacts to it (he is the timed). Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- **Temporal precision profiles** distinguish the agents: Abbott's timing is metronomic (high temporal precision), Costello's is reactive and variable
- The **audience's temporal tracking** is analyzed — they build temporal predictions about when the next exchange, the next confusion, the next laugh will come
- **Tempo control** is presented as a form of agency — Abbott's power in the routine comes partly from his control of the temporal dimension
- The drummer-soloist metaphor is explored: the straight man keeps time, the comic plays against it
