# Module 08: Planning in Riff & Repertoire

## Learning Objectives

1. Analyze how comedians **plan a career** — the long-term strategic decisions about material direction, audience building, and creative evolution.
2. Apply the concept of **special construction** to understand how a year-long creative arc is architected.
3. Examine how **comedy industry navigation** requires strategic planning under uncertainty.

## Introduction

A comedy career is not a sequence of random gigs — it is a planned trajectory through a complex creative and commercial landscape. When to record a special, how to build an audience, whether to pivot styles, when to take creative risks, and how to manage the relationship between artistic integrity and commercial success — these are planning problems that unfold over years and decades.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Special Construction as Long-Range Planning

Building a comedy special is typically a 12-18 month planning project:

- **Months 1-3 (Material generation)**: Write and test large quantities of new material across multiple topics. The planning goal is breadth — cover enough territory to identify which veins of comedy are richest.
- **Months 4-8 (Material refinement)**: The strongest material is isolated, developed, and iterated. The planning goal shifts from breadth to depth — each bit is honed through dozens of performances.
- **Months 9-12 (Set construction)**: Individual bits are assembled into a coherent hour. The planning goal is architecture — ordering, transitions, callback placement, and energy management across 60 minutes.
- **Months 12-15 (Performance polish)**: The complete set is performed nightly in its final form. The planning goal is consistency — every performance should deliver the same quality.
- **Month 15-18 (Recording)**: The special is recorded (often across 2-3 shows for editing options). Post-production finalizes the product.

This planning arc requires the comedian to manage creative exploration (generating new material), creative discipline (cutting beloved material that doesn't serve the whole), and strategic patience (resisting the urge to record before the material is fully developed).

### 2. Audience Development Strategy

Building a comedy audience requires strategic planning:

- **Local scene building**: Establishing a reputation in a local comedy scene through consistent presence and quality — becoming a "regular" at key venues
- **Festival strategy**: Identifying which festivals provide the best exposure for the comedian's style — Edinburgh for long-form, Montreal for industry attention, Melbourne for international audiences
- **Digital presence**: Building an online audience through clips, podcasts, social media, and YouTube — creating a parallel audience that complements the live following
- **Touring strategy**: Expanding from local to regional to national to international touring — each expansion requires more material, more audience resilience, and more logistical planning

Each decision is an EFE evaluation: which path provides the best combination of audience growth (pragmatic value) and creative learning (epistemic value)?

### 3. Creative Risk Management

The comedian must plan when and how to take creative risks:

- **Experimenting with new material in safe venues**: Using small clubs and late-night spots to test risky material before deploying it in high-stakes settings
- **Pivoting styles**: The decision to change one's comedic approach (from one-liners to storytelling, from observational to confessional) is a major planned transition that risks alienating the existing audience while potentially attracting a new one
- **Addressing controversial topics**: Planning how to approach sensitive material — building trust with the audience before the risky bit, constructing the approach to minimize hostile misreadings while preserving the comedic impact
- **Retirement planning**: When to stop performing — recognizing when the creative model is no longer producing sufficient prediction error, or when personal circumstances require a break

### 4. Industry Navigation

The comedy industry requires its own planning:

- **Manager/agent relationships**: Selecting representation that aligns with the comedian's creative direction and career goals
- **Platform decisions**: Netflix vs. HBO vs. self-release — each platform has different audience reach, creative control, and financial structures
- **Collaboration opportunities**: Writing rooms, co-headlining tours, podcast partnerships — each collaboration is an investment in both creative development and audience expansion
- **Financial planning**: The comedy career arc is not linear — income varies wildly between struggling open-micer and touring headliner, requiring financial planning across feast-and-famine cycles

## Applications

- **Bo Burnham's Career Architecture**: Burnham's career demonstrates strategic planning — YouTube comedy (building digital audience) → stand-up specials (establishing live credibility) → film directing (expanding creative range) → "Inside" (pandemic-forced innovation that synthesized all previous skills). Each phase was both a creative expression and a strategic career move.
- **The Clean Comedy Strategy**: Some comedians deliberately plan clean (family-friendly) material careers — targeting the corporate event, cruise ship, and TV-friendly markets that are less competitive but more lucrative per-show. This is a strategic audience and market planning decision that constrains creative content.

## Conclusion

Planning in Riff & Repertoire spans special construction, audience development, creative risk management, and industry navigation. The comedian careers that endure are those that balance creative exploration with strategic discipline — planning not just the next joke but the next decade of jokes.
