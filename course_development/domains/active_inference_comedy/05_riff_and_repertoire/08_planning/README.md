## Learning Objectives

1. **Understand** career planning through the lens of Active Inference and evolution, improvisation, and tradition
2. **Analyze** how planning manifests in "Who's on First?" from the Riff & Repertoire perspective
3. **Apply** finding your voice to practical comedy craft and performance
4. **Connect** audience building to broader patterns in comedy as cultural practice
5. **Evaluate** how the Riff & Repertoire perspective on planning reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points

## Key Terms

- **Career planning**: Long-horizon policy optimization across a comedy career -- decades of material, audience, and identity development
- **Finding your voice**: The convergence of a comedian's career-scale generative model -- the emergence of a distinctive perspective
- **Audience building**: The long-term process of developing a relationship between a comedian and their audience
- **Legacy construction**: How a comedian's body of work, influence, and innovation persist beyond their active career
- **Cultural adaptation**: Adjusting comedy material and approach to evolving cultural norms and audience expectations

## Prerequisites

- Prior modules in Riff & Repertoire and foundational concepts from Courses 01-02
- Familiarity with Active Inference concepts of generative models, prediction error, and precision weighting
