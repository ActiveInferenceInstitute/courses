## Conventions

- **Perspective**: The evolution of comedy -- improvisation, variation, and tradition
- **Topics**: Novelty detection, tradition vs. innovation, the familiar-novel balance, perceiving originality
- **Lab Style**: Improvisation Studio
- **Audience**: Comedians, performers, comedy scholars, cognitive scientists, and the curious
- **Tone**: Smart, antic, deeply technical, never condescending

## Active Inference Focus

The audience must perceive both familiarity (recognizing the comedy form) and novelty (detecting the new variation). Comedy that is too familiar is hack; comedy that is too novel is incomprehensible. This module examines the PERCEPTION of novelty within tradition. Agents working on this module must ensure that:

- Every Active Inference concept is grounded in a concrete comedic experience from the Riff & Repertoire perspective
- The evolution, improvisation, and tradition lens reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points
- "Who's on First?" is referenced substantively through the Riff & Repertoire perspective
- Comedy terminology is used correctly alongside Active Inference formalism

## Key Mappings for This Module

| Comedy Concept | Active Inference Mapping |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Novelty detection | The perceptual process of identifying what is new or unexpected within a familiar framework |
| Familiar-novel balance | The optimal ratio of recognizable structure to surprising content that maximizes comedy |
| Hack detection | Perceiving comedy as overly familiar -- the audience's model perfectly predicts the punchline |
