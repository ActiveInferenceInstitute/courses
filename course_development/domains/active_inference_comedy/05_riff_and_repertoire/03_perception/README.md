## Learning Objectives

1. **Understand** novelty detection through the lens of Active Inference and evolution, improvisation, and tradition
2. **Analyze** how perception manifests in "Who's on First?" from the Riff & Repertoire perspective
3. **Apply** familiar-novel balance to practical comedy craft and performance
4. **Connect** hack detection to broader patterns in comedy as cultural practice
5. **Evaluate** how the Riff & Repertoire perspective on perception reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points

## Key Terms

- **Novelty detection**: The perceptual process of identifying what is new or unexpected within a familiar framework
- **Familiar-novel balance**: The optimal ratio of recognizable structure to surprising content that maximizes comedy
- **Hack detection**: Perceiving comedy as overly familiar -- the audience's model perfectly predicts the punchline
- **Originality perception**: The audience's assessment of how genuinely new a comedy approach or joke is
- **Tradition as prior**: Established comedy conventions functioning as the audience's prior model against which novelty is measured
