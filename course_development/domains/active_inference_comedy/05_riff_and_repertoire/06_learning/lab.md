# Lab: Learning Study (Riff & Repertoire)

## Objective

Track the learning process in a comedy context — either performer learning or audience adaptation.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 06: Learning lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to the evolution of a comedy tradition.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where learning dynamics from the Riff & Repertoire perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights the evolution of a comedy tradition.
5. Focus on how evolution, improvisation, and tradition shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did learning dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of the evolution of a comedy tradition.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Riff & Repertoire perspective change the way you understand learning in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify the evolution of a comedy tradition effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of the evolution of a comedy tradition?
* What aspects of learning in comedy can only be understood through the lens of evolution, improvisation, and tradition?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Riff & Repertoire?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced the evolution of a comedy tradition through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of learning into the remaining modules.
