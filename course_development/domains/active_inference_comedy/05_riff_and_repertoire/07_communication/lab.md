# Lab: Communication Lab (Riff & Repertoire)

## Objective

Analyze and practice the communication channels used in comedy, testing how different channels contribute to humor.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 07: Communication lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to transmitting comedy across generations.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where communication dynamics from the Riff & Repertoire perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights transmitting comedy across generations.
5. Focus on how evolution, improvisation, and tradition shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did communication dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of transmitting comedy across generations.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Riff & Repertoire perspective change the way you understand communication in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify transmitting comedy across generations effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of transmitting comedy across generations?
* What aspects of communication in comedy can only be understood through the lens of evolution, improvisation, and tradition?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Riff & Repertoire?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced transmitting comedy across generations through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of communication into the remaining modules.
