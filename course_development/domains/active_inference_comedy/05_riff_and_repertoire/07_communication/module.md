## Introduction

Every comedian inherits a tradition. 'Who's on First?' inherits from centuries of word-play comedy. Abbott and Costello inherited from Weber & Fields, from commedia dell'arte, from the Fool tradition in Shakespeare. This inheritance is COMEDY TRANSMISSION — the passage of comedic forms, techniques, and conventions across generations through performance, mentorship, and cultural memory.

This module examines how comedy transmits across generations: how traditions form, how they are preserved and disrupted, and what role Active Inference plays in cultural transmission.

## Key Concepts

### Transmitting Comedy Across Generations

At the heart of this module is transmitting comedy across generations — the way communication operates within evolution, improvisation, and tradition. In 'Who\'s on First?', this manifests as the interplay between performers, audience, and the comedy tradition, examined through the temporal, social, and evolutionary dimensions that Riff & Repertoire foregrounds.

### Active Inference Formalization

Active Inference provides the formal toolkit for understanding communication in this context. The key constructs are:

- **Generative model**: The agent's internal model of how communication works in comedy — what patterns to expect, what signals to attend to, and what actions to take.
- **Prediction error**: The mismatch between expected and observed communication dynamics — the signal that drives updating and adaptation.
- **Precision weighting**: The confidence assigned to predictions about communication — higher precision means stronger expectations and larger prediction errors when they fail.
- **Expected free energy**: The anticipated surprise and information gain associated with future actions related to communication.

### Domain-Specific Application

From the Riff & Repertoire perspective, communication in 'Who\'s on First?' reveals dynamics invisible from other vantage points. The routine is not just a text — it is a living performance that operates through evolution, improvisation, and tradition, connecting the mechanics of individual exchanges to the broader patterns of comedy as cultural practice.

## Applications

- **Communication in Stand-Up**: Solo performers navigate communication dynamics without a partner, requiring them to internalize the functions that a duo distributes between two agents.
- **Communication in Improv**: Improvisational comedy pushes communication to its limits, requiring real-time processing without the safety net of rehearsal.
- **Communication Across Cultures**: Different comedy traditions approach communication differently, revealing both universal principles and cultural specificity.

## Conclusion

Communication in Riff & Repertoire reveals dimensions of comedy that are invisible from other perspectives. By examining transmitting comedy across generations through Active Inference, we see how communication is not just a feature of comedy but a fundamental mechanism — the process by which performers and audiences co-create the experience of humor through evolution, improvisation, and tradition.
