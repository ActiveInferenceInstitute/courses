# Lab: System Dynamics (Riff & Repertoire)

## Objective

Map the system dynamics of a comedy performance, identifying temporal, social, or evolutionary systems at work.

## Materials

* A recording of 'Who\'s on First?' or another comedy performance
* Pen and paper or text editor
* A partner for exercises (recommended)
* Timer
* Optional: recording device

## Prerequisites

* Module 01: Systems lecture (module.md)
* Familiarity with Active Inference concepts

## Steps

### Part 1: Observation (15 minutes)

1. Watch a 5-minute comedy segment with specific attention to comedy as an evolving system.
2. Annotate at least 5 moments where systems dynamics from the Riff & Repertoire perspective are visible.
3. For each moment, identify the Active Inference concept at work (prediction error, precision, model updating, etc.).

### Part 2: Active Practice (20 minutes)

4. With a partner, create a short comedy exercise that highlights comedy as an evolving system.
5. Focus on how evolution, improvisation, and tradition shapes the comedy experience.
6. Perform the exercise three times, noting improvements and changes in each iteration.
7. After each iteration, discuss: what changed? What was the audience's response? How did systems dynamics shift?

### Part 3: Analysis (15 minutes)

8. Compare your exercise to the professional performance observed in Part 1.
9. Identify three specific ways the professional performance demonstrated more sophisticated use of comedy as an evolving system.
10. For each difference, explain what the professional's generative model likely includes that yours does not.

### Part 4: Synthesis (10 minutes)

11. Write a brief reflection connecting your experience to the module's key concepts.
12. Address: How does the Riff & Repertoire perspective change the way you understand systems in comedy?
13. Propose one modification to your exercise that would amplify comedy as an evolving system effects.

## Discussion Questions

* How did the practical experience change your theoretical understanding of comedy as an evolving system?
* What aspects of systems in comedy can only be understood through the lens of evolution, improvisation, and tradition?
* How does Active Inference help explain what you observed and experienced?
* Connect your findings to the broader curriculum: how does this lab relate to other modules in Riff & Repertoire?

## Wrap-Up

You have experienced comedy as an evolving system through hands-on practice. The observation-practice-analysis cycle mirrors the Active Inference loop: perceive, act, update, repeat. Carry your updated model of systems into the remaining modules.
