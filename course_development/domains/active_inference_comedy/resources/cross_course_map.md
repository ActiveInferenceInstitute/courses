# Cross-Course Map: Comedy & Active Inference

> How each topic connects across all 5 courses, building a spiral of understanding from the mechanics of a single joke to the evolution of comedic tradition.

---

## Module 1: Systems

| Course | Subtitle | Perspective |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Bit & Boundary | The Routine as a Dynamical System | The "Who's on First?" exchange mapped as a bounded system with internal/external states and a Markov blanket at the dialogue boundary |
| Straight & Setup | The Performer-Audience System | Two performers and an audience as a coupled dynamical system with nested Markov blankets |
| Timing & Tension | The Temporal System of Comedy | The joke as a temporal system: setup phase, precision accumulation, punchline discharge |
| Crowd & Callback | The Room as a System | The comedy venue as a system: performer, audience, room acoustics, social norms, and their boundaries |
| Riff & Repertoire | Comedy Traditions as Self-Maintaining Systems | Comedic genres as self-organizing systems that maintain their identity across generations |

## Module 2: Agents

| Course | Subtitle | Perspective |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Bit & Boundary | Abbott and Costello as Competing Inference Engines | Two agents with incompatible generative models locked in non-convergent exchange |
| Straight & Setup | The Straight Man and the Comic as Agent Archetypes | The straight man as high-precision agent vs. the comic as volatile-model agent |
| Timing & Tension | The Audience as an Inference Agent | How the audience tracks, predicts, and responds to performer actions in real time |
| Crowd & Callback | Collective Agents and Shared Laughter | The audience as a collective agent with emergent behavior (waves of laughter, contagion) |
| Riff & Repertoire | The Comedian as an Evolving Agent | How a comedian's generative model deepens over years of performance |
