# Learning Pathways: Comedy & Active Inference

> Multiple entry points into the curriculum, tailored to different backgrounds and interests.

---

## Pathway 1: The Comedian

**You are**: A performer, comedy writer, or improv player who wants to understand the science behind your craft.

**Start with**: Course 2 — **Straight & Setup** (the performers as agents)

- This course speaks your language: performer strategies, audience management, set construction.
- Active Inference concepts are introduced through performance-grounded examples.

**Then**: Course 3 — **Timing & Tension** (perception)

- The microsecond art of comedic timing gets a technical explication.

**Then**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary** (system mechanics)

- With performance intuition in place, the formal system analysis will click.

**Deep dive**: Course 5 — **Riff & Repertoire** (evolution)

- For the comedian interested in their art's history and future.

---

## Pathway 2: The Cognitive Scientist

**You are**: A researcher in neuroscience, psychology, or cognitive science interested in humor.

**Start with**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary** (the routine as a system)

- Formal system analysis with Markov blankets, internal/external states.
- "Who's on First?" as a POMDP case study.

**Then**: Course 3 — **Timing & Tension** (perception and precision)

- Deep dive into precision dynamics, temporal inference, and perceptual parsing.

**Then**: Course 4 — **Crowd & Callback** (social cognition)

- Theory of mind, collective inference, social signaling through laughter.

**Deep dive**: Course 2 — **Straight & Setup** (agent modeling)

- Abbott and Costello as competing inference agents with contrasting precision profiles.

---

## Pathway 3: The Active Inference Scholar

**You are**: A student or researcher already familiar with the Free Energy Principle looking for a novel domain application.

**Start with**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary** (systems)

- The formal mapping is tightest here: Markov blankets, free energy, belief updating.
- "Who's on First?" as a non-ergodic system with degenerate likelihood mapping.

**Then**: Parallel study of Module 4 (Cognition) across all 5 courses

- See how cognitive model failure, belief perseveration, and model rigidity manifest differently at each scale.

**Then**: Course 5 — **Riff & Repertoire** (evolution)

- Comedy as an evolutionary system: niche construction, attractor dynamics, cultural selection.

**Deep dive**: Module 8 (Planning) across all courses

- Expected free energy in comedy direction, set construction, and career planning.

---

## Pathway 4: The Curious Person

**You are**: Someone who loves "Who's on First?", has no technical background, and wants to understand why it is so funny.

**Start with**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary**, Module 1 (Systems)

- The gentlest entry point. Uses the routine you already love as the primary example.

**Then**: Follow Course 1 sequentially (Modules 1–8)

- Build understanding one concept at a time, always grounded in the routine.

**Then**: Course 2 — **Straight & Setup**

- Now see the routine from the performers' perspective.

**Then**: Explore freely using the [Cross-Course Map](./cross_course_map.md)

- Follow whatever interests you most.

---

## Pathway 5: The Improv Player

**You are**: An improviser who works without scripts and wants to understand the real-time inference dynamics of what you do.

**Start with**: Course 5 — **Riff & Repertoire** (improvisation and evolution)

- This course treats improvisation as epistemic foraging — the heart of what you do.

**Then**: Course 3 — **Timing & Tension** (perception)

- Real-time perception and precision dynamics in the improvised moment.

**Then**: Course 4 — **Crowd & Callback** (social dynamics)

- How audiences co-create the show in improv.

**Deep dive**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary**

- Understand the formal system structure that improvisation navigates.

---

## Pathway 6: The Comedy Scholar

**You are**: An academic studying humor, comedy history, or performance studies.

**Start with**: Course 5 — **Riff & Repertoire** (evolution and tradition)

- Historical and cultural perspectives on comedy, grounded in Active Inference formalism.

**Then**: Course 4 — **Crowd & Callback** (social comedy)

- The sociology of humor: audience dynamics, cultural transmission, norm enforcement.

**Then**: Course 1 — **Bit & Boundary** (formal analysis)

- Structural analysis of "Who's on First?" with full formalism.

**Deep dive**: Resources — **References** and **Glossary**

- The reference list bridges comedy theory (Bergson, Morreall, Hurley/Dennett/Adams) and Active Inference (Friston, Clark, Hohwy).
