## Learning Goals

1. **Describe** yourself as a free energy minimizing agent when you crochet
2. **Explain** how holding a pattern in mind functions as a generative model
3. **Recognize** frogging as a prediction error response — correcting when reality diverges from expectation
4. **Compare** the strategies of changing the work vs. changing the plan as two paths for reducing free energy
5. **Identify** how skill level shapes the depth and precision of the crocheter's generative model

## Key Crochet Terminology

- **Generative model (pattern)**: The written or mental plan that predicts what the finished fabric should look like
- **Frogging**: Ripping back multiple stitches or rows to correct an error (from "rip it, rip it")
- **Tinking**: Undoing one stitch at a time for precise, local corrections
- **WIP**: Work in progress — the partially completed project embodying accumulated predictions
- **FO**: Finished object — the completed work where predictions and reality have been reconciled

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Module 01 (Systems) or understanding of the hook-yarn boundary concept
- Ability to chain and single crochet

## Materials Needed

- Crochet hook (size H/8 or 5.0mm)
- Medium-weight (worsted) yarn
- A simple written pattern (granny square or dishcloth)
- Scissors and stitch markers
