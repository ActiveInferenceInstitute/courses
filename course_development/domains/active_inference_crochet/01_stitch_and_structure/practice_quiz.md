## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In the crochet-as-system framework, the Markov Blanket is best identified as:
A) The pattern instructions
B) The finished object in its gift bag
C) The working edge of the fabric — the live loops, the hook, and the working yarn
D) The crocheter's memory of the pattern

2. When a crocheter frogs (rips back) several rows, they are:
A) Giving up on the project
B) Correcting accumulated prediction error by returning to a point where the generative model and the fabric align
C) Increasing the free energy of the system
D) Removing the Markov Blanket

3. A gauge swatch is best understood in Active Inference terms as:
A) A finished object
B) A way to calibrate the agent's generative model (prior precision) before committing to a full project
C) An unnecessary step for experienced crocheters
D) A purely decorative exercise

4. Yarn tension in crochet corresponds to which Active Inference concept?
A) The C vector (preferences)
B) Precision — the confidence or gain assigned to sensory prediction errors
C) The B matrix (transitions)
D) Expected free energy

5. Placing a stitch marker at the start of a round is an example of:
A) Pragmatic action — directly changing the fabric
B) Epistemic action — reducing uncertainty about position in the pattern
C) Frogging — undoing previous work
D) Planning — choosing the next project

6. An experienced crocheter can stitch without looking because:
A) They have eliminated their Markov Blanket
B) Their generative model has become precise enough to rely on tactile prediction errors alone
C) They no longer perform inference
D) Their yarn has memorized the pattern

7. The internal states of a crochet project include:
A) The unworked yarn in the skein
B) The pattern instructions on the table
C) The completed rows and emerging fabric structure
D) The lighting in the room

## Part B: Reflective Response

1. Pick up a crochet hook and yarn (or imagine doing so). Describe the moment of inserting the hook into a stitch, yarning over, and pulling through. Where is the boundary between you and the fabric in that moment? What are you sensing, and what are you predicting?

2. Think of a time you had to frog several rows of work. Describe the experience in Active Inference language: what was the prediction error, how long did you tolerate it before acting, and what did you learn from the correction?

3. How does your crocheting change when you are working on a familiar pattern versus an unfamiliar one? Describe the differences in terms of precision, prediction error, and the balance between habitual and deliberate action.
