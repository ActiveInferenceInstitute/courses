- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module examines how crocheters **plan** their projects: project selection as policy choice, yarn estimation as planning under uncertainty, and difficulty assessment as predictive modeling. Planning in crochet is where the generative model looks furthest into the future.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Choosing which project to start | Policy selection — evaluating expected free energy |
| Estimating yarn needed for a project | Planning under uncertainty using the generative model |
| Assessing project difficulty before starting | Predicting future states and evaluating own capacity |
| Color planning and palette selection | Multi-step prediction across the project timeline |
| Buying yarn "just in case" (stash building) | Reducing anticipated future uncertainty |
| Deciding to abandon or continue a struggling WIP | Policy revision based on accumulated prediction error |

### Craft Integrity

- Acknowledge that planning in crochet ranges from meticulous (spreadsheets, yardage calculators) to intuitive ("I'll just start and see what happens") — both are valid planning strategies.
- Respect the stash as a real planning resource — crocheters accumulate materials based on generative models of future projects.
- Use real planning scenarios: choosing between a baby blanket and an amigurumi, estimating skeins for a sweater, deciding on colorwork complexity.
- Connect the emotional dimension of project planning — excitement, ambition, realistic self-assessment — to the formal concept of expected free energy.
