# Station: Systems (Circle & Community)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Social dynamics, teaching, cultural transmission in crochet circles
- **Topics**: Systems
- **Lab Style**: Group Activity
- **Audience**: Fiber artists, crafters, crochet circle participants, and the curious
- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module presents the crochet circle itself as a **system** — a social group with a Markov blanket (who is in the circle, what is shared within it), internal states (shared knowledge, group norms, collective projects), and external states (the wider craft community, the venue, the world beyond the circle).

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| The gathering of crafters around a table | Multi-agent system formation |
| Who "belongs" to the circle | Markov blanket of the social system |
| Shared supplies, patterns, and snacks | Internal resources of the collective system |
| The venue, other groups, online communities | External states of the circle |
| The circle's unwritten rules and rituals | Internal norms maintaining system identity |
| Members coming and going over time | System persistence despite changing components |

### Craft Integrity

- Treat the crochet circle as a real social structure, not merely a metaphor — circles have history, culture, and collective intelligence.
- Use real circle dynamics: who brings treats, who always sits in the same chair, how newcomers are welcomed, how the group starts and ends.
- Acknowledge that circles vary enormously: some are formal guilds, others are casual coffee-shop meetups, and some exist entirely online.
