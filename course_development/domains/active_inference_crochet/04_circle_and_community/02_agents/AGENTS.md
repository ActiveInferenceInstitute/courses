## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Social dynamics, teaching, cultural transmission in crochet circles
- **Topics**: Agents
- **Lab Style**: Group Activity
- **Audience**: Fiber artists, crafters, crochet circle participants, and the curious
- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module examines each circle member as an **autonomous inference agent** with their own generative model — shaped by their unique skill level, project history, and craft preferences. The diversity of models within a circle creates conditions for collective learning.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Each crafter at the table with their own project | Individual agents with distinct generative models |
| Different skill levels within the group | Model depth variation across agents |
| The circle collectively knowing more than any individual | Distributed intelligence across a multi-agent system |
| An experienced member mentoring a beginner | Agent-to-agent model transmission |
| Creative differences about technique or aesthetic | Different priors and precision weightings across agents |
| The group settling into a shared rhythm | Multi-agent synchronization and collective free energy minimization |

### Craft Integrity

- Respect the individuality of each circle member — even in a shared space, every crocheter brings a unique perspective, history, and set of skills.
- Acknowledge the natural hierarchy of expertise in circles without making it rigid — the newest member may know something the most experienced does not.
- Use real interpersonal dynamics: the quiet expert, the enthusiastic beginner, the member who always has the best pattern suggestions.
