Lab: Agent Profiles in the Circle

Group Activity — Agents

Objective

Create a profile of each circle member as an Active Inference agent, mapping their generative models and exploring how the diversity of agents in the group creates opportunities for collective learning.

Materials

Index cards (one per member)
Pens or markers
A shared large sheet of paper or whiteboard
Your current crochet projects

Duration

45-60 minutes

Activity Steps

Step 1: Individual Agent Profiles (15 minutes)

Each member fills out an index card as their "Agent Profile." Include:

Stitches I Know (list the stitches in your generative model)
Techniques I Am Learning (current edges of your model)
Techniques I Would Like to Learn (areas of high uncertainty / future prediction error)
My Specialty or Strength (the area where your model is deepest)
My Crochet History (brief: how long, who taught you, what tradition)
A Project That Surprised Me (a time your predictions about a project were wrong)

Be honest and specific. There is no hierarchy here — a card that says "I know chain and single crochet" is just as valid as one listing twenty techniques.

Step 2: Share and Compare (15 minutes)

Go around the circle and read your cards aloud. As each person shares, notice:
Where do generative models overlap? (Shared knowledge)
Where do they differ? (Diversity of models)
Where does one person's strength match another's wish-to-learn? (Teaching opportunities)

Place the cards on the large paper, arranging them to show connections. Draw lines between cards where one person's specialty matches another's learning goal.

Step 3: Map the Collective Agent (10 minutes)

On the large paper, around the individual cards, write down what the circle as a whole knows and can do:
What is the circle's collective range of stitches?
What projects could the group tackle together that no one could do alone?
What is the group's collective aesthetic — what kinds of projects does it gravitate toward?
What are the group's blind spots — techniques no one knows?

Discuss: Does the collective generative model exceed any individual's model? In what ways?

Step 4: Prediction Error Exchange (10 minutes)

Pair up with someone whose profile is different from yours. Each person demonstrates one thing they know that the other does not — a stitch, a technique for holding yarn, a way of reading a pattern. Notice:
What prediction errors arise for the learner?
How does the teacher adjust their explanation?
What does the teacher learn from teaching?

Switch pairs if time allows.

Reflection Prompts

Were you surprised by the range of knowledge in the group?
Did you discover a match between your learning goal and someone else's specialty?
How does seeing everyone as an "agent with a generative model" change how you think about skill differences in the circle?
What would happen to the group's collective model if a member left? If a new member with a very different background joined?

Extension

Keep the Agent Profile cards and revisit them in a month. Has anyone's model expanded? Has someone learned the technique they wished for? Track the circle as a model-expansion machine in action.
