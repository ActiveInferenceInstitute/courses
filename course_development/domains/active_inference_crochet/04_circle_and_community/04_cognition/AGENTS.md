## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Social dynamics, teaching, cultural transmission in crochet circles
- **Topics**: Cognition
- **Lab Style**: Group Activity
- **Audience**: Fiber artists, crafters, crochet circle participants, and the curious
- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module examines **distributed cognition** in the crochet circle — how collective knowledge exceeds what any individual knows, how groups solve problems together, and how shared mental models emerge through interaction.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| The circle collectively solving a pattern problem | Collective active inference — pooling generative models |
| Shared assumptions about difficulty levels | Group-level priors |
| "We always do granny squares for charity projects" | Shared generative model constraining group action |
| A new technique spreading through the circle | Model propagation across a multi-agent network |
| Group consensus on yarn quality or pattern merit | Collective belief formation through social inference |
| The knowledge that "lives" in the circle but no single member holds | Distributed cognition across agents |

### Craft Integrity

- Honor the collective intelligence of craft circles — the wisdom of the group is real and greater than the sum of its parts.
- Use real group problem-solving scenarios: deciphering a confusing pattern together, figuring out why someone's gauge is off, deciding how to divide a charity project.
- Acknowledge that shared understanding in circles often forms without explicit discussion — through observation, imitation, and gradual alignment.
