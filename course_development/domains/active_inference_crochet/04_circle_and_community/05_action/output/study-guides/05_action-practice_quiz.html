Practice Quiz: Action — Teaching a New Stitch Across the Circle

Part A: Multiple Choice

  1. Teaching in a crochet circle is best described as: A) One-way transmission of information from expert to beginner B) An active inference loop where the teacher predicts, observes, and adjusts based on the learner's prediction errors C) Simply demonstrating a stitch and hoping the learner copies it D) An activity that does not involve prediction or updating

  2. When a teacher builds a model of what the learner knows and does not know, this is called: A) First-order inference B) Second-order inference — modeling another agent's generative model C) Self-inference D) External state modeling

  3. A learner's prediction error when attempting a new stitch is: A) A sign of failure that should be avoided B) The raw material of learning — signaling gaps between their model and reality C) Evidence that the teacher is doing a bad job D) Irrelevant to the learning process

  4. When a circle decides to make a communal blanket with each member contributing squares, this is an example of: A) Individual action that happens to occur in a group setting B) Collective action where the group acts as a unified agent toward a shared predicted outcome C) Competition between members to make the best square D) Action that does not require coordination

  5. Encouragement like "You are doing great, keep going" functions in Active Inference as: A) Empty social pleasantry with no cognitive effect B) An action that increases the learner's precision — boosting confidence in their own predictions C) A way to distract the learner from their errors D) A signal that the teacher has given up on helping

  6. When a circle debates between a complex and a simple group project, they are evaluating: A) Who is the most skilled member B) Expected free energy — weighing predicted difficulty against predicted satisfaction C) Whether to disband the circle D) Nothing systematic — it is a random choice

  7. The gift of a finished crochet object to someone outside the circle represents: A) A loss of internal state B) An active state in the Markov blanket, carrying the circle's craft and care into the external world C) A violation of the system boundary D) An action with no relation to Active Inference

Part B: Short Answer

  1. Describe a teaching moment in a crochet circle using the full active inference loop: what does the teacher predict, what do they observe, what prediction error arises, and how do they adjust?

  2. How does a collaborative project (like a communal blanket) reveal the circle acting as a collective agent? What predictions, coordination, and error-correction are involved?

  3. Compare teaching a stitch in person at a circle with following a YouTube tutorial alone. What specific advantages does the in-person active inference loop provide?
