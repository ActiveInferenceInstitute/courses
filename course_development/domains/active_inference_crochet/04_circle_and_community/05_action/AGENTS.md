- **Topics**: Action

- **Audience**: Fiber artists, crafters, crochet circle participants, and the curious
- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module focuses on **teaching and collaborative action** in the circle — how demonstrating a stitch is active inference applied to another person's learning, and how collaborative projects require coordinated action across multiple agents.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Teaching a new stitch across the table | Active inference applied to another agent — adjusting demonstration based on learner's response |
| Collaborative charity projects | Coordinated multi-agent action toward a shared goal |
| Holding someone's yarn while they wind | Cooperative action reducing joint prediction error |
| Showing vs. telling a technique | Selecting the action modality that best transmits the model |
| Correcting a beginner's hook hold | Physical intervention to align the learner's action policy |
| Group decisions about what to make next | Collective action selection under shared expected free energy |

### Craft Integrity

- Respect teaching as a genuine skill — the best crafter is not always the best teacher. Effective teaching requires modeling the learner's model.
- Use real teaching moments: showing someone how to hold the hook, demonstrating a stitch slowly, helping someone count their foundation chain.
- Acknowledge that action in the circle is both physical (stitching, demonstrating) and social (encouraging, deciding together, dividing tasks).
