Practice Quiz: Perception — Reading the Room While You Stitch

Part A: Multiple Choice

Social perception in a crochet circle is best described as:
A) Passive reception of visual information about others
B) An active inference process of generating predictions about other members and updating them based on sensory evidence
C) Watching what others are making so you can copy their project
D) A skill that only experienced crocheters possess

When Rosa unexpectedly stops stitching, a circle member notices because:
A) They were staring at Rosa the whole time
B) The change violates their generative model's prediction of Rosa's typical behavior, creating prediction error
C) Rosa announced she was stopping
D) The room became completely silent

Precision in social perception refers to:
A) How accurately you count stitches
B) The confidence assigned to predictions about another person's behavior
C) The sharpness of your eyesight
D) How precisely you follow a pattern

A beginner crocheter tends to be less aware of the social environment because:
A) They do not care about other people
B) Their attentional resources are consumed by the physical task, leaving little precision for social signals
C) Beginners are always seated facing away from the group
D) Social perception only develops after five years of crochet

The sound of frogging (ripping out stitches) in a crochet circle functions as:
A) Just noise with no social meaning
B) A social signal that triggers predictions and responses in other members (sympathy, offers of help)
C) Evidence that the person should leave the circle
D) A sign that the meeting is ending

Recognizing flow in another crocheter is important because:
A) It tells you to interrupt them with a compliment
B) It signals that the person is in a state of absorbed, productive work that should not be disrupted
C) Flow only happens to beginners
D) It indicates the person is about to leave

A prediction error in social perception (e.g., misreading Devon's silence as boredom) is:
A) A sign of poor social skills that cannot be improved
B) A learning opportunity that updates the generative model and improves future social inference
C) Proof that Active Inference does not apply to social settings
D) Something that only happens to new circle members

Part B: Short Answer

Describe the shared sensory environment of a crochet circle meeting. Include at least three types of sensory information and explain what each tells members about the state of the group.

Explain how attentional allocation changes as a crocheter develops from beginner to expert. How does this shift affect their capacity for social perception?

A new person joins your circle. Compare the precision of your social predictions about them versus a long-time member. How does this difference in precision affect how you perceive and interact with the newcomer?
