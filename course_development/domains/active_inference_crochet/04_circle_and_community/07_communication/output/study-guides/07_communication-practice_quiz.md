# Practice Quiz: Communication — Stories Told Over Stitches

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. The crochet circle's dual-channel communication system consists of:
A) Two people talking at once
B) Hands working (physical inference) while voices engage in conversation (social inference) simultaneously
C) Written patterns and verbal instructions only
D) Online and offline communication

2. Stories told over stitches function as:
A) Idle chatter that distracts from crochet
B) Cultural transmission mechanisms that bind the group's shared generative model
C) Entertainment with no cognitive value
D) Communication that only benefits the storyteller

3. A crochet pattern is described as "inherently incomplete" because:
A) Patterns always have typos
B) It communicates structural instructions but not the embodied knowledge of how stitches should feel and look
C) No one reads patterns fully
D) Patterns are only for beginners

4. When a member says "Row 12 is confusing" while sharing a pattern, they are communicating:
A) That the pattern is poorly written
B) Metacognitive knowledge — predictions about where the learner will encounter difficulty
C) Their own inability to follow the pattern
D) Nothing useful

5. The physical rhythm of crochet creates conditions for deep conversation because:
A) People cannot think while crocheting
B) Hands busy with repetitive work free the mind for unhurried talk, and the craft provides social permission for intimate sharing
C) Crochet makes people tired and less guarded
D) The noise of hooks covers embarrassing pauses

6. Online crochet communities extend the circle's communication by:
A) Fully replacing in-person interaction
B) Adding scale, asynchrony, and diversity while sacrificing some embodied richness
C) Providing superior communication in every way
D) Having no connection to the physical circle

7. Silent communication in a circle (e.g., quietly placing yarn in front of someone who needs it) is:
A) Less meaningful than verbal communication
B) A powerful form of physical-channel communication that demonstrates attention, care, and understanding
C) A sign that members are not friendly enough to talk
D) Not relevant to Active Inference

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Describe a specific example of how the **physical channel** and **social channel** interact during a crochet circle meeting. Show how information flows between them.

2. Explain why **craft stories** (narratives about crochet experiences) are more effective at transmitting technical knowledge than abstract instructions. Use Active Inference concepts.

3. Compare pattern sharing **in person** at a circle with pattern sharing **online**. What does the in-person social context add that the online version lacks?
