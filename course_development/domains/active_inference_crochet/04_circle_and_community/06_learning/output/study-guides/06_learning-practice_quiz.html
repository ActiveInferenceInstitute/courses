Practice Quiz: Learning — How Circles Preserve and Pass On Craft

Part A: Multiple Choice

  1. Legitimate peripheral participation describes: A) A beginner who is not allowed to participate until they prove their skill B) A newcomer who learns by starting at the edges of the community and gradually moving toward full participation C) Participation that requires a formal test to be considered legitimate D) A learning process that only works in academic settings

  2. A newcomer learns by watching experienced crocheters before attempting stitches themselves. In Active Inference terms, this is: A) Wasted time that delays real learning B) Perceptual inference — building internal predictions through observation that will guide later action C) Passive reception that does not involve the generative model D) A sign that the newcomer is not engaged

  3. Cultural transmission of crochet techniques across generations is best understood as: A) Perfect copying of information from one brain to another B) Propagation of generative models across agents over time, with modification, innovation, and sometimes loss C) A process that only happens through written patterns D) Something that ended when the internet was invented

  4. A circle where errors are normalized and frogging is celebrated creates effective learning because: A) Members stop making mistakes B) The social cost of prediction error is reduced, allowing learners to take risks C) Experienced members never need to demonstrate D) Errors have no informational value

  5. Scaffolding in a crochet circle means: A) Using a physical scaffold to hold yarn B) Experienced members providing calibrated support that matches the learner's level without taking over C) Giving the beginner the hardest project to challenge them D) Removing all support so the learner is forced to figure things out alone

  6. The diversity of skill levels in a circle creates a zone of proximal development because: A) Everyone works on the same project B) There is always someone slightly ahead who can model the next step and someone slightly behind whom you can help C) Beginners slow down the experts D) Experts do all the teaching while beginners only watch

  7. When a technique dies with its last practitioner, this represents: A) A natural improvement to the craft B) The dissolution of a generative model that no one inherited C) Evidence that the technique was not valuable D) Something that cannot happen in the age of the internet

Part B: Short Answer

  1. Describe the journey of a newcomer from their first meeting to becoming a full participant in the circle. Use Active Inference concepts (generative model, prediction error, model expansion) to explain what is happening at each stage.

  2. How does a crochet circle function as a vehicle for cultural transmission across generations? Give a specific example of a technique being transmitted, modified, and potentially lost.

  3. Compare learning in a crochet circle with learning from the internet (YouTube, forums). What does each provide that the other lacks? Use Active Inference language.
