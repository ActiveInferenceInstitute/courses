## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Social dynamics, teaching, cultural transmission in crochet circles
- **Topics**: Learning
- **Lab Style**: Group Activity
- **Audience**: Fiber artists, crafters, crochet circle participants, and the curious
- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module examines how crochet circles **preserve and transmit** craft knowledge: apprenticeship as legitimate peripheral participation, cultural transmission across generations, and the way new techniques spread through social networks of makers.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Newcomer watching experienced crafters before attempting | Learning through observation — absorbing the model before testing it |
| Apprenticeship and mentorship in the circle | Legitimate peripheral participation as gradual model acquisition |
| Family crochet traditions passed grandmother to grandchild | Cultural transmission of generative models across generations |
| A new technique going "viral" in the circle | Model propagation through a social network |
| The circle's collective skill growing over years | Multi-agent model refinement through sustained interaction |
| Preserving endangered techniques (bobbin lace, hairpin lace) | Generative model preservation against entropy |

### Craft Integrity

- Honor the depth and value of intergenerational craft transmission — grandmother teaching granddaughter is one of the oldest forms of model sharing.
- Use real learning trajectories: the newcomer who watches for three sessions before picking up a hook, the intermediate crafter who suddenly "gets" a technique they have struggled with.
- Acknowledge that some craft knowledge is at risk of being lost — preservation is an active effort by the community.
