Measurement precision +

How carefully you measure matters. A casual stretch-and-eyeball gives a different reading than pinning the swatch flat and using a ruler. The quality of your sensory data affects the quality of your state estimate.

Explore how Measurement precision connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Where you measure +

Measuring at the center of the swatch versus the edge gives different results. This is analogous to sampling bias — where you observe affects what you infer.

Explore how Where you measure connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Washing and blocking +

A swatch measured before and after washing may give different readings. The "true" gauge is revealed only after the fabric reaches its stable state — there is a hidden variable (how the yarn relaxes) that affects perception.

Explore how Washing and blocking connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Precision weighting +

How much weight you give to gauge information depends on the project. For a dishcloth, gauge barely matters — low precision. For a fitted garment, gauge is critical — high precision.

Explore how Precision weighting connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

High-precision signals +

A clear stitch count on a simple single-crochet row. You can count exactly, and the count is unambiguous. This observation strongly updates your belief about the row's state.

Explore how High-precision signals connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Low-precision signals +

A "this looks about right" assessment of a complex textured pattern. The signal is noisy — you cannot be sure whether the pattern is tracking correctly. This observation weakly updates your belief.

Explore how Low-precision signals connects to the other concepts in this module through the lecture and lab materials.

Centrality to module

Precision-weighted integration +

When you have multiple signals of different quality — a stitch count that matches, but a visual sense that the row looks wrong — your brain weights the more precise signal more heavily. The count says 42, so you move on, even though something looks slightly off.
