## Content Guidelines

This module presents crocheted fabric as a **topological surface** and a **network graph** — stitches as nodes, connections as edges, and the resulting fabric as a manifold with measurable curvature. The system is the geometry itself, and crochet is one of the few crafts that can create hyperbolic surfaces by hand.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Flat fabric (equal stitches per row) | Zero Gaussian curvature — flat manifold |
| Ruffled fabric (too many increases) | Negative Gaussian curvature — hyperbolic surface |
| Bowl/sphere (decreases pulling fabric inward) | Positive Gaussian curvature — spherical surface |
| Stitches as nodes, connections as edges | Graph / network representation of the system |
| The edge of the fabric (boundary stitches) | Markov blanket of the topological surface |
| Euler characteristic (V - E + F) | Topological invariant predicting surface properties |

### Craft Integrity

- Use the Lorenz/Henderson hyperbolic crochet project as a touchstone — real mathematicians have used crochet to model hyperbolic geometry.
- Make topology tangible: learners should crochet flat, ruffled, and cupped fabric pieces and feel the difference.
- Connect the graph-theoretic view of crochet to neural network architecture without forcing the analogy — let the structural parallels speak for themselves.
