Key Concepts

Increases as Positive Curvature +

Increases add stitches, creating positive curvature that makes fabric dome or cup. This is active topology sculpting.

Working two stitches into one base adds material. Symmetric increases produce smooth doming (positive Gaussian curvature). Excessive increases create hyperbolic ruffling. The crocheter sculpts a mathematical manifold with hook and yarn.

Centrality: Core shaping action

Decreases as Negative Curvature +

Decreases remove stitches, pulling fabric inward. Combined with increases, they sculpt any target topology from sphere to torus.

Decreasing (working two stitches together) removes material. On a sphere, decreases past the equator close the form. On a torus, decreases at the inner rim create saddle curvature while increases shape the outer rim.

Centrality: Complementary shaping

Active Inference in Shaping +

Each shaping decision is active inference: predict the curvature effect, execute the stitch, observe the result, update the model.

Before an increase, the crocheter predicts its effect. After execution, they observe via touch and vision. This tight prediction-action-observation loop is Active Inference at the stitch level. Over time, the model becomes so accurate that shaping outcomes are predicted many rows ahead.

Centrality: Active Inference integration
