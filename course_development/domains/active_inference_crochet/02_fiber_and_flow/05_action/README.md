## Learning Goals

1. **Describe** tension adjustment as active inference — continuous correction to maintain desired fabric
2. **Explain** yarn feeding and switching between yarns as action policies minimizing prediction error
3. **Connect** the physical pull-through to the action component of the perception-action cycle
4. **Practice** adjusting tension deliberately for different fibers and notice how your motor policy adapts
5. **Explore** yarn joining techniques as specific action policies for specific material transitions

## Key Crochet Terminology

- **Tension adjustment**: Changing finger pressure to control how tightly yarn is held during stitching
- **Yarn feeding**: The continuous flow of yarn from skein through tension fingers to hook
- **Color change / yarn switch**: Transitioning from one yarn to another within a project
- **Russian join**: A technique for joining yarns by threading them through each other, creating a seamless transition
- **Weaving in ends**: Securing loose yarn tails into the fabric after completing a section or changing yarns

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Modules 01-04 in this course
- Comfort with basic crochet stitches in at least one fiber type

## Materials Needed

- Crochet hook (size appropriate to chosen yarns)
- Two or more yarns in different fibers for tension comparison
- Tapestry needle for weaving in ends
- Small work-in-progress for joining practice
