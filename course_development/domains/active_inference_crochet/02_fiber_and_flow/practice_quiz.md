# Practice Quiz: Fiber & Flow

**Name**: _________________________ **Date**: _________________________

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. The Markov Blanket of a skein of yarn is:
A) The label with fiber content information
B) The surface of the yarn where fiber meets air, moisture, and skin
C) The center pull strand
D) The dye lot number

2. When a crocheter switches from acrylic to silk yarn, what Active Inference process occurs?
A) The Markov Blanket dissolves
B) The generative model recalibrates to accommodate different material properties (slickness, weight, drape)
C) Free energy reaches zero permanently
D) The crocheter stops performing inference

3. Creative flow in crochet corresponds to which Active Inference state?
A) Maximum prediction error
B) No inference activity
C) A steady state of low free energy where the model is well-calibrated to the task's demands
D) Complete randomness of action

4. Cotton yarn is described as having "no memory" because:
A) It cannot be dyed
B) It does not return to its original shape after stretching, requiring the crocheter to adjust their model for drape and sag
C) It is cheaper than wool
D) It always produces the same gauge

5. Yarn substitution is best understood as:
A) Abandoning the project
B) Model transfer — applying the pattern's generative model to a new generative process while updating for material differences
C) Ignoring the pattern entirely
D) Using leftover yarn from another project

6. Hand-dyed yarn requires the crocheter to:
A) Hold color predictions with lower precision while maintaining structural predictions (gauge, count) with high precision
B) Abandon all predictions about color
C) Crochet with eyes closed
D) Only use solid-color patterns

7. Blocking a finished crochet piece reveals:
A) Nothing useful about the crocheter's model
B) The difference between the crocheter's predictions about the finished shape and the actual fabric state
C) That the yarn has no internal states
D) That Active Inference does not apply to finished objects

## Part B: Reflective Response

1. Hold two different yarns in your hands (or imagine two you have worked with). Describe the sensory experience of each — texture, weight, warmth, slickness. How does your generative model generate different predictions for each yarn about stitch formation, tension, and finished fabric?

2. Describe a time you experienced creative flow while crocheting. What made the project the right level of challenge? What would have been too easy (boredom) and what would have been too hard (frustration)?

3. You are substituting a cotton-bamboo blend for the merino wool specified in a shawl pattern. What aspects of your generative model must you update? What predictions from the original pattern remain valid, and which ones must change?
