## Learning Goals

1. **Identify** yarn as a system with internal states (fiber composition, twist, ply) and a boundary (the skein)
2. **Describe** how dye lot and environmental context shape the behavior of fiber systems
3. **Map** the Markov blanket concept onto the physical boundary between yarn and its surroundings
4. **Compare** different fiber types as different systems with different internal states and boundary properties
5. **Explain** how storage conditions, humidity, and handling are external states that affect the yarn system

## Key Crochet Terminology

- **Fiber content**: The raw material of the yarn (wool, cotton, acrylic, silk, blends)
- **Ply**: Number of strands twisted together; affects strength, texture, and stitch definition
- **Twist direction**: S-twist or Z-twist; determines how yarn behaves under tension
- **Dye lot**: The specific batch in which yarn was dyed; lots may vary in color
- **Skein / hank / ball**: Different ways yarn is wound and sold, each a different packaging of the system

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Course 1 (Stitch & Structure) or basic crochet competency
- Access to yarns of different fiber types for comparison

## Materials Needed

- Three or more yarn samples in different fiber types (wool, cotton, acrylic at minimum)
- Yarn labels / band information for each sample
- Bowl of warm water (for fiber behavior testing, optional)
- Notebook for recording observations
