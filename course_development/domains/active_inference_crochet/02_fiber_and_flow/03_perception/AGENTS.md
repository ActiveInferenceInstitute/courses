- **Tone**: Warm, crafty, hands-on, accessible

## Content Guidelines

This module investigates how crocheters **perceive** yarn through touch — reading weight, texture, and irregularities as sensory evidence. Knots, inconsistencies, and color shifts are surprise signals that interrupt the flow of working with a familiar material.

### Key Mappings

| Crochet Concept | Active Inference Concept |
|---|---|
| Feeling yarn weight between fingers | Sensory evidence about material state |
| Detecting a knot hidden in the skein | Surprise (prediction error) |
| Comparing yarn textures blind | Perceptual discrimination via generative model |
| Noticing color variation across skeins | Visual prediction error |
| Yarn halo, sheen, or matte quality | Multi-modal sensory features |
| The "hand" of a fabric (drape, stiffness) | Integrated percept from multiple sensory channels |

### Craft Integrity

- Ground all perception concepts in real fiber handling — invite learners to close their eyes and sort yarns by weight or texture.
- Acknowledge that the perception of yarn is deeply sensuous and personal — the same yarn may feel "warm and cozy" to one crocheter and "scratchy" to another.
- Use the discovery of a knot mid-skein as a vivid example of how surprise interrupts prediction and requires immediate response.
- Connect visual perception of color variation to the formal concept of prediction error without overcomplicating.
