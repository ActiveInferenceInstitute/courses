## Content Rules

1. **Real Methods Only** — No mocks, stubs, placeholder brackets, or `[TODO]` markers. Every module must contain substantive, technically grounded content.
2. **8-Topic Spine** — Systems → Agents → Perception → Cognition → Action → Learning → Communication → Planning. Every unit follows this sequence.
3. **Domain-First Language** — Lead with inventions/design vocabulary, then bridge to Active Inference formalism. The learner is an inventor first.
4. **Audience-Appropriate** — Content should be accessible to creative engineers, industrial designers, and entrepreneurial inventors. Assume comfort with iterative processes, prototyping, and systems thinking.

---

## Applied Examples

Each topic in this domain must use examples drawn from real inventive practice:

- **Systems**: patent ecosystems, innovation supply chains, design-space topologies
- **Agents**: inventor-prototype loops, user-tester feedback agents, design team dynamics
- **Perception**: observing user behavior, sensing material constraints, reading market signals
- **Cognition**: generative models of solutions, belief updating through prototype failure, mental simulation of mechanisms
- **Action**: building prototypes, filing patents, executing design sprints
- **Learning**: iterating on failures, updating design priors, accumulating craft knowledge
- **Communication**: technical documentation, pitching, cross-disciplinary knowledge transfer
- **Planning**: roadmapping product development, expected free energy of design choices, strategic technology selection

### Lab Style

Each unit should feature labs that use one of these formats:

- **Design Sprint**: rapid ideation → prototype → test → iterate cycle
- **Prototype Lab**: hands-on building of functional models or simulations
- **Innovation Workshop**: collaborative problem-solving around a design challenge
- **Patent Analysis**: reverse-engineering existing inventions through an Active Inference lens

---

## Theory Semantics

- **Inventions**: An invention process is active inference made tangible. The inventor maintains a generative model of a desired solution and iterates prototypes to minimize the gap between prediction and reality. Each design cycle is a perception-action loop. Patents are compressed generative models. Innovation ecosystems are multi-agent inference networks.
