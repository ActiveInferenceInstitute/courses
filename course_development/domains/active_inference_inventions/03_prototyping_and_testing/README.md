# Section 3: Prototyping and Testing

## Building, Testing, and Iterating

**Theme**: Physical and digital prototypes, feedback loops, and learning from failure.

Every invention begins as a mental model — a generative model inside the inventor's head. Prototyping is the act of making that model physical, testable, and accountable to reality. Testing is the act of comparing the model's predictions against the world's responses. And iteration is the act of updating the model based on what the world reveals.

This section applies Active Inference systematically to the prototyping and testing process. The inventor is an agent operating under uncertainty, building physical generative models (prototypes), observing their behavior (perception), reasoning about what the observations mean (cognition), taking corrective action (iteration), learning from outcomes (both failure and success), communicating discoveries (documentation), and planning what to test next (strategic test planning).

## Prerequisites

This section assumes familiarity with the foundational Active Inference concepts introduced in Section 1 (Invention Foundations) and the creative process explored in Section 2 (Creative Engineering). Students should have an invention project in progress — the labs are designed around the "bring your own invention" approach.

## Learning Outcomes

By completing this section, students will be able to:

1. Design prototypes as physical generative models with explicitly defined Markov blankets
2. Conduct rigorous testing with diverse agent perspectives, managing creator bias
3. Interpret test signals across quantitative, qualitative, behavioral, and failure categories
4. Reason about test results using Bayesian updating, distinguishing signal from noise
5. Execute disciplined iteration cycles with clear pivot-or-persevere decision criteria
6. Extract transferable lessons from both failures and successes through structured post-mortems
7. Maintain comprehensive documentation that externalizes the generative model for other agents
8. Create strategic test plans that prioritize hypotheses by expected free energy
