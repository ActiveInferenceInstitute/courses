# Study Questions: Prototyping and Testing

1. Explain the analogy between a physical prototype and a generative model. In what sense does a prototype "make predictions," and what constitutes a prediction error when the prototype is tested?

2. How does the fidelity level of a prototype determine its Markov blanket? Compare what is testable with a cardboard mockup versus a functional breadboard versus a production-quality prototype.

3. Why is confirmation bias particularly dangerous during testing, and how does the inventor's role as both creator and evaluator create a structural conflict? What specific strategies can mitigate this bias?

4. Describe how user testing distributes the sensing process across agents with different generative models. Why might a user detect prediction errors that the inventor systematically misses?

5. Classify the following test results as quantitative, qualitative, behavioral, or failure-mode signals, and explain the precision and informational content of each: (a) a load test showing the device bends at 15 kg; (b) a user saying "this feels awkward"; (c) a user turning the device upside down before using it; (d) the device overheating and shutting down.

6. The Wright Brothers conducted over 200 wind tunnel tests with miniature wing shapes. How does this systematic approach exemplify Active Inference's perception-action loop? What role did each test play in updating their aerodynamic generative model?

7. Explain the difference between parameter learning and structure learning in the context of prototype iteration. Provide a concrete example of each from an invention you know about.

8. What are the criteria for deciding to "pivot" (structure learning) versus "persevere" (parameter learning)? How does the concept of model evidence help formalize this decision?

9. James Dyson built 5,127 prototypes. Was this an efficient or inefficient process? Justify your answer using Active Inference concepts of expected free energy and information gain per iteration.

10. How does Toyota's A3 protocol externalize the generative model? What cognitive biases does it specifically counteract by requiring written documentation at each stage?

11. Explain why a prototype that "fails" can be more informative than one that "succeeds." Under what conditions does failure produce the highest information gain?

12. How should an inventor allocate testing resources across multiple uncertain hypotheses? Use the concept of expected free energy to explain why testing the most uncertain critical hypothesis first is generally optimal.

13. Describe the role of version control in iterative prototyping. How does maintaining a complete history of prototypes support both parameter learning and structure learning?

14. What is the sunk cost fallacy, and how does it bias the pivot-or-persevere decision? How does Active Inference's focus on expected future free energy (rather than past investment) provide a corrective?

15. Design a test protocol for a prototype of your own invention that tests the single most critical hypothesis. Specify: (a) the hypothesis, (b) the prototype fidelity needed, (c) the test procedure, (d) the evidence that would confirm or disconfirm the hypothesis, and (e) what you would do next in each case.
