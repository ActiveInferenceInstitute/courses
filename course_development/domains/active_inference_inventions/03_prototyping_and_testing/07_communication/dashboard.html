Core Concepts

1. Documentation as External Generative Model

Documentation is an external representation of the inventor's beliefs. The quality test: could another competent agent reconstruct your understanding from the documentation alone? Good documentation records not just what was done but why. Leonardo da Vinci's notebooks remain valuable after 500 years because they capture his reasoning, not just his designs.

2. The Lab Notebook as Inference Log

A time-stamped record of the Active Inference loop: hypotheses, predictions, tests, observations, belief updates, and decisions. Key practices: date every entry, record in real time, separate observation from interpretation, never erase (use line-throughs), record failures with the same care as successes. Critical for patent protection.

3. Version Control for Physical Objects

Track prototype evolution through photography, change logs, labeling, and preservation of previous versions. Materials science uses "witness samples." 3D scanning captures geometry for archival. Without version control, changes are invisible and irreversible — information about what was tried and why is permanently lost.

4. Design Decision Records

Formalized records of what was chosen, what alternatives existed, what evidence supported the choice, what uncertainties remain, and how reversible the decision is. Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) in software engineering provide the template. Without these records, future engineers cannot understand, question, or safely modify past decisions.

5. Communicating Uncertainty

Responsible documentation reports both beliefs and their precision. "The battery lasts 8 hours" is less honest than "8 +/- 1.5 hours based on 5 tests, untested at extreme temperatures." Use a confidence vocabulary: tested and confirmed, estimated with some confidence, assumed but untested, highly uncertain. Adapt uncertainty communication to the reader's expertise level.
