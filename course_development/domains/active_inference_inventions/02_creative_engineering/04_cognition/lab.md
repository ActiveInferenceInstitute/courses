# Lab: Cognitive Strategies for Inventive Problem Solving

## Objective

Apply four distinct inventive cognition strategies — analogical reasoning, combinatorial creativity, TRIZ contradiction resolution, and mental simulation — to your own invention idea. By comparing the outputs of different cognitive strategies applied to the same problem, you will discover which strategies are most productive for your specific challenge and develop fluency in switching between cognitive modes.

## Materials / Prerequisites

- Your own invention idea or project (any domain, any stage)
- Paper and pen for sketching and diagramming
- A timer
- Completed reading of Module 04: Inventive Cognition

## Discussion / Debrief Questions

1. Did the different cognitive strategies produce genuinely different concepts, or did they converge on similar ideas? What does this tell you about the structure of your problem?

2. Which cognitive strategy felt most natural to you? Which felt most awkward? How does this relate to your inventor archetype from Module 02?

3. The mental simulation step often reveals risks that were invisible during the generative phase. Did your simulation change your assessment of which concept was most promising? How?

4. TRIZ reframes trade-offs as contradictions that can be resolved. Did you find that your problem contained a genuine contradiction, or was the trade-off a result of assumptions that could be questioned?

5. How could you systematically use these four strategies in your regular inventive practice? Would you use them in sequence, in parallel, or selectively based on the problem type?
