Analogical Reasoning

Transferring structural relationships from a well-understood source domain to a target domain. Gutenberg mapped the wine press's uniform-pressure principle to printing. Success requires abstracting relational structure (what transfers) from surface features (what does not). False analogies occur when structural mappings break down in the target domain.

Combinatorial Creativity

Recombining components from different generative models into novel configurations. The smartphone combined telephony, computing, photography, navigation, and audio — none novel individually, but together producing emergent capabilities. Productive combinations require complementarity, emergence, and coherence (Boden).

TRIZ Systematic Inventive Thinking

Genrich Altshuller's framework derived from analyzing 200,000+ patents. Core concept: contradictions (improving parameter A worsens parameter B) can be resolved through 40 inventive principles that transform the generative model. Samsung used Principle 15 (Dynamism) to resolve the display size-portability contradiction with foldable screens.

Mental Simulation

Running the generative model forward to predict outcomes of actions not yet taken. Tesla famously tested machines in imagination before building them. Mental simulation quality depends entirely on model accuracy — beautiful simulations with inaccurate models produce misleading predictions. Alternating mental and physical simulation calibrates both.

Cognitive Fluency and SCAMPER

The ability to generate many alternatives before converging. SCAMPER (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse) provides seven systematic model transformations. Research shows that inventors who generate more alternatives in early phases produce better final designs — breadth of exploration increases probability of finding superior solutions.
