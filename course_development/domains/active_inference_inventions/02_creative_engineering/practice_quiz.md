# Practice Quiz: Creative Engineering

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Bell Labs' success as a creative system is best attributed to:
A) Hiring only the most intelligent scientists
B) A physical and organizational structure that enabled generative models from different domains to interact and recombine
C) Unlimited funding with no accountability
D) A strict top-down management approach that directed all research

2. An inventor who carries a notebook to record anomalous observations throughout the day is primarily engaging in:
A) Pragmatic action -- advancing toward a specific goal
B) Active sensing -- systematically gathering prediction errors from diverse environments
C) Communication -- sharing ideas with future readers
D) Planning -- scheduling future experiments

3. Gutenberg's mapping of the wine press mechanism onto the text reproduction problem is an example of:
A) Combinatorial creativity -- recombining known elements into a novel whole
B) Analogical reasoning -- transferring structural relationships from a source to a target domain
C) TRIZ contradiction resolution -- eliminating a technical contradiction
D) Bricolage -- making do with available materials

4. In the TRIZ framework, a contradiction arises when:
A) Two inventors disagree about the best approach
B) Improving one desirable feature of a system necessarily degrades another desirable feature
C) A prototype fails during testing
D) The inventor runs out of funding

5. The Apollo 13 CO2 scrubber demonstrates that extreme constraints can:
A) Make invention impossible
B) Force creative recombination by restricting the solution space to available components
C) Eliminate the need for a generative model
D) Replace Active Inference with trial and error

6. Biomimicry works as an inventive strategy because:
A) Natural systems are always superior to engineered systems
B) Evolution has performed billions of years of Active Inference, producing generative models that solve many problems human inventors also face
C) Copying nature requires no understanding of mechanisms
D) Natural designs are always cheaper to manufacture

7. An inventor working on a new product should shift from exploration to exploitation when:
A) They run out of money
B) Uncertainty about the best approach has been sufficiently reduced that refining the current solution has higher expected value than searching for alternatives
C) Their manager tells them to stop exploring
D) They have generated exactly three alternative concepts

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Percy Spencer discovered microwave cooking when a chocolate bar melted in his pocket near a magnetron. Explain how his response to this anomaly differs from what a non-inventive observer might have done, using the concept of prediction error and the inventor archetype of the "accidental discoverer."

2. Describe how a design sprint (a structured multi-day creative process) operationalizes the Active Inference perception-cognition-action loop. What role does the time constraint play in managing the exploration-exploitation balance?

3. An inventor is designing a product for elderly users but is herself 25 years old. Using Active Inference concepts, explain why her generative model may produce systematic blind spots about her users' needs, and propose two specific strategies for reducing this prediction error gap.
