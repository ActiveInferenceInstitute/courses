Exploration vs. Exploitation

Early in a project, epistemic value dominates — explore widely. As uncertainty decreases, pragmatic value dominates — exploit the best approach. The "double diamond" model formalizes this: diverge (explore), converge (define), diverge (explore solutions), converge (deliver). Transition when marginal exploration value drops below marginal exploitation value.

Stage-Gate Decision Points

Stages are creative work phases; gates are evidence-based decision points. At each gate: continue (model adequate), pivot (model needs revision), or stop (model fundamentally flawed). Gates formalize expected free energy evaluation by defining explicit criteria for model confidence, preventing both premature convergence and indefinite exploration.

Creative Uncertainty vs. Conventional Risk

Conventional risk: known outcomes with estimable probabilities (parametric uncertainty). Creative uncertainty: unknown unknowns — the model structure itself may be wrong (structural uncertainty). Creative uncertainty requires option-preserving strategies, information-maximizing milestones, and adaptive replanning rather than traditional risk management.

Information-Maximizing Milestones

Test the highest-impact, most-testable uncertainties first. SpaceX's Grasshopper vehicle tested fundamental landing feasibility before investing in high-altitude capability. Reordering milestones by (impact if wrong) x (testability) often produces a plan radically different from the intuitive linear sequence — and far more efficient at reducing uncertainty.

Multi-Horizon Planning

Match planning detail to prediction reliability. Short horizon (2 weeks): specific actions. Medium horizon (2-3 months): objectives and milestones. Long horizon (6-12 months): direction and vision. The common mistake: applying short-horizon detail to long-horizon decisions (detailed 12-month plans) or long-horizon vagueness to short-horizon work (no plan for this week).

Convergence Criteria

When to stop exploring: solution clustering (independent approaches converge), diminishing returns (each cycle adds less), constraint satisfaction (a concept meets all requirements), or deadline-driven convergence. Satisficing — selecting "good enough" and iterating — often outperforms indefinite search for the optimal in complex creative problems.
