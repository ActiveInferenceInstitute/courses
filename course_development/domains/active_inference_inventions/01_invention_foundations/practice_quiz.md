# Practice Quiz: Invention Foundations

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In Active Inference, a Markov blanket separates:
A) The inventor from the invention
B) Internal states from external states through sensory and active states
C) The prototype from the production version
D) The problem space from the solution space

2. Which best describes the inventor's "generative model" in the Active Inference framework?
A) A physical prototype of the invention
B) The inventor's internal model of how the world works, including knowledge, assumptions, and predictions
C) A CAD drawing or digital twin
D) The business plan for the invention

3. An inventor notices that restaurant workers struggle with a particular task that office workers never mention. This is best explained by:
A) Restaurant workers complain more
B) Different generative models produce different prediction errors, making different problems visible
C) Office workers have no problems
D) The inventor has confirmation bias

4. James Dyson's shift from "the motor needs more power" to "the bag is causing suction loss" is an example of:
A) Parameter learning -- adjusting motor specifications
B) Structure learning -- fundamentally changing the causal model of the problem
C) Active sensing -- gathering more data about motor performance
D) Precision weighting -- increasing confidence in the motor hypothesis

5. A prototype that tests whether users can operate a device without instructions provides primarily:
A) Pragmatic value -- it advances the product toward market readiness
B) Epistemic value -- it resolves uncertainty about usability
C) Free energy maximization
D) Communication value -- it demonstrates the device to investors

6. WD-40's name comes from the fact that it was the 40th formulation attempt. The first 39 failures represent:
A) A waste of resources due to poor planning
B) Parameter learning -- each formulation updated specific variables while maintaining the same approach
C) Evidence that the inventors lacked a generative model
D) Structure learning -- each failure required a completely new framework

7. In staged development planning, irreversible decisions should be:
A) Made as early as possible to maintain momentum
B) Deferred until uncertainty about their consequences is sufficiently reduced through evidence
C) Avoided entirely
D) Made by committee vote rather than evidence

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how Edison's decision to design high-resistance filaments illustrates the importance of analyzing an invention at the correct system level. What would have happened if he had optimized the bulb in isolation?

2. Describe how the concept of expected free energy applies to a student deciding whether to build a quick cardboard prototype or a detailed 3D-printed version of their invention. What factors determine which approach has lower expected free energy?

3. The penicillin development story involved three different kinds of cognition: perceptual (Fleming), mechanistic (Chain), and applied (Florey). How does this multi-agent cognitive chain illustrate the limits of any single inventor's generative model?
