Lab: Causal Reasoning and Problem Reframing Workshop

Objective

Practice the core cognitive skills of inventive thinking: building causal models, reframing problems, running mental simulations, and comparing competing explanations. You will apply these skills to your own invention idea and discover how changing the question can open entirely new solution spaces.

Materials and Prerequisites

Your invention idea from previous labs
Paper and pen for diagramming
A timer or clock
No technical background required — all exercises use everyday reasoning

Part 1: Building a Causal Model of Your Problem (15 minutes)

Every invention addresses a problem, and every problem has a causal structure — a chain of causes and effects that produces the undesirable situation. Before you can solve the problem, you need to understand what causes it.

Part 2: Problem Reframing Exercise (15 minutes)

Technique 1: Inversion

Instead of "How do I solve this problem?" ask "How would I create this problem on purpose?"

If I wanted to deliberately cause the problem my invention addresses, what would I do?

Discussion and Debrief

Causal depth: How deep did your causal analysis go? Did the Five Whys reveal root causes that surprised you, or did you feel stuck at surface-level explanations?

Reframing impact: Which reframing technique (inversion, constraint removal, abstraction) was most generative for your specific problem? Why?

Mental simulation limits: Where did your mental simulation break down? What aspects of your solution could you not predict mentally? What does this tell you about what to test first when prototyping?

Cognitive biases: Did you notice any functional fixedness, Einstellung, or confirmation bias in your own thinking during this lab? What triggered it?

Model competition: After evaluating three candidate solutions, did your preference change from Solution A? What does this tell you about the value of generating alternatives before committing?
