Action as World-Changing Inference

Invention is active inference — changing the world to match the inventor's preferred states. Every prototype is a hypothesis expressed in physical form. The prototype is the action, its behavior is the observation, and the comparison between predicted and actual behavior updates the generative model. Building is thinking made physical.

Prototypes as Hypothesis Tests

Effective prototypes test specific assumptions. The minimum viable prototype (MVP) is the simplest construction that tests the riskiest assumption. The Wright Brothers' wind tunnel tested aerodynamic data for pennies. Types: proof of concept, user experience, feasibility, and aesthetic prototypes — each resolves different uncertainties.

The Perception-Action Loop in Building

Building creates a tight loop with materials: act, observe response, update model, act again. This operates at seconds (tool adjustment), days (prototype testing), and months (design iteration). Rapid prototyping tools compress the loop, and each iteration should test something new to generate genuine prediction errors.

Acting Under Uncertainty

Build when mental simulation reaches its limits. Expected free energy balances epistemic value (learning) against pragmatic value (achieving goals). Early prototypes prioritize epistemic value; later ones shift to pragmatic. Experienced inventors build sooner and simpler because they know where their knowledge runs out.

Failure as Informative Action

A prototype that fails tells you exactly where your model was wrong. Edison's 10,000 filament experiments were 10,000 data points. Dyson's 5,127 iterations were systematic uncertainty reduction. Design prototypes so that failure is specific and interpretable — ambiguous failure wastes resources, precise failure generates learning.
