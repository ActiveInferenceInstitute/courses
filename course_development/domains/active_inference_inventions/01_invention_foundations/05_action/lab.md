# Lab: Building Your First Minimum Viable Prototype

## Objective

Design and build (or plan in detail) a minimum viable prototype that tests the single most critical assumption of your invention idea. The goal is not a polished product but an informative action — a physical or experiential test that generates genuine prediction errors and updates your generative model. By the end of this lab, you will have identified your riskiest assumption, designed a test for it, and planned the perception-action loop that will drive your next iteration.

## Materials and Prerequisites

- Your invention idea and causal model from previous labs
- Materials appropriate to your domain (these can be extremely simple: cardboard, tape, paper, string, clay, code, cooking ingredients, drawing tools, etc.)
- A timer or clock
- A partner or test user (optional but valuable)

## Discussion and Debrief

1. **The building experience**: How did building (or detailed planning) change your understanding of your invention compared to thinking about it? What did you learn from materials that you could not have learned from mental simulation alone?

2. **Informative failure**: If your prototype failed to confirm your assumption, was the failure informative? Did you learn something specific, or was the failure ambiguous?

3. **Prototype scope**: Was your MVP truly minimal? Could you have tested the same assumption with an even simpler construction? What would you strip away?

4. **Perception-action speed**: How fast was your perception-action loop during building? What tools, materials, or environments would speed it up?

5. **The value of action**: Reflect on the difference between knowing something in theory and knowing it through building. What is the unique contribution of physical action to the inventive process?
