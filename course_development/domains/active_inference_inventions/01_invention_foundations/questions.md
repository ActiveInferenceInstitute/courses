# Study Questions: Invention Foundations

1. How does the concept of a Markov blanket help an inventor decide what is "inside" versus "outside" their invention? Provide an example from your own invention project.

2. Compare the system boundary of a simple hand tool (e.g., a hammer) with the system boundary of a networked device (e.g., a smart thermostat). How does the complexity of the Markov blanket differ, and what design challenges does each present?

3. What is the difference between an inventor's generative model and the generative model embodied in the invention itself? How do these two models interact during the design process?

4. Explain how the Wright Brothers' complementary skills illustrate the advantages of multi-agent invention. What would have been lost if only one brother had worked on the aircraft?

5. Active Inference defines perception as the detection of prediction errors. How does this definition change the way we think about "seeing a problem worth solving" compared to everyday notions of observation?

6. Describe a real-world example where an inventor's prior beliefs (identity priors) limited their ability to perceive a problem. How might a different set of priors have revealed the opportunity earlier?

7. Problem reframing involves changing the structure of the generative model rather than just updating its parameters. Using the Dyson vacuum case, explain the difference between parameter updating and structural reframing.

8. Why is counterfactual reasoning essential to invention? How does the quality of an inventor's generative model constrain the reliability of their counterfactual predictions?

9. Explain the analogy between a physical prototype and a scientific hypothesis. In what sense does every prototype "make a prediction," and what constitutes evidence for or against it?

10. What is the difference between parameter learning and structure learning in the context of iterating on an invention? Provide an example of each from a real invention.

11. How does the concept of precision weighting apply when an inventor must communicate their idea to an audience with very different background knowledge? What strategies reduce the prediction error gap between inventor and audience?

12. The expected free energy framework balances pragmatic value and epistemic value when planning. Describe a scenario where an inventor should prioritize epistemic value over pragmatic value, and explain why.

13. Edison's electric lighting system demonstrates the importance of drawing the Markov blanket at the right level of nesting. What went wrong for Edison's competitors who drew the boundary only around the light bulb?

14. How does the concept of nested systems apply to a modern invention like an electric vehicle? Identify at least three subsystems and describe how their Markov blankets interact.

15. Explain the accuracy-complexity trade-off in invention design. Why do over-engineered solutions often fail in practice, and how does this relate to variational free energy?
