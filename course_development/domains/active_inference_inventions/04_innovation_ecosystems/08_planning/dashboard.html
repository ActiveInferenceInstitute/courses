Technology Roadmapping

A technology roadmap is a hierarchical planning model linking long-term vision to near-term action. Higher levels predict at longer time scales with more uncertainty. Lower levels predict at shorter scales with more precision. The key skill is maintaining coherence across levels while building in revision conditions.

Example: Musk's Tesla Master Plan specified Roadster, Model S, Model 3 — each stage generating evidence and resources for the next.

Innovation Portfolio Management

Portfolio management distributes investment across three horizons: core optimization (H1), adjacent expansion (H2), and transformative innovation (H3). Each horizon requires different models of success and different evaluation criteria. Protecting H3 from H1 cost pressure is essential for long-term innovation capacity.

Example: 3M targets 30% of revenue from products introduced in the last four years, forcing continued investment across all horizons.

Exploration vs. Exploitation

Organizations naturally drift toward exploitation (optimizing the known) at the expense of exploration (seeking the unknown). Active Inference's expected free energy naturally balances these: epistemic value (learning) against pragmatic value (achieving). Deliberate structures must protect exploration from exploitation's gravitational pull.

Example: Amazon protected AWS exploration by separating it organizationally from the retail exploitation business.

Innovation Pipeline and Stage Gates

Innovation pipelines process ideas through stages with evidence gates. The critical principle: match evidence precision to stage. Early gates should be permissive (low-precision evidence) because ideas have not yet generated data. Late gates should be demanding (high-precision evidence) because investment is large. Requiring too much evidence too early kills exploration.

Example: X Development's "monkey-first" approach tests the hardest challenge first, before investing in easier components.

Adaptive Strategy

No plan survives contact with reality. The competitive advantage lies in having a planning system that updates faster than competitors'. "Commander's intent" specifies vision and principles while leaving tactics flexible. Strategic review processes detect when plans need revision versus restructuring.

Example: Microsoft under Nadella restructured from Windows-centric to cloud-first — a model restructuring that Nokia failed to achieve in mobile.
