# Study Questions: Innovation Ecosystems

1. How does an innovation ecosystem differ from a simple market? Describe the nested Markov blankets that separate the invention, the company, the industry, and the broader society.

2. Using Rogers' diffusion of innovation curve, explain why early adopters and late majority users have fundamentally different generative models. How does this difference affect adoption strategy?

3. Tesla entered the electric vehicle market starting with a luxury sports car (Roadster) rather than an affordable commuter car. Explain this sequencing decision using Active Inference concepts of staged action and expected free energy.

4. How does customer discovery interviewing function as active perception at the ecosystem level? What are the precision characteristics of interview data compared to quantitative market surveys?

5. Describe a business model as a causal model in Active Inference terms. What are the hidden variables, the observable predictions, and the evidence that would update or invalidate the model?

6. Netflix evolved from DVD mail rental to streaming to content production. At each transition, what ecosystem-level prediction errors triggered the model update? Was each transition parameter learning or structure learning?

7. Explain the concept of "crossing the chasm" between early adopters and mainstream market in Active Inference terms. What changes in the marketing message correspond to changes in the target audience's generative model?

8. How does Spotify's squad-based organizational structure function as a distributed Active Inference system? What are the advantages of small autonomous teams for processing market prediction errors?

9. Compare closed intellectual property (patents) and open-source strategies as different approaches to managing the generative model at the ecosystem level. Under what conditions does each strategy minimize expected free energy?

10. How does branding establish persistent priors in the consumer's generative model? Explain why a strong brand reduces prediction error across all interactions with the company.

11. A startup discovers that users love the product but refuse to pay the intended price. Is this a signal to lower the price (parameter learning) or to change the revenue model (structure learning)? What additional evidence would you need to decide?

12. Explain how over-the-air software updates (as used by Tesla) create a continuous perception-action loop between the company and every product in the field. How does this change the traditional product development cycle?

13. Describe a scenario where regulatory constraints on an invention are better understood as boundary conditions on the Markov blanket rather than obstacles to overcome. How does this reframing change the inventor's strategic response?

14. The Moderna mRNA vaccine platform was developed over a decade but achieved mass deployment only during the COVID-19 pandemic. How does this illustrate the role of ecosystem conditions in determining when an invention achieves impact?

15. Design an ecosystem entry strategy for your own invention. Identify the key stakeholders, their generative models, the most critical uncertainties, and the sequencing of actions you would take to navigate the ecosystem.
