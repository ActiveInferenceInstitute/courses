# Lab: Navigating Your Innovation Ecosystem

## Objective

Map the innovation ecosystem for your invention project, model the key stakeholders as inference agents, and design an ecosystem entry strategy grounded in Active Inference principles. This lab integrates concepts from all eight modules to produce a practical go-to-ecosystem plan.

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Sections 1-3 labs, including your validated prototype insights and iteration log
- An invention project that is approaching the stage of external engagement (even if still early)
- Access to basic research tools (web browser, search engine) for stakeholder and market investigation

## Materials

- Your Prototyping and Testing outputs (A3 documentation, iteration log, test results)
- Large paper or digital canvas for ecosystem mapping
- Colored markers or digital drawing tools for distinguishing stakeholder types
- Research access for competitive and market analysis

## Procedure

### Part 1: Ecosystem Mapping (15 minutes)

Draw a multi-layer ecosystem map for your invention.

1. **Center**: Place your invention at the center of the map.
2. **Layer 1 -- Direct stakeholders**: Draw the agents who will directly interact with your invention: users, customers (if different from users), distributors, and service providers. For each, write one sentence describing their generative model -- what do they expect, need, and value?
3. **Layer 2 -- Enabling stakeholders**: Add the agents who support or enable your invention: suppliers, technology partners, investors, and mentors. Note the Markov blanket between each enabling agent and your invention -- what information flows between you?
4. **Layer 3 -- Shaping stakeholders**: Add the agents who shape the conditions your invention operates in: regulators, industry standards bodies, competitors, and complementary innovators. Note whether each is likely to be an ally, a barrier, or neutral.
5. **Identify the critical link**: Which stakeholder relationship is most critical to your invention's success? Mark it clearly.

### Part 2: Stakeholder Agent Profiling (15 minutes)

Select your three most important stakeholders and create an Active Inference agent profile for each.

For each stakeholder:

1. **Generative model**: What does this stakeholder believe about the problem your invention solves? What is their current solution? What would surprise them about your approach?
2. **Prior precision**: How strongly committed are they to their current approach? Is this an early adopter (flexible priors) or a late majority agent (rigid priors)?
3. **Evidence threshold**: What evidence would they need to see before adopting or supporting your invention? Be specific -- not "they need to see it works" but "they need to see three successful case studies from comparable organizations."
4. **Prediction error opportunity**: What prediction error in their current experience does your invention resolve? This is your value proposition for this specific stakeholder.

### Part 3: Market Sensing Design (10 minutes)

Design a lightweight market sensing strategy.

1. **Three key uncertainties**: What are the three most important things you do not know about your ecosystem? (Examples: "How much would users pay?", "Which distribution channel reaches our users?", "What regulations apply?")
2. **Evidence sources**: For each uncertainty, identify the cheapest, fastest way to gather evidence. Consider: customer interviews (5-10 conversations), competitive product analysis, regulatory database searches, industry report summaries, or social media sentiment analysis.
3. **Precision assessment**: For each evidence source, rate its precision (how reliable is the evidence?) and its cost (how much time and money does it require?). Prioritize high-precision, low-cost sources.

### Part 4: Ecosystem Entry Strategy (20 minutes)

Design a staged plan for entering your innovation ecosystem.

1. **Entry point**: Which stakeholder group will you engage first? Why? (Consider: who has the most flexible generative model, who provides the fastest feedback loop, who gives you credibility with others.)
2. **Stage 1 -- Validation**: What specific evidence will you gather from your entry-point stakeholders? What threshold constitutes sufficient validation to proceed?
3. **Stage 2 -- Expansion**: After validation, which stakeholder group will you engage next? How does success with the first group provide evidence or credibility for the second?
4. **Stage 3 -- Scaling**: What must be true for your invention to reach mainstream adoption? What ecosystem infrastructure (partnerships, distribution, regulatory approval) must be in place?
5. **Communication plan**: For your entry-point stakeholders, draft a one-paragraph pitch that addresses their specific generative model. Focus on the prediction error your invention resolves for them, using language they already use.

## Discussion

1. What was the most surprising discovery in your ecosystem mapping? Did you identify stakeholders you had not previously considered?
2. How do the generative models of your three profiled stakeholders differ? What does this imply about the need for different communication strategies for different audiences?
3. Looking at your entry strategy, is your sequencing driven by epistemic value (learning which stakeholders respond) or pragmatic value (generating revenue)? Is the balance appropriate for your project's current stage?
4. If your first-stage validation produces negative results (stakeholders do not respond as expected), what would your contingency plan be? Define a specific pivot option.
