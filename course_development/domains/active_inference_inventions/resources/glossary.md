# Glossary — Active Inference for Inventions

## Active Inference Terms

**Active Inference**: A framework in which agents act to minimize the difference between their predictions and observations, thereby maintaining their existence and pursuing goals.

**Free Energy Principle (FEP)**: The principle that any self-organizing system that persists must minimize its variational free energy — the difference between its model of the world and the world itself.

**Variational Free Energy**: A quantity that bounds surprise (negative log-evidence). Minimizing free energy means either updating beliefs (perception) or changing the world (action).

**Expected Free Energy**: The anticipated free energy under a particular policy (plan of action). Used for planning and decision-making — agents select actions that minimize expected free energy.

**Generative Model**: An internal model that represents how observations are generated from hidden causes. The inventor's mental model of how their invention works and how the world responds to it.

**Markov Blanket**: The statistical boundary separating a system's internal states from external states. Consists of sensory states (input) and active states (output).

**Internal States**: Variables inside the Markov blanket that encode the system's beliefs and identity.

**External States**: Variables outside the Markov blanket — the environment the system interacts with but cannot directly access.

**Sensory States**: Blanket states through which external information reaches internal states. How the system observes.

**Active States**: Blanket states through which internal states influence external states. How the system acts.

**Precision**: The confidence or reliability assigned to a signal. High precision means the signal is trusted; low precision means it is discounted.

**Prediction Error**: The difference between what was expected and what was observed. The fundamental signal that drives learning and adaptation.

**Surprise (Surprisal)**: A technical term for the improbability of an observation given a model. Not the emotional feeling — the information-theoretic quantity.

**Epistemic Value**: The value of an action for its information-gathering properties — reducing uncertainty about the world. "Exploration."

**Pragmatic Value**: The value of an action for achieving preferred outcomes. "Exploitation."

## Invention Terms

**Problem Space**: The landscape of needs, constraints, and possibilities within which an invention operates.

**Solution Space**: The set of possible designs, configurations, and approaches that could address a problem.

**Prototype**: A preliminary version of an invention used to test hypotheses and reduce uncertainty.

**Minimum Viable Prototype (MVP)**: The simplest version of an invention that can generate meaningful feedback.

**Design Iteration**: A cycle of build-test-learn that refines an invention based on evidence.

**Affordance**: What an object or environment offers to an agent — the possibilities for action.

**Constraint**: A limitation on what is possible, which paradoxically enables creative solutions by narrowing the search space.

**Prior Art**: Existing solutions and knowledge relevant to an invention — the inherited generative model.

**Reduction to Practice**: The process of turning an abstract idea into a working invention.

**Technology Readiness Level (TRL)**: A scale from 1-9 measuring how close an invention is to deployment.

**Pivot**: A fundamental change in the generative model of an invention — reframing the problem or solution.

## Combined Terms (Active Inference + Invention)

**Inventive Free Energy**: The gap between the inventor's model of how things should work and how they actually work. The driving force behind inventive activity.

**Invention Blanket**: The Markov blanket of an invention-in-progress — what's inside the design (internal states), what's outside (the world), and the interface between them.

**Generative Model of Mechanism**: The inventor's understanding of how their invention produces its effects — the causal model that can be tested and refined.

**Precision Engineering**: Deliberately adjusting which signals (feedback, data, intuition) are trusted more or less during the inventive process.

**Epistemic Prototyping**: Building prototypes specifically to learn something, not to demonstrate a solution.

**Free Energy Landscape of Innovation**: The terrain of possible inventions, with valleys (stable solutions) and ridges (unstable, transitional designs).
