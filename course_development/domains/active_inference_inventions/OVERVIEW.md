# Active Inference for Inventions — Course Overview

## Vision

Every invention is an act of inference. The inventor perceives a gap between the world as it is and the world as it could be, constructs a generative model of how things work, and acts to reduce the surprise between expectation and reality. This course makes that process explicit, rigorous, and learnable.

Active Inference — the framework built on Karl Friston's Free Energy Principle — provides a unified theory of how adaptive systems perceive, think, act, and learn. This course applies that theory directly to the practice of invention, giving students a principled methodology for:

- Identifying problems worth solving
- Generating novel solutions
- Building and testing prototypes
- Iterating based on evidence
- Communicating and scaling inventions
- Planning for long-term impact

## Pedagogical Approach

### The Active Inference Spine

Every section covers eight foundational topics that form the "spine" of Active Inference:

1. **Systems** — Defining boundaries, identifying components, understanding wholes
2. **Agents** — The inventor as an adaptive agent with goals and models
3. **Perception** — Sensing problems, gathering evidence, updating beliefs
4. **Cognition** — Reasoning about causes, modeling mechanisms, reframing problems
5. **Action** — Building, testing, intervening in the world
6. **Learning** — Updating models from outcomes, parameter learning, structure learning
7. **Communication** — Sharing models, aligning with collaborators, teaching users
8. **Planning** — Evaluating future actions, managing uncertainty, strategic foresight

### Domain Adaptation

Each section applies these eight topics through a different lens of the inventive process:

| Section | Lens | Core Question |
|---------|------|---------------|
| Invention Foundations | What is an invention? | How do we define and understand inventive systems? |
| Creative Engineering | How do we invent? | How does the creative process work through Active Inference? |
| Prototyping and Testing | How do we build and validate? | How do prototypes reduce uncertainty? |
| Innovation Ecosystems | How do inventions scale? | How do inventions survive contact with the world? |

### Inclusive Design

This course is explicitly designed for diverse backgrounds. Labs and exercises use a "bring your own invention" approach — students work on their own projects, applying Active Inference principles to problems in their own domains. Whether you are designing a mechanical device, a software application, an artistic installation, a social program, or a business process, the framework applies.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Frame any invention as an Active Inference system with a Markov blanket, generative model, and free energy landscape
2. Use Active Inference to systematically identify problems, generate solutions, and evaluate ideas
3. Design prototyping strategies that efficiently reduce uncertainty about their invention's viability
4. Apply Bayesian reasoning to iterate on designs based on evidence
5. Communicate their inventive vision using the shared vocabulary of Active Inference
6. Plan invention projects that account for multiple scales of uncertainty
7. Collaborate effectively in inventive teams using shared generative models
8. Reflect on and improve their own inventive practice through metacognitive Active Inference
