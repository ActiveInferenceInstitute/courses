# Practice Quiz: Metallurgical Systems

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In the Active Inference framework applied to metallurgy, a grain boundary serves as:
A) An external state of the crystal system
B) A Markov blanket separating the internal states of adjacent grains
C) A defect that should be eliminated
D) A sensory state used by the metallurgist

2. The equilibrium vacancy concentration in a crystal at temperature T exists because:
A) Manufacturing processes always introduce defects
B) Vacancies increase both internal energy and entropy, with the entropy contribution dominating at elevated temperatures -- a free energy minimization outcome
C) The crystal has not had enough time to reach equilibrium
D) Vacancies are prediction errors that the crystal cannot resolve

3. X-ray diffraction is best described as a metallurgical perception modality that senses:
A) Chemical composition at the atomic scale
B) Average lattice parameters, crystal structure, and phase identity from coherent scattering
C) Individual dislocation positions
D) Mechanical properties like hardness and yield strength

4. Martensite formation during quenching of austenitic steel represents:
A) Complete free energy minimization -- the system reached equilibrium
B) A diffusionless transformation where the system is kinetically arrested before resolving its prediction error through equilibrium decomposition
C) Structure learning -- the steel fundamentally changed its model
D) A failure of the characterization technique

5. In the Al-Cu precipitation sequence (GP zones to theta to equilibrium), each metastable phase represents:
A) A manufacturing defect
B) The system's progressively refined inference about its equilibrium state, with each phase a better approximation of the Gibbs energy minimum
C) Random atomic arrangements
D) Evidence that the material cannot reach equilibrium

6. Fick's first law (J = -D dC/dx) describes diffusion as:
A) Random atomic motion with no driving force
B) The resolution of a spatial prediction error (concentration gradient) through directed atomic migration
C) A purely temperature-dependent process
D) Communication between the metallurgist and the material

7. A metallurgist choosing between TEM and optical microscopy to characterize a sample is practicing:
A) Random experimental design
B) Active perception -- selecting the sensory modality that maximizes information gain for the specific uncertainty being investigated
C) Cost minimization without regard to information content
D) Perception without cognition

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how the mathematical parallel between Gibbs free energy minimization and variational free energy minimization is more than an analogy. What structural features do both share, and why does this parallel matter for the curriculum?

2. A steel component fails in service due to intergranular corrosion. Describe how a metallurgist would investigate this failure using Active Inference concepts: what characterization techniques (perception) would they use, what causal models (cognition) would they consider, and how would they update their model based on the evidence?

3. Describe the nested Markov blanket structure of a dual-phase steel containing both ferrite and martensite. Identify the boundaries at the atomic, grain, phase, and component levels, and explain how properties at one level depend on states at lower levels.
