# Study Questions: Metallurgical Systems

1. Explain how a unit cell functions as a Markov blanket at the atomic scale. What are the internal states, external states, sensory states, and active states of an FCC unit cell?

2. A vacancy in a crystal lattice is a "prediction error" relative to the ideal crystal model. How does the equilibrium vacancy concentration represent an accuracy-complexity trade-off between internal energy (accuracy) and entropy (complexity)?

3. Compare the Markov blankets of a grain boundary and a free surface. How do the sensory and active states differ between these two types of system boundary?

4. Describe how X-ray diffraction serves as a perception modality for the metallurgist. What crystallographic information does it sense (its sensory resolution), and what is it blind to (its conditional independence)?

5. How does EBSD complement XRD as a characterization technique? In Active Inference terms, what additional states does EBSD sense that XRD cannot, and why does this matter for understanding microstructure?

6. Explain the analogy between DFT calculations and Active Inference cognition. In what sense is a DFT total energy calculation an "internal model" of the material system's stability?

7. When a plain carbon steel is quenched from the austenitic region, martensite forms instead of the equilibrium ferrite-cementite mixture. Describe this transformation using Active Inference concepts of prediction error, kinetic arrest, and frustrated free energy minimization.

8. The precipitation sequence in Al-Cu alloys (GP zones to theta-double-prime to theta-prime to theta) can be understood as sequential model updating. Explain this analogy and identify what drives the system from one metastable phase to the next.

9. Fick's first law states that diffusive flux is proportional to the negative concentration gradient. Describe this in Active Inference terms: what is the "prediction error" that drives diffusion, and how is it "resolved"?

10. How does the choice of characterization technique represent active perception? Give an example of a metallurgical question where choosing TEM over optical microscopy would be the correct "active sensing" decision, and explain why.

11. Dislocations can be understood as line defects that represent prediction errors in the crystal's shear plane structure. How does dislocation recovery (rearrangement into lower-energy configurations) represent free energy minimization?

12. Explain the mathematical parallel between Gibbs free energy (G = H - TS) and variational free energy (F = E_q[log q(x) - log p(x,y)]). What does each term in Gibbs free energy correspond to in the variational formulation?

13. How does nested system architecture apply to a multi-phase steel microstructure? Identify at least three levels of nesting (from atomic to component scale) and describe the Markov blanket at each level.

14. An alloy designer must choose between two candidate compositions, each with different predicted properties but different levels of uncertainty. How does the expected free energy framework apply to this design decision?

15. The development of stainless steel required understanding the role of chromium in forming a passive oxide layer. Describe this engineering breakthrough as a case of model updating -- what was the prior model, what prediction error was observed, and how was the model revised?
