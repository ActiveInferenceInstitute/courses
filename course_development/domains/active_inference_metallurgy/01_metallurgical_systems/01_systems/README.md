## Learning Objectives

1. **Define** the concept of a Markov blanket and map it onto crystal lattice boundaries (unit cell surfaces, grain boundaries)
2. **Compare** Bravais lattice types (FCC, BCC, HCP) as distinct generative models encoding different structural expectations
3. **Analyze** how Wigner-Seitz cells define the minimal sufficient boundary for atomic-scale inference
4. **Explain** polymorphic phase transitions (e.g., BCC iron to FCC austenite) as model switching driven by environmental change
5. **Calculate** lattice parameters from XRD data and interpret deviations as prediction errors about interatomic spacing
6. **Connect** the Free Energy Principle to thermodynamic lattice stability — both governed by free energy minimization

## Key Concepts

- **Unit cell as Markov blanket**: The unit cell boundary separates internal atomic positions from the external lattice environment
- **Crystal symmetry as invariance**: Symmetry operations define what the lattice expects to remain unchanged under transformation
- **Lattice defects as prediction errors**: Vacancies, interstitials, and substitutional atoms represent deviations from the ideal crystal model

## Prerequisites

- Introductory materials science (crystal structure, Miller indices)
- Basic thermodynamics (Gibbs free energy, equilibrium)
- Familiarity with Active Inference concepts (Markov blanket, free energy minimization)
