## Learning Objectives

1. **Identify** point defects (vacancies, interstitials, substitutional atoms) and line defects (dislocations) as agents within the Active Inference framework
2. **Describe** how vacancy migration follows an Arrhenius-like belief distribution governed by formation and migration energies
3. **Explain** dislocation glide as an action policy executed to relieve applied stress (prediction error)
4. **Analyze** dislocation networks as multi-agent systems exhibiting emergent collective behavior (work hardening)
5. **Connect** the Peierls-Nabarro stress to the action threshold concept — the minimum driving force for dislocation motion
6. **Distinguish** between thermally activated agents (vacancy diffusion) and mechanically driven agents (dislocation glide under stress)

## Key Concepts

- **Vacancy as diffusion agent**: Migrates through the lattice to minimize configurational free energy
- **Dislocation as deformation agent**: Glides along slip planes under resolved shear stress
- **Solute atom as partitioning agent**: Diffuses to equalize chemical potential between regions
- **Multi-agent emergence**: Work hardening arises from collective dislocation interactions
