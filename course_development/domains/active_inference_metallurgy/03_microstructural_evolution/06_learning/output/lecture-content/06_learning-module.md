# Adaptive Characterization and 4D Imaging

## Introduction

This module explores **Learning** within the context of **Microstructural Evolution**. In the Active Inference framework, learning plays a critical role in how systems maintain their identity, process information, and adapt to perturbation. Here we examine this through the lens of nucleation, grain growth, precipitation, and characterization.

**Key themes**: Serial sectioning, synchrotron 4D-XCT, in-situ TEM, time-resolved characterization

## Key Concepts

### 1. Learning in the Active Inference Framework

In Active Inference, learning refers to the process by which systems maintain and update their relationship with the environment. For metallurgical systems, this maps directly onto physical processes: Serial sectioning, synchrotron 4D-XCT, in-situ TEM, time-resolved characterization.

The Free Energy Principle provides a unifying lens: every metallurgical phenomenon involving learning can be understood as a system minimizing the difference between its current state and its preferred (equilibrium) state.

### 2. Learning at the Atomic Scale

At the most fundamental level, learning in metallurgy operates through atomic-scale mechanisms. Atoms in a crystal lattice interact with their neighbors according to interatomic potentials, and the collective behavior of these interactions gives rise to macroscopic material properties.

### 3. Learning at the Mesoscale

Moving up in scale, learning manifests through microstructural features — grain boundaries, precipitates, phase interfaces, and defect networks. These mesoscale structures mediate between atomic-level events and engineering-scale properties.

### 4. Learning at the Engineering Scale

At the largest scale relevant to this module, learning is expressed through macroscopic material behavior and process-level phenomena. This is where the metallurgist's interventions — heat treatments, deformation processes, and compositional design — interact with the material's inherent tendencies.

### 5. The FEP Bridge: From Thermodynamics to Inference

The deepest insight of this curriculum is that thermodynamic free energy minimization and variational free energy minimization share the same mathematical structure. In the context of learning, this means that the physical processes governing metallurgical behavior are formally analogous to the inference processes governing adaptive systems.

## Applications

### Case Study: Learning in Steel Processing

Consider a plain carbon steel (Fe-0.4wt%C) undergoing heat treatment. The concept of learning manifests concretely: the material system processes information (temperature, composition gradients), updates its internal state (crystal structure, phase fractions), and acts to minimize its free energy through phase transformations.

### Case Study: Learning in Aluminum Alloy Design

In Al-Cu precipitation-hardening alloys (such as 2024-T3), learning operates through the sequence of metastable precipitate formation: GP zones -> theta'' -> theta' -> theta (Al2Cu). Each stage represents the system's ongoing inference about its equilibrium state.
