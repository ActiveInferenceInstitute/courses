Interface Energy and Thermodynamic Signaling

Introduction

This module explores Communication within the context of Thermodynamic Inference. In the Active Inference framework, communication plays a critical role in how systems maintain their identity, process information, and adapt to perturbation. Here we examine this through the lens of phase equilibria, transformation kinetics, and calphad.

Key themes: Interfacial free energy, wetting angles, segregation coefficients, Gibbs adsorption

Key Concepts

Communication in the Active Inference Framework

In Active Inference, communication refers to the process by which systems maintain and update their relationship with the environment. For metallurgical systems, this maps directly onto physical processes: Interfacial free energy, wetting angles, segregation coefficients, Gibbs adsorption.

The Free Energy Principle provides a unifying lens: every metallurgical phenomenon involving communication can be understood as a system minimizing the difference between its current state and its preferred (equilibrium) state.

Communication at the Atomic Scale

At the most fundamental level, communication in metallurgy operates through atomic-scale mechanisms. Atoms in a crystal lattice interact with their neighbors according to interatomic potentials, and the collective behavior of these interactions gives rise to macroscopic material properties.

Communication at the Mesoscale

Moving up in scale, communication manifests through microstructural features — grain boundaries, precipitates, phase interfaces, and defect networks. These mesoscale structures mediate between atomic-level events and engineering-scale properties.

Communication at the Engineering Scale

At the largest scale relevant to this module, communication is expressed through macroscopic material behavior and process-level phenomena. This is where the metallurgist's interventions — heat treatments, deformation processes, and compositional design — interact with the material's inherent tendencies.

The FEP Bridge: From Thermodynamics to Inference

The deepest insight of this curriculum is that thermodynamic free energy minimization and variational free energy minimization share the same mathematical structure. In the context of communication, this means that the physical processes governing metallurgical behavior are formally analogous to the inference processes governing adaptive systems.

Applications

Case Study: Communication in Steel Processing

Consider a plain carbon steel (Fe-0.4wt%C) undergoing heat treatment. The concept of communication manifests concretely: the material system processes information (temperature, composition gradients), updates its internal state (crystal structure, phase fractions), and acts to minimize its free energy through phase transformations.

Case Study: Communication in Aluminum Alloy Design

In Al-Cu precipitation-hardening alloys (such as 2024-T3), communication operates through the sequence of metastable precipitate formation: GP zones -> theta'' -> theta' -> theta (Al2Cu). Each stage represents the system's ongoing inference about its equilibrium state.
