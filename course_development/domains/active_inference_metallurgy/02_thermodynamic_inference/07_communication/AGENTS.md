## Active Inference Integration

Interfaces are the communication channels of thermodynamic systems. The interfacial free energy encodes the cost of maintaining a boundary between two phases — it is the energetic price of the Markov blanket at the mesoscale. Segregation coefficients describe how solute atoms preferentially accumulate at interfaces, carrying chemical information from the bulk to the boundary. The Gibbs adsorption isotherm quantifies this relationship: as interfacial energy changes with composition, the boundary communicates information about the bulk thermodynamic state. Wetting angles encode the relative communication strength between three phases meeting at a triple point.

## Key Mappings

| FEP Concept | Interface Communication Translation |
|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| Communication Channel | Phase interface, grain boundary, surface |
| Channel Cost | Interfacial free energy (J/m^2) |
| Signal | Solute segregation to the interface; composition gradient across the boundary |
| Message Content | Chemical potential information transmitted between adjacent phases |
| Channel Capacity | Segregation coefficient; Gibbs adsorption excess |
| Multi-Channel Junction | Triple point; wetting configuration; contact angle |

## Content Guidelines

- Frame the Gibbs adsorption isotherm as a quantitative relationship between interfacial communication and bulk thermodynamic state
- Treat segregation as selective information transfer — certain elements preferentially carry their chemical potential signal to the interface
- Connect wetting phenomena to the relative strength of inter-phase communication channels
- Emphasize that coherent vs. incoherent interfaces have different communication properties — coherent interfaces transmit crystallographic information while incoherent interfaces do not
