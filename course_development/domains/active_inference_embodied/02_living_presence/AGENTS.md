## Embodied Cognition Domain Guidelines

This course deepens embodied awareness through breath, heartbeat, and the felt sense of being a living organism. Content must be grounded in the following principles:

- **Breath as primary**: Every module begins with breath awareness. The breath is the most accessible bridge between voluntary and involuntary, between the conscious and the autonomic.
- **Autonomic regulation**: The living body is always regulating itself -- heartbeat, blood pressure, temperature, digestion. These processes are the foundation of what Active Inference formalizes as free energy minimization.
- **Allostasis through presence**: Mindfulness practice reveals the body's ongoing predictive regulation. By attending to breath and heartbeat, learners directly observe allostatic processes.
- **Active Inference integration**: Free energy minimization is presented as the body's continuous effort to maintain itself as a living system. The generative model is the body's implicit prediction of its own viability.
- **Interoceptive depth**: This course goes deeper into interoception than Course 1, exploring cardiac awareness, respiratory sensitivity, and visceral sensing as foundations of selfhood.

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding.
2. Frame all concepts through the lens of breath, heartbeat, embodied being.
3. Include mindfulness practice activities that involve actual bodily engagement (not just reading or writing).
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).
6. Avoid equations, code, or formal notation -- use metaphor, first-person description, and phenomenological language instead.
7. Reference key sources: Seth (interoceptive self), Craig (interoception), Barrett (constructed emotion), Sterling (allostasis).
