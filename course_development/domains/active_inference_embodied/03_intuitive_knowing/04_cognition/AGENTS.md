## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Pattern-feeling without numbers
- **Topics**: Cognition as gut knowing -- the intelligence that lives below words
- **Lab Style**: Reflective Journaling
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module explores **cognition** through the lens of embodied intuition, gut feelings, and the body's capacity for pattern recognition without conscious analysis. Content must:

- Present cognition through the body's non-verbal intelligence: the knowing that manifests as a feeling in the gut, a tightness in the chest, or a sense of ease.
- Connect to Gendlin's focusing practice: systematically accessing the body's implicit knowing by attending to the felt sense of a situation.
- Frame embodied intuition as a legitimate and often more comprehensive form of cognition than analytical reasoning.
- Reference the enteric nervous system ('the second brain') as a source of embodied intelligence.
- Integrate Active Inference: gut knowing is the generative model's deep-structure predictions, operating at levels that are not yet articulated but are felt through interoceptive channels.
- Journaling activities should involve Gendlin-style focusing exercises: sitting with a question and attending to the body's response.
