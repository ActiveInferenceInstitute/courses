## Embodied Cognition Domain Guidelines

This course explores how the body knows things before the mind can articulate them. Content must be grounded in the following principles:

- **Intuition as embodied inference**: Gut feelings, hunches, and felt senses of rightness or wrongness are the body's way of integrating vast amounts of information below conscious awareness. In Active Inference, this is the generative model's prediction operating at levels below explicit representation.
- **Pattern-feeling**: The body recognizes patterns -- in people, situations, environments -- through felt sense rather than analytical reasoning. This is a legitimate and often more rapid form of cognition.
- **Reflective journaling as method**: Writing about bodily experience helps bridge the gap between felt sense and conceptual understanding. The journal becomes a tool for making implicit knowledge explicit.
- **Active Inference integration**: Intuitive knowing is framed as the generative model's prior predictions influencing perception and action before conscious deliberation. Precision weighting determines which intuitive signals reach awareness.
- **Enactivism and embodied wisdom**: Drawing on the enactive tradition (Varela, Thompson, Rosch), this course presents knowing as arising from the organism's history of structural coupling with its environment.

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding.
2. Frame all concepts through the lens of pattern-feeling without numbers.
3. Include reflective journaling activities that involve actual bodily engagement (not just reading or writing).
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).
6. Avoid equations, code, or formal notation -- use metaphor, first-person description, and phenomenological language instead.
7. Reference key sources: Gendlin (focusing, felt sense), Dreyfus (expertise and intuition), Varela (ethical know-how), Damasio (somatic markers).
