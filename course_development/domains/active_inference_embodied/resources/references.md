# References: Active Inference for Embodied Experience

> Recommended reading and resources for anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding of embodied cognition through Active Inference.

## Foundational Active Inference

1. Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory? *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127-138.
2. Parr, T., Pezzulo, G., & Friston, K. J. (2022). *Active Inference: The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, and Behavior*. MIT Press.
3. Friston, K., FitzGerald, T., Rigoli, F., Schwartenbeck, P., & Pezzulo, G. (2017). Active inference: A process theory. *Neural Computation*, 29(1), 1-49.

## Embodied Cognition Classics

4. Varela, F. J., Thompson, E., & Rosch, E. (1991). *The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience*. MIT Press.
5. Thompson, E. (2007). *Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind*. Harvard University Press.
6. Clark, A. (1997). *Being There: Putting Brain, Body, and World Together Again*. MIT Press.
7. Clark, A. (2008). *Supersizing the Mind: Embodiment, Action, and Cognitive Extension*. Oxford University Press.
8. Gallagher, S. (2005). *How the Body Shapes the Mind*. Oxford University Press.
9. Gallagher, S., & Zahavi, D. (2012). *The Phenomenological Mind* (2nd ed.). Routledge.
10. Shapiro, L. (2011). *Embodied Cognition*. Routledge.
11. Chemero, A. (2009). *Radical Embodied Cognitive Science*. MIT Press.

## 4E Cognition (Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, Extended)

12. Newen, A., De Bruin, L., & Gallagher, S. (Eds.). (2018). *The Oxford Handbook of 4E Cognition*. Oxford University Press.
13. Hutto, D. D., & Myin, E. (2013). *Radicalizing Enactivism: Basic Minds without Content*. MIT Press.
14. Di Paolo, E. A., Cuffari, E. C., & De Jaegher, H. (2018). *Linguistic Bodies: The Continuity between Life and Language*. MIT Press.
15. Noë, A. (2004). *Action in Perception*. MIT Press.

## Interoception, Allostasis, and Predictive Processing

16. Seth, A. K. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Penguin.
17. Barrett, L. F. (2017). *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain*. Houghton Mifflin Harcourt.
18. Craig, A. D. (2015). *How Do You Feel? An Interoceptive Moment with Your Neurobiological Self*. Princeton University Press.
19. Sterling, P. (2012). Allostasis: A model of predictive regulation. *Physiology & Behavior*, 106(1), 5-15.
20. Hohwy, J. (2013). *The Predictive Mind*. Oxford University Press.
21. Clark, A. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 36(3), 181-204.

## Sensorimotor Contingencies and Enactivism

22. O'Regan, J. K., & Noë, A. (2001). A sensorimotor account of vision and visual consciousness. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 24(5), 939-973.
23. Di Paolo, E. A., & Thompson, E. (2014). The enactive approach. In L. Shapiro (Ed.), *The Routledge Handbook of Embodied Cognition* (pp. 68-78). Routledge.
24. Merleau-Ponty, M. (1945/2012). *Phenomenology of Perception* (D. Landes, Trans.). Routledge.

## Morphological Computation and Ecological Psychology

25. Pfeifer, R., & Bongard, J. (2006). *How the Body Shapes the Way We Think*. MIT Press.
26. Gibson, J. J. (1979). *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*. Houghton Mifflin.
27. Turvey, M. T. (2019). *Lectures on Perception: An Ecological Perspective*. Routledge.

## Phenomenology and Felt Sense

29. Sheets-Johnstone, M. (2011). *The Primacy of Movement* (Expanded ed.). John Benjamins.
30. Johnson, M. (2007). *The Meaning of the Body: Aesthetics of Human Understanding*. University of Chicago Press.
