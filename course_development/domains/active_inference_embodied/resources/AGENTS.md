> **Quick Navigation**: [Resources README](./README.md) | [Curriculum AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Purpose

These shared resources serve as the single source of truth for terminology, notation, and references across all courses in Active Inference for Embodied Experience. They ground the curriculum in the intersection of Active Inference theory and embodied cognition research.

## Embodied Cognition Domain Standards

All resource materials must:

1. Bridge formal Active Inference terminology with experiential, body-centered language.
2. Reference the 4E cognition framework (embodied, embedded, enacted, extended) as the organizing lens for embodied concepts.
3. Include interoceptive, proprioceptive, and exteroceptive dimensions when defining sensory or perceptual terms.
4. Connect allostatic regulation and morphological computation to standard Active Inference constructs where appropriate.
5. Cite primary sources from phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty, Gendlin), enactivism (Varela, Thompson, Di Paolo), ecological psychology (Gibson), and predictive processing (Friston, Seth, Clark).

## Maintenance Rules

1. Update the glossary when new terms are introduced in any module.
2. Keep the notation table synchronized with all courses, ensuring each term has both a formal definition and an embodied-experience translation.
3. Verify cross-course map links after any structural changes.
4. All tables must use proper MD060-compliant formatting (spaces around pipes).
5. When adding references, categorize them under the appropriate heading (Active Inference, Embodied Cognition, Phenomenology, Enactivism, Ecological Psychology, etc.).
6. Ensure learning pathways remain current as new courses or modules are added.

## Tone Guidelines

- Accessible and experiential in primary descriptions; scholarly in citations.
- Always provide a "plain English" and an "embodied meaning" alongside formal definitions.
- Avoid jargon without explanation; when technical terms are necessary, accompany them with a felt-sense description.
