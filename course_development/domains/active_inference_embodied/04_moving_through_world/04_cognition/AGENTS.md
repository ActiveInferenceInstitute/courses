## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Dance, yoga, martial arts
- **Topics**: Cognition as thinking through movement -- the body as a cognitive instrument
- **Lab Style**: Movement Lab
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module explores **cognition** through the lens of full-body movement, sensorimotor coupling, and the inseparability of action and perception. Content must:

- Present cognition through the experience of thinking in and through movement: the choreographer who thinks in spatial patterns, the martial artist who thinks in responsive sequences.
- Connect to the concept of 'body thinking' (Maxine Sheets-Johnstone): kinesthetic consciousness as a form of intelligence.
- Frame problem-solving through movement: how walking, gesturing, and dancing can generate insights that seated thinking cannot.
- Reference embodied metaphor (Lakoff and Johnson): abstract concepts are grounded in bodily experience of space, force, and movement.
- Integrate Active Inference: movement-based cognition is the generative model operating through motor channels, using bodily simulation to explore possibilities.
- Movement activities should involve movement improvisation as problem-solving, gestural brainstorming, and walking meditation as cognitive practice.
