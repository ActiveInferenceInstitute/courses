## Embodied Cognition Domain Guidelines

This course culminates the curriculum through direct movement experience drawn from dance, yoga, and martial arts traditions. Content must be grounded in the following principles:

- **Movement as primary cognition**: Following Sheets-Johnstone, this course treats movement as the foundation of consciousness, not its byproduct. The body thinks through moving.
- **Sensorimotor coupling**: Drawing on ecological psychology (Gibson) and enactivism, movement and perception are presented as inseparable. You perceive by moving; you move by perceiving.
- **Morphological computation in practice**: The body's physical structure -- joint mobility, muscle elasticity, skeletal geometry -- actively participates in intelligent behavior. Dance, yoga, and martial arts make this visible.
- **Active Inference integration**: Movement practices are framed as laboratories for experiencing active inference: the body generates predictions, encounters prediction error through novel movement, and updates its model through practice.
- **Affordances and skilled action**: The environment is perceived in terms of action possibilities (affordances). Skilled movement practitioners perceive richer affordances than novices, demonstrating how learning transforms the agent-environment relationship.

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding.
2. Frame all concepts through the lens of dance, yoga, martial arts.
3. Include movement lab activities that involve actual bodily engagement (not just reading or writing).
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).
6. Avoid equations, code, or formal notation -- use metaphor, first-person description, and phenomenological language instead.
7. Reference key sources: Sheets-Johnstone (primacy of movement), Gibson (ecological psychology), Noë (action in perception), Pfeifer & Bongard (morphological computation).
