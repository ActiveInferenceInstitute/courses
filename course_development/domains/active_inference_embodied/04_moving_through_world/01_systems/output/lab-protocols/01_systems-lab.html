Lab: Feeling the Boundary of Your Skin

Objective

Experience the system boundary through movement lab that reveals how the body defines and maintains the separation between self and world through the lens of dance, yoga, martial arts.

Prerequisites

  • A quiet, comfortable space for 25 minutes
  • Willingness to engage in movement lab
  • No prior experience required

Part 1: Arriving (5 minutes)

Stand in a comfortable position. Shake out your hands and feet gently. Roll your shoulders. Take three breaths. Begin to sway gently, finding your body's natural rhythm of movement.

Now bring your attention to the boundary of your body. Where does the inside end and the outside begin? Through the lens of dance, yoga, martial arts, notice how this boundary is not static but alive and responsive.

Write your response here

(Describe your initial sense of the body boundary. Where is it most vivid? Where is it most vague?)

Part 2: Inside and Outside (5 minutes)

Alternate your attention between internal sensations (heartbeat, breath, warmth, gut) and external sensations (sounds, air, pressure). Through dance, yoga, martial arts, notice: the boundary between inside and outside is what makes you a system.

Write your response here

(What did you notice when switching between inside and outside? How did the lens of dance, yoga, martial arts change your awareness?)

Part 3: Boundary Perturbation (7 minutes)

Explore what happens when the boundary is disrupted. Apply something cool to your skin, use a tool to tap a surface (feeling through the tool), or shift your attention to where clothing meets skin. In 4E cognition, the system boundary can extend, sharpen, or shift.

Write your response here

(Describe how the boundary changed during each perturbation. Where did you feel the system most strongly?)

Part 4: Integration (5 minutes)

Return to stillness. Through the perspective of dance, yoga, martial arts, reflect on what you have experienced about systems and boundaries.

Write your response here

(What did you learn about systems that you could not learn from reading alone? How does dance, yoga, martial arts illuminate the concept of a system boundary?)
