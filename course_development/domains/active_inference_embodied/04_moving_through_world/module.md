# Unit 04: Moving Through World — Ecological Perception, Affordances, and Sensorimotor Coupling

## Learning Objectives

1. Explain J.J. Gibson's **ecological psychology** and the concept of **affordances** as opportunities for action directly perceived by an embodied agent.
2. Analyze how **sensorimotor contingency theory** (O'Regan and Noe) reframes perception as active, skillful exploration rather than passive information processing.
3. Describe how active inference models the agent-environment coupling that underlies spatial navigation, locomotion, and physical interaction with objects.
4. Develop experiential awareness of your own body as a dynamically coupled system moving through a field of affordances.

## Introduction

Walk across a room. Notice how your body navigates around furniture without conscious calculation, how your hand reaches for a doorknob at exactly the right angle, how your stride lengthens on an open sidewalk and shortens on uneven ground. You are not computing trajectories in some internal coordinate system and then executing motor commands. You are moving through a world that speaks directly to your body in the language of affordances — surfaces that support, objects that invite grasping, paths that beckon forward.

J.J. Gibson revolutionized perception science by insisting that we do not perceive a world of meaningless stimuli that the brain must interpret. We perceive a world already structured by possibilities for action. Alva Noe extended this insight: perception is not something that happens to us but something we do — a skillful activity of sensorimotor exploration. Active inference unifies these ecological and enactive perspectives, showing how the agent's generative model and the environment's statistical structure co-constitute the meaningful world through which we move.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Affordances: The Grammar of Embodied Possibility

Gibson (1979) defined affordances as properties of the environment taken relative to an animal's body and capacities. A horizontal surface at knee height affords sitting for an adult human but stepping-up for a toddler. Affordances are neither purely objective (in the environment) nor purely subjective (in the mind) — they exist in the relation between organism and environment. In active inference, affordances correspond to the **expected free energy** landscape: the set of action policies available to the agent, evaluated by how much each would reduce uncertainty and fulfill preferences.

### 2. Sensorimotor Contingencies and Perceptual Skill

Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noe (2001) proposed that perception is constituted by the organism's mastery of **sensorimotor contingencies** — the lawful regularities that relate actions to their sensory consequences. You perceive the solidity of a cup not by computing its three-dimensional structure from retinal images but by knowing implicitly what would happen if you reached out and grasped it, turned it, lifted it. This practical knowledge is encoded in the generative model as a rich repertoire of action-conditioned predictions. Perception, in this view, is not representation but enacted readiness.

### 3. Navigation as Active Inference

Spatial navigation is among the most fundamental forms of embodied cognition. The hippocampal-entorhinal system — with its place cells, grid cells, and head-direction cells — implements a generative model of the agent's position and orientation in the environment. Navigation consists in minimizing the free energy between the model's predicted location and the desired destination. Path integration, landmark recognition, and route planning are all inference operations: the organism continuously updates its beliefs about where it is and selects motor policies that reduce the distance between its current and preferred states.

### 4. The Extended Body and Tool Use

Andy Clark's **extended mind** thesis proposes that cognitive processes extend beyond the skull into the body and its tools. When a carpenter wields a hammer, the tool becomes a transparent extension of the body's sensorimotor apparatus — force, angle, and impact are felt through the hammer as naturally as through the hand. In active inference terms, tool use involves expanding the generative model's body schema to incorporate the tool's dynamics. The Markov blanket of the agent effectively extends to include the tool's contact surface with the world.

### 5. Environmental Coupling and Maxine Sheets-Johnstone's Primacy of Movement

Sheets-Johnstone (2011) argues that movement is not the execution of prior plans but the primordial medium of cognition itself. We think in movement before we think in words or images. An infant's first cognitive acts are kinesthetic — reaching, grasping, crawling — and all subsequent cognition is built on this motile foundation. Active inference honors this insight: the perception-action loop is not a cycle between two separate processes but a single, continuous flow of embodied inference in which moving and knowing are inseparable.

## Active Inference Connection

Moving through the world is active inference in its most literal sense. The agent's generative model predicts the sensory consequences of each motor policy — the visual flow of locomotion, the proprioceptive feedback of limb position, the haptic contact with surfaces. Action is generated to fulfill these predictions, closing the sensorimotor loop. Free energy is minimized not by sitting still and building better internal models but by actively engaging the environment, testing predictions against reality, and updating beliefs through the continuous stream of embodied experience. The world is not a problem to be solved from the armchair; it is a partner in the dance of inference.

## Experiential Applications

* **Practice — Affordance Walk**: Take a 15-minute walk with the explicit intention of noticing affordances. As you move through your environment, silently name the possibilities each object and surface offers your body: "sittable," "graspable," "climbable," "passable." Notice how affordances shift with your body's state — a bench that affords sitting when you are tired affords stepping-over when you are energized. You are making explicit what your generative model computes implicitly at every moment.

* **Case Study — Rehabilitation After Stroke**: Patients recovering from stroke must rebuild the sensorimotor contingencies that their generative models previously took for granted. A patient relearning to walk must reconstruct, step by step, the predictive models of weight transfer, balance, and ground contact. Constraint-induced movement therapy — which forces use of the affected limb — works by compelling the generative model to update its predictions about the body's current capacities, replacing the outdated (pre-stroke) body schema with one that accurately reflects the recovering body. The functional gains correspond to reductions in free energy: the model's predictions increasingly match the body's actual sensorimotor possibilities.

## Summary

Moving through the world is the paradigmatic expression of embodied active inference. Gibson's affordances, Noe's sensorimotor contingencies, Sheets-Johnstone's primacy of movement, and Clark's extended mind all converge on a single insight: cognition is not an internal process that produces behavior as output but an ongoing, dynamic coupling between a moving body and a structured environment. Active inference provides the mathematical framework for this coupling, showing how the generative model and the environment co-constitute the field of possibility through which the organism navigates. To move is to know; to know is to move. The world is not merely perceived — it is inhabited.
