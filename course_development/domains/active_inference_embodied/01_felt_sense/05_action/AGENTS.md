# Station: Action (Felt Sense)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Body awareness & interoception
- **Topics**: Action as moving from the inside out -- impulse, intention, and motor flow
- **Lab Style**: Somatic Exercise
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module introduces **action** as the body's way of bringing the world into alignment with its predictions. Content must:

- Frame action not as the output of a computational process but as the body's ongoing conversation with its environment. Action and perception are inseparable aspects of the same sensorimotor loop.
- Present the felt experience of motor intention: the subtle impulse that precedes overt movement, the sense of readiness or hesitation.
- Connect to Active Inference: action is the process of changing the external world to reduce prediction error. When you reach for a cup, your body is acting to confirm its prediction of how the cup will feel in your hand.
- Reference Sheets-Johnstone on the primacy of movement: kinesthesia (the felt sense of one's own movement) is the foundation of consciousness.
- Introduce morphological computation: the body's physical structure (joint angles, muscle elasticity, limb length) shapes what actions are possible and how they unfold without requiring explicit computation.
- Discuss affordances (Gibson): the environment offers action possibilities to the body. A doorknob affords grasping; a staircase affords climbing.
- Lab activities should involve actual movement: feeling the impulse before the gesture, exploring flowing versus effortful movement, expressing inner states through the body.
