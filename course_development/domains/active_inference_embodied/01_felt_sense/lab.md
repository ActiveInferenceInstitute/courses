# Lab: Felt Sense -- Somatic Exercise Series

## Objective

Develop foundational body awareness through a progressive somatic exercise that integrates interoception, proprioception, and exteroception. This lab introduces the felt sense as the basis for understanding Active Inference through direct bodily experience.

## Prerequisites

- A quiet space where you can sit or lie down undisturbed for 30 minutes
- Willingness to attend to bodily sensations without judgment
- No prior experience required -- just an open curiosity about your own body

## Part 1: Arriving in the Body (5 minutes)

Sit comfortably or lie down. Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Take three slow breaths, each time letting the exhale be slightly longer than the inhale.

Now, without trying to change anything, simply notice: What is the overall quality of your body right now? Not a list of sensations, but a single, vague, holistic impression. Gendlin called this the "felt sense" -- the body's way of knowing a whole situation at once.

## Part 2: The Boundary Scan (5 minutes)

Slowly bring your attention to the surface of your body -- your skin. Begin at the top of your head and move downward. Notice where your body meets the air, meets clothing, meets the chair or floor. This boundary is your Markov blanket in embodied terms: the living membrane that separates your internal states from the external world.

Notice: Are there places where the boundary feels clear and distinct? Are there places where it feels vague or porous?

## Part 3: Interoceptive Inventory (7 minutes)

Turn your attention inward. Move through the following, spending about a minute on each:

1. **Heartbeat**: Can you feel your heart beating without touching your chest? Where do you feel it?
2. **Breath**: Notice the breath moving in and out. Where does the breath feel most vivid?
3. **Gut**: What is the quality of sensation in your belly? Tight, open, warm, cool, churning, still?
4. **Temperature**: Where are you warmest? Where are you coolest?
5. **Muscular tension**: Where do you notice holding? Where is there softness?

## Part 4: Prediction and Surprise (8 minutes)

This exercise explores how the body generates predictions and registers surprise.

1. Close your eyes. Reach slowly for an object near you (a cup, a book, the arm of a chair). Before your hand arrives, notice: your body already has an expectation of what the surface will feel like.
2. When your hand makes contact, notice: Did the sensation match your expectation? What was the quality of match or mismatch?
3. Repeat with a different object. This time, deliberately reach for something you have not touched recently.

## Discussion

Consider: How does attending to the body change the body? In Active Inference terms, the act of interoceptive attention is itself an action that modifies prediction error. By turning attention inward, you shift the precision weighting of interoceptive signals, making them more salient. This is not merely observation -- it is embodied inference.
