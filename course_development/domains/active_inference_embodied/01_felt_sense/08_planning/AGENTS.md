## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Body awareness & interoception
- **Topics**: Planning as listening to what the body is ready for -- emergent readiness and embodied anticipation
- **Lab Style**: Somatic Exercise
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module introduces **planning** as an embodied process of anticipation and preparation. Content must:

- Frame planning not as abstract deliberation but as the body's preparation for future action. The body plans by shifting weight, adjusting posture, tensing muscles, and altering autonomic tone in anticipation of what comes next.
- Connect to Active Inference: planning is the selection of action policies that are expected to minimize future free energy. In embodied terms, this is the felt sense of what the body is ready for, the direction it is leaning toward.
- Present allostasis as embodied planning: the body predicts its future regulatory needs and adjusts in advance. Hunger before mealtime, alertness before a challenge, and relaxation before sleep are all allostatic planning.
- Reference Sheets-Johnstone on kinesthetic anticipation: the body has a forward-looking quality built into its movement. Every gesture carries an implicit "what comes next."
- Introduce the concept of expected free energy as bodily readiness: the body evaluates possible futures not through calculation but through felt anticipation -- which future feels right, which feels unsafe.
- Discuss how embodied planning draws on the habitual body (Merleau-Ponty): past experience shapes what the body is ready for, creating repertoires of action that unfold without deliberation.
- Lab activities should involve sensing bodily readiness, exploring how the body prepares for different types of action, and trusting emergent movement.
