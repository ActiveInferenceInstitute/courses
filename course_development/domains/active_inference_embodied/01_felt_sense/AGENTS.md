- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential. First-person felt sense. Meditative. Zero equations, zero code.

## Embodied Cognition Domain Guidelines

This course introduces the body as the foundation of all knowing. Content must be grounded in the following embodied cognition principles:

- **Interoception first**: Every module begins with inner-body awareness. Before any concept is introduced, the learner is invited to sense what is happening inside their own body.
- **Felt sense as data**: Following Gendlin's focusing tradition, the vague, holistic bodily sense of a situation is treated as legitimate information, not as noise to be filtered out.
- **4E framing**: All concepts are presented through the lens of 4E cognition -- embodied (shaped by the body), embedded (situated in an environment), enacted (brought forth through activity), and extended (distributed beyond the skin).
- **Active Inference integration**: The Markov blanket is introduced as the skin boundary. Free energy minimization is presented as the body's ongoing effort to stay alive and well-regulated. Prediction error is framed as bodily surprise -- the felt jolt when experience deviates from expectation.
- **Proprioception and interoception**: Every module connects to the senses of body position and internal state. Concepts like allostasis (predictive regulation), somatic markers, and morphological computation are introduced through direct bodily experience.

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding.
2. Frame all concepts through the lens of body awareness and interoception.
3. Include somatic exercise activities that involve actual bodily engagement (not just reading or writing).
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).
6. Avoid equations, code, or formal notation -- use metaphor, first-person description, and phenomenological language instead.
7. Reference key embodied cognition sources: Varela, Thompson, & Rosch; Gallagher; Gendlin; Merleau-Ponty.
