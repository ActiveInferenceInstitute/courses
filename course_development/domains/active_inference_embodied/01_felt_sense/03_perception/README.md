# Module 03: Perception

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## What Does Surprise Feel Like?

Part of **Felt Sense**.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Experience** perception as an active, bodily process rather than passive reception of information
2. **Explore** sensorimotor contingencies: how what you perceive changes with how you move
3. **Feel** prediction error as bodily surprise -- the jolt when experience deviates from expectation
4. **Distinguish** interoceptive perception (inner sensing) from exteroceptive perception (outer sensing) as two modes of embodied inference
5. **Investigate** how attention changes perception by modulating precision weighting of sensory channels
6. **Connect** the enactive tradition (Varela, Thompson, Rosch) to the felt experience of perceiving through the body

## Key Concepts

- **Sensorimotor contingencies**: Perception depends on how sensory stimulation changes with movement (O'Regan & Noë)
- **Prediction error as surprise**: The felt mismatch between what the body expected and what it encountered
- **Interoceptive vs. exteroceptive perception**: Sensing the body's interior versus sensing the external world
- **Enactivism**: Perception is not representation but is enacted through the organism's coupling with the environment
- **Precision weighting**: How attention amplifies certain sensory channels, making them more vivid and influential
- **Embodied surprise**: The bodily registration of novelty through startle, orienting, and autonomic shifts

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Modules 01-02 recommended
- A few small objects of different textures
