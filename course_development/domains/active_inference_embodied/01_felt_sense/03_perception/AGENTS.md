## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Body awareness & interoception
- **Topics**: Perception as embodied prediction -- surprise felt in the body
- **Lab Style**: Somatic Exercise
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module introduces **perception** as an active, bodily process rather than passive reception. Content must:

- Frame perception through sensorimotor contingencies: what you perceive depends on how you move. Turning your head changes what you see; shifting weight changes what you feel through your feet.
- Present prediction error as bodily surprise -- the felt jolt, startle, or disorientation when the world does not match the body's expectation.
- Distinguish interoceptive perception (sensing internal states) from exteroceptive perception (sensing the external world), showing how both are active inference processes.
- Reference enactivism (Varela, Thompson, Rosch): perception is not a representation of a pre-given world but is enacted through the organism's sensorimotor coupling with its environment.
- Connect to Noë's sensorimotor theory: perceptual experience is constituted by the perceiver's implicit understanding of how sensory stimulation varies with movement.
- Introduce the concept of precision weighting through bodily attention: focusing on a sensation makes it more vivid, which in Active Inference terms means increasing the precision of that sensory channel.
- Lab activities should involve actual perceptual exploration -- closing eyes and reaching, attending to peripheral vision, noticing shifts in body sensation.
