# Module 06: Learning

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## How the Body Remembers

Part of **Felt Sense**.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Experience** learning as a bodily process: the accumulation of skill, habit, and sensitivity over time
2. **Compare** the felt sense of practiced versus novel movements to understand prediction error in motor learning
3. **Explore** procedural memory: how the body stores learned patterns that can be retrieved without conscious effort
4. **Connect** embodied learning to Active Inference: updating the generative model so future predictions improve
5. **Investigate** how learning changes perception -- how expertise transforms what the body can sense
6. **Distinguish** habitual body from present body (Merleau-Ponty) and understand how accumulated learning shapes present experience

## Key Concepts

- **Embodied learning**: Skill acquisition as a bodily transformation, not just information storage
- **Practiced vs. novel**: The felt difference between low-error (fluent) and high-error (effortful) movement
- **Procedural memory**: The body's library of learned sensorimotor patterns
- **Allostatic learning**: The body learning to predict its own regulatory needs in advance
- **Habitual body**: Merleau-Ponty's concept of the body as a repository of accumulated skills and dispositions
- **Learning and perception**: How expertise changes what and how we perceive
