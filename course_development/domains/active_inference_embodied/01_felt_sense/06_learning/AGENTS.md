## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Body awareness & interoception
- **Topics**: Learning as bodily memory -- how the body remembers, adapts, and accumulates wisdom
- **Lab Style**: Somatic Exercise
- **Audience**: Anyone seeking qualitative, felt understanding
- **Tone**: Somatic, poetic, experiential

## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module presents **learning** as a process that transforms the body, not just the brain. Content must:

- Frame learning as the body's accumulation of skill, habit, and sensitivity over time. The difference between a novice and an expert is felt in the body: ease, fluency, and reduced effort.
- Connect to Active Inference: learning is the updating of the generative model's parameters so that future predictions are more accurate. In embodied terms, this is the body getting better at anticipating what will happen next.
- Present procedural memory and motor learning as paradigmatic examples of embodied learning: riding a bicycle, playing an instrument, and learning to cook all involve the body acquiring new sensorimotor patterns.
- Reference the distinction between habitual body and present body (Merleau-Ponty): the habitual body carries the accumulated learning of a lifetime; the present body encounters the current moment.
- Introduce allostatic learning: the body learns to predict its own regulatory needs, adjusting in advance of anticipated demands (e.g., heart rate rising before exercise begins).
- Discuss how embodied learning changes perception: a trained musician hears differently; a dancer sees movement differently.
- Lab activities should involve comparing the felt sense of practiced versus unfamiliar movements, noticing how the body stores and retrieves learned patterns.
