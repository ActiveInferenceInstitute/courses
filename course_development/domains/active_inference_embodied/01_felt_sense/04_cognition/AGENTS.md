## Embodied Cognition Content Guidelines

This module presents **cognition** as inseparable from the body. Content must:

- Challenge the traditional view that thinking happens only in the brain. Introduce embodied cognition (Shapiro, Gallagher) as the position that cognitive processes are shaped by the body's morphology, sensory capacities, and motor repertoire.
- Frame somatic markers (Damasio) as the body's contribution to decision-making: gut feelings, chest tightness, and felt ease or unease that guide choices before conscious deliberation.
- Connect to interoceptive inference: the body's internal signals are not noise but are integral to cognitive processing. The gut-brain axis, cardiac signals influencing perception, and breath affecting attention are all examples.
- Reference 4E cognition: cognition is embodied (shaped by the body), embedded (dependent on environmental context), enacted (arising through activity), and extended (scaffolded by tools and social structures).
- Integrate Active Inference: cognition is the process of updating the generative model to minimize prediction error. In embodied terms, this is felt as the resolution of confusion, the settling of uncertainty, the "aha" moment that registers in the body.
- Lab activities should involve noticing how the body participates in thinking -- tracking somatic markers during decisions, feeling how posture affects thought.
