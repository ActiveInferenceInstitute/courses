Lab: Probabilistic World Models for Robots

Objective

Design and analyze a probabilistic world model for a mobile robot performing simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). You will specify a generative model that captures the robot's beliefs about its environment, implement a factor graph representation, and compare world model architectures in terms of their free energy properties.

Prerequisites

Understanding of Bayesian state estimation from Module 03
Familiarity with occupancy grids or landmark-based maps
Basic graph theory concepts (nodes, edges, factors)

Part 1: Generative World Model Design

Design a generative model for a robot exploring an unknown office environment:

State variables: Define the hidden states the robot must infer:
   - Robot pose trajectory: {x0, x1, ..., xT}
   - Landmark positions: {l1, l2, ..., lN}
   - Object categories: {c1, c2, ..., cN} (e.g., door, chair, person)

Generative process: Specify the factored joint distribution:
   - p(xt | x{t-1}, ut): motion model conditioned on control input
   - p(zt | xt, lj): observation model for landmark j
   - p(lj): prior over landmark positions
   - p(c_j): prior over object categories

How does this generative model enable the robot to simultaneously reason about "where am I?" and "what is around me?"

Upper level: Topological map -- rooms, corridors, and connectivity (graph of places).

What are the hidden states?
What is the timescale of updates?
