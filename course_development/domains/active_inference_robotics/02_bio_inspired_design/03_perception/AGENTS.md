## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biomimicry & neural architectures
- **Topics**: Bio-inspired sensing, predictive coding, active sensing, foveation, biological sensor organs
- **Lab Style**: Design Challenge
- **Audience**: Robotics engineers and researchers
- **Tone**: Engineering-focused, connecting biological inspiration for robotic design to Active Inference

## Content Guidelines

All content in this module must:

1. Frame biological perception as hierarchical predictive coding, where each processing level predicts the activity of the level below and passes prediction errors upward.
2. Compare biological sensory organs (retina, cochlea, mechanoreceptors) with robotic sensors to identify design principles that improve artificial perception.
3. Emphasize active sensing: biological organisms control their sensors (saccades, whisking, sniffing) to maximize information gain, a direct implementation of Active Inference.
4. Use concrete neuroanatomical examples: retinal ganglion cells, superior colliculus, primary visual cortex as a predictive processing hierarchy.
5. Connect biological sensor adaptation (dark adaptation, habituation, sensitization) to precision modulation in Active Inference.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Sensorimotor loops**: Frame perception as part of the continuous perception-action cycle where the robot generates predictions, computes prediction errors, and updates beliefs or actions to minimize free energy.
- **Proprioceptive inference**: Connect perception to the robot's self-model -- its understanding of its own body, capabilities, and limitations as maintained through proprioceptive prediction errors.
- **Motor commands as predictions**: Relate perception to the Active Inference principle that motor commands are predictions about desired sensory outcomes, selected to minimize expected free energy.
