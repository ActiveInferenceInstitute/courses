# Lab: Biological Agency and Embodied Robot Design

## Objective

Investigate how biological agents achieve autonomy through embodied cognition and morphological computation. You will analyze animal behavior as Active Inference, design a robot that exploits body-environment coupling, and compare biological and artificial agency.

## Prerequisites

- Completion of the corresponding module.md lecture material
- Familiarity with Active Inference concepts (generative models, free energy, prediction errors)
- Basic pseudocode and diagram skills

## Part 1: Animal Behavior as Active Inference

Select an animal behavior (e.g., prey capture in frogs, web building in spiders, nest construction in termites) and analyze it as Active Inference:

1. What is the animal's generative model of its environment during this behavior?
2. What prior preferences drive the behavior (desired sensory outcomes)?
3. How do prediction errors guide the behavior in real time?
4. Where does morphological computation reduce the cognitive load?

## Part 2: Soft Robotics and Embodiment

Design a soft robotic gripper inspired by biological grasping mechanisms:

1. Compare a rigid parallel-jaw gripper with a compliant, bio-inspired gripper (e.g., inspired by elephant trunk, octopus tentacle, or human hand).
2. How does material compliance serve as morphological computation, reducing the generative model's complexity?
3. What sensing modalities does the bio-inspired gripper need (tactile, proprioceptive, force)?

## Part 3: Umwelt Analysis

The umwelt is an organism's subjective sensory world. Analyze umwelts for robotic design:

1. Describe the umwelt of a bat (echolocation), a pit viper (infrared sensing), and a star-nosed mole (touch).
2. For each, how does the umwelt constrain the organism's generative model?
3. Design a robot with an unconventional umwelt (e.g., primarily magnetic or vibrational sensing). How does this shape its agency?
