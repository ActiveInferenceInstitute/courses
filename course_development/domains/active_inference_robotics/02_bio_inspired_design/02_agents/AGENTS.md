# Station: Agents (Bio-Inspired Design)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biomimicry & neural architectures
- **Topics**: Biological agency, embodied cognition, umwelt, morphological computation, soft robotics
- **Lab Style**: Design Challenge
- **Audience**: Robotics engineers and researchers
- **Tone**: Engineering-focused, connecting biological inspiration for robotic design to Active Inference

## Content Guidelines

All content in this module must:

1. Analyze biological agents (animals, insects, microorganisms) as Active Inference systems that minimize free energy through embodied behavior.
2. Emphasize morphological computation: how body shape, material properties, and biomechanics offload cognitive processing.
3. Introduce the umwelt concept -- the organism's subjective sensory world -- as a design principle for robotic perception.
4. Use soft robotics as a concrete example of bio-inspired embodied agency where material compliance replaces computational control.
5. Connect biological autonomy (homeostasis, allostasis, metabolism) to robotic self-maintenance and energy management.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Sensorimotor loops**: Frame agents as part of the continuous perception-action cycle where the robot generates predictions, computes prediction errors, and updates beliefs or actions to minimize free energy.
- **Proprioceptive inference**: Connect agents to the robot's self-model -- its understanding of its own body, capabilities, and limitations as maintained through proprioceptive prediction errors.
- **Motor commands as predictions**: Relate agents to the Active Inference principle that motor commands are predictions about desired sensory outcomes, selected to minimize expected free energy.
