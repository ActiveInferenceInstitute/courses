# Module 01: Systems -- Biological System Architectures for Robots

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Overview

This module covers **systems** in the context of **Bio-Inspired Design**. It applies Active Inference principles to biological inspiration for robotic design, examining how systems operates within robotic systems from the perspective of biomimicry & neural architectures.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Analyze** biological organisms as Active Inference systems with well-defined Markov blanket boundaries.
2. **Map** biological sensory and motor systems to robotic sensor and actuator equivalents.
3. **Identify** design principles from biological system architectures that transfer to robotic hardware.
4. **Compare** centralized (brain-centric) vs. distributed (ganglia-based) computational architectures in biology and robotics.
5. **Evaluate** scaling laws that constrain bio-inspired robotic designs at different size scales.
6. **Design** a bio-inspired robotic system architecture based on a specific biological organism.

## Key Concepts

- Biological organisms as Active Inference systems
- Biomimetic sensor and actuator design
- Centralized vs. distributed neural architectures
- Scaling laws in biology and robotics
- Evolutionary optimization as free energy minimization

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with Active Inference fundamentals (generative models, free energy, prediction errors)
- Completion of prior modules in this course sequence
- Basic robotics background (biomimicry & neural architectures)
