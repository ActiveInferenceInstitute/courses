# Station: Communication (Bio-Inspired Design)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biomimicry & neural architectures
- **Topics**: Swarm intelligence, stigmergy, pheromone communication, collective behavior, flocking, biological signaling
- **Lab Style**: Design Challenge
- **Audience**: Robotics engineers and researchers
- **Tone**: Engineering-focused, connecting biological inspiration for robotic design to Active Inference

## Content Guidelines

All content in this module must:

1. Frame biological collective behavior (ant colonies, bee swarms, fish schools, bird flocks) as distributed Active Inference where each individual minimizes its own free energy while contributing to collective coordination.
2. Present stigmergic communication (pheromone trails, nest modifications) as an indirect form of generative model alignment through environment modification.
3. Connect specific biological communication mechanisms (waggle dance, alarm pheromones, schooling rules) to robotic multi-agent protocols.
4. Emphasize that biological swarms achieve complex collective behavior from simple local rules -- a design principle for scalable robotic swarms.
5. Address the trade-off between individual autonomy and collective coordination in biological and robotic systems.

## Active Inference Integration

- **Sensorimotor loops**: Frame communication as part of the continuous perception-action cycle where the robot generates predictions, computes prediction errors, and updates beliefs or actions to minimize free energy.
- **Proprioceptive inference**: Connect communication to the robot's self-model -- its understanding of its own body, capabilities, and limitations as maintained through proprioceptive prediction errors.
- **Motor commands as predictions**: Relate communication to the Active Inference principle that motor commands are predictions about desired sensory outcomes, selected to minimize expected free energy.
