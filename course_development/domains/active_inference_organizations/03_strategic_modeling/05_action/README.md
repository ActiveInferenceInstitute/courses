## Learning Objectives

1. Frame competitive moves as policy execution in a multi-agent competitive environment
2. Evaluate first-mover advantage vs. fast-follower strategy as exploration-exploitation trade-offs
3. Model competitive dynamics — action-reaction sequences and competitive spirals
4. Apply strategic timing principles — when uncertainty favors action vs. waiting
5. Understand strategic commitment as irreversible signaling that shapes competitor behavior

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Competitive moves**: Pricing, market entry, product launches, and M&A as policies
- **First-mover vs. fast-follower**: Who moves first and why
- **Competitive spirals**: Escalating action-reaction dynamics between rivals
- **Strategic timing**: Acting when the environment is ready, not just when the firm is ready
- **Commitment devices**: Irreversible investments that credibly signal strategic intent

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Sections 1-2 (Organizational Systems and Collective Intelligence)
- Familiarity with basic strategy concepts (competitive advantage, market analysis)
