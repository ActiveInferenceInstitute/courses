Part A: Multiple Choice

  1. A "strategic inflection point" (Grove) is: A) A turning point in stock price B) A moment when the assumptions underlying a firm's strategy are invalidated C) An intersection of strategies D) A meeting point
  2. Assumption-based planning involves: A) Making assumptions B) Surfacing, classifying, and monitoring the assumptions underlying strategy C) Trusting assumptions D) Eliminating assumptions
  3. The planning fallacy causes organizations to: A) Plan too much B) Systematically underestimate time, cost, and risk of strategic initiatives C) Avoid planning D) Plan perfectly
  4. A Red Team exercise involves: A) Team building B) Deliberately stress-testing strategy by assigning a team to find weaknesses C) Competitive analysis D) Military exercises
  5. The narrative fallacy means: A) Stories are useful B) Constructing coherent stories to explain outcomes that were driven by chance, leading to false causal reasoning C) Marketing narratives D) Brand storytelling
  6. Reference class forecasting improves predictions by: A) Creating references B) Comparing the current decision to a class of similar past decisions for more accurate estimation C) Using classrooms D) Academic research
  7. Overconfidence is dangerous in strategy because: A) It motivates people B) It leads organizations to underestimate uncertainty and overcommit to strategic bets C) It builds culture D) It reduces analysis

Part B: Short Analysis

  1. Your organization plans to enter a new market with an estimated investment of $50M over 3 years. Apply reference class forecasting: what similar initiatives exist? What were their actual costs and timelines? How should this adjust your estimate?
  2. Design a Red Team exercise for your organization's current strategy. Specify: team composition, scope, methodology, and how findings will be integrated.
  3. Your organization's strategy failed but the executive team explains it with a compelling narrative ("the market shifted unexpectedly"). Apply the narrative fallacy concept: what alternative explanations should be considered? How would you distinguish genuine causal reasoning from post-hoc rationalization?
