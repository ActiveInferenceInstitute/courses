## Learning Objectives

1. Frame competitive intelligence as sensory inference about competitor and market hidden states
2. Detect weak signals — early indicators of strategic change before they become obvious trends
3. Account for strategic deception — competitors deliberately sending misleading signals
4. Build strategic early warning systems for systematic competitive monitoring
5. Evaluate precision allocation — which competitive signals does the organization trust?

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Competitive intelligence**: Inferring competitor strategies from indirect signals
- **Weak signals**: Early indicators of disruption or strategic change
- **Strategic deception**: Competitors manipulating the signals you observe
- **Early warning systems**: Systematic competitive environment monitoring
- **Strategic attention**: Which competitive variables receive organizational focus

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Sections 1-2 (Organizational Systems and Collective Intelligence)
- Familiarity with basic strategy concepts (competitive advantage, market analysis)

## Recommended Case Studies

- **Intel's competitive intelligence system** — systematic monitoring of semiconductor industry dynamics
- **Kodak's failure to detect digital disruption** — weak signal blindness
- **Amazon's competitive signaling** — using announcements to shape competitor behavior
