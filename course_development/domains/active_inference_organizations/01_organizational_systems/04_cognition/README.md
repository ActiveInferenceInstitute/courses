## Organizational Mental Models and Sensemaking

Part of **Section 1: Organizational Systems**.

Organizations think through distributed processes of sensemaking — forming collective beliefs, updating strategic assumptions, and constructing mental models of the business environment. This module examines the organization's generative model (the set of beliefs driving decisions), how sensemaking maps to posterior inference, and why cognitive pathologies like groupthink and belief rigidity emerge when the inference process breaks down.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Explain** organizational cognition as belief updating under the generative model
2. **Map** the strategic assumptions (priors) that drive your organization's key decisions
3. **Understand** sensemaking (Weick) as the organizational process of posterior inference
4. **Diagnose** cognitive pathologies: groupthink, belief rigidity, sunk cost trap, confirmation bias
5. **Apply** assumption auditing to reveal hidden beliefs shaping strategy

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Organizational generative model**: Customer beliefs, market beliefs, competitive beliefs, capability beliefs
- **Sensemaking**: Weick's framework as organizational posterior inference
- **Strategic priors**: Deep assumptions about the business that resist updating
- **Cognitive pathologies**: Groupthink, rigidity, sunk costs, anchoring
- **Assumption auditing**: Structured process for testing implicit beliefs

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Modules 01-03 (Systems, Agents, Perception)
- Experience with strategic planning processes or organizational decision-making
