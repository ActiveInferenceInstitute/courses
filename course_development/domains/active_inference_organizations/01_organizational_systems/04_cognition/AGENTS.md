# Station: Cognition (Organizational Systems)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Organizations as belief-forming entities that build and maintain generative models of their environment
- **Topics**: Organizational mental models, sensemaking, strategic assumptions (priors), belief updating, cognitive pathologies
- **Lab Style**: Case study — assumption auditing and cognitive diagnosis
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines how organizations think — forming collective beliefs, updating strategic assumptions, and constructing mental models of the business environment. All content should:

1. **Frame organizational cognition as posterior inference**: Updating beliefs about hidden states given new observations, using Weick's sensemaking as the organizational translation
2. **Make the generative model tangible**: Customer beliefs, market beliefs, competitive beliefs, capability beliefs, and causal beliefs that drive strategic decisions
3. **Treat strategic assumptions as priors**: Beliefs held before new evidence arrives, with strength (precision) determining how much evidence is needed to change them
4. **Diagnose cognitive pathologies**: Groupthink, belief rigidity, sunk cost trap, confirmation bias, and anchoring as specific Active Inference failure modes
5. **Introduce assumption auditing**: A structured process for making implicit beliefs explicit and systematically testing them

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Generative model**: The set of beliefs driving organizational decisions
- **Sensemaking**: Organizational posterior inference — interpreting ambiguous signals
- **Priors**: Strategic assumptions held before new evidence arrives
- **Posterior inference**: Updating beliefs based on new observations
- **Cognitive pathologies**: Groupthink, rigidity, sunk costs as inference failures
