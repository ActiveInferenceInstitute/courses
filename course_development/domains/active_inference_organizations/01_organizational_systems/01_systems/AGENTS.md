# Station: Systems (Organizational Systems)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Firms as adaptive systems defined by their boundaries
- **Topics**: Markov blankets, internal/external states, organizational homeostasis, nested systems
- **Lab Style**: Case study — boundary mapping and organizational diagnosis
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module introduces the foundational concept: an organization is a **system** defined by its Markov blanket. All content should:

1. **Ground the Markov blanket concept** in observable organizational boundaries — legal entities, information firewalls, cultural norms, process boundaries
2. **Use the organizational Markov blanket** as the primary analytical lens: what crosses the boundary inward (sensory states like market data, customer feedback) and outward (active states like products, communications, hiring decisions)
3. **Connect homeostasis** to real business metrics: cash flow, talent retention, customer satisfaction, and innovation rate as variables that must stay within viable bounds
4. **Emphasize nested systems**: individuals within teams, teams within departments, departments within the enterprise — each with its own Markov blanket
5. **Draw on diverse organizational examples**: manufacturing firms, hospitals, tech companies, nonprofits, government agencies

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Organizational Markov blanket**: The information interface between the firm and its environment
- **Internal states**: Culture, tacit knowledge, capabilities, strategic assumptions
- **External states**: Markets, competitors, regulators, macroeconomic forces
- **Homeostasis**: Maintaining critical business variables within viable bounds
- **Nested blankets**: Multi-level organizational structure as hierarchical inference
