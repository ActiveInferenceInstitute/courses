## Organizations as Living Systems

Part of **Section 1: Organizational Systems**.

This module introduces the foundational concept of the course: every organization is a system defined by its boundary (Markov blanket), maintaining internal states through regulated exchange with an external environment. You will learn to see organizations not as static structures but as dynamic, adaptive entities that must continuously manage the flow of information, resources, and influence across their boundaries.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Define** what makes an organization a system in the Active Inference framework
2. **Map** the Markov blanket of a real organization — identifying sensory states, active states, internal states, and external states
3. **Identify** organizational homeostatic variables (cash flow, talent, customer trust) and their viable bounds
4. **Analyze** how firms maintain stability through regulated boundary exchange
5. **Explain** the nested structure of organizational systems (teams within divisions within the enterprise)

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Organizational boundary** as a Markov blanket (information interface between firm and environment)
- **Internal states**: culture, tacit knowledge, routines, capabilities
- **External states**: market forces, competitive dynamics, regulatory environment
- **Homeostasis**: keeping critical variables within viable bounds
- **Nested systems**: hierarchical structure where each level has its own blanket

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with basic organizational structure (departments, reporting lines, functional areas)
- No prior knowledge of Active Inference required — this module builds from first principles

## Recommended Case Studies

- **Toyota Production System** — culture and routines as internal states
- **Regional banking** — sensing customer creditworthiness through observable signals
- **Startup vs. established firm** — contrasting boundary permeability and homeostatic flexibility
