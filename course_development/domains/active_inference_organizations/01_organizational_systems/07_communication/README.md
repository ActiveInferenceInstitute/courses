## Internal Communication and Organizational Culture

Part of **Section 1: Organizational Systems**.

Communication is the nervous system of the organization. This module examines how agents within the organization share and align their generative models — exchanging signals that enable coordinated action. You will learn to analyze information architecture, identify communication failures as model misalignment, and design communication systems that reduce organizational free energy.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Understand** organizational communication as model alignment — sharing beliefs and predictions across agents
2. **Analyze** information architecture: reporting structures, meeting cadences, and data flows
3. **Identify** communication failures as model misalignment between organizational subunits
4. **Apply** the concept of common ground as shared priors enabling coordination
5. **Design** communication systems that reduce organizational prediction error

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Model sharing**: Communication as aligning generative models across agents
- **Information architecture**: Formal and informal channels for organizational data flow
- **Common ground**: Shared language, context, goals, and norms as coordination enablers
- **Communication pathologies**: Silos, hoarding, mixed messages, overload, lost-in-translation
- **Boundary objects**: Dashboards, prototypes, and documents that bridge specialist perspectives

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Modules 01-06 (Systems through Learning)
- Familiarity with organizational meeting structures, reporting lines, and collaboration tools

## Recommended Case Studies

- **Spotify's squad model** — multi-scale information architecture with squads, tribes, chapters, and guilds
- **NASA Columbia disaster** — signal distortion where frontline concerns were filtered before reaching leadership
- **Bridgewater Associates** — radical transparency as an extreme approach to organizational model alignment
