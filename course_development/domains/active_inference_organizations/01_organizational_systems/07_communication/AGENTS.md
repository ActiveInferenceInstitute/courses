## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Communication as the process of aligning generative models across organizational agents
- **Topics**: Information architecture, model sharing, common ground, communication failures, boundary objects
- **Lab Style**: Case study — communication flow analysis and information architecture audit
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines communication as the organizational nervous system — how agents within the organization share and align their generative models to enable coordinated action. All content should:

1. **Frame communication as model alignment**: Effective communication means ensuring compatible beliefs, consistent goals, and coordinated action plans across organizational agents
2. **Map information architecture**: Formal channels (org chart, reporting lines, meeting cadences, dashboards) and informal channels (conversations, Slack, mentorship) as the structural design of model sharing
3. **Build common ground**: Shared language, context, goals, and norms as the shared priors that reduce coordination costs
4. **Diagnose communication failures as model misalignment**: Silos, information hoarding, mixed messages, overload, and "lost in translation" as specific failures of the model-sharing process
5. **Design effective communication systems**: Matching cadence to decisions, creating bidirectional channels, building common ground, reducing noise, using boundary objects

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Model alignment**: Sharing and synchronizing generative models across agents
- **Information architecture**: The structural design of information flows
- **Common ground**: Shared priors that reduce coordination costs
- **Communication failures**: Model misalignment between organizational subunits
- **Boundary objects**: Artifacts that bridge different specialist model domains
