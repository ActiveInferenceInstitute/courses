## Market Sensing and Organizational Perception

Part of **Section 1: Organizational Systems**.

How does an organization know what is happening in its environment? This module examines organizational perception as sensory inference — the process of inferring hidden market states (customer needs, competitor strategies, regulatory direction) from noisy, ambiguous signals that cross the organizational Markov blanket. You will learn to audit your organization's sensing channels, diagnose perceptual pathologies, and design improved sensing mechanisms.

## Learning Objectives

1. **Explain** organizational perception as sensory inference — inferring hidden states from noisy observations
2. **Identify** the organization's sensory channels and evaluate their coverage, reliability, and timeliness
3. **Apply** the concept of precision to understand why some signals are weighted heavily and others ignored
4. **Diagnose** perceptual pathologies: blind spots, signal distortion, confirmation bias, information overload
5. **Design** improved sensing mechanisms and active sensing programs for your organization

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **Perception as inference**: Organizations infer market realities from imperfect data
- **Sensory channels**: Financial reports, customer contact, market research, social media, competitive intelligence
- **Precision weighting**: Organizational culture and incentives determine which signals get trusted
- **Perceptual pathologies**: Blind spots, confirmation bias, distortion, overload, lag
- **Active sensing**: Experiments, pilots, and research as epistemic actions

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Modules 01-02 (Systems and Agents) or equivalent understanding
- Familiarity with organizational information flows (reports, dashboards, meetings)

## Recommended Case Studies

- **Kodak** — blind spots in sensing digital disruption due to optimized-for-film sensing channels
- **Blockbuster vs. Netflix** — confirmation bias in interpreting competitive signals
- **Johnson & Johnson Tylenol crisis** — rapid sensing and response to external threat
