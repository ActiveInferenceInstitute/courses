# Station: Perception (Organizational Systems)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Organizations as inference engines that sense their environment through imperfect channels
- **Topics**: Market sensing, environmental scanning, precision weighting, perceptual pathologies, active sensing
- **Lab Style**: Case study — perceptual audit and sensing gap analysis
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines how organizations perceive their environment — inferring hidden states (customer needs, competitor intentions, market dynamics) from noisy observable signals. All content should:

1. **Frame organizational perception as inference**: The organization never directly observes the "true" state of the market — only signals filtered through its sensing mechanisms
2. **Map sensory channels**: Financial reports, customer contacts, market research, social media, competitive intelligence, internal sensors — each with different speed, richness, and bias characteristics
3. **Explain precision as organizational attention**: Which signals the organization trusts and weights heavily, driven by culture and incentive structure
4. **Diagnose perceptual pathologies**: Blind spots, confirmation bias, signal distortion, information overload, and lag
5. **Introduce active sensing**: Organizations do not just passively receive signals — they conduct experiments, run pilots, and commission research to reduce uncertainty

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Sensory inference**: Inferring hidden environmental states from noisy observations
- **Sensory channels**: The mechanisms through which organizational perception occurs
- **Precision**: The weight assigned to different signals — what the organization "trusts"
- **Perceptual pathologies**: Systematic failures in organizational sensing
- **Epistemic action**: Active sensing to reduce uncertainty about the environment
