# Station: Action (Organizational Systems)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Organizations as active inference agents that shape their environment through strategic execution
- **Topics**: Strategy execution, pragmatic vs. epistemic action, policy selection, exploitation-exploration trade-off
- **Lab Style**: Case study — action portfolio analysis and execution diagnosis
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines how organizations act — deploying resources, launching products, executing strategies, and shaping their environment. Under Active Inference, action is the process of changing the external world to match the organization's predictions. All content should:

1. **Frame strategy execution as active inference**: The organization changes its environment to reduce prediction error — making the world match its generative model's expectations
2. **Distinguish pragmatic from epistemic action**: Exploitation (deploying known strategies for predicted returns) versus exploration (generating information to reduce uncertainty)
3. **Apply expected free energy to policy selection**: Strategic options evaluated on both goal achievement (pragmatic value) and learning (epistemic value)
4. **Diagnose execution failures as inference failures**: Wrong model, insufficient sensing, over-commitment, coordination failure, and timing error
5. **Address the exploitation-exploration trade-off**: The central strategic tension — allocating a portfolio across known strategies and experimental ventures

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Active inference**: Changing the environment to match predictions (strategy execution)
- **Pragmatic action**: Goal-directed exploitation of known strategies
- **Epistemic action**: Information-seeking exploration to reduce uncertainty
- **Policy selection**: Choosing among strategic options using expected free energy
- **Expected free energy**: Pragmatic value + epistemic value as the selection criterion
