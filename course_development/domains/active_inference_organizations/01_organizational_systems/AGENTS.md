## Domain Guidelines

This course establishes the foundational perspective: organizations are living adaptive systems that maintain themselves through information exchange with their environment. Every concept in this section should be grounded in the metaphor of the **organizational Markov blanket** — the boundary that separates a firm's internal states (culture, knowledge, routines) from external states (markets, competitors, regulation).

### Key Organizational Active Inference Concepts

- **Organizational Markov blanket**: The information boundary that defines what is "inside" the firm (proprietary knowledge, culture, capabilities) versus "outside" (market forces, competitor actions, regulatory changes)
- **Institutional prediction error**: The gap between what the organization expects (based on its generative model) and what actually occurs in its environment — manifest as missed forecasts, strategic surprises, and performance gaps
- **Collective free energy minimization**: How the organization as a whole reduces surprise by coordinating the sensing, cognition, and action of its subunits toward shared preferred states (profitability, growth, mission fulfillment)
- **Nested inference**: Teams within departments within divisions — each level with its own blanket, model, and inference dynamics

### Integration Principles

1. Always ground formal Active Inference concepts in observable organizational phenomena (budgets, org charts, strategic plans, KPIs)
2. Use real-world case studies from diverse organizational types — corporations, startups, nonprofits, government agencies
3. Frame organizational pathologies (silos, groupthink, strategic drift) as specific failures of the inference process
4. Maintain the nested-systems perspective: individual, team, department, enterprise

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for business leaders, managers, and organizational scientists.
2. Frame all concepts through the lens of firms as adaptive systems that minimize free energy.
3. Include case study activities drawn from real or realistic organizational scenarios.
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).
6. Connect each module to the others in the 8-part Active Inference spine (systems, agents, perception, cognition, action, learning, communication, planning).
7. Avoid purely academic abstractions — every concept should answer "So what does this mean for how I run my organization?"
