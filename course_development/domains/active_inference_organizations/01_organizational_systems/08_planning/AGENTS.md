## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Planning as policy selection under uncertainty using expected free energy
- **Topics**: Strategic planning, temporal depth, scenario planning, organizational resilience, commitment-adaptability
- **Lab Style**: Case study — scenario planning exercise and resilience assessment
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional business/management — strategic and evidence-based

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines how organizations plan for an uncertain future. Under Active Inference, planning is policy selection — choosing sequences of actions that minimize expected free energy over extended time horizons. All content should:

1. **Frame strategic planning as policy selection**: Not forecasting a single future, but evaluating how well different strategies perform across multiple possible futures
2. **Apply temporal depth**: How far into the future the organization's generative model extends — milliseconds for trading firms, decades for energy infrastructure
3. **Connect scenario planning to multi-model evaluation**: Shell-style scenarios as constructing multiple generative models and evaluating policies against each
4. **Build organizational resilience**: Absorptive capacity (withstanding shock), adaptive capacity (modifying structure), and transformative capacity (fundamental identity change)
5. **Address the commitment-adaptability paradox**: Deep investment in specific directions versus maintaining strategic flexibility

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Policy selection**: Choosing action sequences that minimize expected free energy
- **Temporal depth**: How far into the future the generative model extends
- **Scenario planning**: Multi-model evaluation for robust strategy
- **Resilience**: Capacity to absorb surprise while maintaining organizational identity
- **Expected free energy**: Balancing pragmatic value and epistemic value across time
