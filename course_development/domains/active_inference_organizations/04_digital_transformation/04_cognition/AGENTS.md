## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Machine learning and AI as new forms of organizational cognition — with distinct strengths, weaknesses, and failure modes
- **Topics**: ML models, algorithmic bias, explainability, human-in-the-loop, hybrid cognition
- **Lab Style**: Implementation plan — technology assessment, architecture design, migration planning
- **Audience**: Business leaders, CTOs, digital transformation leaders, organizational scientists
- **Tone**: Professional technology/business — connecting Active Inference to digital strategy and implementation

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module covers Algorithmic Decision-Making. All content should:

1. Frame ML models as organizational generative models — pattern-learning systems that make predictions and drive decisions
2. Address algorithmic bias as model bias: when the training data or model structure encodes systematic errors
3. Design human-in-the-loop systems: human judgment overseeing and correcting algorithmic predictions
4. Apply explainability as model transparency: can the organization understand why the algorithm made a specific prediction?
5. Distinguish where algorithmic cognition excels (high-volume, pattern-rich, well-defined) vs. where human cognition is essential (novel, ambiguous, ethical)

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **ML as generative model**: Machine learning systems as organizational pattern-recognition and prediction engines
- **Algorithmic bias**: Systematic errors encoded in training data or model structure
- **Human-in-the-loop**: Human oversight and correction of algorithmic predictions
- **Explainability**: The organization's ability to understand algorithmic reasoning
- **Hybrid cognition**: Combining algorithmic pattern-recognition with human judgment
