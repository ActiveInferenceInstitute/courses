## Learning Objectives

1. Frame ML models as a new form of organizational generative model for prediction and decision-making
2. Diagnose algorithmic bias as systematic model error encoded in training data or architecture
3. Design human-in-the-loop systems where human judgment oversees algorithmic predictions
4. Apply explainability requirements — understanding why algorithms make specific predictions
5. Identify where algorithmic cognition excels and where human cognition remains essential

## Key Organizational Concepts

- **ML as organizational cognition**: Pattern-recognition and prediction at machine scale
- **Algorithmic bias**: Systematic errors in ML models affecting organizational decisions
- **Human-in-the-loop**: Human oversight of algorithmic reasoning
- **Explainability**: Understanding and auditing algorithmic decision-making
- **Hybrid cognition**: Combining machine pattern-recognition with human judgment and ethics

## Prerequisites

- Completion of Sections 1-3 (Organizational Systems, Collective Intelligence, Strategic Modeling)
- Basic familiarity with digital technology concepts (APIs, databases, cloud computing, machine learning)

## Recommended Case Studies

- **Amazon's hiring algorithm** — algorithmic bias encoding gender discrimination
- **Radiology AI** — augmenting physician cognition with pattern recognition
- **Credit scoring** — decades of algorithmic decision-making with bias and fairness debates
