## Domain Guidelines

This course applies Active Inference to digital transformation — examining how technology reshapes organizations from purely human systems into sociotechnical systems where human agents, AI agents, algorithms, and data infrastructure form an integrated whole. The central insight is that digital transformation is not technology adoption — it is **organizational system redesign** where the Markov blanket itself is partially composed of technical infrastructure.

### Key Organizational Active Inference Concepts

- **Sociotechnical system**: The organization as an integrated ecology of human and technological agents, where organizational design and technology architecture are the same problem
- **Digital Markov blanket**: APIs, databases, algorithms, and interfaces as components of the organizational boundary — mediating all interaction between inside and outside
- **AI as organizational agent**: Machine learning systems, automation, and algorithmic decision-making as a new class of organizational agent with distinct capabilities and failure modes
- **Data flywheel**: Self-reinforcing cycles where more usage generates more data, which improves models, which creates more value, which attracts more usage
- **Human-AI complementarity**: Designing systems where human judgment (novel, ambiguous, ethical) and machine capability (high-volume, pattern-rich, fast) complement each other

### Integration Principles

1. Always connect digital transformation to the organizational foundations in Sections 1-3 — technology is a tool for organizational adaptation, not an end in itself
2. Use implementation-focused exercises: technology assessments, architecture decisions, migration plans, change management strategies
3. Address both the promise and peril of digital transformation: automation bias, algorithmic fairness, skill atrophy, digital divide
4. Draw on examples across digital maturity levels: digital-native companies, transforming incumbents, and organizations just beginning their digital journey
