# FAQ: Active Inference for Organizations

> Frequently asked questions for business leaders, managers, and organizational scientists.

---

## What is Active Inference?

Active Inference is a framework from computational neuroscience proposing that all adaptive systems — from single cells to entire organizations — operate by maintaining an internal model of their environment and continuously acting to minimize the gap between their predictions and actual experience. For organizations, this means every department, team, and strategic process can be understood as part of a system that senses its environment, forms expectations, acts on those expectations, and learns from the results.

## How is Active Inference different from other organizational theories?

Most organizational theories describe *specific aspects* of how firms work — e.g., resource-based view (assets), transaction cost economics (boundaries), or behavioral theory (decision-making). Active Inference provides a **unifying principle**: all of these aspects can be derived from a single imperative — minimizing free energy. It doesn't replace existing theories; it offers a common mathematical language that connects them.

## Do I need a math or science background?

No. This curriculum is designed for business professionals. We use **light conceptual notation** — diagrams, tables, and organizational analogies — rather than dense mathematical formalism. Where formal concepts appear, they are always introduced through a business parallel first (e.g., "Strategic surprise" before "prediction error").

## Who is this curriculum for?

This curriculum is designed for **business leaders, managers, consultants, and organizational scientists** who want a principled framework for understanding organizational adaptation, strategy, and collective intelligence. It is particularly valuable for:

- **Executives** seeking a unified lens for strategic planning
- **HR/Culture leaders** working on team alignment and organizational learning
- **Strategy consultants** looking for rigorous scenario planning frameworks
- **IT/Digital leaders** managing AI integration and digital transformation

## What are the 8 modules about?

Each course covers the same 8 foundational topics in Active Inference, translated for organizational contexts:

1. **Systems** — What defines an organization's boundaries and identity?
2. **Agents** — Who are the actors within the system and what drives them?
3. **Perception** — How does the organization sense its market and environment?
4. **Cognition** — How does it form beliefs, mental models, and strategic assumptions?
5. **Action** — How does it execute strategy and implement decisions?
6. **Learning** — How does it adapt, improve, and manage knowledge?
7. **Communication** — How do teams share information and align their models?
8. **Planning** — How does it anticipate the future and manage uncertainty?

## What makes each course different?

All 4 courses follow the same 8-topic structure, but each takes a distinct perspective:

| Course | Lens | You'll Learn To... |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| Organizational Systems | Structure & boundaries | See your org as an adaptive inference engine |
| Collective Intelligence | Teams & culture | Design teams that build shared mental models |
| Strategic Modeling | Planning & risk | Use expected free energy for strategic decisions |
| Digital Transformation | AI & data | Integrate AI systems with human organizational intelligence |

## Can I apply this immediately?

Yes. Every module includes practical exercises — case studies, workshops, strategy exercises, or implementation plans — designed so you can apply the concepts the following week. The curriculum emphasizes actionable tools and frameworks, not just theory.

## How does this relate to AI and machine learning?

Active Inference is the theoretical framework that unifies biological intelligence and artificial intelligence. Course 4 (Digital Transformation) explicitly addresses how AI systems can be understood as active inference agents operating alongside human teams. But the framework applies to any organization, whether or not it uses AI tools.
