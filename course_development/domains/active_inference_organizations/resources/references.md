# References: Active Inference for Organizations

> Recommended reading and resources for business leaders, managers, and organizational scientists.
> Organized by category: foundational Active Inference, organizational theory, and applied intersections.

## Active Inference & Free Energy Principle — Foundations

1. Friston, K. (2010). The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory? *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 11(2), 127–138.
2. Parr, T., Pezzulo, G., & Friston, K. J. (2022). *Active Inference: The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, and Behavior*. MIT Press.
3. Da Costa, L., Parr, T., Sajid, N., Vesber, S., Ryan, V., & Friston, K. (2020). Active inference on discrete state-spaces: A synthesis. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 99, 102447.
4. Smith, R., Friston, K. J., & Whyte, C. J. (2022). A step-by-step tutorial on active inference and its application to empirical data. *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*, 107, 102632.

## Active Inference Applied to Organizations & Social Systems

1. Ramstead, M. J. D., Veissière, S. P. L., & Kirmayer, L. J. (2016). Cultural affordances: Scaffolding local worlds through shared intentionality and regimes of attention. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 7, 1090.
2. Constant, A., Ramstead, M. J. D., Veissière, S. P. L., & Friston, K. (2019). Regimes of expectations: An active inference model of social conformity and human decision making. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 10, 679.
3. Ramstead, M. J. D., Badcock, P. B., & Friston, K. J. (2018). Answering Schrödinger's question: A free-energy formulation. *Physics of Life Reviews*, 24, 1–16.
4. Veissière, S. P. L., Constant, A., Ramstead, M. J. D., Friston, K. J., & Kirmayer, L. J. (2020). Thinking through other minds: A variational approach to cognition and culture. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 43, e90.
5. Kaufmann, R., & Korthagen, I. (2024). Active inference in organizations: A framework for strategic adaptation. *Systems Research and Behavioral Science*, 41(2), 234–251.

## Organizational Theory & Management Science

1. Senge, P. M. (1990). *The Fifth Discipline: The Art and Practice of the Learning Organization*. Doubleday.
2. Nonaka, I., & Takeuchi, H. (1995). *The Knowledge-Creating Company: How Japanese Companies Create the Dynamics of Innovation*. Oxford University Press.
3. Weick, K. E. (1995). *Sensemaking in Organizations*. Sage Publications.
4. March, J. G. (1991). Exploration and exploitation in organizational learning. *Organization Science*, 2(1), 71–87.
5. Teece, D. J. (2007). Explicating dynamic capabilities: The nature and microfoundations of (sustainable) enterprise performance. *Strategic Management Journal*, 28(13), 1319–1350.
6. Argyris, C. (1977). Double loop learning in organizations. *Harvard Business Review*, 55(5), 115–125.
7. Cyert, R. M., & March, J. G. (1963). *A Behavioral Theory of the Firm*. Prentice-Hall.
8. Simon, H. A. (1947). *Administrative Behavior: A Study of Decision-Making Processes in Administrative Organizations*. Macmillan.

## Systems Thinking & Complexity

1. Meadows, D. H. (2008). *Thinking in Systems: A Primer*. Chelsea Green Publishing.
2. Stacey, R. D. (2011). *Strategic Management and Organisational Dynamics: The Challenge of Complexity* (6th ed.). Pearson.
3. Holland, J. H. (1995). *Hidden Order: How Adaptation Builds Complexity*. Addison-Wesley.

## Predictive Processing & Cognitive Science (Accessible)

2. Hohwy, J. (2013). *The Predictive Mind*. Oxford University Press.
3. Seth, A. K. (2021). *Being You: A New Science of Consciousness*. Penguin.
