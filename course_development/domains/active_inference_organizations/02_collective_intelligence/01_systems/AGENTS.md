> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Teams as nested systems with their own Markov blankets, boundaries, and adaptive behavior
- **Topics**: Team boundaries, boundary permeability, multi-scale organization, team coherence
- **Lab Style**: Workshop — team boundary mapping and coherence assessment
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists, team leads
- **Tone**: Professional, collaborative, connecting Active Inference to observable team dynamics

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines teams as systems in their own right — not just collections of individuals, but entities with their own boundaries, internal states, and collective behavior. All content should:

1. **Distinguish teams from groups**: A team is a system with a shared model, clear boundary, joint attention, and coordinated action — a group is just individuals sharing a manager
2. **Analyze boundary permeability**: Too closed leads to groupthink and stale assumptions; too open leads to weak identity and coordination overhead
3. **Map multi-scale nesting**: Individuals within teams, teams within departments — each level with its own Markov blanket and inference process
4. **Assess team coherence**: Whether the group functions as a unified inference agent or a fragmented collection
5. **Use diverse team examples**: Agile software teams, surgical teams, founding startup teams, cross-functional task forces, open-source communities

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Collective Markov blanket**: The team's boundary regulating information flow in and out
- **Boundary permeability**: How open or closed the team is to external information and influence
- **Multi-scale organization**: Nesting of systems at individual, team, and department levels
- **Team coherence**: Whether the group functions as a unified system or fragmented collection
- **Boundary management**: Actively regulating permeability based on context
