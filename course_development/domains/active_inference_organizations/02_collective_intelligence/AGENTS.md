## Identity

- **Course**: Collective Intelligence
- **Number**: 2
- **Perspective**: Teams, culture, and shared mental models as the substrate for distributed organizational cognition
- **Lab Type**: Workshop — facilitated team exercises, group diagnostics, and collaborative design sessions
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists, team leads, HR professionals
- **Tone**: Professional business/management. Collaborative, team-focused, connecting Active Inference to group dynamics and knowledge management.

## Domain Guidelines

This course examines how collective intelligence emerges from the coordination of individual inference agents. The central insight is that teams, departments, and organizations develop emergent cognitive capacities that exceed the sum of individual capabilities — but only when coordination mechanisms are properly designed. Every concept should be grounded in the reality of **how teams actually work** (and fail to work) in organizations.

### Key Organizational Active Inference Concepts

- **Collective Markov blanket**: How teams develop their own boundaries, distinguishing members from non-members and regulating information flow in and out
- **Shared generative models**: The aligned mental models that enable team members to predict each other's behavior and coordinate without constant explicit communication
- **Distributed inference**: How the inference task is partitioned across team members, with each contributing specialized perception, cognition, or action
- **Collective free energy minimization**: How teams reduce surprise as a coordinated unit — joint attention, shared sensemaking, synchronized action
- **Transactive memory**: The team-level memory system where members know who knows what, enabling distributed knowledge storage and retrieval

### Integration Principles

1. Ground all concepts in observable team dynamics — meetings, retrospectives, handoffs, conflicts, coordination successes and failures
2. Use workshop-style activities that participants can apply directly to their own teams
3. Emphasize that collective intelligence is not automatic — it requires design, facilitation, and intentional practice
4. Draw on diverse examples: agile software teams, surgical teams, orchestras, startup founding teams, cross-functional task forces

2. Frame all concepts through the lens of teams, culture, and shared mental models.
3. Include workshop activities that can be run with real teams.
4. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md).
5. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md).

7. Address both the benefits and pathologies of collective cognition (wisdom of crowds and groupthink).
