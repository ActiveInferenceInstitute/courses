# Station: Action (Collective Intelligence)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Collective action as synchronized, interdependent team behavior producing emergent outcomes
- **Topics**: Coordination mechanisms, division of labor, stigmergy, autonomy-alignment tension
- **Lab Style**: Workshop — coordination exercise and division-of-labor design
- **Audience**: Business leaders, managers, organizational scientists, team leads
- **Tone**: Professional, collaborative, action-oriented

## Domain Integration Guidelines

This module examines how teams act together — moving from shared understanding to coordinated execution. All content should:

1. **Distinguish collective action from parallel action**: True team action is interdependent and synchronized, not individuals working side by side
2. **Map coordination mechanisms**: Explicit (plans, schedules, protocols) and implicit (shared mental models, anticipation of teammates' moves)
3. **Analyze division of labor**: How the action task is partitioned — functional, geographic, temporal — and the coordination costs each creates
4. **Introduce stigmergy**: Indirect coordination through the work environment itself (leaving traces that guide others' actions)
5. **Resolve the autonomy-alignment tension**: Individual team members need enough autonomy to respond to local conditions, but enough alignment to contribute to collective goals

## Active Inference Concepts for This Module

- **Coordinated action**: Multiple agents acting in synchronized interdependence
- **Implicit coordination**: Shared generative models enabling prediction of teammates' actions
- **Division of labor**: Partitioning the action space across team members
- **Stigmergy**: Indirect coordination through environmental modification
- **Autonomy-alignment**: Balancing local flexibility with collective coherence
