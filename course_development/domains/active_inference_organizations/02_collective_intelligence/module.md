# Unit 02: Collective Intelligence — Overview

## Learning Objectives

1. Define **collective intelligence** as the emergent inference capacity of a group that exceeds the sum of individual members' capacities.
2. Analyze how shared generative models (culture, standard operating procedures, professional norms) coordinate belief updating across organizational agents.
3. Apply the **Thinking Through Other Minds (TTOM)** framework to explain coordination, leadership, and organizational learning.

## Introduction

A single neuron does not think. Thinking emerges from the coordinated activity of billions of neurons, each performing simple operations but collectively producing inference, planning, and consciousness. Organizations exhibit the same phenomenon: **collective intelligence** emerges when individual agents (employees, teams, departments) coordinate their inference processes through shared generative models.

This unit examines how organizations create, maintain, and evolve collective intelligence. The key Active Inference insight is that collective intelligence is not merely the pooling of individual knowledge — it is the alignment of **generative models** across agents, enabling the organization to infer, predict, and act as a coherent whole.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Shared Generative Models and Culture

Organizational culture is a **shared generative model** — a common set of beliefs, expectations, and interpretive frameworks that members use to predict each other's behavior and coordinate action:

- **Mission statements** encode shared preferred observations (the C vector: "We value innovation and customer satisfaction")
- **Standard operating procedures** encode shared transition models (the B matrix: "When customer complaint arrives, escalate to tier-2 support within 24 hours")
- **Role expectations** encode shared observation models (the A matrix: "When the CFO presents quarterly numbers, finance accuracy is expected to be high")

When cultural alignment is strong, coordination costs drop — employees can predict each other's responses, reducing the mutual free energy of interaction. When alignment breaks (after mergers, rapid scaling, leadership transitions), prediction errors spike and collective intelligence degrades.

### 2. Generalized Synchrony in Teams

High-performing teams exhibit **generalized synchrony** — a functional alignment of team members' internal states that enables fluid coordination. In Active Inference terms, synchronized team members' beliefs about the project state, priorities, and next steps are mutually predictable.

Hallmarks of team synchrony include:

- **Finishing each other's sentences** (shared transition models)
- **Anticipating needs** (accurate models of each other's generative models)
- **Swift trust** (high precision on social prediction errors from familiar teammates)

Team dysfunction is the *loss* of generalized synchrony — members' generative models diverge, prediction errors between team members accumulate, and coordination collapses into explicit negotiation rather than implicit understanding.

### 3. Leadership as Precision Regulation

Leaders in Active Inference terms are **precision regulators** — they modulate the organization's attention. A CEO who declares "This quarter, product quality is our top priority" is increasing the precision on quality-related prediction errors across the organization — making the entire system more sensitive to quality deviations.

Effective leadership balances:

- **Exploitation precision**: Focus on known goals, execute established strategies
- **Exploration precision**: Loosen constraints, encourage experimentation, tolerate prediction errors in novel domains

## Applications

- **Post-merger integration failure**: When Daimler acquired Chrysler (1998), two distinct generative models (German engineering precision vs. American market responsiveness) collided. The failure to align shared generative models produced persistent inter-organizational prediction errors — conflicting design philosophies, incompatible decision-making processes — and the merger was unwound.
- **Agile methodology as synchronized inference**: Scrum ceremonies (daily standups, sprint reviews, retrospectives) are structured opportunities for team members to synchronize their generative models — updating shared beliefs about progress, obstacles, and priorities at regular intervals.

## Conclusion

Collective intelligence is not magic — it is the emergent consequence of generative model alignment across agents. This unit's 8 modules explore how organizations build, maintain, and repair collective intelligence through the mechanisms of shared culture, team synchrony, leadership precision, and distributed inference.
