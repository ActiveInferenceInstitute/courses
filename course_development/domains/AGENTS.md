## Shared Domain Conventions

### Tone

All domain courses use a tone appropriate to their specific field:

- **Technical domains** (Robotics, Metallurgy, Inventions): Engineering/professional tone with equations, code, and technical specifications where appropriate
- **Creative domains** (Comedy, Crochet): Narrative, culturally grounded, practice-oriented — the theory is discovered through the craft, not imposed upon it
- **Embodied domains** (Embodied Cognition): Contemplative, somatic, first-person experiential — bridging felt experience and formal theory
- **Social domains** (Organizations): Strategic, case-study-driven — connecting AIF formalism to real organizational dynamics

### Content Quality Standards

Every module.md must include:

1. **Specific learning objectives** that map to the domain (not generic placeholders)
2. **Substantive Key Concepts sections** with filled-in prose, not template questions
3. **At least two concrete, domain-specific applications** that practitioners would recognize
4. **A conclusion** that connects to the next module in the spiral
5. **Minimum 500 words** of substantive content (excluding headers)

### Applied Examples

Every module should include at least one concrete, domain-specific example that practitioners would recognize:

- **Embodied**: somatic exercises, body scans, movement practices, interoceptive drills
- **Organizations**: case studies from real organizational contexts, strategy frameworks, org charts as Markov blankets
- **Robotics**: sensor-actuator architectures, ROS2 code, hardware configurations, biomimetic designs
- **Crochet Circles**: stitch exercises, pattern analysis, yarn exploration, circle group activities, lace mathematics
- **Inventions**: prototype builds, design challenges, patent analysis, innovation case studies, failure analysis
- **Metallurgy**: alloy experiments, phase diagram exercises, material testing, microstructure analysis, heat treatment protocols
- **Comedy**: bit construction, timing drills, crowd reading, improvisation exercises, callback architecture

### Lab Style

Domain labs are **practice-oriented**, not purely theoretical:

- **Embodied**: Somatic Exercise, Mindfulness Practice, Reflective Journaling, Movement Lab
- **Organizations**: Case Study, Workshop, Strategy Exercise, Implementation Plan
- **Robotics**: Hardware Lab, Design Challenge, Simulation Lab, ROS2 Project
- **Crochet Circles**: Hands-on Stitch Lab, Materials Exploration, Pattern Design Workshop, Group Activity
- **Inventions**: Design Sprint, Prototype Lab, Innovation Workshop, Patent Analysis
- **Metallurgy**: Materials Lab, Phase Diagram Exercise, Testing Workshop, Microstructure Analysis
- **Comedy**: Bit Workshop, Timing Lab, Crowd Reading Exercise, Improv Session

## Theory Semantics for Domains

The domain courses demonstrate that the Free Energy Principle is not confined to neuroscience — it applies wherever systems maintain boundaries and act on predictions:

- **Embodied**: The body itself is an inference engine. Interoception, proprioception, and felt sense are forms of active inference. The Markov blanket is the skin, the breath, the boundary between self and world.
- **Organizations**: An organization is a collective agent with a shared generative model. Departments are nested Markov blankets. Strategy is policy selection under expected free energy.
- **Robotics**: A robot is a physically instantiated active inference agent. Sensors are observation channels, actuators are action channels, and the control loop is the perception-action cycle.
- **Crochet Circles**: A crochet circle is a nested system of active inference agents. Each crocheter maintains a generative model (the pattern) and minimizes prediction error with every stitch. The hook-yarn interface is a Markov blanket. The circle itself is a collective agent with shared generative models.
- **Inventions**: An invention process is active inference made tangible. The inventor maintains a generative model of a desired solution and iterates prototypes to minimize the gap between prediction and reality. Each design cycle is a perception-action loop.
- **Metallurgy**: A metallurgical system is an inference process at the atomic scale. Phase transformations minimize free energy. Crystal structures are emergent Markov blankets. Heat treatment is policy selection under thermodynamic constraints.
- **Comedy**: A comedic performance is prediction error engineered for pleasure. The comedian builds expectations (generative models in the audience) and then violates them (punchlines). Timing is precision of the prediction-error signal. The room itself is a collective inference agent.
