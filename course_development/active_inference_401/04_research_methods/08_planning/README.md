## Learning Goals

1. **Identify** the most promising research frontiers for Active Inference: quantum extensions (Fields et al., 2022), category-theoretic foundations (St. Clere Smithe, 2020), multi-scale biological modeling (Levin), artificial general intelligence, and societal-scale applications
2. **Design** a complete research proposal applying Active Inference to a novel problem, including literature review, formal model specification, methodology, expected results, timeline, and budget, suitable for submission to a major funding agency
3. **Synthesize** the entire 401 course into a unified understanding of Active Inference research, integrating philosophical foundations (Track 1), neuroscientific evidence (Track 2), advanced mathematical theory (Track 3), and practical research methods (Track 4) into a coherent research program
4. **Evaluate** your own research trajectory through the lens of Active Inference: identify how your prior training, current expertise, and future goals can be leveraged to make original contributions to the field

## Prerequisites

- Completion of or concurrent enrollment in all four 401 tracks
- Demonstrated capacity for independent research (prior publications, research experience)
- Familiarity with grant writing conventions and funding agency requirements
