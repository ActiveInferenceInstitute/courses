## Learning Goals

1. **Design** behavioral tasks that dissociate Active Inference computational variables: construct paradigms that independently manipulate volatility (precision of transition dynamics), ambiguity (precision of likelihood mapping), and preference (prior over observations) to isolate their behavioral signatures
2. **Analyze** eye-tracking and pupillometry as measures of prediction and precision, implementing model-based analysis pipelines that link fixation patterns to epistemic value and pupil dilation to precision-weighted prediction errors
3. **Evaluate** paradigms for studying model-based planning, learning, and decision-making under Active Inference, including two-step tasks, information-seeking tasks, explore-exploit dilemmas, and goal-directed navigation, assessing what each reveals about the underlying generative model
4. **Implement** a complete model-based analysis of behavioral data: fit an Active Inference generative model to trial-by-trial choices and reaction times, perform parameter recovery, model comparison, and posterior predictive checks

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level experimental psychology (psychophysics, reaction time analysis, signal detection theory)
- Proficiency in computational modeling of behavior (likelihood specification, optimization, MCMC)
- Familiarity with eye-tracking methodology and pupillometry
