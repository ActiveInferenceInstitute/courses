## Part A: Multiple Choice

A) All variables should be confounded

A) Light sensitivity

B) Low estimated volatility (ω) — the agent's prior is that the environment is stable, causing slow detection of contingency changes

**7.** Hierarchical Gaussian Filter (HGF) models capture learning by:

## Part B: Short Answer

**1.** A participant in a probabilistic reversal learning task shows slow updating after reversals (high perseveration). Under an Active Inference model, this could be due to either low volatility estimates OR high prior precision. Describe how you would dissociate these two explanations using additional behavioral or model-fitting evidence. (200 words)

**2.** Explain why eye-tracking is particularly informative for testing Active Inference predictions about cognition. What specific predictions does Active Inference make about where people look and for how long, and how do these differ from salience-based models? (200 words)

## Part C: Essay Questions

**1.** Design a comprehensive behavioral study testing Active Inference's predictions about the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Include: (a) two tasks that dissociate epistemic from pragmatic value, (b) manipulations of uncertainty and volatility, (c) measures (choices, RT, eye-tracking, pupillometry), (d) computational model specification, (e) parameter recovery validation, (f) expected results. (600 words)

**2.** Compare behavioral evidence for Active Inference with behavioral evidence for Reinforcement Learning on the same set of tasks (reversal learning, bandit tasks, planning). Where do the two frameworks make identical predictions? Where do they diverge? How would you design the critical experiment that distinguishes them? (400 words)

**3.** Write a methods section for a computational phenotyping study using behavioral data from a probabilistic learning task. Include: task description, model specification, fitting procedure, parameter recovery, group comparison analysis, and results reporting standards. (500 words)
