# Study Questions: Research Methods

1. What is Dynamic Causal Modelling (DCM), and how does it implement Active Inference at the level of brain imaging analysis? Compare DCM with Granger causality and structural equation modeling.

2. How does Bayesian Model Selection (BMS) compare competing models of brain connectivity? Explain the role of free energy as a model evidence approximation and how Parametric Empirical Bayes extends this to group-level inference.

3. What is computational phenotyping, and how do individual differences in Active Inference parameters (precision, prior strength, learning rate, volatility estimation) serve as biomarkers for psychiatric conditions?

4. Design a behavioral experiment that dissociates epistemic value from pragmatic value. What task structure, conditions, and dependent measures would you use?

5. How do EEG/MEG oscillatory signatures (gamma, beta, alpha) serve as empirical markers for prediction errors, predictions, and precision modulation respectively? What experimental paradigms best test these mappings?

6. How does the roving oddball paradigm test Active Inference's account of belief updating? What predictions about MMN amplitude, latency, and habituation follow from the framework?

7. How can eye-tracking data (fixation duration, scanpath, pupil dilation) validate Active Inference models of perception and decision-making? What specific predictions does expected free energy minimization make about visual search?

8. Compare the software tools available for Active Inference research: pymdp, SPM/DCM, RxInfer.jl. What applications does each serve best, and what are their respective strengths and limitations?

9. How does Active Inference replace classical inverse kinematics in robotic control? What are the advantages of a unified perception-action framework for robotics, and what are the practical challenges of sim-to-real transfer?

10. What is parameter recovery analysis, and why is it essential for validating computational models? How do you assess whether your fitting procedure can reliably distinguish between competing models?

11. Design a DCM study to investigate the neural basis of hierarchical prediction error processing. Specify the brain regions, hypothesized connectivity, experimental manipulation, and model comparison strategy.

12. How does the Active Inference framework reframe mental disorders as computational dysfunctions? Compare the aberrant precision account of schizophrenia with the pessimistic priors account of depression and the inflexible precision account of autism.

13. What is the 5-step translational pipeline from computational theory to clinical treatment? How does each step build on the previous one, and where are the current bottlenecks?

14. How does pharmacological manipulation (ketamine, psilocybin, scopolamine, L-DOPA) test specific Active Inference predictions about precision and prediction errors? Design a pharmacological study addressing a specific hypothesis.

15. How do pre-registration, open-source code, and reproducible analysis pipelines address the replication crisis in computational neuroscience? What should a fully reproducible Active Inference analysis include?

16. How do the four tracks of the 401 course (Philosophy, Neuroscience, Advanced Theory, Research Methods) integrate to form a complete research toolkit? What does each track uniquely contribute?

17. What are the most important unresolved methodological challenges facing Active Inference research? Consider identifiability, scalability, model complexity, and clinical validation.

18. How should Active Inference be communicated to interdisciplinary audiences — neuroscientists, engineers, clinicians, philosophers, and the general public? What are the most common misunderstandings, and how do you address them?

19. How might deep active inference (neural network approximations to generative models) scale Active Inference beyond hand-crafted models? What are the trade-offs between interpretability and expressiveness?

20. Write a research proposal: Identify an open question in Active Inference, formulate a testable hypothesis, design an experiment or computational study to address it, and describe how you would analyze the results using the methods from this track.
