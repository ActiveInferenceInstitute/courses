## Learning Goals

1. **Apply** Active Inference to computational psychiatry, specifying generative models for specific clinical conditions (schizophrenia, depression, anxiety, addiction) and deriving the parameter perturbations that generate characteristic symptom profiles
2. **Analyze** clinical disorders through the lens of aberrant precision (anxiety as inflated sensory precision, autism as attenuated social precision), prediction error dysregulation (psychosis as aberrant salience), and model structure (depression as overly simple, rigid generative models)
3. **Evaluate** the translational pipeline from computational model to clinical intervention, critically assessing the evidence for Active Inference-informed treatments including neurofeedback, precision-targeted pharmacology, and model-based cognitive behavioral therapy
4. **Design** a translational research protocol that bridges computational modeling and clinical practice, specifying the computational model, clinical population, behavioral task, neuroimaging measure, and therapeutic intervention with appropriate outcome measures

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level psychopathology (symptomatology, neurobiology, treatment modalities)
- Proficiency in computational modeling of clinical data (parameter estimation, group-level analysis)
- Understanding of clinical trial methodology and translational research design
