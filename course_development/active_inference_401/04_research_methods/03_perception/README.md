## Learning Goals

1. **Apply** electrophysiological methods (EEG, MEG, intracranial recordings) to test Active Inference predictions, designing paradigms that isolate prediction, prediction error, and precision signals in the temporal domain
2. **Analyze** the relationship between neural oscillations and Active Inference computations: implement time-frequency analysis, source localization, and dynamic causal modelling of evoked responses to extract computational quantities from neural data
3. **Evaluate** experimental paradigms that dissociate prediction from prediction error and precision, including mismatch negativity (MMN), roving standard, and probabilistic reversal learning tasks, critically assessing what each paradigm can and cannot reveal
4. **Design** a novel electrophysiological experiment with formal model-based analysis, specifying the generative model for both the task and the neural responses, and demonstrating how model inversion recovers computational parameters from trial-by-trial ERP/oscillatory data

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level electrophysiology (EEG/MEG recording, artifact correction, source reconstruction)
- Proficiency in signal processing (spectral analysis, wavelet decomposition, cross-frequency coupling)
- Understanding of DCM for evoked responses and induced responses
