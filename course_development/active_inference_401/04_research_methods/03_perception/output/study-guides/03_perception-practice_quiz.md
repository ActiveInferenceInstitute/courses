## Part A: Multiple Choice

A) Motor planning

A) Motor commands
B) Precision modulation — increased alpha suppresses irrelevant prediction errors, functionally implementing precision down-weighting
C) Sensory input
D) Random noise

**3.** Bottom-up prediction errors preferentially travel in:
A) Alpha/beta bands
B) Gamma and theta frequency bands, consistent with their role in driving fast, stimulus-related neural computation

**4.** The roving oddball paradigm tests:
A) Motor learning
B) Belief updating — how repeated exposure strengthens predictions, producing larger prediction errors when expectations are violated
C) Emotional regulation
D) Language processing

A) Only noise
B) Ascending prediction errors (superficial layers L2/3) from descending predictions (deep layers L5/6), directly testing the canonical microcircuit model

A) Two types of reward
B) Local prediction errors (automatic, early, temporal cortex) from global prediction errors (awareness-dependent, late, frontal-parietal) — testing hierarchical prediction

**7.** Steady-state visual evoked potentials (SSVEPs) test Active Inference by:
A) Measuring saccades

## Part B: Short Answer

**1.** A researcher records EEG during an auditory oddball task and finds that MMN amplitude decreases when participants are told the deviants are "unimportant." Interpret this in Active Inference terms: what computational variable is being modulated by the instruction, and how does this relate to the neural response? (200 words)

**2.** Explain why cross-frequency coupling (e.g., gamma amplitude nested within beta phase) is predicted by hierarchical predictive coding. What would disruption of this coupling (e.g., by ketamine) predict for perception? (200 words)

## Part C: Essay Questions

**1.** Design a comprehensive EEG experiment testing three specific predictions of Active Inference about perception: (a) prediction errors are precision-weighted, (b) predictions travel top-down in beta frequencies, and (c) higher hierarchical levels generate later, more frontal responses. For each prediction, specify the paradigm, expected neural signature, and statistical test. (600 words)

**2.** Compare EEG, MEG, and fMRI as methods for testing Active Inference predictions. For each modality: strengths, limitations, what it can uniquely reveal, and what it cannot. Design an ideal multimodal study combining two or more modalities. (400 words)

**3.** Critically evaluate: Some researchers argue that MMN and related signals can be explained by simpler adaptation mechanisms (stimulus-specific adaptation, SSA) without invoking prediction. How would you distinguish the Active Inference account from the adaptation account experimentally? (400 words)
