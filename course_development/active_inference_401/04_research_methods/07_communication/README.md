## Learning Goals

1. **Design** interdisciplinary research programs applying Active Inference across domains (neuroscience, psychology, robotics, social science, ecology), specifying how the shared mathematical framework enables principled translation of hypotheses and findings between fields
2. **Analyze** methods for communicating Active Inference to non-specialist audiences, developing expository strategies that convey the core formalism without sacrificing mathematical rigor -- including visual representations, worked examples, and progressive formalization
3. **Evaluate** the role of open science practices (preregistration, open data, open code, reproducible analyses) in advancing Active Inference research, and implement these practices in your own research workflow
4. **Formalize** the Active Inference research community as itself an instance of multi-agent Active Inference: model how shared generative models (theoretical frameworks), precision-weighted prediction errors (empirical challenges), and model selection (theory change) operate at the collective scientific level

## Prerequisites

- Experience in at least two research domains relevant to Active Inference
- Understanding of open science practices and reproducibility standards
- Familiarity with collaborative research tools (GitHub, OSF, preprint servers)
