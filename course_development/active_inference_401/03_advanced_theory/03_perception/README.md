## Learning Goals

1. **Analyze** Bayesian mechanics as the physics of systems whose dynamics can be described as performing inference, starting from the Langevin equation on a particular partition and deriving the conditions under which internal states parameterize beliefs about external states
2. **Derive** the particular partition (internal, external, sensory, active states) from the conditional independence structure of a random dynamical system at nonequilibrium steady state, proving that the Markov blanket condition entails a separation of flow into solenoidal and dissipative components
3. **Examine** the mathematical conditions under which physical dynamics can be read as belief updating: show that internal states track the sufficient statistics of the posterior over external states when the system is at NESS
4. **Evaluate** the scope and limitations of Bayesian mechanics: under what conditions does the "as if" inference interpretation break down, and what are the implications for the universality claims of the FEP?

## Prerequisites

- Stochastic differential equations (Langevin dynamics, Fokker-Planck equation, stationary distributions)
- Non-equilibrium statistical mechanics (NESS, detailed balance violation, entropy production)
- Familiarity with Bayesian inference at the measure-theoretic level
