## Part B: Short Answer

**1.** A neuroscience lab measures neuronal avalanche size distributions in cortical slices and finds a power law with exponent α ≈ 1.5. Explain what this finding suggests about the brain's operating point, how it connects to Active Inference, and name one confound that could produce a spurious power law. (200 words)

**2.** Consider a three-level hierarchy: cell → tissue → organism. For each transition, identify what degrees of freedom are "integrated out" in the RG sense and what effective parameters remain. How does the Markov Blanket change at each scale? (200 words)

## Part C: Essay Questions

**1.** Perform a detailed RG analysis of a multi-scale biological system (your choice: immune system, ecosystem, neural circuit). Identify at least 3 scales, the states at each scale, the coarse-graining procedure between scales, and whether the effective Active Inference equations preserve their form. Where does the analysis work well? Where does it break down? (500 words)

**2.** Evaluate the "brain at criticality" hypothesis. Present the strongest evidence for and against. How would you test whether the brain is: (a) at a critical point, (b) near but not at a critical point, or (c) somewhere else entirely? What would each finding imply for Active Inference? (400 words)

**3.** Write a theoretical essay on how Active Inference applies to social systems. Choose a specific social phenomenon (political polarization, market dynamics, cultural evolution) and model it using scale-free Active Inference. Identify the generative model, the prediction errors, the active inference policies, and the Markov blanket. What novel predictions does this framework generate? (500 words)
