## Learning Goals

1. **Evaluate** the scope and limits of Active Inference as a universal theory of adaptive systems: assess the claim that any system at NESS with a Markov blanket can be described as performing inference, and identify the precise conditions under which this description is vacuous versus substantive
2. **Analyze** the connections between Active Inference and other major theoretical frameworks: prove formal equivalences with optimal control (Pontryagin's maximum principle), reinforcement learning (KL control), information theory (minimum entropy coupling), and thermodynamics (Jarzynski equality, fluctuation theorems)
3. **Synthesize** the Advanced Theory track by constructing a unified mathematical framework that encompasses variational inference, information geometry, Bayesian mechanics, deep temporal models, renormalization, model selection, and multi-agent systems as special cases of a single variational principle
4. **Identify** the most promising open mathematical problems in Active Inference and formulate at least one as a precise conjecture with a proposed proof strategy

## Prerequisites

- Completion of all prior Advanced Theory modules (01--07)
- Breadth across mathematical physics (statistical mechanics, optimal control, information theory)
- Capacity for independent mathematical reasoning at the research level
