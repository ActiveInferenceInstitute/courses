## Learning Goals

1. **Connect** Active Inference's learning mechanisms to philosophy of science: map parameter learning onto normal science, structure learning onto paradigm shifts, and Bayesian model reduction onto Occam's razor
2. **Analyze** Bayesian Model Reduction (BMR) as a formalization of scientific parsimony, proving that models with fewer effective parameters are favored when evidence is equivalent
3. **Evaluate** the FEP's implications for the demarcation problem: does the framework provide a formal criterion for distinguishing scientific from non-scientific theories via model evidence?
4. **Critique** the Kuhnian account of scientific revolutions through the lens of Bayesian model comparison, assessing whether paradigm shifts are better understood as discontinuous structure learning

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level philosophy of science (Popper, Kuhn, Lakatos, Bayesian confirmation theory)
- Understanding of Bayesian model comparison (log model evidence, Bayes factors)
- Familiarity with BMR and its relationship to sleep, pruning, and structure learning
