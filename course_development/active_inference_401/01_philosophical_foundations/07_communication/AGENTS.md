## Content Guidance

This module must address whether the FEP can account for the distinctive normativity and shared character of human communication and social cognition. Content must engage Tomasello's developmental account of shared intentionality — the capacity to engage in joint attention, cooperative communication, and collective intentionality — and evaluate whether the FEP's framework of shared generative models and social Markov blankets captures what Tomasello identifies as uniquely human or merely redescribes coordination in inferential language. The module must also address the philosophical dimensions of cultural niche construction (Ramstead et al., 2016; Veissiere et al., 2020) — the way cultural practices, institutions, and artifacts structure the "regimes of attention" through which agents reduce uncertainty — and whether this framework can accommodate the Wittgensteinian point that rule-following is irreducibly social: one cannot follow a rule privately, and communication is not merely aligning internal models but participating in a practice that constitutes the meaning of one's utterances.

## Key Concepts

- **Joint attention and shared intentionality (Tomasello)**: The developmental progression from dyadic interaction to triadic joint attention to full-blown shared intentionality (we-intentionality). Tomasello (2014) argues this progression is uniquely human and constitutive of human cognition. Can the FEP's multi-agent framework — coupled inference between agents with aligned generative models — capture the *normativity* of shared intentionality (not just that agents coordinate, but that they are *supposed to* coordinate in specific ways)?
- **Shared generative models and cultural affordances**: Veissiere et al. (2020) propose that cultural practices function as shared generative models — collectively maintained priors that structure attention and behavior. This framework treats culture as a form of "thinking through other minds" (TTOM). The philosophical question is whether this reduces culture to shared Bayesian priors (losing the hermeneutic dimension of cultural meaning) or genuinely captures the way culture shapes cognition.
- **Cultural niche construction**: Ramstead et al. (2016) develop the idea that human agents do not merely adapt to their environment but actively construct cultural niches — patterned regularities in the social environment that scaffold inference. This connects to the extended cognition debate (Module 04) but adds the specifically social dimension: the relevant cognitive scaffolding is not merely tools and notebooks but institutions, norms, and shared narratives.
- **Regulative vs. constitutive rules**: The distinction (originating with Searle, developed by Wittgenstein scholars) between rules that regulate pre-existing activities (traffic rules regulate driving) and rules that constitute activities (the rules of chess constitute chess-playing). Does the FEP's framework of shared priors account for constitutive rules, or only for regulative coordination? If the latter, it may miss what is philosophically distinctive about language and social institutions.
- **Wittgensteinian rule-following and private language**: Wittgenstein's argument that meaning is constituted by use within a form of life, not by internal mental states. If the FEP treats communication as alignment of internal generative models, does it commit the "private language" fallacy — assuming that meaning originates inside individual agents and is then transmitted, rather than being constituted by the social practice itself?

## Key References

- **Tomasello, M. (2014)**: *A Natural History of Human Thinking*. Develops the thesis that human cognition is fundamentally cooperative — that shared intentionality is not an add-on to individual cognition but its constitutive ground. Essential for evaluating whether the FEP's multi-agent framework captures genuine shared intentionality or merely parallel individual inference.
- **Ramstead, M., Veissiere, S., & Kirmayer, L. (2016)**: "Cultural affordances: Scaffolding local worlds through shared intentionality and regimes of attention." Introduces the concept of cultural affordances within the FEP framework and argues that culture shapes cognition by structuring the landscape of affordances that agents engage with. The key bridge paper between the FEP and cultural anthropology.
- **Veissiere, S., Constant, A., Ramstead, M., Friston, K., & Kirmayer, L. (2020)**: "Thinking through other minds: A variational approach to cognition and culture." Develops the "TTOM" framework — the idea that human cognition is fundamentally organized around expectations about other minds. Provides the most developed FEP account of social cognition and cultural transmission.
- **Vasil, J., Badcock, P., Constant, A., Friston, K., & Ramstead, M. (2020)**: "A world unto itself: Human communication as active inference." Applies the FEP to language and communication specifically, arguing that linguistic communication is a form of active inference in which interlocutors minimize mutual uncertainty. Critical for evaluating whether this framework captures the richness of linguistic meaning or merely models information exchange.

## Prerequisite Modules

Module 02 (Agents) must be completed first. The analysis of agency — what distinguishes genuine agents from mere systems, the conditions for autonomy and intentionality — is presupposed when asking how agents communicate. Without a prior account of what it means to be an agent with intentional states, the question of how agents *share* intentional states cannot be properly formulated. Additionally, familiarity with the Markov blanket formalism from Modules 01-02 is needed to evaluate the concept of "shared" or "social" Markov blankets.

## Cross-Unit Connections

- **Advanced Theory (Module 07)**: The theory treatment formalizes multi-agent active inference — shared Markov blankets, coupled dynamical systems, mean-field games. The philosophical treatment here should interrogate the formal assumption that communication consists in aligning generative models: is this a substantive thesis about the nature of communication, or a tautology that follows from the framework's definitions? Does the mathematical formalism of coupled inference do justice to the *content* of communication, or only to its dynamics?
- **Neuroscientific Frontiers (Module 07)**: The neuroscience treatment examines mirror systems, mentalizing networks, and social prediction errors. The philosophical module should ask whether the neuroscience of social cognition supports an inferentialist picture (we simulate others' mental states via internal models) or an enactivist picture (social understanding arises from direct perceptual engagement in interaction, not from internal simulation). The philosophical stakes of the mirror neuron debate (Gallese vs. Gallagher) are directly relevant.
- **Research Methods (Module 07)**: The methods treatment covers hyperscanning paradigms and computational phenotyping of social learning. The philosophical module should address whether experimental paradigms (trust games, joint attention tasks) adequately capture the philosophical phenomena of interest (shared intentionality, linguistic meaning, cultural transmission) or artificially simplify them into dyadic coordination tasks that miss the normative dimension.
