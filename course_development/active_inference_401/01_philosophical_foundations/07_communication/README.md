## Learning Goals

1. **Analyze** Active Inference's account of intersubjectivity, formalizing how agents align their generative models through reciprocal prediction and comparing this to phenomenological (Husserl), developmental (Tomasello), and analytic (Bratman) accounts
2. **Evaluate** the language-as-active-inference hypothesis: communication as mutual model alignment via precision-weighted prediction errors at syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic levels
3. **Formalize** shared generative models as the basis for social ontology, examining how institutions, norms, and collective intentionality emerge from multi-agent free energy minimization
4. **Critique** the explanatory reach of Active Inference for social phenomena, identifying what it illuminates (coordination, alignment) and what remains unexplained (power, conflict, ideology)

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level philosophy of language and social ontology (speech act theory, collective intentionality)
- Understanding of multi-agent generative models and their Markov blanket structure
- Familiarity with predictive processing accounts of language (Kuperberg, Pickering & Garrod)
