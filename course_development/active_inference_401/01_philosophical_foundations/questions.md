# Study Questions: Philosophical Foundations

1. How does Active Inference dissolve the mind-body problem through the Markov Blanket formalism, rather than solving it within the traditional Cartesian framework?

2. How does the Free Energy Principle reframe classical epistemological debates (rationalism vs. empiricism, realism vs. anti-realism) as limit cases of a single variational inference framework?

3. Contrast the spectator theory of knowledge (classical empiricism) with Active Inference's action-oriented epistemology. What does this shift imply for the relationship between knowing and doing?

4. How does the FEP's treatment of perception as "controlled hallucination" relate to the phenomenological tradition (Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger)? Where does the analogy hold and where does it break down?

5. Evaluate the claim that Active Inference provides a "process philosophy" (Whitehead, Deleuze). How does the continuous minimization of free energy replace static ontological categories with dynamic processes?

6. How does the concept of the generative model bridge the gap between 4E cognition (embodied, embedded, enacted, extended) and computational approaches to the mind?

7. Compare the pragmatist account of truth (James, Dewey, Peirce) with model evidence in Active Inference. Are "what works" and "what minimizes free energy" extensionally equivalent?

8. How does Active Inference's account of agency (graduated ontology from thermostats to self-conscious agents) avoid both the Scylla of panpsychism and the Charybdis of arbitrary cognitive thresholds?

9. Evaluate the ethical implications of the FEP: if preferences (C vector) are descriptive rather than normative, can Active Inference ground moral claims, or does it merely formalize instrumental rationality?

10. How does Active Inference handle the problem of other minds? Contrast its "active inference about active inference" approach with simulation theory and theory-theory.

11. Does the FEP's circular epistemology (the model defines what counts as evidence, and evidence updates the model) represent a vicious circle or a productive hermeneutic spiral? Compare with Quine, Heidegger, and Maturana & Varela.

12. How does Active Inference's treatment of scientific revolutions (Kuhn) as Bayesian Model Selection or Bayesian Model Reduction differ from Lakatos's account of research programs?

13. Is the Free Energy Principle falsifiable? Evaluate the arguments for and against, and consider what kind of principle the FEP is (empirical law, transcendental argument, normative framework).

14. How does Active Inference explain the emergence of social norms, institutions, and cultural practices as collective free energy minimization? What are the strengths and limitations of this account?

15. Compare the FEP's account of language as coupled inference with Wittgenstein's "meaning is use" and Grice's conversational maxims. Does formalization add genuine insight?

16. How does Active Inference's concept of "precision control" provide a novel lens for analyzing political power, propaganda, and epistemic injustice?

17. Trace the philosophical lineage of Active Inference from Helmholtz's "unconscious inference" through Kant's transcendental idealism to contemporary predictive processing. What is gained and what is lost at each stage?

18. How does the FEP relate to non-Western philosophical traditions — e.g., Buddhist anattā (no-self), Daoist wu wei (non-action), or Nagarjuna's śūnyatā (emptiness)? Are these connections substantive or superficial?

19. Evaluate the claim that Active Inference provides a unified framework for consciousness, agency, and social cognition. Where does it genuinely unify, and where does it merely redescribe?

20. Write a synthesis essay: What are the most important philosophical contributions of Active Inference, and what are its most serious philosophical limitations?
