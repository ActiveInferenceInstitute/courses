# Module 04: Cognition — Enactivism and 4E Cognition

> **Course**: Active Inference 401 | **Unit**: Philosophical Foundations | **Audience**: Advanced undergraduates / graduate students

## Learning Objectives

1. Evaluate the relationship between Active Inference and the **4E cognition** framework (Embodied, Embedded, Enacted, Extended).
2. Analyze the **enactivist challenge**: Does Active Inference honor or betray the enactivist program?
3. Examine the **extended mind thesis** through the lens of Markov Blankets.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The 4E Framework

The "4E" movement in cognitive science challenges classical cognitivism (computation over internal representations):

- **Embodied**: Cognition depends on the body's morphology and sensorimotor capacities
- **Embedded**: Cognition exploits environmental structure (scaffolding, cognitive niche construction)
- **Enacted**: Cognition is constituted by the organism-environment coupling, not by internal computation
- **Extended**: Cognitive processes can extend beyond the brain into tools, notebooks, and social structures

Active Inference engages with all four dimensions:

- **Embodied**: The generative model necessarily includes a body model; active inference is inherently sensorimotor
- **Embedded**: Environmental regularities are captured in the generative model's priors
- **Enacted**: The agent-environment coupling through the Markov Blanket is constitutive of cognition
- **Extended**: The extended mind thesis is contested — does the Markov Blanket *prevent* or *enable* extension?

### 2. The Enactivist Challenge

Enactivism (Varela, Thompson, Rosch, 1991; Thompson, 2007) makes strong claims:

**Radical enactivism**: Cognition is *not* internal representation — it is *sense-making* through organism-environment interaction. There are no representations, only dynamic coupling.

**The tension with Active Inference**: Active Inference is explicitly representationalist — it involves generative *models* that *represent* (in some sense) the causal structure of the world. This creates a philosophical clash:

- **Enactivist critique**: Active Inference smuggles in the very representationalism that enactivism rejects. Calling the generative model a "model" implies representation.
- **Active Inference response**: The generative model is not a static representation but a *lived structure* — it's enacted through perception-action loops. Ramstead et al. (2020) argue "Active Inference *is* enactive inference."
- **Middle ground**: Perhaps the debate is about the word "representation." If "representation" means a detached, context-independent symbol, then Active Inference isn't representationalist. If it means any internal structure that covaries with external states, then it is.

### 3. The Extended Mind and Markov Blankets

Clark & Chalmers' (1998) extended mind thesis: cognitive processes can extend beyond the skull (Otto's notebook is part of his memory).

**The Markov Blanket paradox**: If the MB defines the boundary of the cognitive system, doesn't it *prevent* extension by construction? The MB separates internal from external states — how can cognition extend beyond it?

Responses:

- **Nested blankets**: The MB is scale-dependent. At one scale, the brain has a blanket. At another scale, the brain-notebook system has its own blanket. Extension happens through nesting.
- **Cognitive niche construction**: The organism reshapes its environment (building Markov Blankets into the world) — this is the formal version of "extended cognition."
- **Cultural Markov Blankets**: Social institutions, languages, and technologies create group-level statistical boundaries that support collective cognition.

### 4. Autopoiesis and the FEP

**Autopoiesis** (Maturana & Varela): Living systems are self-producing — they maintain their own organization through their own processes.

The FEP's relationship to autopoiesis:

- **Convergence**: Both emphasize self-organization, operational closure, and the fundamental circularity of living systems
- **Divergence**: Autopoiesis rejects representationalism; the FEP embraces generative models
- **Synthesis**: The FEP can be seen as a mathematical formalization of autopoietic principles — self-organization as free energy minimization

### 5. Cognitive Niche Construction

Active Inference naturally explains **cognitive niche construction**: organisms don't just adapt to their environment — they *modify* their environment to reduce future surprise:

- Building shelter reduces prediction error about temperature
- Creating written language extends the generative model into external media
- Developing institutions creates shared predictive structures

This connects to Sterelny's (2010) "scaffolded minds" and Heyes' (2018) "cognitive gadgets."

## Summary

Active Inference engages deeply with the 4E cognition framework but is contested by radical enactivists who reject its representationalism. The extended mind thesis creates a productive tension with Markov Blankets. Cognitive niche construction provides a formal account of how organisms shape their environments to support inference.
