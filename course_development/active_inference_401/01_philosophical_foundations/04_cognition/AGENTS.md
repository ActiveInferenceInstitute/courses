## Content Guidance

This module must engage with the most consequential philosophical debate surrounding the FEP's implications for cognitive science: does the Markov blanket formalism support or undermine the extended mind thesis? Content must reconstruct the Hohwy-Clark debate in full dialectical detail. Hohwy (2016) argues that the Markov blanket constitutes an evidentiary boundary — cognition is necessarily internal because inference operates on the internal side of the blanket. Clark (2016, 2017) responds that Markov blankets are scale-free and can nest: the relevant blanket for cognitive analysis need not be the skull but could encompass organism-plus-tool systems. Kirchhoff (2018) develops a third position using the concept of "nested Markov blankets" to argue for a form of extended inference that respects the formalism. The module must also address the parity principle (Clark & Chalmers, 1998), epistemic actions (Kirsh & Maglio, 1994), and Menary's cognitive integration as background frameworks, evaluating how the FEP transforms rather than merely continues these debates.

## Key Concepts

- **Clark's extended mind thesis**: The claim that cognitive processes can constitutively include elements of the environment (notebooks, smartphones, cultural practices) when they play the right functional role. The original "Otto and Inga" thought experiment and the parity principle: if an external process would count as cognitive were it happening internally, it should count as cognitive regardless of location.
- **Cognitive integration (Menary)**: A development of the extended mind thesis that emphasizes not parity but *integration* — the way external vehicles are incorporated into cognitive routines through enculturation and practice. Potentially more compatible with the FEP than parity-based arguments because it foregrounds the dynamics of coupling rather than static functional equivalence.
- **Where cognition ends — the Hohwy-Clark debate**: Hohwy's argument that the Markov blanket is an "evidentiary boundary" making cognition necessarily internal (the skull-bound view), versus Clark's argument that blankets are observer-relative and scale-free, allowing cognitive boundaries to extend. This is not merely a terminological dispute but a deep disagreement about what the FEP's formalism *shows* about the nature of mind.
- **Epistemic actions**: Actions performed not to achieve a practical goal but to simplify or transform a cognitive task (Kirsh & Maglio, 1994). In FEP terms, these are actions that reduce expected free energy primarily through their epistemic (information-gaining) component rather than their pragmatic (preference-satisfying) component. Do epistemic actions directed at external structures constitute extended cognition?
- **The parity principle and its critics**: The original formulation (Clark & Chalmers, 1998) and the "cognitive bloat" objection (Adams & Aizawa, 2008). Does the FEP provide principled criteria for determining which external processes are genuinely cognitive, thereby avoiding the bloat problem?

## Key References

- **Clark, A. & Chalmers, D. (1998)**: "The extended mind." The foundational paper for the extended mind thesis. Introduces the parity principle and the Otto/Inga thought experiment. Essential background for understanding what is at stake in the debate about Markov blankets and cognitive boundaries.
- **Clark, A. (2016)**: *Surfing Uncertainty*. Clark's synthesis of predictive processing with his longstanding extended cognition program. Argues that prediction-driven brains are naturally open to environmental scaffolding and that the Markov blanket does not entail internalism.
- **Kirchhoff, M. (2018)**: "The body as part of the Markov blanket." Develops the argument that Markov blankets can nest and that the relevant blanket for cognitive analysis may encompass body-environment systems. Provides the most technically informed defense of extended active inference.
- **Menary, R. (2007)**: *Cognitive Integration: Mind and Cognition Unbounded*. Develops an alternative to parity-based extended mind arguments that emphasizes the transformative integration of external vehicles into cognitive practices. Relevant because integration-based arguments may sit more comfortably with the FEP's dynamics than static parity claims.
- **Hohwy, J. (2016)**: "The self-evidencing brain." Argues that the Markov blanket formalism supports an internalist, skull-bound view of cognition because the blanket creates an evidentiary boundary that separates the inferring system from its environment. The central philosophical foil for extended readings.

## Prerequisite Modules

Module 03 (Perception) must be completed first. The analysis of perception as inference established in Module 03 — including the debate between direct and indirect realism, and the status of the Markov blanket as an epistemic boundary — is directly presupposed. The cognition module extends the question from "is perception inference?" to "where does inference stop?" and this extension requires the perceptual groundwork.

## Cross-Unit Connections

- **Advanced Theory (Module 04)**: The theory treatment covers variational methods — mean-field approximation, Bethe free energy, message passing — providing the mathematical machinery of approximate inference. The philosophical module should ask whether the choice of variational family (mean-field vs. structured) has philosophical implications: does a mean-field assumption (factored posterior) entail a modular view of cognition, and does a structured approximation better capture the integrated character of conscious experience?
- **Neuroscientific Frontiers (Module 04)**: The neuroscience treatment examines prefrontal hierarchies, precision, and working memory — the neural substrate of higher cognition. The philosophical module should ask whether the neural localization of these functions supports internalism (cognition is where the neurons are) or whether neural evidence is neutral on the extended cognition question (the neural system might function as part of a larger cognitive system).
- **Research Methods (Module 04)**: The methods treatment covers Bayesian model comparison and structure learning. The philosophical module should connect model comparison to the cognition debate: when we compare models of cognitive systems, are we adjudicating between theories of internal computation, or could a "model" of cognition legitimately include environmental structure as part of the generative model?
