# Practice Quiz: Philosophical Foundations

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. How does Active Inference dissolve the mind-body problem?
A) By proving the mind is identical to the brain (identity theory)
B) By showing that internal and external states are conditionally separated by a Markov Blanket, making the distinction statistical rather than ontological
C) By eliminating the concept of mind entirely (eliminative materialism)
D) By treating mind and body as parallel but non-interacting substances

2. Which philosophical tradition is MOST closely aligned with Active Inference's action-oriented epistemology?
A) Cartesian rationalism
B) British empiricism
C) American pragmatism (Peirce, James, Dewey)
D) Logical positivism

3. What does Active Inference mean by "self-evidencing"?
A) The agent has direct access to its own mental states
B) The agent gathers evidence to confirm its prior beliefs (confirmation bias)
C) The agent's very persistence provides evidence for its own generative model
D) The agent can prove its own existence through introspection

4. How does Active Inference handle the problem of other minds?
A) By denying that other minds exist
B) By performing inference about the generative models of other agents ("active inference about active inference")
C) By assuming all agents share the same generative model
D) By using the argument from analogy exclusively

5. What is the philosophical status of the C vector (preferences) in Active Inference?
A) It provides categorical moral imperatives
B) It bridges the is-ought gap by grounding normativity in biology
C) It specifies what the agent expects to observe, not what it should observe, leaving the is-ought gap intact
D) It eliminates the need for ethics entirely

6. How does Active Inference relate to Kuhn's account of scientific revolutions?
A) It contradicts Kuhn by showing that science is purely cumulative
B) It formalizes paradigm shifts as Bayesian Model Selection or Bayesian Model Reduction
C) It proves that scientific revolutions are irrational
D) It has no relationship to philosophy of science

7. Which philosophical position about perception does Active Inference most closely adopt?
A) Naïve direct realism
B) Indirect representationalism (Locke's veil of ideas)
C) A middle path — "enacted directness" or "cybernetic realism" — where perception is model-mediated but action-coupled
D) Eliminativism about perceptual experience

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Compare the FEP's circular epistemology (the model defines what counts as evidence, and evidence updates the model) with Heidegger's hermeneutic circle and Maturana & Varela's autopoiesis. Where do these comparisons illuminate and where do they break down?

2. Is the Free Energy Principle falsifiable? Argue both sides and explain what kind of principle the FEP is (empirical law, transcendental argument, or normative framework).

3. How does Active Inference account for the emergence of social institutions and norms? Explain using the concepts of shared generative models, cultural Markov Blankets, and collective free energy minimization.
