## Learning Goals

1. **Connect** Active Inference to the American pragmatist tradition (James, Dewey, Peirce), showing how the primacy of action over representation echoes pragmatist commitments
2. **Analyze** the philosophical claim that action is more fundamental than representation: evaluate whether expected free energy minimization constitutes a pragmatist theory of meaning
3. **Formalize** the relationship between Gibsonian affordances and expected free energy, demonstrating how affordances emerge as policies with low expected free energy given the agent's generative model
4. **Evaluate** the implications of Active Inference for the philosophy of action: does minimizing free energy through action constitute genuine agency, or merely sophisticated mechanism?

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with American pragmatism (Peirce's semiotics, James's radical empiricism, Dewey's transactionalism)
- Understanding of expected free energy and its decomposition into pragmatic and epistemic value
- Knowledge of affordance theory and ecological psychology
