## Learning Goals

1. **Analyze** the normative implications of Active Inference: evaluate whether the FEP commits the naturalistic fallacy by deriving "ought" from "is," or whether it provides a descriptive framework that is normatively neutral
2. **Formalize** the relationship between prior preferences (the C vector), values, and ethics: show how the expected free energy framework encodes teleological structure without requiring external goal specification
3. **Evaluate** Active Inference's implications for AI ethics, examining how preference specification in artificial agents relates to value alignment, corrigibility, and the control problem
4. **Synthesize** the entire Philosophical Foundations track into a unified assessment of the FEP's philosophical significance, identifying the strongest contributions and most pressing unresolved questions

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level ethics and metaethics (consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics, moral naturalism)
- Understanding of expected free energy and its decomposition into pragmatic and epistemic components
- Familiarity with AI safety and value alignment literature
