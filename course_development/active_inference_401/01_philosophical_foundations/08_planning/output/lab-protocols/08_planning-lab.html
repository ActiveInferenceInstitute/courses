Lab: Ethics, Values, and the Normative Dimension

Learning Goal: Apply philosophical analysis to the ethical implications of Active Inference.

Part 1: The Is-Ought Analysis

Exercise: Evaluate three attempts to bridge the is-ought gap using Active Inference:

Attempt Argument Strength (1-5) Weakness
Autopoietic values: "To be a living system is to value homeostasis — existence itself grounds values" Bridges is-ought through identity: being = valuing The transition from "maintains homeostasis" to "ought to maintain homeostasis" is unclear
Epistemic imperative: "A system that minimizes expected free energy must be curious and open-minded — intellectual virtue is built into the math" EFE's epistemic term grounds a virtue (openness to evidence) Curiosity can serve bad ends; openness ≠ goodness
Coherence norm: "Incoherent preferences generate unnecessary suffering (high free energy) — coherence is inherently preferable" Internal consistency = well-being; inconsistency = suffering Many coherent systems are morally monstrous (a perfectly coherent psychopath)

Write a 400-word analysis: Can any of these arguments successfully bridge the is-ought gap? If not, what does this tell us about the limits of Active Inference as an ethical framework?

Part 2: The C Vector Debate

Learning Goal: Analyze the philosophical status of preferences.

Exercise: Consider these scenarios:

Scenario A: An agent's C vector encodes strong preferences for helping others, learning, and social connection. The agent acts accordingly and experiences low free energy.

Scenario B: An agent's C vector encodes strong preferences for dominating others, hoarding resources, and avoiding uncertainty. The agent acts accordingly and experiences low free energy.

Both agents are equally good at minimizing free energy. Both are "rational" within their own models.

  1. Is there any basis within Active Inference to say Agent A's preferences are better than Agent B's?
  2. If we invoke external criteria (evolutionary fitness, social harmony, human flourishing), are these part of Active Inference or supplements to it?
  3. How does this relate to the philosophical debate between moral realism and moral relativism?

Part 3: AI Ethics Case Study

Learning Goal: Apply Active Inference to a concrete AI ethics problem.

Exercise: You are designing an Active Inference-based AI assistant. Address these design decisions:

  1. Value specification: How do you set the C vector? Who decides what the AI should prefer?
  2. Autonomy: Should the AI's generative model be allowed to develop its own preferences through learning? What are the risks?
  3. Transparency: How does Active Inference's interpretability help or hinder ethical oversight?
  4. Moral status: If the AI develops self-modeling capabilities (Module 02), does it acquire moral claims? Should we care about its "free energy"?
  5. Shutdown problem: An Active Inference agent that models its own continued existence may resist shutdown (shutting down = high surprise). How do you handle this?

Write a 400-word ethical analysis of your design choices.
