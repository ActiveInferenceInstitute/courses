## Content Guidance

This module must confront the central philosophical challenge posed by the FEP's treatment of agency: if any system at NESS with a Markov blanket can be described *as if* it performs inference, what distinguishes genuine agents from mere systems? Content must engage with the "rock problem" (the objection that the FEP's formalism applies even to trivially simple systems) and evaluate proposed solutions from the enactivist tradition (Barandiaran's autonomy conditions, Di Paolo's adaptivity criterion, Froese and Di Paolo's participatory sense-making). The module must examine whether the gap between possessing a Markov blanket and being an agent is a matter of degree (a continuum of agency) or kind (a categorical threshold), and what philosophical commitments each answer entails. The intentionality debate — whether the FEP's "beliefs" are genuinely intentional states or merely mathematical parameters with a misleading label — must be treated as a central question, not a peripheral concern.

## Key Concepts

- **Agency and the FEP**: The tension between the FEP's universality (applicable to any system at NESS) and the intuition that agency is a distinctive achievement. The "mere description" objection: does the FEP *explain* agency or merely *redescribe* the persistence of certain systems in inferential vocabulary?
- **Minimal cognition and the life-mind continuity thesis**: The enactivist claim that cognition is continuous with life (Thompson, 2007) and that even the simplest organisms are cognitive agents. How does this relate to the FEP's gradient from simple to complex inference?
- **Barandiaran's autonomy conditions**: Three individually necessary conditions for genuine agency — (1) individuality (a self-constituted identity), (2) interactional asymmetry (the system modulates its coupling with the environment, not merely reacting), (3) normativity (the system's behavior is evaluable as better or worse relative to its own viability). The question is whether these conditions are captured by or additional to the Markov blanket formalism.
- **The intentionality debate**: Whether the recognition density $q(s)$ constitutes a genuine belief (with intentional content, truth conditions, and normative force) or is merely a mathematical object that can be interpreted "as if" it were a belief. This engages with the broader debate between realist, instrumentalist, and deflationary readings of the FEP.
- **Agent vs. mere system**: The spectrum from thermostats to humans. Where does the FEP draw the line, and is that line philosophically defensible? Kirchhoff and Froese (2017) argue for a strong demarcation based on sense-making; others (e.g., Ramstead et al., 2020) favor a graded view.

## Key References

- **Barandiaran, X., Di Paolo, E., & Rohde, M. (2009)**: "Defining agency: Individuality, normativity, asymmetry, and spatio-temporality in action." Provides the most rigorous enactivist account of what distinguishes agents from non-agents, articulating conditions that go beyond the Markov blanket formalism.
- **Froese, T. & Di Paolo, E. (2011)**: "The enactive approach: Theoretical sketches from cell to society." Develops the participatory sense-making framework and argues that agency requires more than statistical independence — it requires the capacity for sense-making, for mattering.
- **Kirchhoff, M. & Froese, T. (2017)**: "Where there is life there is mind: In support of a strong life-mind continuity thesis." Argues that the FEP, properly interpreted through an enactivist lens, supports the view that all living systems are minded, but that this requires reading "inference" in a non-intellectualist way.
- **Moreno, A. & Mossio, M. (2015)**: *Biological Autonomy: A Philosophical and Theoretical Enquiry*. Develops the organizational account of biological autonomy, providing a framework for understanding why some systems are genuinely autonomous (self-maintaining, self-producing) and others merely persist.

## Prerequisite Modules

Module 01 (Systems) must be completed first. The concept of a self-organizing system at NESS, the Markov blanket formalism, and the autopoietic tradition established in Module 01 are presupposed. Without the ontological groundwork of what constitutes a system, the question of what distinguishes an *agent* among systems cannot be properly framed.

## Cross-Unit Connections

- **Advanced Theory (Module 02)**: The theory treatment formalizes POMDPs and belief MDPs on statistical manifolds, providing the mathematical structure of agent models. The philosophical treatment here interrogates what this formalism *means* — whether the POMDP framework's assumption of a utility function (encoded in the C vector) smuggles in a notion of preference that the FEP was supposed to derive rather than presuppose.
- **Neuroscientific Frontiers (Module 02)**: The neuroscience treatment examines the neural architecture that implements active inference agents — prefrontal-basal ganglia circuits, dopamine as precision. The philosophical module should ask whether neural implementation is necessary for agency (biocentrism) or whether any system that satisfies the right functional conditions qualifies (functionalism about agency).
- **Research Methods (Module 02)**: The methods treatment covers building and validating agent models in PyMDP. The philosophical module should clarify what it means for a computational model to "capture" agency — the relationship between simulation and genuine agency, and the philosophical limits of agent-based modeling.
