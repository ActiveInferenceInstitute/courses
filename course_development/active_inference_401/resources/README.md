## Mathematical Prerequisites

This course assumes graduate-level proficiency in the following areas:

- **Measure theory and probability**: Sigma-algebras, Radon-Nikodym derivatives, conditional expectation, convergence theorems
- **Differential geometry**: Riemannian manifolds, connections, curvature, fiber bundles, Lie groups
- **Category theory**: Functors, natural transformations, adjunctions, monoidal categories, lenses and optics
- **Information theory**: Relative entropy, Fisher information, rate-distortion theory, maximum entropy methods
- **Stochastic processes**: Langevin dynamics, Fokker-Planck equations, Ito calculus, ergodic theory
- **Variational calculus**: Euler-Lagrange equations, functional derivatives, calculus of variations on manifolds

## Open Problems for Dissertation Research

1. **Category-theoretic unification**: Formalize the full Active Inference framework within the language of polynomial functors and dependent lenses (cf. St. Clere Smithe, 2020; Smithe, 2021).
2. **Renormalization of generative models**: Develop a rigorous RG flow for hierarchical generative models and characterize fixed points corresponding to cognitive universality classes.
3. **Bayesian mechanics on non-trivial geometries**: Extend Bayesian mechanics (Sakthivadivel, 2022) to systems on manifolds with non-trivial topology and curvature.
4. **Quantum Active Inference**: Formalize quantum generalizations of the free energy principle using density operator formalism and quantum channels (cf. Fields et al., 2022).
5. **Multi-scale Markov blankets**: Resolve the "blankets all the way down" problem with rigorous scale-separation arguments from renormalization theory.
