# Glossary: Active Inference 401: Advanced PhD Seminar

> Canonical definitions for all technical terms used in the curriculum.
> All modules must use these definitions consistently.

### Active Inference
A corollary of the Free Energy Principle applied to agents: biological (and artificial) systems act to minimize expected free energy, thereby jointly performing perception (state estimation), action (environmental intervention), and learning (model updating). Formalized as planning as inference over policies $\pi$ that minimize $G(\pi)$. See Friston et al. (2017); Parr et al. (2022).

### Autopoiesis
The self-producing organization of a living system, whereby the system's components recursively generate and sustain the network of processes that produced them. Introduced by Maturana & Varela (1980). In FEP terms, autopoietic organization is related to the maintenance of a Markov blanket over time.

### Bayesian Mechanics
An emerging branch of mathematical physics that studies the dynamics of systems whose internal states can be interpreted as parameterizing probability distributions over external states. Grounded in NESS formalism: if a system possesses a particular partition, its internal dynamics implement approximate Bayesian inference. See Da Costa et al. (2021); Sakthivadivel (2022).

### Bayesian Model Reduction (BMR)
A post-hoc method for comparing reduced models (with parameters set to their priors) against a full model, using the free energy difference as an approximation to the log Bayes factor. Avoids refitting. See Friston et al. (2016).

### Bayesian Model Selection (BMS)
The practice of comparing generative models using their model evidence $p(o \mid m)$. At the group level: random-effects BMS uses the Dirichlet distribution over model frequencies. Key metric: protected exceedance probability. See Stephan et al. (2009); Rigoux et al. (2014).

### Bethe Free Energy
A variational free energy approximation that relaxes the mean-field assumption by allowing pairwise dependencies between variables. Defined on a factor graph. The fixed points of belief propagation correspond to stationary points of the Bethe free energy. See Yedidia et al. (2005).

### Belief Propagation (BP)
A message passing algorithm on factor graphs that computes marginal posterior distributions. Exact on trees; approximate on loopy graphs (where it minimizes Bethe free energy). Related to predictive coding under Gaussian assumptions. See Pearl (1988); Wainwright & Jordan (2008).

### Blanket States
The union of sensory states $s$ and active states $a$ in the Markov blanket formalism: $b = (s, a)$. Blanket states mediate all statistical coupling between internal states $\mu$ and external states $\eta$. See Pearl (1988); Friston (2019).

### Canonical Microcircuit
A cortical circuit motif hypothesized to implement predictive coding, with superficial pyramidal cells encoding prediction errors (ascending) and deep pyramidal cells encoding predictions (descending). Precision is modulated by gain control on error units. See Bastos et al. (2012); Shipp (2016).

### Consciousness (in FEP context)
An open problem. Proposals include: (1) consciousness arises from high-level self-modeling within the generative model (Seth, 2021), (2) consciousness requires temporal depth and counterfactual richness (Friston et al., 2021), (3) consciousness is the "controlled hallucination" generated by predictive processing (Clark, 2019). No consensus.

### Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM)
A framework for Bayesian inference on coupled dynamical systems, typically applied to neuroimaging data. Specifies a generative model of observed brain responses, inverts it using variational Bayes, and compares models using free energy. See Friston et al. (2003); Stephan et al. (2009).

### Empirical Bayes
A hierarchical Bayesian scheme where hyperpriors are estimated from data rather than fixed a priori. In cortical hierarchies, lower-level posteriors serve as empirical priors for higher levels. Connects to predictive coding as hierarchical empirical Bayes. See Friston (2005).

### Epistemic Communities
Groups of agents that share generative models and coordinate inference through cultural practices, institutions, and shared narratives. Formalized via shared Markov blankets and cultural affordances. See Ramstead et al. (2016); Veissiere et al. (2020).

### Expected Free Energy (EFE)
A functional of policies $G(\pi) = \sum_\tau G(\pi, \tau)$ that decomposes into: Risk (pragmatic value) = $D_{KL}[q(o_\tau \mid \pi) \| p(o_\tau)]$ and Ambiguity (epistemic value) = $\mathbb{E}_{q(s_\tau \mid \pi)}[H[p(o_\tau \mid s_\tau)]]$. Policies are selected via $p(\pi) = \sigma(-\gamma G(\pi))$. See Friston et al. (2015); Parr et al. (2022, Ch. 8).

### Fokker-Planck Equation
A PDE describing the time evolution of the probability density $p(x, t)$ of a stochastic process: $\partial_t p = -\nabla \cdot (fp) + \frac{1}{2}\nabla \cdot (\Gamma \nabla p)$, where $f$ is the drift and $\Gamma$ the diffusion tensor. At NESS, $\partial_t p^* = 0$. See Pavliotis (2014).

### Generative Model
A probabilistic model $p(o, s \mid \theta)$ that specifies how hidden states $s$ generate observations $o$ given parameters $\theta$. In active inference, the agent's generative model is inverted (via variational inference) to infer hidden states, and its predictions are fulfilled through action.

### Generative Process
The actual data-generating process in the environment, from which the agent receives observations. Distinguished from the generative model (which is the agent's internal model of the process). The gap between them drives learning and adaptation.

### Information Geometry
The study of probability distributions as points on a Riemannian manifold $\mathcal{M}$, equipped with the Fisher information metric $\Gamma$. Natural gradient descent follows geodesics on $\mathcal{M}$. In active inference, internal state dynamics trace paths on the statistical manifold. See Amari (2016); Ay et al. (2017).

### KL Control
A framework for optimal control where the cost function includes a KL divergence penalty between the controlled and uncontrolled dynamics. Renders the Bellman equation linear (linearly solvable MDPs). Active inference's EFE can be seen as a form of KL control. See Todorov (2009); Kappen (2005).

### Langevin Dynamics
A stochastic differential equation of the form $dx = f(x)dt + \sigma dW$ where $f$ is the drift, $\sigma$ is the diffusion coefficient, and $W$ is a Wiener process. The FEP assumes systems follow Langevin dynamics, with the drift decomposable into solenoidal and dissipative components.

### Markov Blanket
The set of nodes in a Bayesian network that renders a target node conditionally independent of all other nodes. In the FEP: the blanket of internal states $\mu$ comprises sensory states $s$ and active states $a$, such that $\mu \perp \eta \mid b$. Named after Pearl (1988). See Friston (2019).

### Mean-Field Approximation
A variational method that assumes the approximate posterior factorizes: $q(s) = \prod_i q_i(s_i)$. Each factor is updated while holding others fixed, yielding coordinate ascent on the ELBO. Exact when the true posterior factorizes; otherwise a lower bound. See Wainwright & Jordan (2008).

### Message Passing Algorithms
A family of algorithms (belief propagation, variational message passing, expectation propagation) that perform inference on graphical models by passing local messages between nodes. Predictive coding can be derived as Gaussian message passing on a hierarchical factor graph. See Dauwels (2007).

### Model Evidence
The marginal likelihood $p(o \mid m) = \int p(o, s \mid m) ds$, which scores how well a model $m$ explains observations $o$, integrating over all hidden states. VFE provides a tractable lower bound: $\ln p(o \mid m) \geq -F$. Used for Bayesian model comparison.

### Non-Equilibrium Steady State (NESS)
A stationary probability distribution $p^*$ for a dissipative stochastic system, maintained by a balance between drift and diffusion, with non-zero probability current (unlike thermodynamic equilibrium). The FEP requires systems to possess a NESS with a particular partition.

### Particular Partition
The decomposition of a system's states into particular states $(\mu, b)$ and external states $\eta$, defined by the conditional independence structure of the NESS density: $p^*(\eta, s, a, \mu) = p^*(\eta \mid s) \cdot p^*(\mu \mid s, a) \cdot p^*(s, a)$. This ensures internal states can be interpreted as parameterizing beliefs about external states. See Friston (2019).

### Path Integral Control
A formulation of stochastic optimal control using Feynman-Kac path integrals. The optimal control is expressed as an expectation over noise-driven trajectories, avoiding the need to solve the HJB equation directly. Connected to active inference via KL control. See Kappen (2005).

### POMDP (Partially Observable Markov Decision Process)
A formal framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty: the agent maintains beliefs over hidden states, selects actions via policies, and receives observations. Active inference uses POMDPs with policy selection via EFE minimization rather than reward maximization.

### Precision
The inverse of variance: $\Pi = \Sigma^{-1}$. In active inference, precision weights prediction errors in message passing (high precision = high influence). Precision is modulated by attention (psychologically) and neuromodulators (neurally: dopamine modulates reward precision, acetylcholine modulates sensory precision).

### Precision Weighting
The process by which prediction errors are scaled by their estimated precision before propagating through the generative model hierarchy. Implements gain control: high-precision errors dominate inference; low-precision errors are suppressed. Neural substrate: synaptic gain modulation.

### Predictive Coding
A hierarchical inference scheme where each level predicts the activity of the level below, and prediction errors propagate upward. Under Gaussian assumptions, equivalent to variational message passing on a hierarchical generative model. Originally proposed by Rao & Ballard (1999); formalized within FEP by Friston (2005).

### Scale-Free Inference
The conjecture that the same variational principles (VFE minimization, EFE-driven policy selection) apply at all scales of biological organization: from single cells to neural circuits to organisms to social groups. An open problem: whether the formalism is explanatory or merely descriptive at each scale. See Ramstead et al. (2023).

### Shared Markov Blankets
A formal construction for multi-agent active inference: when two agents' blanket states overlap, they form a coupled dynamical system with shared statistical dependencies. Enables alignment of generative models through interaction. See Friston & Frith (2015).

### Solenoidal Flow
The component of a system's dynamics generated by the antisymmetric matrix $Q$ in the Helmholtz decomposition of the flow: $f = (Q - \Gamma)\nabla \ln p^*$. Solenoidal flow is divergence-free and does not change the steady-state density; it enables oscillatory, exploratory behavior.

### Sophisticated Inference
A recursive form of active inference where the agent considers the consequences of future inference steps (i.e., how future observations will update beliefs) when evaluating policies. Implements planning with epistemic depth. See Friston et al. (2021).

### Stigmergy
Indirect coordination between agents through environmental modification (e.g., pheromone trails in ants). In active inference terms: agents modify their shared environment, which constitutes part of each agent's Markov blanket, thereby coordinating inference without direct communication.

### Stochastic Optimal Control
The problem of selecting controls (actions) to minimize an expected cost functional for a system governed by SDEs. Connected to active inference through KL control, path integrals, and the identification of policy selection with inference over future trajectories. See Todorov (2009).

### Variational Free Energy (VFE)
An upper bound on surprisal (negative log evidence): $F = D_{KL}[q(s) \| p(s \mid o)] - \ln p(o) \geq -\ln p(o)$. Equivalently: $F = \mathbb{E}_q[\ln q(s) - \ln p(o, s)]$. Minimizing $F$ w.r.t. $q$ makes $q$ approximate the true posterior; the residual equals the KL divergence from the true posterior.

### Variational Laplacian
A Laplace approximation to the variational posterior: $q(s) = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$ where $\mu$ is the mode of the variational energy and $\Sigma$ is the inverse curvature at the mode. Computationally efficient; widely used in DCM and continuous active inference.

### Variational Message Passing (VMP)
A message passing algorithm for conjugate-exponential models that iteratively updates each factor of the approximate posterior using messages from neighboring factors. A generalization of mean-field variational Bayes to factor graphs. See Dauwels (2007); Winn & Bishop (2005).
