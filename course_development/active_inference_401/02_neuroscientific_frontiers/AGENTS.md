# Course AGENTS: Neuroscientific Frontiers

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course README](./README.md) | [Curriculum AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Identity

- **Course**: Neuroscientific Frontiers
- **Number**: 2
- **Perspective**: Predictive processing, precision, neural dynamics
- **Lab Type**: Paper Review
- **Audience**: PhD students and researchers in neuroscience, computational neuroscience, and cognitive neuroscience
- **Tone**: Empirical, evidence-driven, mechanistically specific. Claims must be grounded in experimental data. Neural circuit models must specify cell types, laminar organization, and connectivity patterns. Effect sizes and replication status should be noted.

## Core Question

What is the *neural evidence* for Active Inference? Which brain mechanisms implement variational inference, precision weighting, prediction error signaling, and policy selection? Where does the evidence strongly support the framework, and where does it remain speculative?

## Intellectual Traditions

This unit draws on and engages critically with:

- **Predictive Processing**: Rao & Ballard (1999), Friston (2005), Clark (2013) — perception as hierarchical Bayesian inference
- **Canonical Microcircuits**: Bastos et al. (2012), Shipp (2016) — cortical circuit motifs implementing predictive coding
- **Precision and Neuromodulation**: Feldman & Friston (2010), Kanai et al. (2015) — attention as precision optimization, neuromodulatory control of gain
- **Motor Active Inference**: Adams et al. (2013), Friston et al. (2010) — motor control as prediction error minimization at the spinal level
- **Computational Psychiatry**: Stephan et al. (2009), Friston et al. (2014) — aberrant precision as a model of psychopathology
- **Neural Oscillations and Dynamics**: Bastos et al. (2020), Breakspear (2017) — gamma/beta as prediction error/prediction signals

## Key Journals

*Nature Neuroscience*, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, *Neuron*, *Current Biology*, *Journal of Neuroscience*, *Cerebral Cortex*, *NeuroImage*, *PLOS Computational Biology*, *Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience*

## Conventions

All modules in this course must:

1. Use language appropriate for PhD students with strong neuroscience training
2. Ground every claim in specific experimental evidence — cite studies, paradigms, species, methods, and effect sizes
3. Include Paper Review lab activities requiring structured critique of empirical papers (methods, statistics, interpretation, replicability)
4. Specify neural mechanisms at the level of circuits, cell types, and neuromodulatory systems — avoid vague "the brain does X" claims
5. Distinguish between well-supported neural implementations and speculative/computational-level proposals
6. Note when evidence comes from animal models vs. human neuroimaging, and flag translational limitations
7. Adhere to notation standards in [../resources/notation_table.md](../resources/notation_table.md) — link mathematical symbols to measurable neural quantities (firing rates, BOLD signal, LFP power)
8. Cross-reference the shared [../resources/glossary.md](../resources/glossary.md)
9. Link to the corresponding modules in other units

## Assessment Philosophy

Paper Reviews should require students to:
- Evaluate the experimental design (controls, confounds, statistical power)
- Assess whether the data actually support the theoretical interpretation offered
- Identify alternative explanations not considered by the authors
- Propose follow-up experiments that would discriminate between competing accounts
- Evaluate replication status and generalizability across species/methods
