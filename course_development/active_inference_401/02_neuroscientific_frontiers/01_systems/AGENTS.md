## Content Guidance

This module must examine the brain as a physical system operating at non-equilibrium steady state (NESS). It must cover the empirical evidence for criticality in neural systems — including neuronal avalanches exhibiting power-law distributions (Beggs & Plenz, 2003), long-range temporal correlations in neural activity, and scale-free dynamics measured via MEG, EEG, and multi-electrode arrays. The module should detail how neural oscillations relate to attractor dynamics in large-scale cortical networks, and evaluate the specific evidence that cortical activity operates near a critical phase transition between ordered and disordered regimes. The criticality hypothesis must be evaluated critically: address alternative explanations for power-law scaling (e.g., quasi-criticality, Touboul & Destexhe, 2017), the difficulty of distinguishing true criticality from near-critical dynamics in finite neural systems, and the methodological challenges of fitting power laws to neural data.

## Key Concepts

- **Neuronal avalanches and criticality**: Power-law distributed cascades of neural activity (exponent ~3/2 for size, ~2 for duration) observed in cortical slice cultures, anesthetized animals, and awake primates — interpreted as evidence for self-organized criticality
- **Neural oscillations as attractor dynamics**: Alpha, theta, gamma rhythms as signatures of distinct attractor states in cortical networks; multistability and phase transitions between oscillatory regimes
- **NESS in neural systems**: Cortical dynamics as dissipative, far-from-equilibrium processes maintained by metabolic energy flux; the brain as a system with non-zero entropy production
- **Power-law scaling and 1/f noise**: Long-range temporal correlations in EEG/MEG power spectra; the relationship between 1/f scaling, criticality, and information processing capacity

## Prerequisite Modules

None — this is the entry point for the Neuroscientific Frontiers unit. Students should have completed introductory coursework in systems neuroscience and dynamical systems theory.

## Cross-Unit Connections

- **Advanced Theory (Module 01)**: The Theory treatment provides the formal NESS formalism — Fokker-Planck equations, Langevin dynamics, and the derivation of steady-state densities from first principles. The neuroscience treatment here grounds those abstractions in measured neural quantities (LFP power spectra, avalanche distributions, fMRI BOLD dynamics).
- **Philosophical Foundations (Module 01)**: The Philosophy treatment examines the ontology of self-organizing systems (autopoiesis, organizational closure). The neuroscience treatment here asks whether specific neural systems satisfy these organizational criteria and what the empirical signatures would be.
- **Research Methods (Module 01)**: The Methods treatment covers how to empirically identify and model self-organizing systems — SDE parameter estimation, model identifiability tests. The neuroscience treatment here provides the neural datasets and paradigms to which those methods are applied.
