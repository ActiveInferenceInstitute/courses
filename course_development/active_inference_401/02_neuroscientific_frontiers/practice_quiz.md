# Practice Quiz: Neuroscientific Frontiers

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In the canonical microcircuit for predictive coding (Bastos et al., 2012), which cortical layers generate prediction errors?
A) Layers I and II (superficial layers)
B) Layers II/III (superficial pyramidal neurons)
C) Layer IV (granular layer)
D) Layers V/VI (deep pyramidal neurons)

2. What is the proposed role of gamma oscillations in predictive coding?
A) They carry top-down predictions from higher to lower cortical areas
B) They carry bottom-up prediction errors from lower to higher cortical areas
C) They implement precision modulation by gating irrelevant information
D) They synchronize the default mode network during rest

3. Which neuromodulator is most closely associated with the precision of sensory prediction errors and attentional gain?
A) Dopamine
B) Norepinephrine
C) Acetylcholine
D) Serotonin

4. How does Active Inference explain the rubber hand illusion?
A) It is a failure of visual processing in V1
B) The brain updates its body ownership model (self-model) to minimize multisensory prediction errors
C) It proves that body ownership is hard-wired and cannot be altered by inference
D) The illusion occurs because the brain cannot integrate proprioceptive signals

5. What is the HIPPEA (High Inflexible Precision of Prediction Errors in Autism) hypothesis?
A) Autism results from abnormally low precision on prediction errors, causing insensitivity
B) Autism results from inflexibly high precision on prediction errors, causing enhanced detail perception and sensory overwhelm
C) Autism results from disruption of the default mode network
D) Autism results from excessive dopaminergic signaling in the striatum

6. How does Active Inference reconceptualize motor control?
A) As descending motor commands from M1 to spinal motor neurons
B) As proprioceptive prediction error minimization — the motor cortex sends predictions, and spinal reflexes resolve the error
C) As a feedforward inverse model that computes joint angles from desired trajectories
D) As random exploration that is gradually shaped by reward

7. What is the primary neural mechanism through which sleep implements Bayesian Model Reduction?
A) Increased dopaminergic activity during REM sleep
B) Synaptic homeostasis (downscaling), sharp-wave ripple replay, and spindle-slow oscillation coupling
C) Activation of the prefrontal cortex during dreaming
D) Suppression of all neural activity to allow metabolic recovery

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how the four neuromodulatory systems (dopamine, norepinephrine, acetylcholine, serotonin) each implement a distinct aspect of precision weighting. For each, specify what computational quantity it modulates and what happens when it is disrupted.

2. Compare the Active Inference model of schizophrenia (aberrant sensory precision) with the Active Inference model of depression (pessimistic priors). How do these distinct computational dysfunctions produce different symptom profiles?

3. Design a neuroscientific experiment to test the prediction that hippocampal replay implements prospective evaluation of candidate policies. What signals would you measure, what task would you use, and what results would confirm or disconfirm the hypothesis?
