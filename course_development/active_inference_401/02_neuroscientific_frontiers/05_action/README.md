## Learning Goals

1. **Analyze** how motor cortex and spinal cord implement active inference through proprioceptive predictions, where movement arises from descending predictions fulfilled by classical reflex arcs rather than explicit motor commands
2. **Evaluate** the roles of cerebellum (forward model, temporal prediction), basal ganglia (policy selection, action precision), and supplementary motor areas (sequential planning) within the Active Inference motor architecture
3. **Derive** the mathematical relationship between equilibrium point control, optimal feedback control, and Active Inference formulations of movement
4. **Connect** motor Active Inference to clinical motor disorders (Parkinson's disease, dystonia, functional movement disorders) and rehabilitation strategies informed by the framework

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level motor neuroscience (corticospinal tract, spinal reflexes, cerebellar circuitry, basal ganglia pathways)
- Understanding of classical motor control theories (equilibrium point, optimal feedback control, internal models)
- Familiarity with continuous-time Active Inference and generalized coordinates of motion
