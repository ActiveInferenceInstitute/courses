## Content Guidance

This module must examine the neural substrates of social inference through the lens of active inference — treating social cognition as the problem of inferring the hidden states (beliefs, intentions, goals) of other agents using one's own generative model. The mirror neuron system must be presented using Kilner et al.'s (2007) hierarchical predictive coding account: mirror neurons in premotor cortex (F5/ventral premotor in macaques, homologous regions in humans) as implementing a generative model of observed actions that generates predictions at multiple levels — the goal of the action, the motor plan, and the expected kinematic trajectory. The mirror neuron controversy must be addressed directly: the adaptation account (Cook et al., 2014), the associative learning account (Heyes, 2010), and whether single-unit "mirror" responses necessarily indicate a generative model or could arise from simpler sensorimotor associations. The mentalizing network must be covered in detail: medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) for inferring others' beliefs and intentions (including the anterior rostral mPFC for metarepresentation), temporoparietal junction (TPJ) for distinguishing self from other and computing social prediction errors, precuneus for contextual/narrative integration, and posterior superior temporal sulcus (pSTS) for biological motion processing and action understanding. Social prediction errors and their neural signatures must be addressed — including evidence from Koster-Hale & Saxe (2013) on TPJ encoding the discrepancy between expected and observed behavior of social agents. Oxytocin's role in social precision should be covered: the hypothesis that oxytocin increases the precision (gain) of social information, thereby enhancing social salience and potentially reducing social prediction errors (Shamay-Tsoory & Abu-Akel, 2016). Hyperscanning evidence from Hasson et al. (2012) and subsequent fNIRS/EEG studies should be presented as demonstrating inter-brain coupling — neural alignment between speaker and listener that may reflect the alignment of generative models during communication. The module must critically evaluate the inferential gap between neural correlation patterns observed in hyperscanning and the strong theoretical claim of "shared generative models."

## Key Concepts

- **Mirror neuron system as social generative model**: Kilner et al.'s hierarchical account — mirror neurons encoding predictions about observed actions at the goal level (why), the motor plan level (how), and the kinematic level (what specific movements); the predictive coding interpretation vs. direct-matching and associative learning alternatives
- **Mentalizing network and social prediction errors**: mPFC for belief/intention inference, TPJ for self-other distinction and social prediction error computation, pSTS for action observation — together implementing a hierarchical generative model of other agents' mental states; evidence from false-belief tasks, social prediction error paradigms, and TMS disruption studies
- **Oxytocin and social precision**: The social salience hypothesis — oxytocin increasing the precision of social sensory signals, thereby enhancing both prosocial and competitive social behavior depending on context; evidence from intranasal oxytocin studies, genetic variation in OXTR, and the relationship to autism spectrum conditions
- **Hyperscanning and inter-brain coupling**: Neural synchronization between communicating individuals (measured via fMRI, EEG, fNIRS) as potential evidence for aligned generative models; Hasson et al.'s speaker-listener coupling in temporal cortex; challenges in distinguishing genuine inter-brain coupling from shared stimulus-driven responses

## Prerequisite Modules

- Module 02 (Agents) — understanding of the neural architecture of active inference agents is required before examining how agents model each other, as social cognition fundamentally requires representing another system as an agent with its own generative model.

## Cross-Unit Connections

- **Advanced Theory (Module 07)**: The Theory treatment formalizes multi-agent active inference, shared Markov blankets, and coupled dynamical systems. The neuroscience treatment here asks what neural evidence supports the claim that brains actually perform multi-agent inference, and whether inter-brain coupling data can distinguish coupled inference from simpler coordination mechanisms.
- **Philosophical Foundations (Module 07)**: The Philosophy treatment examines shared intentionality, linguistic communication as generative model alignment, and the social Markov blanket. The neuroscience treatment here provides the mechanistic substrate: mirror neuron systems, mentalizing networks, and oxytocin modulation as the neural implementation of these philosophical constructs.
- **Research Methods (Module 07)**: The Methods treatment covers hyperscanning experimental design, computational phenotyping of social learning, and agent-based social simulation. The neuroscience treatment here provides the neural phenomena (inter-brain synchrony, social prediction errors, mentalizing network activation) that these experimental methods aim to measure and model.
