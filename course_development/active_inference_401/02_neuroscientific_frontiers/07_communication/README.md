## Learning Goals

1. **Analyze** the neural mechanisms of social inference, examining how the brain models other minds through the temporoparietal junction (belief attribution), medial prefrontal cortex (mentalizing), and superior temporal sulcus (biological motion and intention reading)
2. **Evaluate** the mirror neuron system through the lens of Active Inference, reinterpreting mirror activity as predictive social modeling rather than direct simulation, and assessing empirical evidence for this reinterpretation
3. **Formalize** language processing as hierarchical predictive inference, connecting neural evidence for syntactic prediction (left inferior frontal gyrus), semantic prediction (temporal cortex), and pragmatic inference (right hemisphere, prefrontal cortex)
4. **Design** a hyperscanning experiment to study coupled inference between interacting agents, specifying interpersonal neural synchronization measures that test the "duet for one" hypothesis

## Prerequisites

- Graduate-level social neuroscience (theory of mind networks, mirror neuron system, mentalizing circuitry)
- Understanding of language neuroscience (Broca's area, Wernicke's area, dual-stream models)
- Familiarity with multi-agent Active Inference and the concept of shared generative models
