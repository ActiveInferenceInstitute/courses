## Part 4: The Rubber Hand Illusion

> **Learning Goal:** Understand body ownership as Bayesian inference.

**Exercise**: Describe the rubber hand illusion step by step using Active Inference:

1. **Before stimulation**: The brain's body model includes the real hand. How confident is the model? (precision = high)

2. **During synchronous stroking**: Visual observation (seeing rubber hand being stroked) and tactile sensation (feeling your real hand being stroked) become correlated. What prediction error arises? How does the brain resolve the conflict?

3. **During asynchronous stroking (control condition)**: Why does the illusion NOT occur? What is different about the prediction errors?

4. **After illusion onset**: Participants report a temperature drop in the real hand. What does this reveal about the brain's proprioceptive precision weighting? (It reduces precision for the real hand because the model has "reassigned" ownership)

5. **Extension**: Design a variation of the rubber hand illusion that would test a specific Active Inference prediction about body ownership. What would you change, and what would the result tell you?
