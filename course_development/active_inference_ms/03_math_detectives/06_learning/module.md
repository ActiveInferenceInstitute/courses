## Applications

In Middle School, we see Learning manifest in:
*   **Specific Example 1**: The first time you see fractions, your brain's model says "numbers are whole things you can count." Then you encounter 1/2 and that model breaks -- a prediction error. Over weeks of practice cutting pizzas into slices and shading parts of rectangles, your brain builds a richer model where numbers can live between whole numbers. That struggle IS the learning; your old model had to break before a better one could replace it.
*   **Specific Example 2**: When you learn to solve for x in algebra, you are updating your model of what the equals sign means. Young kids think "=" means "the answer comes next," but algebra demands a new model: "=" means "both sides are balanced." Every time you subtract 3 from both sides and it works, your brain gets a small reward signal confirming the new model. Each successful problem strengthens that updated understanding.
