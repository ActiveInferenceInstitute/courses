## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In Active Inference, a "prediction" is most like which math concept?

A) A random number with no pattern
B) An educated guess based on the information you have — similar to probability
C) A number that is always exactly right
D) Something that only scientists can calculate

2. You flip a coin 10 times and get heads 8 times. Your friend says the coin must be unfair. In Active Inference terms, your friend is:

A) Ignoring the data
B) Updating their mental model because the data doesn't match the prediction of a fair coin
C) Making a prediction error on purpose
D) Not using any reasoning at all

3. A detective finds three clues at a crime scene. Each new clue helps the detective narrow down the suspects. This process is most similar to:

A) Guessing randomly
B) Bayesian updating — using new evidence to improve your best guess
C) Ignoring evidence and going with your gut
D) Memorizing all possible answers

4. If an event has a 90% chance of happening and it does happen, your brain experiences:

A) A lot of surprise
B) Very little surprise — the outcome matched the prediction
C) No prediction at all
D) Maximum prediction error

5. You notice that every Monday your math teacher gives a pop quiz. By the fourth Monday, you start studying on Sunday night. This pattern recognition is an example of:

A) Cheating
B) Your brain building a model from repeated observations and using it to predict the future
C) A coincidence
D) Your teacher telling you about the quiz

6. What does "uncertainty" mean in math terms?

A) You are 100% sure about everything
B) There is a range of possible outcomes, and you don't know exactly which one will happen
C) Numbers don't make sense
D) You can never learn anything new

7. A weather forecast says there is a 70% chance of rain. It doesn't rain. This means:

A) The forecast was totally wrong and useless
B) The forecast was a probability — 30% of the time it won't rain, and this was one of those times
C) Weather can never be predicted
D) Prediction errors never happen with weather

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Imagine you are a detective and you find a footprint at a scene. Before finding the footprint, you thought there were 5 equally likely suspects. The footprint matches only 2 of them. Explain how this **new evidence** changes your predictions. What were the chances before, and what are they now?

2. Explain the difference between **surprise** (something unexpected happening) and **uncertainty** (not knowing what will happen). Give a math-related example of each.

3. You are playing a guessing game where you have to figure out a number between 1 and 100. Your friend tells you "higher" or "lower" after each guess. What is the smartest first guess and why? How does each round of feedback help you **reduce uncertainty** about the right answer?
