# FAQ: Active Inference for Middle School

> Frequently asked questions for Grades 6-8.

## What is Active Inference?

Active Inference is a theory from neuroscience that says all living things work by making predictions about the world and then acting to make those predictions come true. Your brain is constantly guessing what's going to happen next — when you walk down a hallway, talk to a friend, or play a game. When reality doesn't match your prediction, that's called a **prediction error**, and your brain either updates its beliefs or takes action to fix the mismatch. This predict-act-update loop is happening all the time, in everything from individual cells to entire friend groups.

## Who is this curriculum for?

This curriculum is designed for **Grades 6-8** (ages 11-14). You don't need any special background in science or math. If you've ever wondered why your brain plays tricks on you, why habits are hard to break, or how you learn new skills, you already have the curiosity this course is built for.

## How should I use this curriculum?

See [Learning Pathways](./learning_pathways.md) for suggested routes. You can work through one section at a time (like starting with Real Life Skills), or you can explore the same module topic across all four sections to see how systems, agents, perception, and other ideas show up in different areas. Each module includes a lesson, study questions, a practice quiz, and a hands-on lab activity.

## What are the four sections about?

1. **Real Life Skills** — Active Inference through everyday decisions: friendships, school, social media, and planning your day.
2. **Body Science** — Active Inference through your body: how your brain, heart, immune system, and muscles all make predictions.
3. **Math Detectives** — Active Inference through probability and patterns: using numbers and clues to figure out how predictions work.
4. **Code & Create** — Active Inference through coding: build simple programs and simulations that show how prediction and action work together.

## What are the 8 modules about?

1. **Systems** — What is a system and where are its boundaries? Systems are groups of parts that work together, from your phone to your school to your body.
2. **Agents** — What makes something an agent? Agents are things that have goals, sense the world, and take actions — like you, your pet, or even a robot.
3. **Perception** — How do we sense the world? Your brain doesn't just record what's out there — it builds a best guess and fills in the gaps.
4. **Cognition** — How do we think and form beliefs? Your brain uses mental models to make sense of confusing situations and predict what comes next.
5. **Action** — How do we act on the world? Every action is an experiment — your brain testing a prediction to see if it's right.
6. **Learning** — How do we get better over time? Learning is what happens when your brain updates its models based on new information.
7. **Communication** — How do we share with others? Communication lets agents share predictions and reduce uncertainty together.
8. **Planning** — How do we think about the future? Planning means imagining different futures and picking the path that best matches your goals.

## Do I need to know any math?

Just the basics — fractions, percentages, and some simple probability (like "what are the chances?"). The Math Detectives section goes deeper into numbers, but you don't need advanced math for any section.

## What if I get stuck?

That's actually a great sign! In Active Inference, feeling confused means your brain just noticed a prediction error — and that's the first step to learning something new. Go back and re-read the module, talk it over with a classmate or teacher, or try the lab activity to see the idea in action.

## Can I do the sections in any order?

Yes. Each section covers the same eight module topics but through a different lens. Some students like to go straight through one section. Others prefer to do all four "Systems" modules, then all four "Agents" modules, and so on. Both approaches work.
