## Brain Plasticity: How Your Brain Rewires Itself

Part of **Body Science**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Define** neuroplasticity as the brain's ability to physically change through experience
2. **Demonstrate** neuroplasticity through a mirror drawing challenge
3. **Describe** real examples of brain structure changing with learning
4. **Explain** how sleep helps the brain consolidate new learning
5. **Connect** physical brain changes to Active Inference model updating
6. **Analyze** how practice and repetition drive neural rewiring

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Neuroplasticity** | The brain's ability to change its physical structure through experience |
| **Synapse** | The connection point between two neurons |
| **Neural pathway** | A chain of connected neurons that fires together |
| **Consolidation** | When the brain strengthens new learning, especially during sleep |
| **Gray matter** | Brain tissue that contains neuron cell bodies |
| **Pruning** | When the brain removes unused or weak connections |

## Why This Matters

Your brain is not fixed -- it is changing right now as you read this. Every time you learn something new, your brain physically rewires itself. This means you are not stuck with the brain you were born with. Understanding neuroplasticity helps you realize that effort and practice literally change your brain, that sleep is not wasted time but essential for learning, and that your brain is an incredibly adaptable prediction machine.
