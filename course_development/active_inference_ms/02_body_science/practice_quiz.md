## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Your body maintains a steady internal temperature of about 98.6 degrees F. In Active Inference, this is an example of:

A) Your body making no predictions at all
B) Your body predicting what its internal state should be and acting to keep it there
C) A random coincidence
D) Something only your brain controls

2. Which of the following is a "system" inside your body?

A) Just your heart by itself
B) Your circulatory system — heart, blood vessels, and blood working together
C) A single blood cell with no connections
D) Your shoe

3. Your immune system detects a virus it has never seen before. In Active Inference terms, this is best described as:

A) A prediction error — the body encounters something it didn't expect
B) The body ignoring the problem
C) A system that has no boundaries
D) The immune system shutting down

4. When a doctor checks your reflexes by tapping your knee, the automatic kick that follows is an example of:

A) You choosing to kick
B) A prediction built into your nervous system that acts without conscious thought
C) A random muscle spasm
D) Something that has nothing to do with your brain

5. Your brain is about 2% of your body weight but uses about 20% of your energy. Why?

A) It is inefficient and wasteful
B) It is constantly running predictions and updating mental models, which takes a lot of energy
C) It is the largest organ in the body
D) It doesn't actually use that much energy

6. When you exercise and your heart rate increases, this is your body:

A) Making a mistake
B) Taking action to match the predicted need for more oxygen in your muscles
C) Breaking down
D) Entering a random state

7. Getting a vaccine trains your immune system by:

A) Making you permanently sick
B) Giving your immune system a preview so it can build a model and predict how to fight the real virus later
C) Replacing your immune cells with artificial ones
D) Eliminating all prediction errors forever

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how **sweating** on a hot day is an example of your body taking action to reduce a prediction error. What is the prediction, and what is the error?

2. Think about what happens when you eat something that makes you sick. How does your body **learn** from this experience? How might your behavior change next time you encounter that food?

3. Your body has many systems (digestive, respiratory, nervous, etc.) that all work together. Pick two systems and describe how they **communicate** with each other to keep you healthy. How is this like agents sharing information?
