## Senses and Signals: How Your Body Gathers Information

Part of **Body Science**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Name** at least eight senses your body uses (beyond the traditional five)
2. **Explain** sensory adaptation as the brain reducing attention to expected input
3. **Demonstrate** sensory prediction errors through temperature and smell experiments
4. **Describe** how the brain integrates information from multiple senses
5. **Identify** what happens when senses send conflicting information (motion sickness)
6. **Connect** sensory processing to Active Inference prediction and error correction

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Sensory organ** | A body part specialized for detecting a type of information |
| **Adaptation** | When your senses stop noticing a constant stimulus |
| **Integration** | Combining information from multiple senses into one experience |
| **Interoception** | Sensing what is happening inside your body |
| **Proprioception** | Knowing where your body parts are without looking |
| **Afterimage** | A visual impression that stays after the stimulus is removed |
| **Sensory conflict** | When different senses send contradictory information |

## Why This Matters

Right now your eyes are reading, your skin is sensing temperature, your inner ear is keeping you balanced, and your stomach is telling your brain whether you are hungry. Your senses are not just passively recording -- they are actively helping your brain check its predictions. Understanding how your body perceives the world helps you:
- Know why you stop noticing smells after a while
- Understand what causes motion sickness
- Appreciate how much work your brain does behind the scenes
- See perception as prediction, not just sensation
