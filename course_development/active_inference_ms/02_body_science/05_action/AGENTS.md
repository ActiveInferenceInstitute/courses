## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are physically active and many participate in sports, dance, or other activities requiring motor skills. They can immediately relate to the experience of improving at a physical skill through practice. Use ball throwing, balance challenges, and reflex tests to make motor control tangible.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Action as Prediction Fulfillment**: In Active Inference, action is not just a reaction -- it is the body making its predictions come true. When you reach for a glass of water, your brain predicted that the glass would be in your hand, and your muscles made that prediction happen.
- **Motor Prediction Error**: When a throw misses the target, the brain calculates a motor prediction error and adjusts the next throw. This is identical to perceptual prediction error but in the motor domain.
- **Reflexes as Prior Policies**: Reflexes are action policies that are so well-established they do not require conscious inference.

### Lab Design Principles
- Reflex testing for immediate physical engagement
- Ball toss accuracy tracking for motor learning data
- Scenario analysis for Active Inference connection
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes with simple materials

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Action, reflex, voluntary movement, motor command, muscle, accuracy, motor learning
