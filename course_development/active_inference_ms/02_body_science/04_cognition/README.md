# Module 04: Cognition

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Brain Processing: How Your Neurons Think

Part of **Body Science**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Identify** major brain regions and describe their primary functions
2. **Explain** how neurons transmit information along a chain
3. **Demonstrate** the Stroop Effect and explain why processing conflicts slow you down
4. **Describe** how different brain regions handle different levels of prediction
5. **Compare** automatic brain processing to deliberate processing
6. **Connect** brain processing to Active Inference's hierarchical prediction model

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Neuron** | A brain cell that sends and receives electrical signals |
| **Brain region** | A specific area of the brain with a specialized job |
| **Processing** | How the brain transforms raw input into understanding |
| **Signal** | An electrical or chemical message passed between neurons |
| **Stroop Effect** | Slower processing when two sources of information conflict |
| **Automatic** | Brain processing that happens without conscious effort |
| **Hierarchy** | A system of levels, from simple to complex |

## Why This Matters

Your brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons, and they are all working together right now to let you read, understand, and think about these words. Understanding how the brain processes information helps you appreciate how incredibly complex and efficient your thinking really is, and why sometimes your brain gets "stuck" on conflicting information.
