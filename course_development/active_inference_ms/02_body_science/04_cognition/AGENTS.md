## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Anatomy & health

- **Lab Style**: Investigation Lab
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are curious about what different parts of the brain do and how neurons work. The Stroop Effect is consistently engaging for this age group because it demonstrates their own brain "glitching" in real time. Keep neuroscience accessible -- use analogies like "your brain is like a city with different neighborhoods" rather than technical neural architecture descriptions.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Brain Regions as Hierarchical Models**: Different brain regions maintain different levels of the generative model. The visual cortex handles low-level predictions, while the prefrontal cortex handles high-level planning predictions.
- **Neural Signal Transmission**: Neurons pass prediction errors up and predictions down the hierarchy. The telephone game demonstrates how information transforms along a chain.
- **Processing Conflicts**: The Stroop Effect shows what happens when two predictions conflict (word meaning vs. ink color). The brain must resolve competing predictions.

### Lab Design Principles
- Brain region mapping for anatomical knowledge
- Neural telephone game for experiential understanding of signal transmission
- Stroop Effect for immediate, personal experience of processing conflicts
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Neuron, brain region, processing, signal, Stroop Effect, conflict, automatic, deliberate
