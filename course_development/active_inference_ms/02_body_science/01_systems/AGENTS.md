## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Anatomy & health
- **Topics**: Systems -- body systems, homeostasis, feedback loops, organ system interconnections
- **Lab Style**: Investigation Lab
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are learning about body systems in their regular science classes. This module connects that knowledge to Active Inference by framing body systems as prediction-and-correction machines. Students are interested in their own bodies, especially during puberty when their bodies are changing rapidly. Use concrete examples like heart rate recovery, sweating, and hunger signals.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Body Systems as Markov Blankets**: Each organ system has a boundary separating internal and external states. The skin is the most obvious boundary, but each organ system has its own functional boundary.
- **Homeostasis as Free Energy Minimization**: The body's drive to maintain stable conditions (temperature, pH, blood sugar) is a concrete example of minimizing prediction error. When conditions deviate from the "set point," the body acts to correct the deviation.
- **Prediction in the Body**: The immune system predicts self vs. non-self. The nervous system predicts sensory input. The digestive system predicts meal timing.

### Lab Design Principles
- Physical exercise component for direct experience of homeostasis
- Body system mapping for visual, hands-on learning
- Connection drawing for understanding system interdependence
- Prediction challenge for Active Inference connection
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Body system, homeostasis, feedback loop, organ, boundary, set point, regulation
