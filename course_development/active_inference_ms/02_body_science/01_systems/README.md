## Body Systems: Your Internal Network

Part of **Body Science**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Name** at least six major body systems and describe their primary functions
2. **Identify** the boundary, inputs, and outputs of each body system
3. **Explain** homeostasis as the body's way of maintaining stable internal conditions
4. **Demonstrate** a feedback loop in action through a heart rate recovery experiment
5. **Map** connections between body systems showing how they depend on each other
6. **Connect** body system regulation to Active Inference prediction and correction

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Body system** | A group of organs that work together to perform a specific job |
| **Homeostasis** | Your body keeping itself in a stable, balanced state |
| **Feedback loop** | When the result of a process feeds back to adjust the process |
| **Organ** | A body part with a specific function (heart, lungs, brain, etc.) |
| **Set point** | The target value your body tries to maintain (e.g., 98.6 degrees F) |
| **Regulation** | The process of keeping something within a normal range |
| **Boundary** | What separates one body system from others and from the outside world |

## Why This Matters

Your body is the most amazing prediction machine on the planet. Right now, your heart is pumping at exactly the right speed, your lungs are breathing at exactly the right rate, and your immune system is fighting off invaders you do not even know about. How does it all stay in balance? Body systems use the same principles as Active Inference -- constantly predicting, checking, and adjusting. Understanding how your body works as a system of systems helps you:
- Take better care of your health
- Understand why you feel the way you feel (tired, hungry, stressed)
- See the connection between biology and the prediction framework
