## Nerve Signals: How Your Body Sends Messages

Part of **Body Science**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Describe** how nerve signals travel through the body as electrical impulses
2. **Compare** the nervous system (fast, precise) with the endocrine system (slow, widespread)
3. **Measure** signal transmission speed through a group chain reaction activity
4. **Explain** why the body needs two different communication systems
5. **Identify** what happens when body communication breaks down
6. **Connect** internal body communication to Active Inference prediction maintenance

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Nerve signal** | An electrical impulse that travels along a neuron |
| **Synapse** | The gap between two neurons where chemical signals are exchanged |
| **Hormone** | A chemical messenger that travels through the blood |
| **Endocrine system** | The system of glands that produces hormones |
| **Electrical impulse** | A wave of electrical charge that moves along a nerve |
| **Myelin** | A coating around nerves that speeds up signal transmission |

## Why This Matters

Your body is constantly sending messages to itself. Nerve signals tell your muscles to move, your heart to beat, and your stomach to digest. Hormones tell your body to grow, respond to stress, and regulate your mood. Understanding how your body communicates internally helps you make sense of physical sensations, understand puberty and stress responses, and see your body as a prediction system that needs constant internal coordination.
