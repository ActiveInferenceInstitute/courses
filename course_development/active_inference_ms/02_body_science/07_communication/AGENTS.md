## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Anatomy & health
- **Topics**: Communication -- nerve signals, endocrine system, hormones, signal speed, communication breakdown
- **Lab Style**: Investigation Lab
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are experiencing hormonal changes firsthand (puberty), making the endocrine system personally relevant. The nervous system fascinates them because of its speed and connection to reflexes they tested earlier. Use the chain reaction game to make signal transmission physical and measurable.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Internal Communication as Prediction Maintenance**: The body's internal communication systems exist to maintain accurate predictions about the body's own state. The nervous system sends fast updates; the endocrine system sends slow, widespread updates.
- **Communication Failure as Prediction Error**: When signals go wrong (numbness, allergies, phantom limb pain), the body experiences internal prediction errors -- the brain's model of the body does not match what is actually happening.
- **Two Time Scales**: The nervous system operates on a fast time scale (perception-action), while the endocrine system operates on a slow time scale (learning and adaptation).

### Lab Design Principles
- Physical chain reaction for signal speed measurement
- Comparison table for understanding two communication systems
- Communication breakdown analysis for clinical relevance
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Nerve signal, neuron, synapse, hormone, endocrine system, electrical impulse, chemical messenger
