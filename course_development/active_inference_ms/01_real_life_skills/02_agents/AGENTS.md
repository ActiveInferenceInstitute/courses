# Station: Agents (Real Life Skills)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Agents -- what counts as an agent, goals, sensing, modeling, and acting
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Students already understand that people make choices, but they may not have thought about what makes a "chooser" different from a "thing." Use relatable comparisons: you vs. a rock, a pet vs. a toy, an NPC vs. a background object in a game. Students at this age are deeply interested in identity and autonomy, making "What makes an agent?" a naturally engaging question.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Agents as Free Energy Minimizers**: Frame agents as things that try to avoid surprise. Do not use the term "free energy" -- instead say "agents try to keep the world matching their expectations."
- **Generative Models**: Introduce the idea that every agent carries an internal picture of how the world works. For middle schoolers, call this a "mental model" or "your brain's best guess."
- **Goal-Directed Behavior**: Agents do not just react -- they have preferences about what should happen. Connect this to students' own goals (grades, friendships, games).

### Lab Design Principles
- Use agent profile cards and sorting activities for hands-on engagement
- Partner work for comparing different agents' perspectives
- Include a design challenge (robot agent) to make concepts concrete
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes with no tech required
- Use {fill:textarea} for written reflection

Agent, object, sensor, goal, internal model, action, surprise, prediction
