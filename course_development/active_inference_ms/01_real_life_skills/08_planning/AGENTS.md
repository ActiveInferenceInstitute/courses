# Station: Planning (Real Life Skills)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Planning -- decision trees, risk vs. reward, imagining futures, robust plans
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are beginning to plan more independently -- managing homework schedules, balancing social commitments, thinking about high school. They also understand planning from strategy games, sports, and project-based learning. Use weekend scheduling dilemmas and game strategy as entry points. Keep the emphasis on practical, real-world planning rather than abstract optimization.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Planning as Future Simulation**: In Active Inference, planning means simulating possible action sequences and evaluating their expected outcomes. Frame this as "planning is imagining different futures and picking the one with the least surprise."
- **Expected Free Energy**: Without using this term, convey that the best plan is the one that both gets you what you want (reward) and reduces uncertainty (information gain). Middle schoolers can understand "good plans give you what you want AND help you learn."
- **Robust Plans**: Good plans account for uncertainty. Connect this to Plan B thinking and flexible strategies.

### Lab Design Principles
- Decision tree building for visual, hands-on planning
- Risk-reward scoring for analytical thinking
- Dice rolls for experiencing uncertainty in planning
- Real plan design for immediate application
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Planning, decision tree, risk, reward, uncertainty, robust, flexible, scenario, outcome
