> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Thinking Fast and Slow

Part of **Real Life Skills**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Explain** cognition as the process of updating beliefs based on new evidence
2. **Distinguish** between fast automatic thinking and slow deliberate thinking
3. **Demonstrate** belief updating through a step-by-step inference activity
4. **Identify** common cognitive biases and explain why they happen
5. **Apply** slow thinking strategies to a real decision in your life
6. **Describe** how Active Inference views cognition as minimizing surprise

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Cognition** | The process of thinking, reasoning, and updating your beliefs |
| **Belief** | What you currently think is true about the world |
| **Updating** | Changing your belief when you get new evidence |
| **Inference** | Figuring something out based on clues and evidence |
| **Bias** | A mental shortcut that can lead to errors |
| **Fast thinking** | Quick, automatic, gut-feeling decisions |
| **Slow thinking** | Careful, step-by-step, deliberate reasoning |

## Why This Matters

Every day, you make hundreds of decisions -- most of them without even thinking about it. Should you raise your hand in class? What should you eat for lunch? Is that person being sarcastic or serious? Your brain handles most of this on autopilot (fast thinking), but sometimes you need to slow down and really think things through. Understanding how your brain does this helps you:
- Catch yourself when you are jumping to conclusions
- Make better decisions on tests and in social situations
- Recognize when biases are steering you wrong
- Understand how your brain is always trying to minimize surprise
