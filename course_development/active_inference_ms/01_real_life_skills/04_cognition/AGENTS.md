# Station: Cognition (Real Life Skills)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Cognition -- belief updating, fast vs. slow thinking, inference, and cognitive biases
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are at a developmental stage where they are beginning to think about their own thinking (metacognition). They notice when their gut feelings conflict with logic. Use puzzles, mystery scenarios, and real decision-making situations to make cognition tangible. Avoid framing biases as "mistakes" -- instead frame them as "shortcuts that usually work but sometimes misfire."

### Active Inference Integration
- **Belief Updating**: In Active Inference, cognition is about updating internal models based on new evidence. Frame this as "your brain changes its best guess when it gets new information."
- **Fast vs. Slow Inference**: Fast thinking corresponds to habitual, well-practiced predictions. Slow thinking corresponds to deliberate model revision. Both are forms of inference.
- **Cognitive Biases as Priors**: Biases are strong prior beliefs that resist updating. Help students see biases not as character flaws but as overly rigid predictions.

### Lab Design Principles
- Use classic cognitive puzzles (bat-and-ball problem) for immediate engagement
- Mystery box game for hands-on belief updating
- Bias spotting connects to social awareness and media literacy
- Decision design exercise applies concepts to real life
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Cognition, belief, updating, inference, bias, fast thinking, slow thinking, evidence
