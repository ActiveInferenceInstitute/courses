# Station: Action (Real Life Skills)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Action -- exploration vs. exploitation, policies, uncertainty reduction
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers face explore-exploit dilemmas constantly: trying a new activity vs. sticking with a familiar one, sitting with a new group at lunch vs. staying with friends. They also understand action sequences from gaming (combo moves, quest chains). Use these natural connections to make the concepts of policies and active exploration feel familiar.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Action as World-Changing**: In Active Inference, action is how an agent changes the world to better match its predictions. Frame this as "you act to make reality match your expectations."
- **Exploration vs. Exploitation**: Exploration reduces uncertainty (epistemic value) while exploitation maximizes expected reward (pragmatic value). Use the multi-armed bandit metaphor through a physical game.
- **Policies**: A policy is a planned sequence of actions. Help students see that good planning means picking action sequences, not just single actions.

### Lab Design Principles
- Physical game (mystery bags) for explore/exploit dilemma
- Real-life scenario sorting for application
- Action sequence mapping for policy thinking
- Competitive element (scoring) increases engagement
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Action, exploration, exploitation, uncertainty, policy, decision, risk, reward
