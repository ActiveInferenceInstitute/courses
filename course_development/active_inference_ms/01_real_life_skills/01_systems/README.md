# Module 01: Systems

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Systems Everywhere: Phones, Schools, and You

Part of **Real Life Skills**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Define** a system by identifying its boundary, parts, and connections
2. **Identify** at least three systems you interact with every day (social, technological, biological)
3. **Explain** what a feedback loop is and give a real-world example
4. **Compare** how different systems (like your school and your phone) share similar structures
5. **Predict** what happens when part of a system changes or breaks down
6. **Connect** the idea of systems to how your brain makes sense of the world

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **System** | A group of parts that work together inside a boundary |
| **Boundary** | The line between what is part of the system and what is not |
| **Feedback loop** | When the result of something circles back to change what happens next |
| **Input** | What goes into a system from the outside |
| **Output** | What a system produces or sends out |
| **Environment** | Everything outside the system boundary |
| **Homeostasis** | A system keeping itself balanced and stable |

## Prerequisites

- Curiosity about how things work
- Basic understanding of cause and effect
- No math or coding skills required for this module

## Estimated Time

- **Lesson**: 25-30 minutes
- **Lab**: 30-40 minutes
- **Quiz**: 10-15 minutes

## Why This Matters

You are surrounded by systems -- your school, your phone, your family, your body, your favorite video game. Understanding how systems work helps you:
- Figure out why things happen the way they do
- Predict what might happen next
- Make better decisions when things get complicated
- Understand how your own brain organizes the world

This is the foundation of Active Inference: your brain is constantly building mental models of the systems around you so it can predict what will happen and reduce surprise.
