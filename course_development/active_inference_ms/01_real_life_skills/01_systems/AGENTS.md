## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Systems -- boundaries, parts, connections, and feedback loops in daily life
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Students are 11-14 years old. They understand social dynamics intuitively but may not have vocabulary for systems thinking yet. Use their existing knowledge of friend groups, school structure, social media algorithms, and gaming ecosystems as entry points.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Systems as Markov Blankets**: Introduce the idea that every system has an inside and an outside separated by a boundary -- without using the term "Markov blanket." Use language like "what counts as part of the system and what does not."
- **Prediction Connection**: A system is something an agent needs to understand in order to make good predictions. Frame systems as "the world your brain is trying to figure out."
- **Surprise and Boundaries**: When the boundary of a system is unclear, surprise goes up. Relate this to real confusion students feel (e.g., "Wait, is this person in our friend group or not?").

### Lab Design Principles
- Activities should be completable in 30-40 minutes
- Require no technology beyond paper and markers (tech is optional enrichment)
- Include group discussion and collaborative mapping
- Use {fill:textarea} prompts for written reflection
- Include at least one table for structured data collection
- End with a reflection that connects back to Active Inference concepts

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
System, boundary, feedback loop, input, output, parts, connections, environment
