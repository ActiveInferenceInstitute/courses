## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Perception -- how your brain builds experience from predictions and sensory input
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers are fascinated by optical illusions and "brain tricks." Use this natural curiosity as a gateway to deeper concepts. Students at this age are also highly attuned to social perception -- reading facial expressions, interpreting texts, worrying about what others think. Both visual and social perception examples will land well.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Perception as Prediction**: The core Active Inference insight is that perception is not passive recording -- it is active prediction-checking. Frame this as "your brain is always guessing what will happen next, and perception is how it checks those guesses."
- **Prediction Error**: When reality does not match the brain's prediction, that gap is called prediction error. Use illusions to make this tangible.
- **Prior Beliefs**: Your past experiences shape what you expect to perceive. This explains why different people can see the same situation differently -- their priors are different.

### Lab Design Principles
- Start with a tactile prediction game (engaging, low-stakes)
- Use optical illusions for visual demonstration of prediction error
- Include social perception scenarios to connect to students' daily experience
- Journaling extension for ongoing observation
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Perception, prediction, prediction error, illusion, prior, sensory input, expectation
