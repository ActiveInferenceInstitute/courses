> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Seeing is Believing... Or Is It?

Part of **Real Life Skills**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Explain** that perception is not passive recording but active prediction-checking
2. **Define** prediction error as the gap between what your brain expects and what actually happens
3. **Demonstrate** how optical illusions reveal your brain's prediction machinery
4. **Analyze** how past experiences (priors) shape what you expect to perceive
5. **Recognize** prediction errors in social situations like reading texts or body language
6. **Connect** everyday perception to the Active Inference framework

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Perception** | How your brain builds an experience of the world from predictions and senses |
| **Prediction** | Your brain's best guess about what will happen next |
| **Prediction error** | The gap between what you expected and what actually happened |
| **Prior** | A belief or expectation based on past experience |
| **Sensory input** | Raw information coming in through your senses |
| **Illusion** | When your brain's prediction overrides what is actually there |
| **Updating** | Changing your prediction after getting new information |

## Why This Matters

Have you ever misread a text and thought someone was mad at you when they were not? Or seen something out of the corner of your eye that turned out to be nothing? That is your brain's prediction system at work. Understanding perception helps you:
- Catch yourself when your brain jumps to conclusions
- Understand why optical illusions fool everyone
- Realize that what you "see" is partly constructed by your own expectations
- Be more careful about assuming you know what others are thinking
