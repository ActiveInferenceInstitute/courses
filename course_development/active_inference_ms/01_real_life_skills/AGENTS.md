- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable. Light math (fractions, percentages). Scratch/block coding.

## Content Guidelines

### Course Theme
Real Life Skills is the gateway course for the Active Inference middle school curriculum. Every concept is introduced through the lens of decisions students make every day -- choosing friends, navigating social media, studying for tests, planning weekends. The goal is to make Active Inference feel personally relevant before students encounter it in more formal settings (Body Science, Math Detectives, Code & Create).

### Audience Awareness
Students are 11-14 years old. They are navigating social dynamics, developing independence, and forming identities. They respond well to content that takes them seriously, uses humor without being condescending, and connects to their actual experiences. Avoid "baby talk" but also avoid jargon dumps. Use examples from texting, gaming, social media, sports, school drama, and family life.

### Active Inference Integration
- Introduce all 8 spine concepts (Systems through Planning) through everyday scenarios
- Use the language of "predictions," "surprise," and "mental models" consistently
- Avoid formal notation -- use plain English equivalents for all Active Inference terms
- Build from concrete examples to general principles, never the reverse
- Each module should help students see themselves as Active Inference agents

### Lab Design Principles
- All labs are Group Challenges requiring 2-4 students
- Activities should be completable in 30-40 minutes
- Minimal materials needed (paper, markers, common classroom items)
- Technology is optional enrichment, never required
- Every lab includes {fill:textarea} prompts and at least one data collection table
- Labs end with a reflection connecting back to Active Inference
