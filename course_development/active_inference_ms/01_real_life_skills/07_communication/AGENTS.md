## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Everyday decision-making
- **Topics**: Communication -- shared prediction, modeling other minds, social inference, persuasion
- **Lab Style**: Group Challenge
- **Audience**: Grades 6-8
- **Tone**: Curious, meme-aware, relatable

## Content Guidelines

### Audience Awareness
Middle schoolers live in a world of constant communication -- texts, DMs, group chats, face-to-face conversations. Miscommunication is a daily source of drama. They intuitively understand that "K" means something different from "Okay!" but may not have a framework for WHY. Use texting, social media, and in-person social scenarios as the primary examples.

### Active Inference Integration
- **Communication as Shared Prediction**: In Active Inference, communication is how agents align their generative models. Frame this as "when you communicate, you are trying to get your mental model and someone else's mental model to match."
- **Theory of Mind**: Modeling another person's mental states is a core Active Inference operation. Middle schoolers are developing sophisticated theory of mind -- help them see it explicitly.
- **Persuasion as Belief Updating**: Persuading someone means providing evidence that causes them to update their priors. This explains why it is hard to change strongly held beliefs.

### Lab Design Principles
- Telephone game for experiencing prediction failure in communication
- Text message interpretation for social relevance
- Miscommunication analysis for real-world application
- Persuasion exercise for deeper engagement with belief updating
- All activities completable in 30-40 minutes

### Key Vocabulary for This Module
Communication, shared prediction, mental model, misunderstanding, theory of mind, persuasion, signal
