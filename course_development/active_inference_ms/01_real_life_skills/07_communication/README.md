# Module 07: Communication

> **Quick Navigation**: [Course Home](../README.md) | [Curriculum Home](../../README.md)

## Connected: Social Brains

Part of **Real Life Skills**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. **Explain** communication as the process of aligning mental models between people
2. **Demonstrate** how two-way communication reduces prediction error better than one-way
3. **Analyze** text messages and social signals using the concept of shared prediction
4. **Identify** how prior beliefs cause miscommunication
5. **Describe** persuasion as an attempt to update someone else's mental model
6. **Apply** Active Inference concepts to improve your own communication

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Simple Definition |
|------|------------------|
| **Communication** | Sharing information to align mental models between agents |
| **Shared prediction** | When two people have similar expectations about a situation |
| **Theory of mind** | The ability to model what someone else is thinking or feeling |
| **Misunderstanding** | When the sender's intended meaning does not match what the receiver understands |
| **Signal** | Any information sent from one agent to another |
| **Persuasion** | Trying to change someone else's beliefs by providing evidence |
| **Feedback** | Information sent back to the sender about whether the message was understood |

## Why This Matters

How many times has a text message been misunderstood? How often do you wonder what someone really meant? Communication is one of the hardest things humans do, and understanding it through Active Inference explains:
- Why "K" feels so different from "Okay!" even though they mean the same thing
- Why arguments happen when both people think they are right
- Why explaining something to a friend helps YOU understand it better
- Why listening is just as important as talking

Getting better at communication is one of the most useful skills you can develop -- for school, friendships, and everything else.
