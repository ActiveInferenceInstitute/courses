Applications

In High School, we see Action manifest in: * Specific Example 1: In calculus-based optimization, gradient descent is the mathematical analog of Active Inference action: just as rolling a ball downhill follows the steepest slope to reach a valley (minimum), an agent selects actions that follow the gradient of free energy downward -- each step reduces the gap between predicted and observed states, and the learning rate parameter controls how aggressively the agent acts on prediction errors. * Specific Example 2: The mathematical framework of control theory shows action as solving a differential equation: a thermostat's PID controller computes the error between desired temperature (setpoint) and measured temperature, applies proportional, integral, and derivative corrections, and outputs a heating action -- this closed-loop control is a concrete mathematical implementation of Active Inference's action-as-prediction-fulfillment principle.
