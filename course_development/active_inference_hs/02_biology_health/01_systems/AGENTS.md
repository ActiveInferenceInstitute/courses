# Station: Systems (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- cells, organs, and body systems as physical realizations of Active Inference principles
- **Topics**: Cell Membranes as Markov Blankets, Organ System Boundaries, Nested Systems, Homeostasis
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with biological research, diagram-drawing, and self-observation

## Content Guidelines

- Ground every Active Inference concept in real biology: the Markov blanket is not just a metaphor -- the cell membrane literally functions as one.
- Use diagrams and tables extensively; biology students expect visual aids.
- Reference specific biological structures (receptors, ion channels, membranes) rather than abstract placeholders.
- Connect to content from biology classes students may be taking concurrently (AP Bio, honors biology).

## Active Inference Integration

- The cell membrane is the canonical biological Markov blanket: receptor proteins are sensory states, transport proteins and secretion are active states.
- Homeostasis is the biological expression of free energy minimization -- the body acts to maintain preferred physiological states.
- Nested systems (organelle, cell, tissue, organ, organism) show how Markov blankets compose hierarchically.
- System failure (disease, injury) maps to blanket dissolution and loss of homeostatic capacity.

## Lab Design Principles

- Labs should combine research tasks (cell biology lookup) with safe self-observation (pulse, temperature).
- Include a nested-systems exercise to show hierarchy.
- Homeostasis investigations should map biological feedback loops to Active Inference terminology.
- Tables should bridge biology vocabulary and Active Inference vocabulary.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can map biological structures to Markov blanket components.
- Include questions about what happens when a biological boundary fails (disease, membrane damage).
- At least one question should compare boundaries at different biological scales.
