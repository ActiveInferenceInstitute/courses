# Station: Communication (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- mirror neurons, empathy circuits, hormonal signaling, and the social brain
- **Topics**: Mirror Neurons, Biological Empathy, Social Hormones, Multi-scale Communication
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with video observation, self-monitoring, and biological research

## Content Guidelines

- Present mirror neurons as a biological implementation of social Active Inference -- the brain simulates others' actions using its own motor model.
- Make empathy tangible through self-observation exercises during emotional video viewing.
- Cover communication at multiple biological scales: cell-to-cell, organ system, organism-to-organism, microbiome-to-host.
- Name specific chemicals (oxytocin, cortisol) and brain regions (anterior insula, anterior cingulate cortex).

## Active Inference Integration

- Social communication is model synchronization between biological agents at every scale.
- Mirror neurons implement social prediction by running the observer's motor model during observation.
- Empathy is the brain predicting another agent's internal states using shared neural circuits.
- Social hormones (oxytocin, cortisol) modulate the precision of social predictions.

## Lab Design Principles

- Mirror neuron research should trace the discovery and function with scientific detail.
- Empathy exercises should use safe video viewing with self-monitoring of bodily responses.
- Hormonal communication should be presented with specific chemicals and their social functions.
- Multi-scale comparison should show that communication principles are the same from cell to organism.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can explain mirror neurons as Active Inference mechanisms.
- Include questions about hormonal effects on social behavior and bonding.
- At least one question should compare communication at two different biological scales.
