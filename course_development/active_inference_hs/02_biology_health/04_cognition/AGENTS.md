# Station: Cognition (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- brain regions, neural hierarchies, and neurotransmitter systems
- **Topics**: Brain Region Mapping, Hierarchical Processing, Neurotransmitters as Precision Signals, Cognitive Disorders
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with neuroscience research and brain mapping exercises

## Content Guidelines

- Name specific brain regions and their functions; students should learn real neuroanatomy.
- Connect neurotransmitters to Active Inference precision-weighting -- this is one of the most powerful bridges between neuroscience and the framework.
- Frame cognitive disorders compassionately: they are not moral failures but disruptions of the generative model.
- Use diagrams and hierarchical maps to visualize brain organization.

## Active Inference Integration

- The brain's generative model is physically implemented across cortical hierarchies.
- Neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, acetylcholine) modulate precision-weighting.
- Cognitive disorders can be understood as disruptions in model accuracy, precision-weighting, or model updating.
- The hierarchical brain architecture directly implements hierarchical predictive coding.

## Lab Design Principles

- Brain mapping exercises should name at least six key regions with their cognitive roles.
- Hierarchical processing should use a concrete example (face recognition) to illustrate layers.
- Neurotransmitter analysis should bridge chemistry and computation.
- Disorder analysis should be respectful and connect to real treatment mechanisms.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can map brain regions to generative model components.
- Include questions about neurotransmitter roles in precision-weighting.
- At least one question should analyze a cognitive condition using Active Inference language.
