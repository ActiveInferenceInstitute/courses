# Station: Action (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- motor cortex, cerebellum, spinal reflexes, and proprioception
- **Topics**: Reflex Arcs, Proprioception, Motor Learning, Movement Disorders
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with safe motor experiments and neuroscience research

## Content Guidelines

- Frame motor control as "action as prediction fulfillment" -- the brain predicts where the body should be, and muscles move to make that prediction true.
- Use safe physical experiments (nose-touch, mirror tracing) that students can do in class.
- Name specific motor structures (motor cortex, basal ganglia, cerebellum, spinal cord).
- Connect movement disorders to prediction failures to show clinical relevance.

## Active Inference Integration

- In Active Inference, action works by the motor system fulfilling proprioceptive predictions (predicted body states become actual body states through muscle contraction).
- Reflexes are low-level, fast prediction-action loops that bypass higher cortical processing.
- Motor learning is the updating of cerebellar forward models through practice and prediction error correction.
- Movement disorders arise when motor predictions are disrupted at various levels of the hierarchy.

## Lab Design Principles

- Include at least two safe motor experiments (proprioception test, mirror tracing).
- Reflex mapping should trace the complete arc with named structures.
- Motor learning should be demonstrated through a task that shows improvement over trials.
- Disorder analysis should connect anatomy to computational failure.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can explain voluntary movement as prediction fulfillment.
- Include questions comparing reflexive and voluntary action in terms of Active Inference hierarchy.
- At least one question should connect a movement disorder to a specific prediction failure.
