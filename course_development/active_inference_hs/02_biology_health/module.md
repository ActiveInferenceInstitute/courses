## Introduction

Your body is a prediction machine. Long before neuroscientists formalized Active Inference, your immune system was already doing it -- maintaining models of what belongs inside you and what does not, predicting threats, and mounting responses to minimize biological surprise. Your body temperature, blood sugar, heart rate, and hormone levels are all regulated by systems that predict what the body will need next and act in advance to provide it.

This unit explores Active Inference as a biological principle. We will see that the same formal framework that describes decision-making and perception also describes how a cell maintains its membrane, how the immune system learns to recognize a virus, why the adolescent brain undergoes such dramatic rewiring, and why chronic stress can make you physically sick.

The payoff is not just theoretical. Understanding your body as an inference engine gives you a more precise vocabulary for understanding health, disease, and the relationship between mind and body -- a relationship that turns out to be far more intimate than most people realize.

## Key Themes

### The Body Predicts, Not Just Reacts

Traditional biology often describes the body as reactive -- something happens, and the body responds. Active Inference inverts this picture. Your body is constantly generating predictions about its future states and acting to fulfill those predictions. You do not shiver *because* you are cold; your body begins thermoregulatory processes *in anticipation* of cooling. This shift from reactive to predictive is one of the most important insights in modern biology.

### Health as Successful Inference

From an Active Inference perspective, health is the condition in which an organism's generative models accurately predict its sensory states, both internal and external. Disease, injury, and chronic stress are conditions in which prediction errors become persistently high -- the body's models no longer match its reality. This framework provides a powerful way to understand why stress is so damaging: it represents a sustained state of elevated free energy.

### The Adolescent Brain Is a Model Under Revision

During adolescence, the brain undergoes massive synaptic pruning and myelination -- essentially, it is revising and optimizing its generative models. This is why adolescence involves heightened emotional intensity, risk-taking, and identity exploration. Your brain is not broken; it is being rebuilt for the adult environment it predicts it will inhabit.
