# Station: Learning (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- synaptic plasticity, memory systems, and the developing teenage brain
- **Topics**: LTP/LTD, Memory Types, Synaptic Pruning, Critical Periods, Learning and Health
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with neuroscience research and health behavior analysis

## Content Guidelines

- Make synaptic plasticity concrete: LTP is the biological mechanism by which the generative model updates its parameters.
- The teenage brain angle is especially relevant -- students are living this experience right now.
- Connect learning biology to health behaviors (sleep, exercise, stress) that students can control.
- Present synaptic pruning positively: it is not losing brain power but gaining efficiency.

## Active Inference Integration

- Learning in Active Inference is model parameter updating; the biological substrate is synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD).
- Memory systems (working, episodic, procedural, semantic) correspond to different components of the generative model.
- Sleep-dependent memory consolidation maps to offline model optimization.
- The teenage brain's high plasticity represents a period of rapid model updating -- high sensitivity to prediction errors.

## Lab Design Principles

- LTP mechanism should be traced step by step at the synaptic level.
- Memory systems comparison should cover at least four types with brain regions.
- Teenage brain section should feel personally relevant and empowering, not alarming.
- Health factor analysis should give students actionable knowledge.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can explain LTP as a biological model update.
- Include questions about why sleep matters for learning (consolidation).
- At least one question should connect a health behavior to its effect on synaptic plasticity.
