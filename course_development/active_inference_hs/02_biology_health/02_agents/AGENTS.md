# Station: Agents (Biology & Health)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Biological mechanisms -- organisms as prediction machines across the complexity spectrum
- **Topics**: Biological Agency, Chemotaxis, Nested Agents, Agent Failure and Disease
- **Lab Style**: Investigation lab with biological research and comparative analysis

## Content Guidelines

- Use real organisms at multiple complexity levels: bacteria, plants, simple animals, mammals, humans.
- Chemotaxis in E. coli is the ideal entry point -- it is the simplest, most concrete biological Active Inference agent.
- Frame disease as a failure of biological agency, not just "something going wrong."
- Reference specific biological mechanisms (receptor proteins, neurotransmitters, hormones) rather than abstract descriptions.

## Active Inference Integration

- Every living organism is an Active Inference agent that maintains preferred states through action.
- Complexity varies enormously: bacteria have simple models, humans have deeply hierarchical ones.
- Nested agency is a key biological insight -- your immune cells are agents living within the larger agent that is you.
- Disease maps to breakdowns in the prediction-action loop: faulty sensors, wrong models, or ineffective actions.

## Lab Design Principles

- The complexity spectrum table should span at least four levels of biological complexity.
- Chemotaxis should be explored in enough detail for students to see the prediction-error comparison.
- "Hidden agents" within the body make the concept of nested agency concrete.
- Disease analysis should connect treatment to restoration of agency.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can identify agency in non-obvious organisms (plants, bacteria).
- Include questions that connect specific diseases to Active Inference failure modes.
- At least one question should ask students to compare agent complexity across species.
