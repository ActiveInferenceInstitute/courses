# Station: Perception (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- sensory experiences students have every day
- **Topics**: Prediction, Prediction Error, Top-down Processing, Priors, Optical Illusions
- **Lab Style**: Hands-on sensory experiments and partner activities (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Frame perception as "controlled hallucination" -- the brain predicts before it perceives.
- Use optical illusions and everyday surprises as entry points. Students love illusions.
- Encourage students to experiment on themselves and partners (safely).
- Keep the tone curious and fun -- perception is one of the most personally engaging topics.

## Active Inference Integration

- Perception is inference: the brain uses its generative model to predict sensory input, then corrects based on prediction errors.
- Prediction errors drive learning -- they tell the brain what to update.
- Priors (expectations) shape perception. The same sensory data can produce different percepts depending on context.
- Connect to the Free Energy Principle: perception minimizes surprise by updating internal states.

## Lab Design Principles

- Include at least one illusion-based activity and one real-world prediction error exercise.
- Partner activities encourage social engagement and make abstract concepts tangible.
- Sensory deprivation mini-experiments should be safe and require no special equipment.
- All observations should be recorded in structured tables.

## Question Design Standards

- Ask students to explain why illusions work, not just describe them.
- Include questions that link everyday perception errors to Active Inference vocabulary.
- At least one question should ask students to predict what would happen if a prior changed.
