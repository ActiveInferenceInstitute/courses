# Station: Action (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- decisions, habits, and the explore-exploit trade-off
- **Topics**: Pragmatic Action, Epistemic Action, Policies, Expected Free Energy, Self-Fulfilling Predictions
- **Lab Style**: Decision-making scenarios, observation exercises, and reflective writing (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Frame action not as a reaction to stimuli but as the fulfillment of predictions.
- Use relatable dilemmas: choosing a restaurant, studying for a test vs. going out, picking a college.
- Introduce the explore-exploit trade-off as something students experience every day.
- Emphasize that in Active Inference, action and perception work together -- they are not separate processes.

## Active Inference Integration

- Action is one of two ways to minimize free energy (the other is perception/belief updating).
- Pragmatic action moves the world toward predicted (preferred) states.
- Epistemic action gathers information to reduce uncertainty in the generative model.
- A policy is a sequence of actions evaluated by expected free energy, which balances reward-seeking and information-seeking.

## Lab Design Principles

- Include a concrete explore-exploit scenario with a decision table.
- Ask students to observe self-fulfilling prophecies in their own behavior.
- Policy mapping should use real decisions, not hypothetical grid worlds.
- Observation of others adds a social dimension and connects to later communication module.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should distinguish pragmatic from epistemic action in realistic scenarios.
- Include at least one question where students must identify the policy that minimizes expected free energy.
- Scenario-based questions should have nuanced answers (both exploration and exploitation can be justified).
