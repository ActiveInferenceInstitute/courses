# Station: Communication (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- conversations, texting, social media, and misunderstandings
- **Topics**: Model Synchronization, Social Inference, Shared Narratives, Communication Channels, Empathy
- **Lab Style**: Group games, text analysis, and partner exercises (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Frame communication as model synchronization: two agents aligning their internal states through signals.
- Use examples from texting, social media, and face-to-face conversation -- the three modes students use most.
- Present miscommunication not as failure but as a natural consequence of agents having different generative models.
- Connect empathy to Active Inference: understanding someone means predicting their internal states.

## Active Inference Integration

- Communication reduces mutual surprise between agents by aligning their generative models.
- Language, tone, facial expressions, and gestures are all signals that carry prediction-relevant information.
- Social inference is the brain's process of modeling other minds -- a generative model of another agent's generative model.
- Shared narratives are jointly maintained models that coordinate group behavior.

## Lab Design Principles

- Include at least one group game (telephone, collaborative storytelling) to make concepts social and fun.
- Text message analysis connects to students' daily communication experiences.
- Empathy exercises should use video or real observation, not hypothetical scenarios.
- Partner activities build the very communication skills the module describes.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can explain miscommunication using model divergence.
- Include questions about different communication channels and their effects on model synchronization.
- At least one question should ask students to analyze empathy as a form of Active Inference.
