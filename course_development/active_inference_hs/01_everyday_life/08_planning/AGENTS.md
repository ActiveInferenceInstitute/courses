# Station: Planning (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- decisions about the future, goals, and imagination
- **Topics**: Future Simulation, Counterfactual Thinking, Decision Trees, Risk vs. Ambiguity, Planning Horizon
- **Lab Style**: Decision-tree building, thought experiments, and reflective writing (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Frame planning as running the generative model forward in time to simulate possible futures.
- Use decisions students actually face: class selection, social events, career exploration, saving money.
- Distinguish risk (known odds) from ambiguity (unknown odds) -- this is a key Active Inference insight.
- Connect imagination and daydreaming to the computational purpose of planning.

## Active Inference Integration

- Planning is the evaluation of policies by simulating expected free energy over future time steps.
- Counterfactual thinking is the brain running its generative model under alternative action sequences.
- Risk and ambiguity map onto distinct components of expected free energy (pragmatic vs. epistemic value).
- The planning horizon is limited by the complexity and accuracy of the generative model -- distant futures are harder to simulate.

## Lab Design Principles

- Decision tree exercises should use real, personal decisions to keep engagement high.
- Counterfactual exercises should be reflective but grounded in recent events, not fantasy.
- Risk vs. ambiguity should use concrete examples with numbers so students can compare.
- Planning horizon experiments reveal how uncertainty grows with temporal distance.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can distinguish risk from ambiguity in novel scenarios.
- Include questions that ask students to build simple decision trees and identify the best policy.
- At least one question should connect planning to earlier modules (perception, action, learning).
