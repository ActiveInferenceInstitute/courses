# Station: Systems (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- use examples from a teen's daily routine
- **Topics**: Systems, Boundaries, Markov Blankets, Homeostasis
- **Lab Style**: Observation and mapping activity (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Write at a high school reading level (grades 9-12). Avoid jargon unless it is defined on first use.
- Every abstract concept (e.g., Markov blanket) must be paired with a concrete everyday example (e.g., the walls of a house, the case of a phone).
- Use second-person voice ("you," "your") to connect directly to the student's experience.
- Keep paragraphs short (3-5 sentences max).

## Active Inference Integration

- Frame "system" as anything that maintains a boundary between itself and everything else.
- Introduce the Markov blanket informally: the set of things that let a system sense the world and act on it.
- Connect homeostasis to everyday experiences: body temperature regulation, phone battery management, a school maintaining a schedule.
- Emphasize that systems are defined by their boundaries, not just their parts.

## Lab Design Principles

- Labs should require only observation, drawing, and writing -- no special equipment.
- Include at least three examples at different scales (personal, household, community).
- Provide fill-in tables so students organize their thinking visually.
- End with open-ended discussion questions that link back to Active Inference vocabulary.

## Question Design Standards

- Multiple-choice questions should have one clearly correct answer and three plausible distractors.
- Short-answer questions should ask students to apply concepts to a novel example (not just recall definitions).
- Include at least two questions that ask students to compare systems at different scales.
