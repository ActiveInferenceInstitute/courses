# Station: Cognition (Everyday Life)

> **Quick Navigation**: [Module README](./README.md) | [Course AGENTS](../AGENTS.md)

## Conventions

- **Perspective**: Real-world intuition -- mental models, biases, and everyday reasoning
- **Topics**: Generative Models, Latent States, Cognitive Biases, Bayesian Updating
- **Lab Style**: Reflective journaling, drawing, and scenario analysis (no coding required)

## Content Guidelines

- Use the metaphor "the map is not the territory" as the central framing device.
- Connect generative models to things students already understand: mental maps, expectations, assumptions.
- Present cognitive biases not as flaws but as features of a brain that must make fast predictions with limited information.
- Keep language accessible: "internal model" before "generative model," "hidden cause" before "latent state."

## Active Inference Integration

- Cognition is the maintenance and updating of generative models -- internal representations of how the world works.
- Latent states are the hidden variables that the brain infers from observable sensory data.
- Cognitive biases arise when the generative model uses overly rigid priors that resist updating.
- Bayesian updating is the formal mechanism: new evidence shifts the prior toward the posterior.

## Lab Design Principles

- Include a drawing or mapping exercise to make internal models visible and concrete.
- Bias identification exercises should use relatable scenarios (school, social media, sports).
- Bayesian updating should be practiced with a personal narrative, not abstract numbers.
- End with discussion questions that challenge students to think about when biases help vs. hurt.

## Question Design Standards

- Questions should test whether students can identify latent states in novel scenarios.
- Include questions that ask students to walk through a belief update step by step.
- At least one question should present a cognitive bias and ask students to reframe it using Active Inference vocabulary.
