# Practice Quiz: Cognitive Behavioral Science & Active Inference

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. In the cortical hierarchy, what do top-down connections primarily carry?
A) Raw sensory data
B) Motor commands
C) Predictions about expected inputs
D) Reward signals

2. Which neuromodulator is most associated with precision weighting of reward prediction errors?
A) Serotonin
B) Acetylcholine
C) Dopamine
D) GABA

3. The default mode network is most active during:
A) Intense visual processing
B) Motor execution
C) Rest and self-referential thought
D) Fear conditioning

4. In the Active Inference account of schizophrenia, hallucinations may arise from:
A) Reduced activity in the visual cortex
B) Aberrantly high precision on internally generated predictions
C) Complete loss of the Markov Blanket
D) Excessive motor output

5. The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that:
A) Body ownership is hardwired and cannot be altered
B) The brain's generative model of the body can be updated by multisensory prediction errors
C) Visual information always dominates tactile information
D) Markov Blankets are purely physical boundaries

6. In predictive coding, what is the function of a "prediction error"?
A) It signals that the brain has stopped making predictions
B) It represents the difference between predicted and observed sensory input
C) It always leads to motor action
D) It indicates a permanent failure of the generative model

7. Which brain structure is most associated with the salience network's role in precision weighting?
A) Hippocampus
B) Anterior insula and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex
C) Cerebellum
D) Primary visual cortex (V1)

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how anxiety disorders can be understood as a miscalibration of precision in the Active Inference framework. What specific prediction errors are overweighted, and what behavioral consequences follow?

2. Describe the relationship between synaptic plasticity and Bayesian model updating. How does long-term potentiation (LTP) serve as a neural mechanism for updating the generative model?

3. A patient with damage to the right parietal lobe denies that their left arm is paralyzed (anosognosia). Explain this condition using Active Inference concepts, specifically addressing the failure to update the self-model in response to prediction errors.
