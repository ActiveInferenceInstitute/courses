# Study Questions: Cognitive Behavioral Science & Active Inference

1. How does the hierarchical organization of the cortex — from primary sensory areas to higher association cortex — correspond to the hierarchical depth of the generative model in Active Inference?

2. Explain the role of prediction errors in the visual cortex, referencing Rao and Ballard's (1999) predictive coding model. How do top-down predictions and bottom-up errors interact in V1?

3. Describe the brain-body-environment loop as a Markov Blanket. What structures constitute the sensory blanket states, and what structures constitute the active blanket states?

4. How does precision weighting relate to the neuromodulatory systems (dopamine, acetylcholine, norepinephrine, serotonin)? Give a specific example of how one neuromodulator adjusts precision in the brain.

5. Explain how schizophrenia might be understood as a disorder of precision in the Active Inference framework. What specific prediction does this account make about neural activity in patients with hallucinations?

6. Compare the Active Inference account of autism (high sensory precision) with the weak central coherence theory. What empirical evidence distinguishes these accounts?

7. How do resting-state fMRI findings about the default mode network support the idea that the brain maintains an ongoing generative model even in the absence of external stimulation?

8. Describe the rubber hand illusion in Active Inference terms. What prediction errors drive the illusion, and what does the illusion reveal about the generative model of the body?

9. How does Active Inference account for the difference between habitual and goal-directed behavior? What neural systems are associated with each, and how does precision modulate the transition between them?

10. Explain how synaptic plasticity implements the gradient descent on variational free energy that constitutes learning. What is the relationship between Hebbian learning and Bayesian updating?

11. What role does sleep play in the Active Inference account of learning? How might REM sleep and slow-wave sleep implement different aspects of model optimization?

12. Describe anosognosia (unawareness of deficit after stroke) as a failure of generative model updating. What does this condition reveal about the brain's self-model?

13. How does the Active Inference framework explain selective attention? What happens at the neural level when attention is directed to a particular sensory modality?

14. Evaluate the strengths and limitations of Active Inference as a unifying theory of brain function. What phenomena does it explain well, and where does it struggle?

15. How does the concept of interoception (sensing the body's internal states) extend the Active Inference framework beyond exteroceptive perception? What neural structures are involved?
