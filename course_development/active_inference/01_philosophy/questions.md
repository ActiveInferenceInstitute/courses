# Study Questions: The Philosophy of Active Inference

1. How does the Markov Blanket formalism address the classical philosophical problem of demarcation — distinguishing a system from its environment? What advantages does it offer over purely material or spatial definitions of boundaries?

2. Trace the intellectual lineage from Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis to Friston's Free Energy Principle. What is preserved in this transition, and what is added or transformed?

3. Evaluate the life-mind continuity thesis. Does treating all self-organizing systems as performing inference commit the "fallacy of equivocation," or does it reveal a genuine structural unity in nature?

4. In what sense are beliefs "physical states" in the Active Inference framework? How does this compare to Fodor's language of thought and Ryle's dispositional account?

5. The dark room problem objects that free-energy-minimizing agents should seek maximally predictable environments. Reconstruct this objection and explain how the role of prior preferences (the C vector) resolves it.

6. How does Active Inference relate to phenomenological accounts of perception, particularly Merleau-Ponty's claim that the body is not merely in the world but of the world?

7. Does the Free Energy Principle function as a law of nature, a modeling framework, or a tautological redescription? Present arguments for at least two of these positions and defend your own view.

8. Explain the concept of precision in Active Inference and connect it to philosophical theories of attention. How does precision weighting determine what an agent "cares about"?

9. How does the Active Inference account of action as inference relate to the philosophical debate about free will? Can an agent whose actions are determined by expected free energy minimization be said to act freely?

10. Compare Gibson's ecological psychology (direct perception through affordances) with the Active Inference account of perception (inference through generative models). Are these frameworks compatible or fundamentally opposed?

11. What philosophical work does the concept of "surprise" do in Active Inference? How does it differ from the everyday use of the word, and what are the risks of equivocation?

12. Explain the extended mind hypothesis (Clark and Chalmers, 1998) in terms of Markov Blankets. If cognitive processes extend beyond the brain, where does the Markov Blanket lie?

13. How does the concept of epistemic action — acting to gain information rather than to achieve a goal — challenge traditional instrumental accounts of rationality?

14. Describe how Active Inference addresses the problem of other minds. How does coupled inference between agents formalize intersubjectivity?

15. What role does teleology play in the Active Inference account of planning? Is sophisticated inference genuinely purposive, or does it merely simulate purpose through expected free energy optimization?
