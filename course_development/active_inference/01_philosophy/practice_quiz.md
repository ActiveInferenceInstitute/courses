# Practice Quiz: The Philosophy of Active Inference

## Part A: Multiple Choice

1. Which philosophical tradition is most directly connected to the Active Inference concept of the body as constitutive of cognition rather than merely housing it?
A) Logical positivism
B) Phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty)
C) Cartesian dualism
D) Eliminative materialism

2. The life-mind continuity thesis holds that:
A) Consciousness requires a central nervous system
B) There is no sharp boundary between being alive and having a mind
C) Artificial systems cannot exhibit cognition
D) Life and mind are entirely independent phenomena

3. In Active Inference, what resolves the "dark room problem"?
A) Agents cannot minimize free energy in practice
B) The dark room has maximum entropy
C) Prior preferences (the C vector) make the dark room surprising relative to the agent's expectations
D) Agents always maximize rather than minimize surprise

4. The Markov Blanket in Active Inference formalizes:
A) The skull as the boundary of cognition
B) The statistical separation of internal and external states through sensory and active states
C) The physical membrane of a biological cell
D) The difference between conscious and unconscious processes

5. How does Active Inference treat the relationship between perception and action?
A) Perception occurs first, then action follows as a separate process
B) Action is primary and perception is epiphenomenal
C) Both are manifestations of the same inferential process — free energy minimization
D) Perception and action are unrelated processes that happen to co-occur

6. Autopoiesis, as introduced by Maturana and Varela, refers to:
A) The evolution of species through natural selection
B) The self-producing organization of living systems that generate and maintain their own boundaries
C) The computational theory of mind
D) The social construction of knowledge

7. The concept of "precision" in Active Inference corresponds most closely to which philosophical and cognitive concept?
A) Logical validity
B) Attention — the weighting of certain prediction errors over others
C) Memory consolidation
D) Linguistic accuracy

## Part B: Short Answer

1. Explain how the Ship of Theseus paradox is addressed from a Markov Blanket perspective. What does Active Inference suggest about the nature of identity through change?

2. Compare the philosophical implications of treating beliefs as probability distributions (Active Inference) versus treating them as sentences in a language of thought (Fodor). What is gained and what is lost in each account?

3. Construct a brief philosophical argument either for or against the claim that the Free Energy Principle is a genuine law of nature rather than a useful modeling framework. Reference at least one philosopher or tradition discussed in this section.
