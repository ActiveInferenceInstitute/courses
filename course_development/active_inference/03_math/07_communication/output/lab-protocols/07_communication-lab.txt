Lab: Derivation Exercise — Coupled Inference and Multi-Agent Free Energy

Objective

Derive the mathematics of communication between Active Inference agents, from coupled generative models to generalized synchrony conditions.

Part 1: Two-Agent Generative Models

Goal: Formalize how two agents model each other.

Setup: Agent A and Agent B, each with 2 states. Agent A's generative model:

pA(oA | sA, sB) — likelihood depends on both agents' states
pA(sA) = N(sA; 0, 1) — prior over own states
pA(sB) = N(sB; 0, σ²B) — prior over B's states (higher uncertainty)

Agent B's generative model is symmetric.

Task:

Write A's free energy FA as a function of qA(sA) and qA(sB)
Derive the update equations for A's beliefs about sA and sB
Show that A's beliefs about B depend on A's observations (which are influenced by B's actions)

Part 2: Joint Free Energy Decomposition

Goal: Derive the relationship between individual and joint free energy.

Task:

Write the joint free energy: Fjoint = FA + FB - I(sA; sB)
Show that the mutual information I(sA; sB) = E[ln q(sA, sB) / (q(sA)q(sB))] captures the coupling
Explain why minimizing Fjoint simultaneously minimizes individual free energies and maximizes mutual predictability
Interpret: when I(sA; sB) is high, the agents are in tight communication; when it is low, they are operating independently

Part 3: Generalized Synchrony Conditions

Goal: Derive the conditions for synchronization.

Setup: Two coupled dynamical systems:

dx₁/dt = f₁(x₁) + κ · (x₂ - x₁)
dx₂/dt = f₂(x₂) + κ · (x₁ - x₂)

where κ is the coupling strength.

Task:

Define the synchronization error e = x₁ - x₂
Derive the dynamics of e: de/dt = f₁(x₁) - f₂(x₂) - 2κ · e
Show that for identical systems (f₁ = f₂ = f), e → 0 is stable if κ > λmax/2, where λmax is the maximum Lyapunov exponent of f
Interpret: coupling must be strong enough relative to the internal dynamics for synchronization to occur

Part 4: Theory of Mind as Hierarchical Modeling

Goal: Formalize Theory of Mind mathematically.

Setup: Agent A's hierarchical model:

Level 1: pA(oA | sA) — how A's states generate A's observations
Level 2: pA(sA | sB model) — how B's model of the world influences A's states
Level 3: pA(sB model | s_B meta-model) — A's model of B's model of A

Task:

Write the free energy for this three-level hierarchy
Show how increasing hierarchical depth enables more sophisticated social inference
Compute the additional cost (complexity) of each added level
Discuss: what is the optimal depth of Theory of Mind modeling?
